Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the gods of the inhabitants are a snare for Israelites who serve them, and the Israelites are told not to invoke, bow down to, serve, or make a covenant with them. In Deuteronomy, other gods are the deities of surrounding and distant peoples, unknown to Israel and its fathers, whose gold and silver images are an abomination to the LORD and whose worship Israel is warned against. In Deuteronomy, other gods are the strange gods of the land after whom Israel will play the prostitute, causing the LORD to hide his face and bringing evils upon Israel. In The Book of Joshua, the gods of the nations were served by Terah beyond the River and are presented as alternatives to the LORD, which the people are told to put away. In The Book of Judges, the gods of the peoples are foretold as a snare to Israel, and Israel's worship of them provokes the LORD to anger. In Psalms, the gods of the peoples are declared to be idols, in contrast to the LORD who made the heavens, and the LORD is said to be feared above all gods.

other gods in Deuteronomy

author unknown · 624 BC · the work’s page · read it where other gods first appears

Abstract written by a model

Other gods are the deities of surrounding and distant peoples, unknown to Israel and its fathers. Their gold and silver images are an abomination to the LORD. Chapter 7 warns that intermarriage could lead Israel's sons to serve them, kindling the LORD's anger and becoming a snare, so engraved images must be burned. Chapter 13 shows prophets, secret enticers, and wicked city fellows all calling Israel to serve them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

Other gods are what the nations' influence could lead Israel's sons to serve if intermarriage occurs, which would kindle the LORD's anger. Israel is told not to serve their gods, because doing so would be a snare. The engraved images of these gods are to be burned, and they are called an abomination.

Chapter 13

The other gods are described as gods Israel and its fathers have not known, belonging to the peoples near and far, from one end of the earth to the other. Calls to go and serve them are the common element in all three cases: the prophet, the secret enticer, and the wicked fellows of a city.
