Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, the famine follows seven years of plenty, becomes very grievous over all the earth, and leads Joseph to open Egypt’s storehouses and sell grain. In Surah 12: Joseph (Yusuf), the famine follows seven years of abundance, consumes the prepared provisions, and leads people to seek food through Joseph’s management of the storehouses.

famine in Genesis

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where famine first appears

Abstract written by a model

A seven-year famine follows seven years of plenty, which Joseph interprets from the thin cattle and blasted grain. It is very grievous and severe over all the earth, though Egypt alone has bread, and Joseph opens the storehouses to sell grain. As it continues, the grain from Egypt is eaten up, driving the sons back to buy more food so they may live.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 41

Joseph interprets the thin cattle and blasted grain as seven years of famine that will follow the plenty, make the plenty forgotten, and consume the land. The famine begins just as Joseph said and is severe over all the earth. Egypt alone has bread, and Joseph opens the storehouses to sell grain.

Chapter 43

The famine is severe in the land. It drives the story: the grain brought from Egypt is eaten up, and their father sends the sons back to buy more food. Judah argues that going is necessary so that they and their little ones may live and not die.
