Yi Yin

person · 3 works · 17 quoted facts · 6 connections

Abstract written by a model

In Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou), Yi Yin instructs Tang, becomes his minister, and takes office through both good government and disorder. In Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang), Yi Yin serves as a farmer, sage, and servant of sovereigns who instructs Tang, presses the attack on Xia, places Tai Jia in Tong, and seeks benefits for all. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), Yi Yin banishes disobedient Tai Jia to Tong and brings him back after he becomes virtuous.

Yi Yin in Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang)

Mencius · about 300 BC · the work’s page · read it where Yi Yin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Yi Yin is a farmer, sage, and servant of sovereigns. First farming in the lands of the prince of Xin, he delighted in the principles of Yao and Shun. Invited three times, he entered Tang’s service to instruct the people and pressed Tang’s attack on Xia to save them. He later placed Tai Jia in Tong until repentance and reform. Taking office in good government and confusion, he accepts charge of the kingdom, instructs slower people, and seeks benefits for all.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang) / Part I / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 7

Yi Yin first lived as a farmer and delighted in the principles of Yao and Shun. He entered Tang’s service to instruct the people, urged Tang to attack Xia, and later placed Tai Jia in Tong until he repented and reformed.

Part II / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 5

Yi Yin believed he could serve any ruler and command any people if service or command made them his sovereign or people. He took office in both good government and confusion and felt responsible for instructing the uninformed and securing benefits for all people.
