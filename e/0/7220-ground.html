Abstract written by a model

Ground where dogs or men have died lies fallow for a year, without sowing or watering. It is then inspected and made fit for tillage by removing bones, hair, flesh, dung, and blood. Ground exposed to a dead man recovers after one year; buried corpses require fifty years, and beneath a Dakhma recovery awaits mingled dust.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard VI.

Ground where dogs or men have died must lie fallow for a year and must not be sown or watered during that time. Before it is tilled again, it must be inspected for bones, hair, flesh, dung, and blood.

Fargard VII.

Ground where a dead man's corpse lies becomes itself again after one year, while earth containing a buried corpse becomes itself again after fifty years. Ground beneath a Dakhma does not recover until the corpse's dust mingles with the earth.
