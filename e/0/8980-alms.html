Abstract written by a model

Alms concern food, drink, dainties, and spices obtained on a begging-tour. They may be accepted only when pure and acceptable. Food procured through theft, force, sinful conduct, or preparations for ascetics must be rejected. Monks and nuns should seek other families when entertainment food would cause sin. Alms from Kshatriyas, kings, messengers, or kings’ relations are impure and unacceptable.

Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book II, translated by Hermann Jacobi / Second Book / First Part. / First Lecture, Call

Alms may be accepted only when the food or drink is pure and acceptable. Food procured through theft, force, sinful conduct, or certain preparations for others must be rejected.

Third Lesson.

Monks and nuns should obtain their meal from other families when food at an entertainment would be unacceptable or lead to sin. Alms received from the houses of Kshatriyas, kings, messengers, and relations of kings are impure and unacceptable.

Ninth Lesson.

âdhâkarmika food is food procured or prepared for the sake of ascetics or a particular mendicant. It is described as impure and unacceptable and should not be accepted.
