Abstract written by a model

In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, birth continues without number for beings in Samsâra and ends through true knowledge, restraint, and the true Law. In Sutrakritanga, Book II, birth describes beings arising in various bodies and connects their origins with Karman. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, birth is part of the precarious and transitory condition of human life and motivates abandonment of worldly life. In Chuang Tzŭ, birth is a stage in the natural transformations governed by destiny, following the assumption of form and preceding death and return to the great Scheme.

Abstract written by a model

Birth continues without number for beings in Samsâra following wrong doctrines, taking many existences and repeatedly being born after harming living beings. Human birth is difficult to obtain; men may die at any stage. Birth ends through true knowledge, restraint, and the true Law.

birth in Chuang Tzŭ

Chuang Tzŭ · the work’s page · read it where birth first appears

Abstract written by a model

The pure men of old did not rejoice in birth or strive to postpone dissolution. Birth is treated as one of the conditions included within the transitions governed by destiny. Birth is described as a stage following the addition of substance to spirit and the assumption of form. It is one phase in a sequence of natural transformations that continues into death and return to the great Scheme.
