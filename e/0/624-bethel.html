Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Bethel is where Jacob dreams, names the place God's house, later builds an altar, and where Deborah is buried. In The Book of Judges, Bethel, formerly Luz, is spied out and struck by the sword by the house of Joseph. In The First Book of Kings, Bethel is where Jeroboam establishes a golden calf, altar, high-place priests, and sacrifices, and where a man of God prophesies against the altar. In The Second Book of Kings, Bethel is where Josiah breaks down and burns Jeroboam's altar and high place, fulfilling the man of God's word. In The Book of Amos, Bethel is called Israel's king's sanctuary and royal house, which the LORD declares will come to nothing.

Bethel in Genesis

Abstract written by a model

Bethel is a place in Canaan, formerly the city Luz, west of Abram's tent with Ai to the east. Jacob names it after sleeping and dreaming there, calling it God's house and the gate of heaven. Later God speaks with Jacob there again; he builds an altar, calls it El Beth El, sets up a stone pillar, and Deborah, Rebekah's nurse, is buried below it.

Chapter 28

Bethel is the name Jacob gives to the place where he slept and dreamed, which was formerly called Luz as the name of the city. Jacob declares it none other than God's house and the gate of heaven.

Chapter 35

Bethel, also called Luz, is in the land of Canaan, and Jacob builds an altar there and calls the place El Beth El because God was revealed to him there when he fled from his brother. God speaks with Jacob there again, and Jacob sets up a stone pillar and names the place Bethel. Deborah, Rebekah's nurse, is buried below it under the oak called Allon Bacuth.

Bethel is a place where Jeroboam set a golden calf, built an altar, installed high-place priests, and sacrificed and burned incense at a feast. It then becomes the site of the man of God's prophecy against that altar, home of the old prophet, and the prophecy extends to high-place houses across Samaria's cities.

Chapter 12

Bethel was where Jeroboam set one of the golden calves and built an altar. There he placed priests of the high places, sacrificed to the calves, and burned incense at a feast on the fifteenth day of the eighth month.

Chapter 13

Bethel is the place where Jeroboam's altar stands and where the man of God delivers his prophecy against it. It is also where the old prophet lives and where the man of God was forbidden to eat bread or drink water. The prophecy extends against all the high-place houses in the cities of Samaria.
