Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, Ahura Mazda is the supreme Creator, recipient of worship, source of sacred words, ruler of the Kingdom, and giver of salvation. In Yasna, Ahura Mazda is the resplendent holy ruler who creates the Kine, waters, and plants, fathers Fire, grants protection and rewards, and converses with Zarathustra. In Yasna, Ahura Mazda is the glorious Creator and greatest lord whose worship affirms his Kingdom and includes care for the poor. In Yasna, Ahura Mazda is the all-wise Creator who grants prosperity, protection, strength, righteous order, and salvation to his worshippers. In Visparad, Ahura is addressed by the Zaotar in connection with his wives and the bounteous Âramaiti. In Visparad, Mazda is associated with creating the stormy wind and Sleep and with remedies accompanying offerings. In Visparad, Ahura is associated with questions and lore and with the Ahurian holy lords of ritual order. In Visparad, Ahura Mazda is the holy Lord of ritual order who receives worship and offerings, reveals lore, and directs sacred ritual.

In Afrinagan, Gahs and Miscellaneous Fragments, Ahura Mazda is the radiant Lord and highest ritual object who creates, rules, receives worship, and declares rewards. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII, Ahura Mazda is the holy Maker who establishes lands and waters, teaches Yima and Zarathustra, and prescribes religious law. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Ahura Mazda is the Holy One and Maker who creates, heals, teaches authoritative law, and prescribes purity and protection. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Ahura Mazda is the Holy Maker and source of sacred law who prescribes atonement, purification, worship, and the souls’ fate. In Sirozahs (Sîrôzahs), Ahura Mazda is the bright and glorious Maker to whom prayers and sacrifices are offered. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, Ahura Mazda is the holy creator Lord and teacher who establishes creation, directs other divine beings, and aids the faithful against evil. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, Ahura Mazda is the supreme creator and ruler who establishes the world, commands the Amesha-Spentas, teaches Zarathustra, and directs worship. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, Ahura Mazda is the Holy Creator of the good world who creates beings and Zarathustra, preserves the Holy Word, protects the faithful, and grants rewards. In Nyayish, Ahura Mazda is the honored Lord invoked before all creatures, source of Âtar, and giver of piety and strength. In Nyayish, Ahura Mazda is the lord who wills holiness, creates waters and plants, brings down Anahita’s river, and instructs Zarathustra to sacrifice to her.

Abstract written by a model

Ahura Mazda is the Creator: radiant, glorious, greatest, best, wisest, holy, and most beneficent. He made the speaker and the good creation, including the corporeal worlds, creatures, Fire, waters, plants, the Kine, mountains, the Wind, and the dwelling-place. Fire is called his son, while the waters and other sacred things are Mazda-made. He fashions, nourishes, protects, and provides nurture, especially for the poor; he rules the Kingdom and is asked to reign with a saving rule over his creatures. He is the central recipient of worship: sacrifice, homage, propitiation, praise, prayers, gifts, offerings, and confessions of debt are directed to him. Ritual offerings and sacred objects are presented to him, and he is associated with H(a)oma through the girdle he bears, the portions he chooses, and the offering he gives to bless H(a)oma. He is also the holy lord of the ritual order and the source or speaker of sacred words, including Ashem Vohû and the Ahuna-vairya. The records portray him as prior to the whole creation and supreme over it: he thought, spoke, disposed, and did all things good; recognizes sanctity and superior fidelity; repels the evil one; and grants salvation. He tells Zarathustra that Ahuna-vairya preceded sky, waters, land, creatures, and the corporeal world, and that its proper recitation can bring a soul to Heaven. His holy Fravashi is worshipped as greatest and best, attaining its ends through Righteousness.

Ahura Mazda is invoked as the supreme recipient of worship, sacrifice, gifts, prayers, and confessions of debt. He is said to have thought, spoken, disposed, and done all things good, and the Kingdom belongs to him to provide nurture to the poor.

Yasna XV (Sp. XVI).

Ahura Mazda is described as most beneficent and holy. The sacrifice is offered to Ahura Mazda, for whom Sraosha (Obedience) is asked to be present.

Yasna XVI (Sp. XVII).

Ahura Mazda is worshipped as the holy lord of the ritual order, the greatest and most beneficent Yazad, and the creator of good creatures. He is also worshipped as the creator of the Fire, waters, the soul of the Kine, the Wind, and the dwelling-place.

Yasna XVII.

Ahura Mazda is named as the father of the Fire, which is called his son. The waters, plants, Mount Ushi-darena, and other sacred things are described as Mazda-made.

Yasna XVIII.

Mazda is described as the maker of the Kine, plants, and waters. Mazda is asked to grant Immortality, Weal, and Spirit bounteous.

Yasna XIX.

Ahura Mazda tells Zarathustra that the Ahuna-vairya was pronounced before the sky, waters, land, creatures, and the entire corporeal world. He says that reciting it correctly has great spiritual value and can bring a soul across the Bridge of Kinvat to Heaven, while omitting parts draws the soul away from the better world. He is presented as the creator, ruler, and source of the creatures, the Kingdom, and the nourishment of the poor.

Yasna XX.

Ahura Mazda is said to have spoken forth the words of the Ashem Vohû. Every word and the entire utterance of the prayer are identified as the word of Ahura Mazda.

Yasna XXI.

Ahura Mazda recognises superior fidelity by reason of sanctity. He is described as the absolute ruler who grants salvation, and the Yasna is offered as his command or institution.

Yasna XXIII.

His Fravashi is approached by the speaker with praise and worship.

Yasna XXIV.

Ahura Mazda receives presentations of Haomas, Myazdas, Zaothras, the Baresman, flesh, milk, Hadhânaêpata, ritual objects, prayers, and all good Mazda-made objects. He is addressed as the maker of the good objects and as the father of the Fire.

Yasna XXVI.

Ahura Mazda's Fravashi is worshipped as the greatest, best, most beautiful, firmest, wisest, and best in form. It attains its ends most because of Righteousness.

Ahura Mazda in Yasna

L.H. Mills · the work’s page · read it where Ahura Mazda first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ahura Mazda is a deity: the all-wise Lord, Creator, bountiful and most beneficent holy one. He made the stormy wind and is described as maker of the Kine, plants, waters, and all good things; he also causes plants to flourish for the Kine’s nurture. He possesses the Bountiful Spirit, wise intelligence, Good Mind, Righteous Order, Sovereign Power, and a sovereign realm. Through these powers he establishes and grants Weal, Welfare, Immortality, royal power, saving support, and prosperity, especially to his chosen saint and worshippers. He is father of Good Mind and Righteous moral Order, gives Âramaiti and Fire, and made the joy-creating Kine. He despises the Daêva-gods. Across the records, he is praised and sought through Righteousness, Good Mind, Piety, ritual offerings, and worship with the Bountiful Immortals and Sraosha. Worshippers ask him for protection, truth, strength, courage, good rule, victory, reward, blessing, healthy herds and settlements, and salvation. He shows Zarathustra how to pray and offer to the waters, and is asked to grant Immortality and Welfare through Good Mind in the doctrine. At the last ending of life, he promises to keep the soul away from Hell when prescribed words are pronounced.

Ahura Mazda in Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Ahura Mazda first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ahura Mazda is a creator and Maker of the material world, described as holy, most beneficent Spirit and Holy One, and as the father of Fire. He makes the good lands and countries dear to their dwellers, is highly renowned in Airyana Vaêgô by the good river Dâitya, and governs life-giving waters: sending water from sea Vouru-kasha with wind and clouds, bringing it to Dakhma, returning it unseen to sea Pûitika, and raining plants upon the earth for food and fodder. He teaches the law of Ahura, first conversing with Yima and instructing him to nourish, rule, protect the material world, and build the Vara against overcrowding and fatal winters. He then speaks with Spitama Zarathustra, answering questions about the Earth, happiness and grief, cultivation, food, sin, contracts, corpses, dead matter, water, fire, purity, pollution, the Drug Nasu, healing, sexual conduct, and the Daêva. His teaching supplies detailed rules for cultivation, confession and cleansing, contracts, atonements, penalties, treatment and removal of the dead, Dakhmas, purification, and protection from defilement. Across the records, he establishes the world’s lands and sustenance, gives law and guidance, and repeatedly answers questions by prescribing order, cleansing, rewards, and penalties.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Vendidad (Vendîdâd) / Fargard I.

Ahura Mazda tells Spitama Zarathustra that he made every land dear to its dwellers. He created the good lands and countries described in the unit.

Fargard II.

Ahura Mazda teaches Yima the law of Ahura and instructs him to nourish, rule, and watch over the material world. He warns Yima about overcrowding and fatal winters, commands him to build the Vara, and answers questions about its lights, law, and ruler.

Fargard III.

Ahura Mazda answers Zarathustra's questions about the Earth's happiness and grief, the treatment of corpses, cultivation, food, and sin. He teaches that sowing corn fulfills and strengthens the law of Mazda, and that the law of Mazda can cleanse confessed sin.

Fargard IV.

Ahura Mazda identifies six contracts: the word-contract, hand-contract, sheep-contract, ox-contract, man-contract, and field-contract. He states the required atonements and stripe penalties for broken contracts, assaults, and knowingly lying before the truth-knowing water.

Fargard V.

Ahura Mazda answers Zarathustra’s questions about corpses, dead matter, water, fire, purity, pollution, and the treatment of a still-born child. He teaches the rules for houses for the dead, Dakhmas, contaminated clothing, and the law of Mazda and Zarathustra.

Fargard VI.

Ahura Mazda answers Zarathustra’s questions about defiled ground, bones, corpses, water, Haoma, and the treatment of the dead. He prescribes purification procedures and penalties, including stripes with the Aspahê-astra and the Sraoshô-karana.

Fargard VII.

He explains when the Drug Nasu attacks, how pollution is cleansed, how healers qualify and are paid, how long ground remains polluted, and how a woman with a still-born child is treated.

Fargard VIII.

Ahura Mazda answers questions about corpses, houses, graves, purification, penalties, sexual conduct, the law of Mazda, and the Daêva. He instructs the worshippers of Mazda how to remove dead bodies, cleanse the unclean, and drive away the Drug Nasu.

VIII.

Ahura Mazda answers questions about corpse-burning fire, rewards for bringing fires to the Dâityô-gâtu, and cleansing after touching a corpse. He prescribes the required actions, rewards, penalties, and atonements.

Abstract written by a model

Ahura Mazda is a bright, glorious, holy, most beneficent Spirit: Lord, Maker, creator deity, and creator of the material world and creation. He has strength, vigour, complete knowledge, unde­ceivable understanding of truth, and the will of the Lord. He makes earth, water, the good Waters, plants, wisdom, Saoka, Gaokerena, and beings including Vohu-Manô, Asha-Vahista, Khsathra-Vairya, Spenta-Ârmaiti, Tistrya, Sraosha, and help, comforts, enjoyments, and pleasures for the faithful. Worship, sacrifice, prayer, propitiation, glorification, holiness, and the laws of Ahura surround him; worshippers of Mazda obey those laws and are distinguished from the wicked. He speaks repeatedly as teacher to Spitama Zarathustra, answering questions, revealing names, teaching the Law and ritual practice, explaining Haurvatât and the Amesha-Spentas, and instructing the increase of Asha-Vahista through worship and good thoughts, words, and deeds. He acts through and establishes others: Ardvi Sûra Anâhita descends and keeps holy creation; Zarathustra becomes master of the material world; Tistrya oversees the stars and receives aid against Apaosha; Mithra maintains the moving world; and Sraosha defends against Aêshma. Across the records he is praised and invoked for aid, victorious strength, cleanliness of the earth, protection, truth, and destruction of Angra Mainyu, Daêvas, fiends, and malice.

15 sections; each heading opens the text

I. Ormazd Yast.

Ahura Mazda is praised as bright and glorious, the Maker of the material world, the Holy One, and the most beneficent Spirit. He answers Zarathustra's questions, reveals his names, promises help and joy, and is invoked to destroy Angra Mainyu, Daêvas, fiends, and malice.

Ahura Mazda speaks unto Spitama Zarathustra and instructs him to increase Asha-Vahista through hymns, worship, invocations, sacrifice, blessings, and adoration. He is associated with Good Thoughts, Good Words, and Good Deeds, and keeps the Garô-nmâna.

Ahura Mazda speaks unto Spitama Zarathustra about the benefits of Haurvatât and the Amesha-Spentas. He explains how the faithful man parts from the wicked and prescribes the proper transmission of the spell.

Ahura Mazda tells Spitama Zarathustra to offer sacrifice to Ardvi Sûra Anâhita. He is said to have brought her waters down with mighty vigour and to have offered her a sacrifice in Airyana Vaêgah, where he received the boon of bringing Zarathustra to follow his law.

XX.

Ahura Mazda orders Ardvi Sûra Anâhita to descend from the stars to the earth so that great lords, masters of countries, their sons, and others may worship her. He establishes Spitama Zarathustra as master of the material world and Ardvi Sûra Anâhita as keeper of the holy creation.

VI. Khôrshêd Yast.

Ahura Mazda is the being to whom sacrifice to the undying, shining, swift-horsed Sun is offered. The earth made by Ahura is described as becoming clean when the sun rises.

Ahura Mazda is hailed, and the speaker identifies as a worshipper of Mazda who obeys the laws of Ahura. The earth is described as made by Mazda.

VIII. Tîr Yast.

Ahura Mazda establishes Tistrya as a lord and overseer above the stars and creates him as worthy of sacrifice, prayer, propitiation, and glorification. He assists Tistrya, offers him a sacrifice invoked by his own name, and restores to him the strength needed to overcome the Daêva Apaosha.

IX. Gôs Yast.

Ahura Mazda is invoked at the beginning as the one whose worship and laws are followed, and the unit calls the world and Drvâspa made by Mazda.

Ahura Mazda is described as the creator of Mithra and as the source of the law of holiness. Fire is called the son of Ahura Mazda, and Ahura Mazda gives victorious strength.

XXIV.

Ahura Mazda is worshipped as supreme in holiness and foremost in doing deeds of holiness. He created Sraosha to withstand Aêshma and taught Sraosha the Law.

XII. Rashn Yast.

Ahura Mazda is asked by Zarathustra about the qualities of the Holy Word and answers that its most glorious form is true, progress-making, discerning, healthful, wise, happy, and powerful to destroy. He instructs the preparation of the baresma and the invocation of his friendship toward the var prepared and associated ritual offerings. Ahura Mazda in Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV

Abstract written by a model

Ahura Mazda is a Lord, creator, ruler, Maker, heavenly god, Good Spirit, and the greatest and best of all gods. He is the most beneficent Spirit and Maker of the material world, with a Fravashi praised as supreme in holiness and in many excellences. Through the brightness and glory of the Fravashis of the faithful, he establishes and maintains the sky, waters, earth, and plants; he makes the earth wide and broad, the waters, and Victory. He is father and commander of the seven Amesha-Spentas, father of Ashi Vanguhi, maker of Vanant, and creator of Verethraghna. He formed Aryan nations through Gaya Maretan and made the Aryan Glory, Ashi Vanguhi, and the strong wind. He teaches and speaks to Spitama Zarathustra and Zarathustra, whose follower and worshipper is identified in the records, including revelations of his Law. He directs worship, hymns, sacrifice, prayer, propitiation, offerings, invocations, and rites, including those concerning Verethraghna. He sacrifices to Vayu and receives Vayu's help against the creation of Angra Mainyu. His Law is upheld by Vîstâspa, made for worshippers of Mazda, and obeyed by a worshipper of Mazda.

Ahura Mazda is named as the maker of Vanant. His laws are obeyed by the worshipper, who identifies himself as a worshipper of Mazda.

Abstract written by a model

Ahura Mazda is a creator, Maker of the material and good world, Holy One, most beneficent Spirit, and source of Glory.
