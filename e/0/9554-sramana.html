Abstract written by a model

In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, The Bad Sramana is a topic describing a Sramana who indulges in sleep, eating, and drinking, neglects learning and discipline, harms living beings, acts carelessly, and is despised as nobody in this world and the next. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, Sramana is a religious figure whose bad form is defined by comfort-seeking, neglect of learning and discipline, disrespect for preceptor and teachers, lack of self-control, harm to living beings, carelessness, quarrelsomeness, and other misconduct, while the pious among his brethren avoid these sins and conquer both worlds. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, the monk (the bad Sramana) is a Sramana who appears to be a monk but fails to protect himself from sins, is despised like poison, and has no status in this world or beyond.

Sramana in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Sramana first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sramana is a religious figure whose bad form is defined by comfort-seeking, neglect of learning and discipline, disrespect for preceptor and teachers, and lack of self-control. He harms living beings, seeds, and sprouts; uses furnishings carelessly; moves hastily; inspects inattentively; quarrels and perverts truth. He neglects austerities, eats improperly, leaves his preceptor for heretics, changes school, and lives by fortune-telling. Yet when pious among his brethren, he avoids these sins, is welcomed like nectar in this world, and conquers both this world and the next.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Seventeenth Lecture.

A Sramana is called bad when he neglects discipline, fails to respect his preceptor and teachers, harms living beings, acts without care, quarrels, indulges in improper eating, or follows other improper practices. A Sramana who avoids these sins and is pious among his brethren is welcomed like nectar and conquers this world and the next.

Abstract written by a model

The bad monk is a Sramana who appears to be a monk but fails to protect himself from sins like heretics. Lowest among worthy brethren, he is despised like poison and has no status in this world or beyond.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Seventeenth Lecture.

The bad monk does not protect himself from sins like the heretics, although he has the appearance of a monk. He is the lowest among his worthy brethren and is despised in this world like poison.
