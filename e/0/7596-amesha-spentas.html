Amesha-Spentas

group · 5 works · 38 quoted facts · 15 connections
Also called: Amesha-Spentas!, Amesha-Spentas, Vohu-Manô, Asha-Vahista, Khshathra-Vairya, Spenta-Ârmaiti, Haurvatât, and Ameretât, Amesha-Speñtas, all the Amesha-Spentas, the Amesha Spentas, the Amesha-Spentas, the bright ones, the other Amesha-Spentas

Abstract written by a model

In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), the Amesha-Spentas give Zarathustra the sacred Word and rule the seven Karshvares of the earth. In Sirozahs (Sîrôzahs), the Amesha-Spentas are invoked and sacrificed to alongside Ahura Mazda and associated with virtues, powers, and prosperity. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, the Amesha-Spentas are holy, all-ruling, all-beneficent beings who protect, receive worship, and hold the Moon’s glory. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, the Amesha-Spentas are seven bright, holy, undecaying beings who govern, preserve, and will restore Ahura Mazda’s creations. In Nyayish, the Amesha-Spentas are holy beings hailed as one with the Sun and offered prayers, sacrifice, and the worshipper’s life.

Amesha-Spentas in Yashts (Yasts) I-XII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Amesha-Spentas first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Amesha-Spentas are holy, all-ruling, all-beneficent beings, described as Ahura Mazda's creatures and the strongest part of the Holy Word. They receive worship through sacrifice, prayer, propitiation, and glorification; their help and joy are invoked. Their sevenfold presence protects against enemies, and their names are said to smite men turned to Nasus by the Druges. They include Vohu-Manô, Asha-Vahista, Khshathra-Vairya, Spenta-Ârmaiti, Haurvatât, and Ameretât. They have shining, beautiful abodes, can go along the seven Karshvares, and are linked with a way made easy through proclaiming Asha-Vahista. They also hold the Moon’s glory and pour it upon Mazda's earth.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

I. Ormazd Yast.

The Amesha-Spentas are worshipped with sacrifice, prayer, propitiation, and glorification, and their names are identified as the strongest part of the Holy Word. They are described as Ahura Mazda's creatures, and their help and joy are invoked.

II. Haptân Yast.

The Amesha-Spentas are described as all-ruling and all-beneficent, and sacrifice is offered to them. Their sevenfold presence is taken as a shield against enemies.

III. Ardibehist Yast.

The Amesha-Spentas are invoked through sacrifice and prayer. The way to their abode is made easy by proclaiming Asha-Vahista.

IV. Khordâd Yast.

The Amesha-Spentas include Vohu-Manô, Asha-Vahista, Khshathra-Vairya, Spenta-Ârmaiti, Haurvatât, and Ameretât. Their names smite men turned to Nasus by the Druges, and heavenly Yazatas in company with them free the faithful man from harmful beings.

VII. Mâh Yast.

The Amesha-Spentas are hailed and are said to stand up holding the Moon’s glory and pouring it upon the earth made by Mazda.

Amesha-Spentas in Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Amesha-Spentas first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Amesha-Spentas are seven bright, holy, undecaying beings, united in thought, speech, and deed under their father and commander, Ahura Mazda. They think of good thoughts, words, deeds, and Garô-nmâna; govern, preserve, and will restore Ahura Mazda’s creations. They longed for Zarathustra as lord and master of the world and praiser of Asha. The Fravashis of the faithful carry hymns and worship to them. Invoked for greatness, brightness, glory, plenty, protection, and guidance, they impart brightness and Glory to Vîstâspa and promise Zarathustra the way to the spiritual world and Paradise.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

XXIII.

The Amesha-Spentas are described as seven beings united in thought, speech, and deed, sharing one father and commander, Ahura Mazda. They longed for Zarathustra as lord and master of the world and praiser of Asha.

XXVI.

The Amesha-Spentas are named among those to whom the Fravashis may carry hymns and worship.

XIX. Zamyâd Yast.

The Amesha-Spentas are seven bright, holy, undecaying beings of one thought, speech, and deed, whose father and commander is Ahura Mazda. They govern, preserve, and will restore the creations of Ahura Mazda.

XXIV. Vîstâsp Yast.

The Amesha-Spentas are invoked to grant greatness, brightness, glory, plenty, protection, and guidance. They promise to show Zarathustra the way to the spiritual world and Paradise.
