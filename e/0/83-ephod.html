Abstract written by a model

The ephod is a skillfully crafted garment made of gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen. It has two shoulder straps joined at the ends and a skillfully woven band of the same piece and materials, used to fasten it. Its most notable feature is the two onyx stones on the shoulder straps, engraved with the names of the children of Israel, six tribal names each, set in gold with braided chains, serving as memorial stones. Chapter 28 describes its design, including the breastplate bound with blue lace and an all-blue robe. Chapter 39 repeats the construction, adding gold beaten into thin plates and cut into wires.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 28

The ephod is made of gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen as skillful work, with two shoulder straps and a skillfully woven band of the same piece. Two onyx stones engraved with six tribal names each, set in gold, sit on its shoulder straps as memorial stones, attached with gold settings and braided chains. The breastplate is bound to its rings with a blue lace so it does not swing out, and the robe of the ephod is to be all blue.

Chapter 39

The ephod is made of gold, blue, purple, scarlet and fine twined linen, with gold beaten into thin plates and cut into wires to be worked in. It has joined shoulder straps and a skillfully woven band of the same work, and onyx stones engraved with the names of the children of Israel are set on the shoulder straps as stones of memorial.
