living beings

Abstract written by a model

Living beings are individually embodied and include earth-, water-, fire-, and wind-bodies, plants and their growth, animals, people, and creatures such as crows, cats, and dogs. They are fond of life and pleasure, dislike pain and destruction, and experience both pleasant and unpleasant feelings, terror, unhappiness, and many forms of suffering. Their varied conditions, births, and miseries arise through action and karman; beings may harm one another and become sinners through their actions. Because pain is feared and disagreeable to every sort of living being, they must not be slain, violently treated, abused, tormented, disturbed, or harmed directly, through another, or through careless acts. The account culminates in careful recognition of life in ground, bodies, and the world: a monk should protect beings, and Mahâvîra avoids injuring them while wandering and begging.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I / First Book. / First Lecture, Called Knowledge of the Weapon. / First Lesson. .. Seventh Lesson.

Beings are individually embodied and experience pleasure or displeasure, pain, great terror, and unhappiness. They are harmed by acts involving the elements, plants, and animals.

Second Lecture, Called Conquest of the World. / First Lesson. .. Sixth Lesson.

Living beings experience various conditions, including blindness, deafness, dumbness, one-eyedness, hunchbackedness, and varied colors. They are fond of life, like pleasure, hate pain, and shun destruction.

Third Lecture, Called Hot and Cold. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson.

Living beings suffer through action and karman. They must not be killed, and no one should kill them, cause others to kill them, or consent to their killing.

Fourth Lecture, Called Righteousness. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson.

All breathing, existing, living, sentient creatures should not be slain, treated with violence, abused, tormented, or driven away. Pain is unpleasant, disagreeable, and greatly feared for all sorts of living beings.

Fifth Lecture, Called Essence of the World. / First Lesson. .. Sixth Lesson.

Living beings may be harmed through injurious deeds, including acts committed through carelessness. A monk must mind living beings and should neither kill nor cause others to kill them.

Sixth Lecture, Called the Cleaning. / First Lesson. .. Fifth Lesson.

Beings are born in various families to reap the fruit of their acts and experience pleasant and unpleasant feelings. Beings torment beings, and many pains are the lot of creatures.

Seventh Lecture, Called Liberation. / First Lesson. .. Eighth Lesson.

Living beings must not be harmed, killed, or caused pain, whether directly or through another. A wise person should recognize the presence of living beings when examining and preparing ground, and should neither kill nor disturb animals feeding on an ascetic’s body.

Eighth Lecture, (Called) the Pillow of Righteousness. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson.

Living beings included earth-bodies, water-bodies, fire-bodies, wind-bodies, lichens, seeds, sprouts, animals, people, crows, cats, and dogs. Mahâvîra understood that many were imbued with life and carefully avoided injuring them while wandering and begging.
