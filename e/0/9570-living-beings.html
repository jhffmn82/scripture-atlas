Abstract written by a model

living beings in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where living beings first appears

Abstract written by a model

Living beings move through Samsâra according to their actions: born in different families, castes, and non-human forms, suffering pains and recurring births without disgust, like warriors in life’s battle. They may become gods, hells, Asuras, Kshattriyas, Kandâlas, Bukkasas, worms, moths, Kunthu, or ants. By cessation of Karman, they may in due time be born as men and reach a pure state. They people the world, moving or unmoving, and should not be injured by a monk in thought, word, or act.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Third Lecture.

Living beings are born in different families, castes, and forms according to their actions. They undergo misery in non-human births, but through cessation of Karman may eventually be born as men.

Eighth Lecture.

Living beings people the world whether they move or not. A monk should do nothing injurious to them in thought, word, or act.

Living Beings in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

Hermann Jacobi · the work’s page · read it where Living Beings first appears

Abstract written by a model

Living Beings are a constituent of the world (Lôka), described by substance, place, time, and development. They comprise six kinds: earth-bodies, water-bodies, fire-bodies, wind-bodies, plants, and animals. Their distinction from Things without life supports a monk’s self-control. Careful inspection matters because building, preparing food and drink, and fire may injure or destroy them. They unfold as beings still in Samsâra, movable or immovable, subtle or gross, with many subdivisions, or as perfected souls (siddhas).

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Twenty-Sixth Lecture.

The six kinds of living beings are earth-bodies, water-bodies, fire-bodies, wind-bodies, plants, and animals. Careful inspection protects them from injury.

Thirty-Fifth Lecture.

Living beings, both movable and immovable, subtle and gross, may be killed when a house is built. Beings living in water, corn, earth, and wood are destroyed in the preparation of food and drink, and fire can destroy many beings.

Thirty-Sixth Lecture.

Living Beings, together with Things without life, make up the world (Lôka). Their division from Things without life is taught for the knowledge of a monk who exerts himself in self-control.

B. Living Beings.

Living Beings are divided into those still belonging to the Samsâra and perfected souls (siddhas). Those still belonging to the Samsâra are movable or immovable, with numerous subdivisions.
