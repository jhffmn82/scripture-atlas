happiness in Chuang Tzŭ

Chuang Tzŭ · the work’s page · read it where happiness first appears

Abstract written by a model

Happiness is found in contentment and is complete when desire is attained, with nothing further to add. Yet it is not exempt from sorrow: office and its absence cause unhappiness. In the human world it is uncertain whether common happiness, following the majority, is real; wealth and reputation bring attachment, anxiety, and fear.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVI.

Complete happiness means that nothing can be added to one's happiness and that one remains equally happy under all conditions. In the present age, both having and not having office cause unhappiness, so even happiness is not exempt from sorrow.

CHAPTER XVIII.

Human happiness is questioned because ordinary people seem to find it in slavishly following the majority. The text says it is uncertain whether this common happiness is real.

Tzŭ Chang asked Man Kou Tê,

Happiness is said to be found in contentment rather than in accumulating wealth or pursuing reputation. Excessive wealth is described as causing unhappiness through attachment, anxiety, and fear.
