Abstract written by a model

The Galatians are the recipients of Paul's letter, addressed as the assemblies located in Galatia. They were called in Christ's grace and once served false gods before being known by God. The records trace a movement from rebuke to reassurance to exhortation. In Chapter 1 they are greeted, then rebuked for quickly deserting the one who called them for a different 'good news', troubled by some who would pervert the gospel. In Chapter 3 they are called foolish and asked whether they received the Spirit by works of the law or by hearing with faith. They are told they are children of God through faith, baptized into Christ and having put him on, and, as Christ's, Abraham's offspring and heirs according to promise. In Chapter 4 they are sons and heirs, adopted through Christ's redemption and no longer bondservants, yet rebuked for turning back to elemental principles and observing days, months, seasons, and years, though they once welcomed Paul as an angel of God. They are children of promise, like the free woman's son, not of the servant. In Chapter 5 they are urged to stand firm in liberty, avoid a yoke of bondage, serve one another in love, and walk by the Spirit, since they were running well until interfered with and Paul is confident of them.

Paul’s Letter to the Galatians / Chapter 1

The Galatians, addressed as the assemblies of Galatia, are greeted with grace and peace and then rebuked because Paul marvels that they are so quickly deserting the one who called them in Christ's grace for a different "good news." They are being troubled by some who want to pervert the gospel, and Paul reminds them that they have heard of his former way of life in the Jews' religion.

Chapter 3

The Galatians are called foolish and are asked whether they received the Spirit and saw miracles by works of the law or by hearing with faith. Having begun in the Spirit, they are questioned about being completed in the flesh, and they are told they are all children of God through faith in Christ Jesus, baptized into Christ, and, if Christ's, Abraham's offspring and heirs according to promise.

Chapter 4

The readers are told that, as children, they are now sons and heirs of God through Christ, having received the Spirit crying "Abba, Father!" They are rebuked for turning back to the elemental principles and observing days, months, seasons, and years, though they once received the author as an angel of God and would have given him their eyes. They are called children of promise, like Isaac, and not children of a servant.

Chapter 5

The readers are urged to stand firm in their liberty and not be entangled again in a yoke of bondage. They were running well until someone interfered, and Paul is confident they will think no other way. They are called for freedom, told to serve one another in love, warned against biting and devouring each other, and urged to walk by the Spirit.
