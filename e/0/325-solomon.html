Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Samuel, Solomon is David's son by Bathsheba whom the LORD loves and names Jedidiah through Nathan. In The First Book of Kings, Solomon is king of Israel who succeeds David, builds the temple, gains great wisdom and wealth, and later turns from the LORD. In 1 Chronicles, Solomon is David's chosen and anointed successor who sits on the LORD's throne and is charged with building the temple. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Solomon is David's successor who builds and dedicates the temple, gains wisdom and wealth, and reigns forty years. In Proverbs, Solomon is David's son and king of Israel identified as the source of the proverbs. In Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher, Solomon is the reflective king and narrator who tests life's pursuits and teaches that all is vanity before God. In The Song of Solomon, Solomon is the Lover and king who speaks in a poetic exchange of mutual devotion with the Beloved. In The Song of Solomon, Solomon is the king and credited author who is crowned by his mother and owns a luxurious carriage and vineyard.

In The Wisdom of Solomon, Solomon is the king and speaker who prays for wisdom to rule and judge God's people rightly. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Solomon is David's wise son who reigns in peace, builds God's house, becomes renowned, and later falls through women. In Surah 21: The Prophets (Al-Anbiyaa), Solomon receives understanding in judgment and is given command over the wind and certain evil beings. In Surah 27: The Ant (An-Naml), Solomon inherits David, commands armies, understands birds, communicates with the Queen of Sheba, and asks Allah for gratitude. In Surah 34: Sheba (Saba), Solomon receives the wind, copper, and jinn who work for him by Allah's permission. In Surah 38: The letter Saad (Saad), Solomon is Allah's repentant servant who receives sovereignty and command over the wind, builders, and divers.

Solomon in The First Book of Kings

Abstract written by a model

Solomon is a king of Israel, son of David and Bathsheba and younger brother of the elder Adonijah. The records follow him from accession to decline. In Chapter 1, David, recalling his oath, has Zadok anoint him at Gihon, the people proclaim him, and he sits on David's throne. In Chapter 2 he consolidates power: Adonijah, Joab and Shimei are killed, Abiathar is removed from the priesthood, Benaiah is set over the army and Zadok made priest, and he honors Bathsheba at his right hand. He allies with Pharaoh by marrying his daughter. He loves the LORD yet sacrifices at high places. At Gibeon he asks God for an understanding heart, receives unequaled wisdom, riches and honor, and shows his wisdom by judging the dispute of two women over a child. He rules all Israel through a list of named officers, holds dominion from the River to the Philistines and Egypt's border, receives tribute, enjoys peace on all sides, keeps forty thousand horse stalls and twelve thousand horsemen, and speaks three thousand proverbs and a thousand and five songs. With rest from enemies, he builds the LORD's house as promised to David. He gets cedar from Hiram of Tyre, raises forced labor levies and large crews of burden bearers and stone cutters, and finishes the temple in seven years. He then spends thirteen years on his own house, the House of the Forest of Lebanon, and a house for Pharaoh's daughter, makes the golden furnishings and deposits David's dedicated gifts. He brings the ark in, prays the dedicatory prayer and offers great sacrifices. The LORD appears again with a conditional promise and warning. He gives Hiram twenty cities, builds a fleet at Ezion Geber, receives the queen of Sheba, and builds an ivory and gold throne, gold shields and bucklers. He gathers 1,400 chariots and trades horses and chariots with Egypt, the Hittites and Syria, exceeding all earth's kings in riches and wisdom. The end turns bleak: he takes seven hundred wives and three hundred concubines, who turn his heart in old age to Ashtoreth, Milcom, Chemosh and Molech, with high places built for them. His heart is not perfect with the LORD as David's was. The LORD raises adversaries, Hadad the Edomite and Rezon. He seeks to kill Jeroboam, reigns forty years, dies, is buried in the city of David, and his son Rehoboam succeeds him.

Chapter 1

Solomon is not invited to Adonijah's feast, but David, recalling his oath, orders him to ride the king's mule to Gihon. There Zadok anoints him, the people proclaim "Long live King Solomon!", and he sits on the throne. He then promises Adonijah safety if he proves worthy and tells him to go to his house.

Chapter 2

Solomon takes David's throne and his kingdom is firmly established. He has Adonijah and Joab killed, removes Abiathar from the priesthood, appoints Benaiah over the army and Zadok as priest, and has Shimei killed for breaking his confinement. He honors his mother Bathsheba by seating her at his right hand.

Chapter 3

Solomon allies with Pharaoh by marrying his daughter, sacrifices at the Gibeon high place, and in a dream asks the LORD for an understanding heart. After waking he offers sacrifices in Jerusalem and holds a feast. He then settles the dispute between two women by ordering the living child divided, identifying the true mother, and thereby earns the awe of all Israel.

Chapter 4

King Solomon is king over all Israel, with a list of princes and officials serving him. He rules over all the kingdoms from the River to the land of the Philistines and the border of Egypt, receives tribute, and has peace on all sides. He keeps forty thousand stalls of horses and twelve thousand horsemen, and is renowned for his wisdom, proverbs, and songs.

Chapter 5

Solomon, anointed king in place of his father David, wrote to Hiram explaining his intention to build a house for the LORD's name and requesting cedar from Lebanon, offering wages and his servants' help. He paid Hiram twenty thousand cors of wheat and twenty cors of pure oil year by year, and the LORD gave him wisdom as promised. He raised a levy of thirty thousand men, kept seventy thousand burden bearers and eighty thousand stone cutters, and commanded that large costly stones be cut for the foundation.

Chapter 6

Solomon, in the fourth year of his reign over Israel, began to build the LORD's house and finished it after seven years. He received the LORD's word promising to establish his word if he walked in the LORD's statutes, ordinances, and commandments. He lined the interior with cedar, overlaid it with gold, made the cherubim and the olive-wood doors, and built the inner court.

Chapter 7

Solomon spent thirteen years building his own house, including the House of the Forest of Lebanon, the porch of judgment, and a house for Pharaoh's daughter, whom he had taken as wife. He sent for Hiram of Tyre, had the bronze vessels cast in the Jordan plain, and left them unweighed because there were so many. He made the gold furnishings of the LORD's house and put the silver, gold, and vessels that David his father had dedicated into its treasuries.

Chapter 8

Solomon assembles Israel's elders and leaders, brings the ark into the house he built, and blesses the assembly, saying the LORD has fulfilled his word to David. He prays the long dedicatory prayer, blesses the people again, offers enormous sacrifices, and holds the feast before sending the people home.

Chapter 9

Solomon finishes the LORD's house and his own house, and receives a second appearance of the LORD with a conditional promise and warning. He gives Hiram twenty cities, conscripts forced labor for his many building projects, offers sacrifices three times a year, and builds a fleet at Ezion Geber that brings gold from Ophir.

Chapter 10

Solomon answered all the queen of Sheba's hard questions, showed her his house, table, servants, and ascent to the LORD's house, and gave her all she desired besides his royal bounty. He built a great ivory and gold throne, made golden shields and bucklers, and used almug wood for pillars and instruments. He exceeded all earth's kings in riches and wisdom, gathered 1,400 chariots and 12,000 horsemen, and traded horses and chariots from Egypt to the kings of the Hittites and Syria.

Chapter 11

Solomon loved many foreign women, had seven hundred wives and three hundred concubines, and was led by them after other gods in his old age. He built high places for Chemosh and Molech, and his heart was not perfect with the LORD as David's was. He sought to kill Jeroboam, reigned forty years, died, and was buried in David's city, with Rehoboam reigning in his place.

Solomon in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Solomon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Solomon is the son of David and his successor as king of Israel, firmly established in his kingdom with the LORD with him and delighting in him. Early on he offers a thousand burnt offerings at Gibeon and asks God for wisdom and knowledge; God grants it and promises riches, wealth and honor unmatched by any king before or after him. He gathers chariots, horsemen and horses from Egypt and Kue, and reigns from Jerusalem. What matters most about him is his building of the LORD's house. He decides to build a house for the LORD's name and one for his kingdom, enlisting tens of thousands of laborers and asking Huram of Tyre for a craftsman and Lebanon timber. He begins the temple on Mount Moriah in the fourth year of his reign, overlaying it with gold and making the cherubim, veil and the pillars Jachin and Boaz. He makes the bronze altar, basins, lamp stands, tables, courts and golden vessels, casting bronze in the Jordan plain. He then brings the ark up, prays at length, offers vast sacrifices, and holds a seven-day feast. The LORD appears to him, promising to establish his throne if he walks as David did and warning of consequences if he turns away. Over twenty years he completes the LORD's house and his own, rebuilds cities, prevails against Hamath Zobah, builds Tadmor, and conscripts forced labor from other peoples but not Israelites. He moves Pharaoh's daughter to her own house, organizes priests, Levites and doorkeepers per David's ordinance, and receives gold from Ophir through ships sent by Huram. His fame brings the queen of Sheba, whose questions he answers, and tribute from kings of the earth and Arabia. He owns an ivory throne overlaid with gold, hundreds of gold shields, and ships of Tarshish, and rules from the River to the Philistines' land and Egypt's border. He reigns forty years in Jerusalem, dies, is buried in David's city, and is succeeded by his son Rehoboam. The final chapter recalls Rehoboam making the people's yoke heavy, with Jeroboam having fled Solomon's presence to Egypt.

The Second Book of Chronicles / Chapter 1

Solomon son of David is firmly established in his kingdom, offers one thousand burnt offerings at Gibeon, and asks God for wisdom and knowledge to judge the people. God grants this and promises him unmatched riches, wealth, and honor; he then reigns from Jerusalem, amassing chariots and horsemen and trading horses and chariots.

Chapter 2

Solomon decides to build a house for the LORD's name and a house for his kingdom, and counts out 70,000 burden bearers, 80,000 stone cutters, and 3,600 overseers. He asks Huram of Tyre for a skilled craftsman and Lebanon timber, promising wheat, barley, wine, and oil, and says no one can truly build God a house, so the purpose is to burn incense before him. He also counts the foreigners in Israel, finding 153,600, and assigns them to labor.

Chapter 3

Solomon began building the LORD's house at Jerusalem on Mount Moriah in the second month of the fourth year of his reign. He laid the foundations, overlaid the house with gold, made the most holy place, the cherubim, the veil, and the two pillars, and named the pillars Jachin and Boaz.

Chapter 4

Solomon is the king for whom all the bronze and gold furnishings of God's house were made, and he is said to have made the altar, basins, lamp stands, tables, court, and vessels. He cast the bronze vessels in the clay ground of the Jordan plain between Succoth and Zeredah, producing so many that the weight of the bronze could not be determined.

Chapter 5

Solomon finished all the work on the LORD's house and put the silver, gold, and vessels dedicated by his father David into the treasuries of God's house. He assembled Israel's elders and leaders at Jerusalem to bring up the ark, and he and the congregation sacrificed sheep and cattle too numerous to count before it.

Chapter 6

Solomon blesses the assembly of Israel and the LORD, declaring that he has built the house and sits on his father David's throne as promised. He kneels on a bronze platform in the court, spreads his hands toward heaven, and prays at length, asking the LORD to hear prayers made toward the house and to remember David.

Chapter 7

Solomon finishes praying, offers twenty-two thousand cattle and a hundred twenty thousand sheep, and makes the middle of the court holy because the bronze altar cannot hold the offerings. He holds the feast for seven days, sends the people home, and completes the LORD's house and his own. The LORD appears to him by night, promising to establish his throne if he walks as David did and warning of consequences if he turns away.

Chapter 8

Solomon, at the end of twenty years of building the LORD's house and his own, rebuilt cities, prevailed against Hamath Zobah, and conscripted forced labor from the remaining peoples but not from Israelites. He moved Pharaoh's daughter to her own house, offered burnt offerings as Moses commanded, organized the priests and Levites per David's ordinance, and received four hundred fifty talents of gold from Ophir via ships Huram sent.

Chapter 9

Solomon answered all the questions of the queen of Sheba, received her gifts, and gave her all she desired. He made gold shields, an ivory throne overlaid with gold, and gold vessels, was visited and paid tribute by kings, and ruled from the River to the land of the Philistines and the border of Egypt. He reigned in Jerusalem forty years, slept with his fathers, was buried in David's city, and was succeeded by his son Rehoboam.

Chapter 10

Solomon is described as the father of Rehoboam who made the people's yoke grievous and heavy and chastised them with whips. Jeroboam had fled from his presence to Egypt, and the old men had stood before him while he lived.

the Preacher in Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher

Abstract written by a model

The Preacher is a king in Jerusalem, the son of David, who rules Israel and claims great wisdom above all who preceded him there. The records present him as a reflective, teaching figure who tests life under the sun and repeatedly judges it vanity, a chasing after wind. In the opening chapter he introduces himself, applies his heart to seek out all that is done under the sky, and finds even the pursuit of wisdom, madness and folly to be a chasing after wind. In the second chapter he tests mirth, builds great works, amasses wealth and withholds no joy from himself, yet finds no profit. He hates his life and labor because death comes to the wise and the fool alike and he must leave his work to a successor, and he slides into despair. He then sees that eating, drinking and enjoying good in labor come from God's hand. In the third chapter he asks what profit labor brings, observes wickedness in the place of justice, says God will judge and tests people to show they are like animals, and concludes nothing is better than rejoicing in one's works. In the seventh chapter he says he tested everything by wisdom and resolved to be wise, but wisdom remained far from him. He found one man among a thousand but no woman, and concludes that God made mankind upright but people seek many inventions. In the eighth chapter he commends mirth, calls certain outcomes vanity, and concludes that the work of God cannot be found out. In the ninth chapter he finds that one event comes to all, that time and chance govern outcomes, and that wisdom is better than strength. In the final chapter he declares 'Vanity of vanities! All is vanity!' and is described as wise, still teaching the people knowledge, and setting in order many proverbs while seeking acceptable words of truth written blamelessly. He moves from search and despair toward enjoyment, humility before God, and teaching.

Lover in The Song of Solomon

author unknown · 299 to 199 BC · the work’s page · read it where Lover first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Lover is a central speaking figure in a poetic exchange of mutual devotion with the Beloved. The records present him as a king who brought her into his rooms, a keeper of a flock that he grazes and rests at noon, and someone born under an apple tree where his mother conceived him. He is called beautiful, likened to henna blossoms from the vineyards of En Gedi, and loved by virgins because of his name. His love is described as better than wine. The records also have him describe himself as a rose of Sharon and a lily of the valleys, and he is faint with love. The relationship is mutual belonging. In Chapter 1 he directs her to follow the sheep's tracks, compares her to a steed in Pharaoh's chariots, and praises her cheeks, neck, and doves' eyes. In Chapter 2 he calls the beloved to rise because winter is past, and charges the daughters of Jerusalem not to stir up love before it desires. In Chapter 4 he calls her love, sister, and bride, says she has ravished his heart, and plans to go to the mountain of myrrh until the day is cool. In Chapter 6 he praises her as lovely as Jerusalem and awesome as an army with banners, and sets her above sixty queens and eighty concubines as his unique dove. In Chapter 8 he is the one she wishes to kiss and lean on, embracing her, dwelling in gardens with friends and asked to let her hear his voice. The records mix perspectives, with him both speaking and being described.
