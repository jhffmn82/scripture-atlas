Gazara

Also called: a stronghold, the fortress

Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of the Maccabees, Gazara is besieged and captured by Simon, who expels its people, cleanses its idol houses, settles law-keeping men, fortifies it, and builds a dwelling there; John later leads forces there, and Simon offers one hundred talents with Joppa when Antiochus demands it back. In The Second Book of the Maccabees, Gazara is a very strong fortress commanded by Chaereas, where Timotheus flees; after a four-day siege, young men scale its wall, burn the towers, and Jews take the city.

Gazara in The First Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 99 BC · the work’s page · read it where Gazara first appears

Abstract written by a model

Gazara is a city on the borders of Azotus. Simon besieges it with a siege engine, captures a tower, expels the people, cleanses idol houses, settles law-keeping men, fortifies it, and builds a dwelling; his son John later leads forces there. Antiochus demands it back; Simon cites its harm and offers one hundred talents with Joppa.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

Simon besieges Gazara with a siege engine, strikes a tower, and captures it, causing uproar. The people beg for mercy and Simon expels them, cleanses the idol houses, settles law-keeping men there, strengthens the city, and builds himself a dwelling. His son John later lives there as leader of the forces.

Chapter 15

Gazara is named by Antiochus as one of the cities of his kingdom that Simon holds, and he demands it back. Simon says it harmed the people of the country and offers one hundred talents for it together with Joppa.

Abstract written by a model

Gazara is a very strong fortress commanded by Chaereas, where Timotheus fled. Its defenders trusted its strength and blasphemed through a four-day siege; on day five young men scaled the wall, burned the towers, and Jews took the city.

Chapter 10

Gazara was a very strong fortress where Timotheus fled and where Chaereas was in command. Those inside trusted its strength and blasphemed during a four-day siege. On the fifth day, young men scaled the wall, burned the towers and blasphemers, opened the gates, and the Jews occupied the city.
