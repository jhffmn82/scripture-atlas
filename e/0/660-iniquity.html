Abstract written by a model

In Judith, Israelite sin and its consequences include departing from God's way, resulting in destruction in battle, captivity, the razing of the temple, and the loss of cities. In Psalms, sins are beyond human endurance if recorded by the LORD, yet the LORD promises to redeem Israel from all its sins. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, sins and unrighteousness are fully known to the Lord, who repays deeds and urges people to forsake iniquity and hate evil. In The Book of Isaiah, iniquity and sin separate the people from God, expose their violent and deceitful deeds, and bring repayment, while a Redeemer comes for those who turn back. In The Book of Isaiah, sin and iniquity carry away the speakers, lead to the LORD's anger and consumption, and prompt a plea that iniquity not be remembered forever. In The Book of Jeremiah, Israel's iniquity has become great and its sins have increased, causing incurable pain, chastisement, and punishment. In The Lamentations of Jeremiah, sin and iniquity are confessed as inherited from the fathers and as causes of suffering and the fallen crown. In Daniel (Greek), Israel's sin is rebellion against God's law that brought the written curse and great evil upon Jerusalem.

and 10 more works

In The Book of Hosea, sin and wickedness involve divided loyalty, false covenants, multiplying altars, and persistent wrongdoing, producing judgment. In The Second Book of Nephi, iniquity burdens souls, brings sorrow and divine judgments, and causes conflict, famine, captivity, and destruction. In The Book of Mosiah, sin is rebellion against God that brings judgment, bondage, affliction, and a fallen state, while confession and sincere repentance bring forgiveness. In The Book of Mosiah, wickedness appears in Noah's people's iniquities and abominations, bringing prophetic warnings of affliction, bondage, pestilence, and destruction. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, wickedness causes loss of freedom, sorrow, inequality, death, destruction, affliction, and the church's decline, requiring repentance. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, iniquity causes mourning, hardened hearts, secret combinations, suffering, and destruction, while leaders and rebels greatly increase it. In The Book of Helaman, iniquity brings curses, spiritual death, chastening, and increasing wickedness unless the people repent through Jesus Christ. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, iniquity involves secret combinations, murders, contention, rebellion, and the pursuit of power, and is ultimately ended. In The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record, wickedness prevails among the Nephites, causing unbelief, loss of spiritual gifts, sorrow, bloodshed, and threatened destruction. In The Book of Ether, wickedness fills the land through idolatry, secret plans, robbery, and wrongdoing, bringing a curse and impending destruction unless the people repent.
