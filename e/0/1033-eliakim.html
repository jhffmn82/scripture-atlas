Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Eliakim son of Hilkiah oversees Hezekiah's household, meets Rabshakeh with Shebnah and Joah, asks him to speak Syrian, and reports his words to Hezekiah in torn clothes. In The Book of Isaiah, Eliakim son of Hilkiah is the LORD's servant who succeeds Shebna, receives authority over David's house, is likened to a nail fastened in a sure place, and later serves as the official over Hezekiah's household who meets Rabshakeh and reports to Hezekiah.

Chapter 18

Eliakim son of Hilkiah, who was over the household, went out with Shebnah the scribe and Joah the recorder to meet Rabshakeh. He asked Rabshakeh to speak in the Syrian language rather than the Jews' language in the hearing of the people on the wall. Afterward he came to Hezekiah with torn clothes and reported Rabshakeh's words.

Eliakim in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · 739 to 680 BC · the work’s page · read it where Eliakim first appears

Abstract written by a model

Eliakim, son of Hilkiah, is called the LORD's servant. He is set to succeed Shebna, receiving his robe, belt and government, becoming a father to Jerusalem's inhabitants and Judah's house, and carrying the key of David's house, opening and shutting without hindrance. He is likened to a nail fastened in a sure place, bearing his father's house's glory, though that nail is finally said to give way and be cut down. Later, as the official over the household under Hezekiah, he meets Rabshakeh, asks him to speak Aramaic, stays silent at the king's command, and reports to Hezekiah with torn clothes.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 22

Eliakim, son of Hilkiah, is called the LORD's servant and will be clothed with Shebna's robe and belt and given his government. He will be a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem and the house of Judah and will carry the key of David's house, opening and shutting without anyone reversing it. He is fastened like a nail in a sure place and bears the glory of his father's house, yet that nail is finally said to give way, be cut down, and fall.

Chapter 36

Eliakim son of Hilkiah, who is over the household, goes out to meet Rabshakeh. He asks him to speak in Aramaic so the people on the wall will not understand, then remains silent at the king's command. He returns to Hezekiah with torn clothes to report Rabshakeh's words.
