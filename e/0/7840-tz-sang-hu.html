Tzŭ Sang Hu

Also called: Sang Hu

Abstract written by a model

Tzŭ Sang Hu is one of four friends and three conversing friends. He discusses being, action, and wandering beyond space; teaches Confucius that divine bonds outrank money; recounts Shun’s instructions; dies, prompting a companion’s song and Confucius’s account of men beyond life’s rule.

Nan Po Tzŭ K'uei

Tzŭ Sang Hu converses with Mêng Tzŭ Fan and Tzŭ Ch'in Chang about being and not being, doing and not doing, and wandering beyond the limits of space. After his death, one of his companions sings beside his corpse, and Confucius explains that such men travel beyond the rule of life.

CHAPTER XX.

Tzŭ Sang Hu tells Confucius that what is from God is valued above money and that the friendship of the superior man is negative like water. On another occasion, he recounts Shun's instructions concerning the physical body, feelings, and outward embellishment.
