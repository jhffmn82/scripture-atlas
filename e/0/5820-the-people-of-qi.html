people of Qi in Mencius, Book I (Liang Hui Wang)

Mencius · about 299 BC · the work’s page · read it where people of Qi first appears

Abstract written by a model

The people of Qi are a people whose responses reveal the condition of their rule. They hear the king’s music and see his hunting; they complain when these pleasures bring distress, separation, and scattering, but rejoice when they signal his freedom from sickness. Lacking a secure livelihood, they may fall into moral deflection and depravity; benevolent government would support their families. They attack and conquer Yan, then plan to fortify Xue, alarming duke Wen of Teng.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

The people are said to lack security when they do not have a certain livelihood, causing them to abandon themselves to moral deflection, depravity, and wild license. Under benevolent government, they would have sufficient support for parents, wives, and children and would follow what is good with ease.

Chapter 1

The people of Qi hear the king's music and hunting and complain when these pleasures are accompanied by distress, separation, and scattering. When the king's enjoyment appears to show that he is free from sickness, they respond with delight and joyful looks.

Chapter 10

The people of Qi attacked Yan and conquered it in an achievement described as beyond mere human strength.

Chapter 14

They are going to fortify Xue, causing duke Wen of Teng great alarm.

the people of Qi in Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou)

Mencius · about 299 BC · the work’s page · read it where the people of Qi first appears

Abstract written by a model

The people of Qi belong to Qi. Their sayings value favorable opportunity over wisdom and proper farming seasons over instruments, supporting the importance of timing. They approve Qi Wa’s course for Qi while questioning his own course. After Shen Tong’s question about Yan, the people of Qi smote Yan.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou) / Chapter 1

The people of Qi are associated with a saying that wisdom is less important than favorable opportunity and that farming instruments are less important than waiting for the proper seasons. Their saying supports Mencius’s claim that timing is crucial.

Chapter 8

The people of Qi smote Yan after Shen Tong asked Mencius whether Yan might be smitten.
