Abstract written by a model

The Roman believers are the recipients of Paul's letter, addressed across chapters 13, 15 and 16. They are instructed to submit to authorities, pay taxes, customs, respect and honor, and owe only love. They are urged to awaken, cast off darkness, and put on the armor of light and the Lord Jesus Christ. Paul urges them to bear with the weak, accept one another as Christ did, and be of one mind. He considers them full of goodness and knowledge, able to admonish others. He hopes to visit them on his way to Spain and asks for prayer. Finally they are asked to welcome Phoebe, avoid dividers, and be wise in good; their obedience is known to all, and God will crush Satan under their feet.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

The readers are told to be in subjection to the authorities, to pay taxes, customs, respect, and honor as owed, and to owe nothing except love. They are urged to awaken from sleep, throw off the deeds of darkness, put on the armor of light, walk properly as in the day, and put on the Lord Jesus Christ.

Chapter 15

The Roman believers are urged to bear with the weak, please their neighbors for good, be of the same mind, and accept one another as Christ accepted them. Paul is persuaded they are full of goodness and knowledge and able to admonish others. He hopes to visit them on his way to Spain, to be helped on his way by them, and asks them to strive with him in prayer.

Chapter 16

The recipients are asked to receive and assist Phoebe, to greet one another with a holy kiss, and to look out for and turn away from those causing divisions. Their obedience is said to have become known to all, and Paul rejoices over them and desires that they be wise in good and innocent in evil. They are promised that the God of peace will quickly crush Satan under their feet.
