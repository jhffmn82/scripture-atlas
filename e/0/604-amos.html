Abstract written by a model

Amos is a herdsman and sycamore-fig farmer from Tekoa whom the Lord GOD called to prophesy to Israel during the reigns of Uzziah and Jeroboam, two years before the earthquake. His book opens with the LORD roaring from Zion. He receives visions, twice pleading for small Jacob so the LORD relents. Accused by Amaziah of conspiracy and told to flee to Judah, he denies being a professional prophet, pronounces judgment on Amaziah, and repeats that Israel will go captive. A basket of summer fruit signals the end has come, and he denounces those oppressing the needy.

The Book of Amos / Chapter 1

Amos is identified as one of the herdsmen of Tekoa, who saw the words of this book concerning Israel in the days of Uzziah and Jeroboam, two years before the earthquake. He relays that the LORD will roar from Zion and utter his voice from Jerusalem.

Chapter 7

Amos receives three visions and twice pleads for Jacob, who is small, and the LORD relents. Accused by Amaziah of conspiracy and told to flee to Judah, he replies that he was no prophet nor a prophet's son but a herdsman and farmer of sycamore figs whom the LORD called. He then announces the LORD's judgment against Amaziah and repeats that Israel will be led away captive.

Chapter 8

Amos is shown a vision of a basket of summer fruit and, when asked what he sees, answers correctly. He is then told by the Lord GOD that the end has come on Israel, and he delivers the message against those who oppress the needy.
