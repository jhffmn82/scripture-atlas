Abstract written by a model

In Judith, Judea is the land where the Israelites live after returning from captivity and gather for fasting before the sanctuary. In The First Book of the Maccabees, Judea is contested, defended, expanded, inherited, invaded, and raided. In The Book of Isaiah, Judah is a land and people that falls into ruin before being gathered, restored, and exalted. In The Book of Jeremiah, this land is where the remnant of Judah is told to remain, with promises of rebuilding and planting if it obeys. In The Book of Ezekiel, Judah is a tribal territory and people that is mocked by neighboring nations before being joined with Joseph. In The Book of Joel, Judah is promised enduring habitation, flowing brooks, and vengeance for the sale and violence inflicted on its children. In The Book of Amos, Judah is condemned for rejecting the LORD's law, and Jerusalem's palaces are threatened with fire. In The Book of Nahum, Judah is told to keep its feasts because the wicked one will no longer pass through it.

and 2 more works

In The Book of Zephaniah, Judah is ruled by Josiah when the LORD declares judgment against Baal and idolatrous priests. In The Book of Zechariah, Judah is a people and land whose fortunes reverse from judgment and scattering to salvation, blessing, victory, and holiness.

Judea in The First Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Judea first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judea is a land contested and defended across the records. First guarded by Joseph and Azarias, it shelters rescued Jews and marks the pursuit's limit. Jonathan then secures tribute freedom and expanded territory from Demetrius. Finally Antiochus blames Simon for harm there, Simon calls it the fathers' inheritance, and Cendebaeus invades, killing and raiding.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Judea is guarded by Joseph and Azarias while Judas and Simon are away. It receives the rescued Jews of Galilee and Gilead, and its borders are the point to which the routed force of Joseph and Azarias is pursued.

Chapter 11

Jonathan asked Demetrius to make Judea free from tribute, promising three hundred talents. Demetrius confirmed its borders and added the governments of Aphaerema, Lydda and Ramathaim from Samaria, with the king's dues and taxes given back.

Chapter 15

Judea is the land where Antiochus claims Simon has done harm, and Simon says it is the fathers' inheritance. Antiochus orders Cendebaeus to encamp against it, and Cendebaeus comes from Jamnia to invade it, capture and kill people, and raid its highways from Kidron.

Judah in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Judah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judah is a land and people, called Immanuel's land, whose fortunes swing from ruin to restoration. It begins fallen: with Jerusalem it loses supply, support and leadership because its words and deeds provoke the LORD's glory. Syria and Ephraim plot against it but fail, yet Assyria is foretold to overrun it like a flood, leaving briers, thorns and lost vineyards. Later its dispersed are gathered from the four corners, persecutors are cut off, and with Ephraim it plunders and rules neighboring peoples. Finally it is the land where the song of a strong city is sung, and it becomes a terror to Egypt.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

Judah is said to have fallen because their words and deeds provoke the eyes of the LORD's glory. Along with Jerusalem, it loses its supply, support, and leadership.

Chapter 7

Judah was attacked by Syria and Ephraim, who plotted to tear it apart, divide it, and set up the son of Tabeel as king, but they could not prevail. Later its land is foretold to be overrun by Assyria and to become briers and thorns, with vineyards lost and hills used only for oxen and sheep.

Chapter 8

Judah is the land the Assyrian flood will sweep into, overflow and pass through, reaching to the neck. Its land is addressed as belonging to Immanuel, and the stretching wings of the flood will fill its width.

Chapter 11

Judah's dispersed will be gathered from the four corners of the earth. Those who persecute Judah will be cut off, and Judah will not persecute Ephraim. Judah, together with Ephraim, will fly down on the shoulders of the Philistines, plunder the children of the east, and extend power over Edom and Moab, while the children of Ammon obey them.

Chapter 26

The land of Judah is where the song is to be sung on that day. It is the setting for the declaration that the people have a strong city.

Abstract written by a model

Judah is a tribal territory and people, bordering Reuben's portion and having a city gate on its north side. It is mocked and resented by neighboring nations (Ammon, Moab, Seir, Edom) after captivity, then is joined with Joseph's stick, symbolizing the end of division between two nations.

Judah in The Book of Zechariah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Judah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judah is both a people and a land, the house of Judah and its cities, whose fortunes the LORD reverses across the records. It begins under the LORD's indignation for seventy years and scattered by the horns of the nations, once a curse among the nations. It is then promised salvation and a role as a blessing: the LORD plans good for it, and its fasts become joy, gladness, and cheerful feasts. It is the LORD's bow, loaded with Ephraim, and his flock, made like a majestic horse in battle, from which come the cornerstone, tent peg, battle bow and every ruler. Its sons are stirred up against the sons of Greece. In the siege of Jerusalem the LORD opens his eyes on Judah and saves its tents first. It ends fighting at Jerusalem, gathering the nations' wealth, with every pot in Judah holy to the LORD of Armies.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

Judah and its cities have been under the LORD's indignation for seventy years. Judah was scattered by the horns so that no man lifted up his head, and the craftsmen come to cast down the horns of the nations that lifted up their horn against the land of Judah.

Chapter 8

The house of Judah, together with the house of Israel, was a curse among the nations but is promised salvation and a role as a blessing, with the command not to be afraid and to let their hands be strong. The LORD plans good for Judah, and its fasts of the fourth, fifth, seventh, and tenth months will become joy, gladness, and cheerful feasts. A Jew is also the one whose skirt ten men from the nations will take hold of.

Chapter 9

Judah is described as a chieftain-like place for the Philistine remnant and as a bow the LORD bends for himself, loaded with Ephraim. Its sons are to be stirred up against the sons of Greece.

Chapter 10

The house of Judah is the LORD's flock, which he has visited and will make like his majestic horse in battle. The LORD says he will strengthen it, and from it will come the cornerstone, tent peg, battle bow and every ruler.

Chapter 12

Judah is affected by the siege against Jerusalem, but the LORD will open his eyes on the house of Judah and save the tents of Judah first, so that the glory of David's house and of Jerusalem's inhabitants is not magnified above Judah.

Chapter 14

Judah also fights at Jerusalem in that day. The wealth of the surrounding nations, gold, silver and clothing, is gathered in great abundance. Every pot in Judah becomes holy to the LORD of Armies.
