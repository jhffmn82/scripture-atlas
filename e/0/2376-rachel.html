Abstract written by a model

Rachel is Laban's beautiful younger daughter, sister of Leah, and wife of Jacob, who loves her after meeting her at the well with her father's sheep. Barren at first, she envies Leah, demands children, and gives her servant Bilhah to Jacob; Bilhah bears Dan and Naphtali, which Rachel names. She trades mandrakes for a night with Jacob, and God finally opens her womb, so she bears Joseph. She joins Leah in telling Jacob they have no portion in their father's house, then steals Laban's teraphim, hides them in a camel's saddle, and deceives him by claiming her period. Later, in hard labor on the way to Ephrath, she bears Benjamin, naming him Benoni, and dies. Jacob buries her on the way to Ephrath (Bethlehem) and sets a pillar on her grave.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 29

Rachel, Laban's younger daughter, is beautiful in form and attractive, and arrives at the well with her father's sheep, which she keeps. Jacob kisses her and tells her he is Rebekah's son, and she runs to tell her father. Jacob loves her and eventually marries her, but she is barren.

Chapter 30

Rachel, childless, envies Leah and demands children from Jacob, then gives him her servant Bilhah so she may have children through her. She names Bilhah's sons Dan and Naphtali, trades Leah mandrakes for a night with Jacob, and finally has her own womb opened by God, bearing Joseph.

Chapter 31

Rachel, with Leah, answers Jacob that they have no portion left in their father's house, are treated as foreigners, and that Jacob should do whatever God has said. She steals her father's teraphim, hides them in the camel's saddle, and sits on them, telling Laban she cannot rise because she is having her period, so he does not find them.

Chapter 35

Rachel goes into hard labor on the way from Bethel before reaching Ephrath, and the midwife tells her she will have another son. She dies as her soul departs, after naming the child Benoni, and is buried on the way to Ephrath (Bethlehem), where Jacob sets a pillar on her grave that remains to this day. She is listed as the mother of Joseph and Benjamin.
