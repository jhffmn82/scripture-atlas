Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, Samaria is the hill Omri bought, built on, and named after Shemer, and it became the city where Omri was buried and Ahab reigned. In The Second Book of Kings, the city of Samaria suffers famine before its people discover that the Syrian army has fled and plunder its camp. In The Second Book of Kings, Samaria is the capital of Israel under Hoshea, besieged by Benhadad, associated with Jehu's actions, and eventually captured and resettled by Assyria. In The Book of Ezekiel, Samaria is represented as Oholah, the elder sister of Oholibah, and is judged for spiritual prostitution and idolatry. In The Book of Hosea, Samaria is a rebellious place whose people worship a calf idol and whose king and inhabitants face destruction. In The Book of Amos, Samaria is a mountainous city marked by oppression, hoarded plunder, and judgment against its houses and inhabitants. In The Book of Micah, Samaria is identified as Jacob's disobedience and is doomed to become rubble, with its idols destroyed and burned. In The Acts of the Apostles, Samaria receives Philip's preaching of Christ, experiences great joy, and receives Peter and John after the apostles hear it has accepted God's word.

Samaria in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Samaria first appears

Abstract written by a model

Samaria is a city and capital of Israel under Hoshea. It appears first in Elisha's account, where a blinded Syrian army is led in and fed, then Benhadad besieges it and famine follows until Elisha's prophecy of cheap flour and barley is fulfilled. It holds Ahab's seventy sons, and Jehu kills Ahab's remnant there and is buried there. Finally Assyria besieges and captures it in Hoshea's ninth year and settles foreigners there.

Chapter 6

Elisha leads the blinded Syrian army into Samaria, where their eyes are opened in the middle of the city and they are fed. Later Benhadad besieges Samaria, and it suffers a great famine.

Chapter 7

Samaria is the place where the gate will see flour and barley sold for a shekel, according to Elisha's word. The people there are in famine, and the prophecy is fulfilled after the Syrian camp is plundered.

Chapter 10

Samaria is where Ahab's seventy sons are kept and where Jehu's letters are sent. Jehu later strikes all who remained to Ahab there, and he is buried there at his death.

Chapter 17

Samaria, Hoshea's capital, is besieged for three years by the king of Assyria and taken in Hoshea's ninth year. Its cities are then occupied by foreign settlers brought by Assyria in place of the Israelites, and its high places house their gods.

Samaria in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Samaria first appears

Abstract written by a model

Samaria, represented as Oholah, is the elder sister of Oholibah (Jerusalem). Belonging to the LORD, she nonetheless practiced spiritual prostitution from Egypt onward, lusting after Assyrians and defiling herself with idols, child sacrifice, a defiled sanctuary and profaned Sabbaths. The LORD delivered her to her Assyrian lovers, who took her children and killed her with the sword, making her a byword. Yet her sins were under half of Jerusalem's, and her captivity will be reversed.

Oholah is the elder sister, representing Samaria, who played the prostitute in Egypt and again when she was the LORD's, doting on the Assyrians and defiling herself with their idols. Samaria in The Book of Hosea

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Samaria first appears

Abstract written by a model

Samaria is a place with a king, whose people rebel against the LORD, their God. It begins with a command to discard its calf idol, which is called a workman's creation and will be broken. It then faces terror over the calves, with its king floating away like a twig, and ends bearing guilt through violent destruction by the sword.

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

Samaria is told to throw out its calf idol, and the LORD's anger burns against them. Its calf is declared the work of a workman, not God, and will be broken in pieces.

Chapter 10

The inhabitants of Samaria will be in terror for the calves of Beth Aven. Samaria and her king are said to float away like a twig on the water.

Chapter 13

Samaria will bear her guilt because she has rebelled against her God. Its people will fall by the sword, its infants will be dashed in pieces, and its pregnant women will be ripped open.

Samaria is a mountainous place marked by unrest and oppression. Its elites feel secure on the mountain and hoard plunder in palaces. Ashdod and Egypt are summoned to witness its wrongdoing. Its comfortable are barely rescued, and the LORD will deliver up the city, striking down its houses, great and little.
