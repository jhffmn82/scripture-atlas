Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, the covenant with Abram is described as the LORD's promise of the land from Egypt's river to the Euphrates to his offspring. In Genesis, God's covenant with Abraham is described as an everlasting covenant requiring Abraham and his offspring to keep it, with circumcision as its token. In Exodus, the LORD's covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob is described as the basis for bringing Israel into the land of Canaan. In Leviticus, the covenant is described as the LORD's bond with Israel's ancestors, remembered even in exile and not utterly broken. In Deuteronomy, the covenant is described as the oath to the fathers that motivates the LORD's deliverance of Israel and his promise of loving-kindness. In The First Book of the Maccabees, the covenant of the fathers is described as the commandment Mattathias and his family vow to keep faithfully. In Psalms, the covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob is described as an everlasting covenant promising Canaan as their inheritance. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the covenant is described as an everlasting bond passed from Noah through Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and the twelve tribes.

and 2 more works

In Daniel (Greek), the holy covenant is described as the object of hostility, profanation, and corruption by the king and covenant-breakers. In The Letter to the Hebrews, the first covenant is described as the covenant made with Israel's fathers, now obsolete and near vanishing through the new covenant.

first covenant in The Letter to the Hebrews

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where first covenant first appears

Abstract written by a model

The first covenant is the one the Lord made with Israel's fathers when he led them out of Egypt; they did not continue in it. It brought the Levitical priesthood and tithes, yet made nothing perfect, appointing weak men as high priests. Its commandment is annulled as weak and useless, and the priesthood's change requires a change of law. Found faulty, since a second was sought, it is now obsolete, aging, and near vanishing through the new covenant.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

The law gave the people the Levitical priesthood and commanded tithes, but it made nothing perfect. Because the priesthood has changed, the law must change too, and a foregoing commandment is annulled for its weakness and uselessness. It appoints men with weakness as high priests, while the later word of the oath appoints a perfected Son forever.

Chapter 8

The first covenant is the one the Lord made with the fathers of Israel when he took them by the hand to lead them out of Egypt; they did not continue in it. It is said not to have been faultless, since a second was sought, and the announcement of a new covenant has made it obsolete, aged, and near to vanishing away.
