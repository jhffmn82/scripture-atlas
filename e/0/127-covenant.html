Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, the covenant with Abram concerns the promised land, which the LORD gives to Abram's offspring from Egypt's river to the Euphrates. In Genesis, God's covenant with Abraham is everlasting, promising to multiply Abraham, make him the father of nations and kings, be their God, and give them Canaan, with circumcision as its token. In Exodus, the LORD's covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob concerns the land of Canaan and grounds His promise to bring Israel into the land. In Leviticus, the covenant is established with the obedient, while those who break it face its vengeance; even in exile, the LORD remembers it with Jacob, Isaac, Abraham, and their ancestors. In Deuteronomy, the covenant is the oath the LORD swore to Israel's fathers, which He keeps with loving-kindness toward those who love Him and keep His commandments. In The First Book of the Maccabees, the covenant of the fathers is a commandment to be kept faithfully as Mattathias, his sons, and their kindred walk in it and defend its law and ordinances. In Psalms, the covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob is an everlasting covenant promising the land of Canaan as their inheritance. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the covenant is a lasting bond for the righteous men's children, made everlasting with Noah and passed through Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and the twelve tribes.

In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, the covenant with the people Israel is made by the Father with all the house of Israel and is fulfilled through their gathering, gospel knowledge, and restoration. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, the covenant to their fathers is fulfilled when the dwellings of the house of Israel no longer remain desolate. In The Book of Ether, the covenant God made with Abraham is fulfilled for the house of Israel through the gathering of those scattered from the four quarters of the earth and from the north countries. In The Book of Moroni, the covenants of the Eternal Father made with the house of Israel are to be fulfilled.

first covenant in The Letter to the Hebrews

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where first covenant first appears

Abstract written by a model

The first covenant is the one the Lord made with Israel's fathers when he led them out of Egypt; they did not continue in it. It brought the Levitical priesthood and tithes, yet made nothing perfect, appointing weak men as high priests. Its commandment is annulled as weak and useless, and the priesthood's change requires a change of law. Found faulty, since a second was sought, it is now obsolete, aging, and near vanishing through the new covenant.

Chapter 7

The law gave the people the Levitical priesthood and commanded tithes, but it made nothing perfect. Because the priesthood has changed, the law must change too, and a foregoing commandment is annulled for its weakness and uselessness. It appoints men with weakness as high priests, while the later word of the oath appoints a perfected Son forever.

the covenants of the Lord in The Second Book of Nephi

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the covenants of the Lord first appears

Abstract written by a model

The covenants of the Lord are promises made by the Lord God with His covenant people, the house of Israel, and the children of men. They concern Joseph’s lineage, Israel’s restoration, gathering, preservation, and knowledge. Spoken by holy prophets from the beginning, they unfold through the Lord’s remembrance of Abraham and his seed and culminate in His fulfillment of these covenants.

the covenant with the people Israel in The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman

Mormon · the work’s page · read it where the covenant with the people Israel first appears

Abstract written by a model

The covenant with the people Israel is made by the Father with all the house of Israel. It matters because the Father remembers and fulfills it by gathering his people, bringing them to knowledge of the fulness of the gospel, and restoring them to their fathers’ lands for inheritance. Its fulfillment begins when these sayings come forth among the Gentiles and proceeds through works from the Gentiles to the seed of the house of Israel. Those who repent and hearken come into it; among robbers, repentance requires a promise to murder no more and brings liberty. In the Father’s due time, the covenant is fulfilled through Israel’s gathering and restoration.

The Father has made a covenant with all the people of the house of Israel. He will remember and fulfill that covenant by gathering them and bringing them to knowledge of the fulness of the gospel.

Chapter 20

The covenant made by the Father with the house of Israel will be fulfilled when the words of Isaiah are fulfilled. The Father will remember the covenant, gather his people in his own due time, and give them again the land of their fathers for their inheritance.

Chapter 21

The covenant of the Father will be fulfilled through works that come forth from the Gentiles to the seed of the house of Israel. Those who repent and hearken will come into the covenant.

Chapter 29

The covenant concerns the restoration of the children of Israel to the lands of their inheritance. The Lord remembers it and will fulfill it.
