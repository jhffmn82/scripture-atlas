wealth

topic · 3 works · 9 quoted facts · 7 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The Tao Teh King, or The Tao and Its Characteristics, wealth is described as valued over life, though pursuing it brings sorrow and pain, and arrogance over it brings evil. In Chuang Tzŭ, wealth is described as pursued for power, influence, and pleasures, but excessive pursuit and accumulation bring confusion, misery, disease, shame, unhappiness, and fear. In Mencius, Book I (Liang Hui Wang), wealth is described as a desire admitted by King Xuan of Qi, which Mencius argues should be gratified for the people.

wealth in Chuang Tzŭ

Chuang Tzŭ · the work’s page · read it where wealth first appears

Abstract written by a model

Wealth is pursued for power, influence, and pleasures and requires confidence according to Man Kou Tê. When excessive, it becomes a curse: pursuit and accumulation bring confusion, misery, disease, shame, unhappiness, and fear from thieves and bandits.
