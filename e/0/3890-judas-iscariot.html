Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Matthew, Judas Iscariot betrays Jesus for thirty pieces of silver, identifies him with a kiss, returns the silver in remorse, and hangs himself. In The Good News According to Mark, Judas Iscariot betrays Jesus for money and identifies him at Gethsemane for arrest with a kiss. In The Gospel According to Luke, Judas Iscariot bargains to deliver Jesus for money, and then leads the crowd and approaches to kiss Jesus. In The Gospel According to John, Judas Iscariot is one of the twelve disciples, steals from the money box, objects to the costly ointment, and betrays Jesus by leading soldiers and officers to the garden. In The Acts of the Apostles, Judas guides those who take Jesus, obtains the field Akeldama with the reward of his wickedness, falls headlong and bursts open, and falls away to his own place.

Judas Iscariot in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Judas Iscariot first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve disciples, betrays Jesus to the chief priests for thirty pieces of silver. At the meal he asks if it is him and is told 'You said it.' He leads an armed crowd and identifies Jesus with a kiss. Seeing Jesus condemned, he feels remorse, returns the silver, is refused, throws it in the sanctuary, and hangs himself.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 26

Judas, one of the twelve, goes to the chief priests and receives thirty pieces of silver to deliver Jesus. At the meal he asks 'It isn't me, is it, Rabbi?' and is told 'You said it.' He later leads the armed crowd and identifies Jesus with a kiss.

Chapter 27

Judas, who betrayed Jesus, feels remorse on seeing him condemned and returns the thirty pieces of silver, saying he sinned by betraying innocent blood. The chief priests refuse responsibility, and he throws the silver down in the sanctuary and hangs himself.

Judas Iscariot in The Gospel According to John

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Judas Iscariot first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judas Iscariot, son of Simon, is one of the twelve disciples and the one who betrays Jesus. He keeps the money box and steals from it, and he objects to the costly ointment while caring nothing for the poor. At the meal the devil has put betrayal into his heart; Jesus gives him the dipped bread, Satan enters him, and he leaves into the night at Jesus's urging to act quickly. He then leads soldiers and officers with lanterns, torches, and weapons to the garden he knows, standing with them when Jesus identifies himself.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 12

Judas Iscariot, Simon's son and one of the disciples who would betray Jesus, objects that the ointment should have been sold for three hundred denarii and given to the poor. The text says he did not care for the poor but was a thief who held the money box and used to steal from it.

Chapter 13

Judas Iscariot, son of Simon, is described as having the devil put betrayal into his heart and as holding the money box. Jesus gives him the dipped piece of bread, Satan enters him, and Jesus tells him, "What you do, do quickly." He takes the morsel and goes out immediately, and it was night.

Chapter 18

Judas, who betrayed Jesus, knew the garden because Jesus often met there with his disciples. He led a detachment of soldiers and officers there with lanterns, torches, and weapons, and stood with them when Jesus identified himself.

Abstract written by a model

Judas guided those who took Jesus, despite being counted among the apostles and sharing their ministry. He obtained a field Akeldama with the reward of his wickedness, fell headlong and burst open, and fell away to his own place, so the apostles prayed for another to take his place.

The Acts of the Apostles / Chapter 1

Judas was guide to those who took Jesus, though he was counted with the apostles and received a portion in their ministry. He obtained a field with the reward for his wickedness, fell headlong, and his body burst open. The apostles prayed that his place might be taken by another, since he fell away to go to his own place.
