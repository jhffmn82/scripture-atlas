Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, Zarathustra is a saint who founds the Mazda-worshipper order, authors the Yasna, and repudiates the Daêvas. In Yasna, Zarathustra is a prophet and spiritual master who proclaims Ahura Mazda’s doctrines and guides the faithful. In Visparad, Zarathustrôtema is an Ahurian ritual title whose remedies accompany offered Haomas and Zaothras. In Visparad, Zarathustra Spitâma is a holy terrestrial lord and regulator associated with the Avesta, offerings, sacrifice, and homage. In Afrinagan, Gahs and Miscellaneous Fragments, Zarathustra is a saint, founder, model, and order-giver who instructs disciples in sacrificial worship. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII, Zarathustra is the first mortal taught the law after Yima and questions Ahura Mazda about creation, mortality, purity, and the dead. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Zarathustra is a holy law-abiding man who seeks Ahura Mazda’s guidance on cleansing, defilement, sickness, and lawful practice. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, Zarathustra is Ahura Mazda’s Zaotar and disciple who receives ritual instruction, offers sacrifices, and restores Mazda’s Law.

and 2 more works

In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, Zarathustra is a holy religious leader who receives divine instruction, teaches the good Law, and opposes the Daêvas. In Nyayish, Spitama Zarathustra is a holy man with a Fravashi whom worshippers follow and whom Ahura Mazda instructs in worship and sacrifice.

Zarathustra in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Zarathustra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Zarathustra, also called Zarathustra Spitâma, is a saint, holy man, just figure, Mazda-worshipper, friend of Mazda’s lore, and foe of the D(a)êvas. Born as the son of Pourushaspa, he founds the Mazda-worshipper order and is central to its ritual order, religion, faith, lore, utterances, and law. He abjures the Daêvas’ shelter and headship through questions and consultations with Ahura Mazda, and causes the demon-gods to vanish into the ground. He recites the Ahuna-vairya first, intoning it four times, and asks Ahura Mazda about the primordial word that existed before the created world; he receives its explanation, meaning, structure, and proper performance. He authors the Yasna and speaks a word for it. Zarathustra praises Mazda-made H(a)oma, encounters and questions it, offers it carefully, and seeks blessings and protections. He praises good thoughts, words, and deeds, repudiates their evil counterparts, and declares that he will incite households, villages, Zantus, and provinces to follow the Religion of Ahura in thought, word, and deed. Across the records, his holy Fravashi is repeatedly approached, offered to, propitiated, praised, and worshipped, alongside his sanctity and his religious inheritance.

Abstract written by a model

Zarathustra is a righteous man, prophet, spiritual master, herald of Ahura Mazda, and chieftain of both the people’s spiritual cause and the rite. He alone among those mentioned has hearkened to the enunciations of Ahura and Asha, and he seeks to declare their doctrines, truths, mighty acts, regulations, and laws to waiting people and living believers. His central commitment is to Righteous Order, Good Mind, Obedience, Ahura Mazda’s will, and the worthy aims of the faith. He rejects disobedience, Evil Mind, arrogance, falsehood, the Lie, and neglect of the Kine, while opposing the wicked and the disciples of the Karpans and Kavis. He begins in prayer, asking Ahura Mazda for spiritual help, long-lasting life, instruction, guidance, protection, and the capacity to teach. He offers his body, thought, word, deeds, Good Mind, Obedience, and Sovereign Power, makes Yasna confessions, and offers propitiation. He seeks Ahura Mazda’s Realm, the good abode, straight paths from Righteous Order, and eternal life in the Realm of Good Mind. As the account unfolds, Zarathustra establishes Ahura Mazda’s precepts, speaks for the faithful, teaches the corporeal world what Ahura Mazda showed him, and guides his voice toward glory through Mãthras and regulations. He is supported by Kavi Vîstâspa and addresses Haêkat-aspas, Frashaostra, and Gâmâspa in advancing the great cause. He is associated with the five holy Gâthas; his words and deeds are worshipped in ritual. In the end, he answers concerning rites and sacrifices, is worshipped with sacrifice as chieftain of the rite and holy lord of ritual order, and receives instruction for prescribed prayers and the last ending of life.

Abstract written by a model

Zarathustra is a person, a worshipper of Mazda, follower of Ahura Mazda, Zaotar, son of Pourushaspa, master of the material world, and enemy of the Daêvas. Spitama Zarathustra is instructed to offer sacrifice to Ardvi Sûra Anâhita. He is identified as the son of Pourushaspa and as the person whom Ahura Mazda sought to bring to think, speak, and act according to his law.

XX.

Spitama Zarathustra is repeatedly instructed to offer sacrifices to Ardvi Sûra Anâhita. He asks her how to worship and forward her, offers her a sacrifice in Airyana Vaêgah, and receives the boon that he may bring Kavi Vîstâspa to think, speak, and act according to the law.

VIII. Tîr Yast.

Spitama Zarathustra receives Ahura Mazda’s teachings about Tistrya, his worship, transformations, battle, and role in sending waters to the world. He asks what sacrifice and invocation should properly be performed in Tistrya’s honour.

IX. Gôs Yast.

Zarathustra is described as a worshipper of Mazda and a follower of Zarathustra, and he offers Drvâspa a sacrifice in Airyana Vaêgah to obtain Hutaosa’s adherence to the law and support for the Mazdean law.

XXIV.

Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda how a faithful man should drink the cleanly prepared libations offered to Mithra. He is instructed concerning bodily washing, strokes, and knowledge of the staota yêsnya.

XI. Srôsh Yast Hâdhôkht.

Zarathustra is addressed in instructions concerning good prayer, the pronouncement of protective words, and the Law. The text also worships all the words of Zarathustra and all his good deeds, done and to be done.

XII. Rashn Yast.

Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda to answer truthfully about what in the Holy Word is true, progress-making, discerning, healthful, wise, happy, and powerful to destroy.

Zarathustra in Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Zarathustra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Zarathustra is a pure, holy Spitama and Âthravan presented as the foremost mortal and religious leader of the material world. He is the first thinker, speaker, and doer of what is good; the first Priest, Warrior, and Plougher; and the first to know and teach Mazda’s good creations. He possesses Bull, Holiness, the Word, dominion, and kingly Glory, follows and proclaims the Law of Ahura and Mazda, praises Asha, worships Mazda and the faithful Fravashis, and rejects the Daêvas. His birth and growth bring joy to waters, plants, and good creations, while his word and song drive the Daêvas beneath the earth. He is described as lord and master of the world, wisest, best-ruling, brightest, most glorious, and uniquely worthy of sacrifice, prayer, propitiation, and glorification. He receives revelation and instruction from Ahura Mazda, including teachings about Glory, the faithful and wicked after death, words of holiness, remedies, and Verethraghna. He invokes powers for danger and battle, makes sacrifices with libations and baresma, and seeks righteousness in thought, speech, and deed, along with speed, strength, health, and exceptional sight. Zarathustra’s work unfolds as the spread and teaching of the good Law throughout the seven Karshvares. He teaches Vîstâspa and others about holiness, worship, sacrifice, charity, the defeat of evil, and the faithful soul’s path to Garô-nmâna. He also blesses Vîstâspa with long life, welfare, victory, virtues, and freedom from sickness and death. Across the records, he appears as a holy worshipper, recipient of divine instruction, destroyer of the Daêvas’ influence, and bearer of a Law that directs thought, speech, action, and the good life.

XIII. Farvardîn Yast.

Spitama Zarathustra is instructed to proclaim the power and assistance of the Fravashis of the faithful. He is told to recite their fiend-smiting words when traveling on frightening and dangerous roads.

XXIII.

Zarathustra is worshipped as the first thinker, speaker, and doer of what is good, and as the first Priest, Warrior, and Plougher of the ground. He first taught and possessed the Bull, Holiness, the Word, obedience to the Word, dominion, and the good things made by Mazda; his birth and growth caused the waters, plants, and good creations to rejoice.

XXVI.

Zarathustra is described as the first and best among those heard of as worthy of sacrifice, as a follower of Ahura and performer of the law. He is also called the lord and master of the material world and the wisest, best-ruling, brightest, most glorious, and most worthy of worship among beings.

XIV. Bahrâm Yast.

Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda who is the best-armed of the heavenly gods and what remedies and invocations concern Verethraghna. He offers sacrifice and prayer to Verethraghna, seeking victorious thinking, speaking, doing, addressing, and answering.

XV. Râm Yast.

Zarathustra is addressed as holy and is instructed to invoke Vayu’s names during battles, attacks, imprisonment, and other dangers. Vayu also tells Zarathustra how to use the baresma in worship.

XVI. Dîn Yast.

Zarathustra sacrifices to the most right Kista and calls her to rise from her seat and accompany him. He seeks righteousness of thought, speech, and deed, as well as speed, strength, health, and extraordinary sight.

XVII. Ashi Yast.

Zarathustra is described as the first mortal to recite the praise of Asha and sacrifice to Ahura Mazda and the Amesha-Spentas. Ashi Vanguhi identifies him as the one who can daunt Angra Mainyu, and she grants his request that Hutaosa follow, speak, and act according to the law and spread it.

XVIII. Âstâd Yast.

Zarathustra, called Spitama Zarathustra, receives Ahura Mazda's teaching about the Aryan Glory and its benefits.

XIX. Zamyâd Yast.

Zarathustra is addressed by Ahura Mazda and is associated with the holy Law and the Aryan nations. The kingly Glory clave unto him, causing him to think, speak, and act according to the Law; the Ahuna Vairya he sang drove the Daêvas beneath the earth.

XXI. Yast Fragment.

Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda about the word containing the glorification of all good things and about the recitals of the praise of Holiness. He is addressed as Spitama Zarathustra and as holy Zarathustra.

Yast XXII.

Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda where the souls of faithful and wicked people abide after death and what they experience.

Âfrîn Paighambar Zartûst / XXIII. Âfrîn Paighambar Zartûst.

Âfrîn Paighambar Zartûst presents Zarathustra speaking a blessing to king Vîstâspa. The blessing invokes a good, exalted, and long life, many virtues, sons, welfare, victory, and freedom from sickness and death.

XXIV. Vîstâsp Yast.

Zarathustra blesses and teaches the young king Vîstâspa, instructing him in holiness, the Law of the worshippers of Mazda, proper sacrifice, prayer, charity, and the defeat of evil. He teaches how the faithful soul reaches Garô-nmâna and addresses Ahura Mazda with worship.
