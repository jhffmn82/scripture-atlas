Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Isaiah, it is the topic of Israel's sins and iniquities, which burdened the LORD but were to be blotted out despite their transgressions. In Baruch, it is the topic of Israel's sin and confession, including their acknowledgment of disobedience, idolatry, and the resulting plagues and curse.

Israel's sin and confession in Baruch

author unknown · about 299 BC · the work’s page · read it where Israel's sin and confession first appears

Abstract written by a model

Israel's sin and confession: the people acknowledge the Lord's righteousness and their own shame, admitting disobedience since the exodus from Egypt, ignoring the prophets, and serving strange gods by following wicked hearts, which brought plagues and the curse upon them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Baruch / Chapter 1

The people are to confess that righteousness belongs to the Lord and confusion of face to Judah, Jerusalem, and their kings, princes, priests, prophets, and fathers. They admit disobedience since the exodus from Egypt, ignoring the prophets and following their own wicked hearts to serve strange gods, which brought the plagues and the curse upon them.
