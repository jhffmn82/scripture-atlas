Also called: Kiriath Arba, the cities of Hebron

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Joshua, Hebron is a city in Judah's hill country given to Caleb as an inheritance, from which he drove out Anak's three sons, and it serves as a city of refuge. In The Second Book of Samuel, Hebron is where David settles, is anointed king over Judah, reigns seven years and six months, and where Absalom proclaims himself king. In 1 Chronicles, Hebron is where David reigns seven years and six months, has six sons, and is anointed king by Israel's elders and tribes.

Hebron in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · about 600 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hebron first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hebron is a city in the hill country of Judah, formerly called Kiriath Arba after the greatest man among the Anakim, described as the father of Anak. Joshua gave it to Caleb son of Jephunneh as an inheritance because he followed the LORD wholeheartedly, and it remained his to this day. Caleb drove out Anak's three sons from it. It appears in Judah's hill country list, in the group of nine, and serves as a city of refuge for the man slayer.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

Hebron was given by Joshua to Caleb the son of Jephunneh as an inheritance, and it remained his inheritance to this day because he followed the LORD wholeheartedly. Its earlier name was Kiriath Arba, after the greatest man among the Anakim.

Chapter 15

Hebron, also called Kiriath Arba, is named after the father of Anak and is given to Caleb as his portion among Judah. Caleb drove out the three sons of Anak from it. It is listed again among the hill country cities in the group of nine.

Hebron in The Second Book of Samuel

author unknown · about 950 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hebron first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hebron is a city David is directed by the LORD to go up to, where his men and their households settle. Judah anoints him king there, and he reigns over Judah seven years and six months. Events there include the birth of his six sons, Abner's feast and killing at the gate, and Abner's burial with David weeping. Israel's tribes and elders later covenant with David and anoint him king. Finally, Absalom uses Hebron as a pretext for a vow and as the base for proclaiming himself king.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Hebron is the city the LORD directed David to go up to. David's men and their households lived in its cities, and the men of Judah anointed David king there. Joab and his men returned to Hebron at daybreak after burying Asahel.

Chapter 3

Hebron is where David's six sons are born, where Abner comes with twenty men and is feasted by David, and where Abner is brought back and killed in the gate. Abner is buried there, and David weeps at his grave.

Chapter 5

Hebron is where the tribes and elders of Israel come to David, where he makes a covenant and is anointed king over Israel. David reigned there over Judah seven years and six months.

Chapter 15

Hebron was the place Absalom claimed he would go to pay a vow to the LORD. It was actually where he arranged for spies to proclaim him king at the sound of the trumpet.

Hebron in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · 449 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hebron first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hebron is the place where David reigned seven years and six months and where six sons were born to him. Israel's elders came there to covenant with him and anoint him king, and the tribes' armed men gathered, wholehearted, to transfer Saul's kingdom to David, feasting three days.

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

Hebron is where six sons were born to David: Amnon, Daniel, Absalom, Adonijah, Shephatiah, and Ithream. David reigned there seven years and six months.

Chapter 11

Hebron is the place where all Israel and its elders come to David. There David makes a covenant with them before the LORD and they anoint him king.

Hebron is where the armed men of the tribes gathered to turn Saul's kingdom to David according to the LORD's word. They came with a perfect heart to make David king over all Israel and stayed with him three days, eating and drinking.
