Abstract written by a model

Benevolence is the feeling of commiseration, Heaven-conferred dignity, and man’s quiet home. It can be practised without hindrance and brings glory to a prince; without it, propriety and righteousness are absent. It begins as either force-backed pretence or virtue-grounded practice, with virtue producing sincere submission, and ends by likening the benevolent man to an archer.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

Benevolence is presented as either a pretence supported by force or a practice grounded in virtue. When practised through virtue, it leads people to submit sincerely.

Chapter 4

Benevolence brings glory to a prince, while its opposite brings disgrace.

Chapter 6

Benevolence is the principle represented by the feeling of commiseration.

Chapter 7

Benevolence is described as the most honourable dignity conferred by Heaven and the quiet home in which man should dwell. No one can prevent a person from being benevolent, and lacking benevolence leads to the absence of propriety and righteousness.
