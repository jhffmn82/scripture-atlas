Abstract written by a model

This document is John's First Letter, a five-chapter pastoral epistle in which the author writes to his readers, addressing them as little children, to ground them in fellowship with God, the Father, and Jesus Christ, his Son. It opens with the authors' testimony to what they heard, saw, and touched of the Word of life, and announces the central message that God is light with no darkness in him. Walking in the light brings fellowship and the cleansing blood of Jesus; denying sin is self-deception, while confessing sins brings forgiveness from the faithful and righteous God. Chapter 2 presents Jesus Christ as the righteous Counselor and atoning sacrifice for the whole world. Knowing him means keeping his commandments, above all loving one's brother. The readers are told not to love the passing world and are warned about antichrists who deny that Jesus is the Christ, and they are urged to let what they heard from the beginning and the anointing remain in them. Chapter 3 calls believers children of God who will be like him when he is revealed. It contrasts righteousness with sin, which belongs to the devil, and shows love through Cain's murder of his brother as the counterexample and through laying down one's life and helping a brother in need. It ends with confidence before God and the command to believe in his Son and love one another, with the Spirit as the sign that he remains in us. Chapter 4 urges testing spirits, with confession that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh as the test, and grounds love in God's nature, shown by sending his only born Son. Perfect love casts out fear, and claiming to love God while hating a brother is a lie. Chapter 5 teaches that believing Jesus is the Christ means being born of God, and that faith overcomes the world. The Spirit, the water, and the blood agree in testifying that God gave eternal life in his Son. The letter closes with confident prayer according to God's will, remarks on sin that does and does not lead to death, and a warning to keep from idols.

1 John / Chapter 1

The opening chapter of John's First Letter has the authors declare what they have heard, seen, and touched concerning the Word of life, which was with the Father and was revealed to them. They write so that the readers may share fellowship with them and with the Father and his Son, Jesus Christ, and so that the authors' joy may be fulfilled. The message they announce is that God is light and in him is no darkness at all. The chapter then contrasts walking in darkness, which makes claims of fellowship a lie, with walking in the light, where the blood of Jesus Christ cleanses from all sin. It closes by saying that denying sin deceives us and makes God a liar, while confessing sins brings forgiveness and cleansing from the faithful and righteous God.

Chapter 2

In Chapter 2 the writer addresses his readers as little children, telling them he writes so they may not sin and that Jesus Christ, the righteous Counselor with the Father, is the atoning sacrifice for the sins of the whole world. Knowing him is shown by keeping his commandments and walking as he walked, and the old commandment, which is also new, is to love one's brother rather than hate him. The readers are told not to love the world, which is passing away with its lusts, while whoever does God's will remains forever. The writer warns that many antichrists have arisen, who went out from the community and deny that Jesus is the Christ, the Father and the Son. He urges the readers to let what they heard from the beginning and the anointing from the Holy One remain in them, so they remain in the Son and the Father and have boldness at his appearing.

Chapter 3

Chapter 3 describes believers as children of God, loved by the Father, who will be like him when he is revealed and who purify themselves in hope. It contrasts those who do righteousness and remain in him with those who sin and are of the devil, noting that the Son of God was revealed to take away sins and destroy the devil's works. The chapter makes love for one another the message from the beginning, contrasting it with Cain, who killed his brother, and says the world hates believers. It defines love by the laying down of his life for us and calls for love in deed and truth, including help for a brother in need. It closes with confidence toward God, who is greater than our hearts, and the commandment to believe in the name of his Son, Jesus Christ, and love one another, with the Spirit as the sign that he remains in us.

Chapter 4

The author addresses beloved readers, urging them to test spirits because many false prophets have gone into the world, and giving confession that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh as the test of what is of God. He says the readers have overcome the false teachers because the one in them is greater than the one in the world. He then calls them to love one another, since God is love and God showed his love by sending his only born Son as the atoning sacrifice for sins. The author teaches that remaining in love means remaining in God, that perfect love casts out fear, and that anyone who claims to love God but hates his brother is a liar. The chapter ends with the commandment that he who loves God should also love his brother.

Chapter 5

Chapter 5 teaches that whoever believes Jesus is the Christ has been born of God, and that loving God means keeping his commandments, which are not grievous. Faith in Jesus as the Son of God is the victory that overcomes the world. Jesus Christ came by water and blood, and the Spirit, the water, and the blood agree as one in testifying about him. God's testimony is that he gave eternal life, which is in his Son, so those who have the Son have life. The writer closes by discussing confident prayer according to God's will, sin leading and not leading to death, the one born of God who keeps himself from the evil one, and a warning to little children to keep themselves from idols.
