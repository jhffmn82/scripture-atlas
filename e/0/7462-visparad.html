Visparad

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This document is a sequence of Visparad ritual units centered on Yasna offerings, praise, worship, and correctly performed sacred speech. It opens with offerings to the lords of all parts of creation, spiritual, earthly, aquatic, animal, seasonal, and ritual, and repeatedly joins Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Zarathustra Spitâma, the Gâthas, the Yasna Haptanghâiti, Haoma, Zaothra, Baresman, Fire, the Kine, waters, lands, plants, and holy communities within one ritual order. Visparad III formally begins the Haoma Offering through the Zaotar’s roll-call of ritual officers and community members, with the Ratu affirming their duties. Subsequent units present the speaker as priest, invoker, reciter, chanter, and confessor, offering body, life, ritual implements, prayers, sacred words, pressed Haomas, and praise. The text stresses exact memorising, chanting, recital, and offerings made without addition or omission, especially for the Gâthas and Yasna Haptanghâiti. It invokes numerous holy beings and principles, including Sraosha, Ashi, Âramaiti, Rashnu, Mithra, Fravashis, and Asha Vahista, and seeks blessing, righteousness, wisdom, health, fertility, protection, victory, salvation, and the advancement of settlements, houses, herds, and communities. Ethical instruction appears in the call to pursue good thoughts, words, and deeds, lawful conduct, husbandry, generosity to the needy, and Righteousness, while rejecting evil and unlawful acts. The later sections broaden worship to saints past, present, and future, the holy and clean creation, Heaven and the Best World, and the path toward them. The work closes by linking memorised good thoughts, words, and deeds, the Ahuna-vairya, and Airyemâ-ishyô with reward, healing, growth, victory, and the best approach to Heaven.

Visparad / Visparad I.

Visparad I announces and completes Yasna offerings to the lords of spiritual, earthly, aquatic, terrestrial, winged, wild, and cloven-hoofed creatures. It concerns the Yearly festivals, the Gâthas, the Yasna Haptanghâiti, ritual chants, seasons, mountains, deities, and other holy lords of the ritual order. It also includes offerings to settlements of the future, praises and Myazdas, questions and lore of Ahura, and a farm-house with pastures for blessed cattle.

Visparad II.

The speaker uses the Zaothra and Baresman to approach and praise spiritual, earthly, aquatic, terrestrial, animal, seasonal, ritual, and other chieftains. The unit concerns Ahura Mazda, Zarathustra Spitâma, Asha Vahista, the holy Yearly festivals, the Gâthas, several named divine beings, settlements, and ritual authorities. It presents praise and homage as directed toward those who uphold the ritual order, righteousness, creation, fertility, victory, blessing, and the advancement of settlements.

Visparad III.

Visparad III. begins the Haoma Offering with the Zaotar’s roll-call of priests and other members of the community. The Zaotar calls for the Hâvanan, Âtarevakhsha, Frabaretar, Âberet, Âsnatar, Raêthwiskar, and Sraoshâvareza, while the Ratu answers that he will come or fulfil their duties. The Zaotar also summons priests, warriors, tillers, householders, rulers, youths, women, holy men, chiefs of the Mazdayasnians, the Bounteous Immortals, and the Saoshyants. At the close, the Zaotar and Ratu identify their ritual roles, and the Zaotar declares that he will recite the Staota Yêsnya with memorised intoning, chanting, and praise.

Visparad IV (Sp. V).

The unit concerns sacrifice to thoughts of the mind, good wisdom, good and blessed sanctity, good religious knowledge, and good health of soul and body. It states that confession is to be made for the Kine. Zarathustrian Mazdayasnians celebrate at the sacrificial time for the Myazda-offering and at the time for the Ratufrîti. These acts include prayer for blessings, sacrificial worship, homage, propitiation, and praise of the entire creation of the holy (and the clean).

Visparad V (Sp. VI).

The speaker comes to the Bountiful Immortals as a praiser priest, invoker, memoriser, reciter, and chanter for their sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise. The speaker also prepares for the Saoshyants, their prayer for blessings and sanctification, and the victorious smiting of foes, which is beneficial and holy for their souls. The speaker offers the Bountiful Immortals the flesh of his body and all the blessings of his life, and confesses belief in Ahura Mazda as a Mazdayasnian of the order of Zarathustra and in accordance with his Faith.

Visparad VI (Sp. VII).

Visparad VI (Sp. VII) calls the good Amesha Spenta by their names of beauty and worships the Bountiful Immortals by their beautiful names. It does so in accordance with the precept, with praise, and with the joyful reception of grace. It includes intelligently offered Zaothras and correctly spoken sacrificial words. The ritual Order and Righteousness the good are invoked through blessing.

Visparad VII (Sp. VIII).

The unit concerns worship of Sraosha, Ashi, Nairya-sangha, Peace, the Fravashis, the Kinvat Bridge, and the Garô Nmâna of Ahura, identified with Heaven and the Best World. It praises the better path to the Best World, Arstât as Justice and the Mazdayasnian Faith, Rashnu, Mithra, Parendi, Heroism, and Mazda-made Sleep. It venerates ancient institutions predating the sky, water, land, plants, and the Kine, along with the sea Vouru-kasha, Mazda-made stormy wind, and the shining heaven. It also worships Fire as Ahura Mazda's son, the ritual Baresman, and Apãm-napât.

Visparad VIII (Sp. IX).

The unit addresses Ahura Mazda with a proper word and asks that He be present. Ahura Mazda is invoked together with the good Yazads, identified as the Bountiful Immortals who rule and dispose of all aright. The Kingdom is assigned to Him who rules the best.

Visparad IX (Sp. X).

Visparad IX concerns homage and praise offered to Haomas and Zaothras, their future offerings, and the remedies and virtues accompanying them. It also honors Kisti, Mazda, Zarathustra, Zarathustrôtema, the good Mazdayasnian Faith, Prayer for blessings, and other sacred words and practices. The unit approaches the strength of the strong, Rectitude and Blessedness, Kisti, the Bountiful Immortals, Good Mind, Universal Weal and Immortality, the Kine and Kine's Soul, the Fire of the spoken name, and a sanctified farm-house. It presents sacrifice, homage, and praise as directed toward Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Mãthra Spenta, the Mazdayasnian Religion, the Yasnas, the lords of the ritual, and the entire creation of the holy.

Visparad X (Sp. XI).

The speaker desires to approach several entities with praise. These include the Arezahis, Savahis, Fradadhafshu, Vîdadhafshu, Vouru-baresti, Vouru-garesti, and the Karshvar which is Hvaniratha. The speaker also praises ritual objects, the Ahuna-vairya, ritual prayers beside Ahuna, and the standing offices of the Mazdayasnian Faith.

Visparad XI (Sp. XII).

The unit concerns offerings, celebrations, and ritual announcements made to Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, divine beings, Fravashis, and the entire creation of the holy and clean. Haomas, ritual implements, waters, prayers, recitations, and other sacred objects are presented for sacrifice, homage, propitiation, praise, victory, sanctification, and the well-being of souls. The celebrations are also intended to benefit and protect the house, its herds and retainers, and the saints connected with it. The Avesta is declared according to the directions of Ahura Mazda and Zarathustra, and the speakers identify themselves as holy Saoshyants.

Visparad XII (Sp. XIV).

The unit concerns offerings of Haomas to Ahura Mazda and Zarathustra Spitama, seeking abundance in cattle and men and the aid of Sraosha. It offers the Ahuna-vairya, the two mortars, correctly spoken words, Zarathustrian utterances, ceremonies, Baresmans, pressed Haomas, praises, Yasnas, and the doctrines of the Mazdayasnian Religion. These ritual deeds, precepts, and teachings are connected with Ahura Mazda, Vohu Manah, the Righteous Order, and Spenta Mainyu's world. The two mortars are asked to become full of wisest meaning for the Mazdayasnians and their communities.

Visparad XIII (Sp. XV).

Visparad XIII (Sp. XV) describes ritual worship and sacrifice. It names Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Zarathustra, the Mãthra, the blessings for the saints, and the hail addressed to the Bountiful Immortals. It also honors the three principal chapters, their Hâs, metrical lines, words, word-structure, recital, memorising, chanting, and steadfast offering, without addition or omission.

Visparad XIV (Sp. XVI).

Visparad XIV (Sp. XVI). concerns worship and sacrifice directed to Ahura Mazda, the Gâtha Ahunavaiti, and the Ahuna-vairya. It describes the Gâtha Ahunavaiti through its measures, word-structure, Zand, questions and counter-questions, words, metric feet, recitation, worship, wisdom, clearness, loving intention, sovereignty, ritual order, and acquired boon. It also identifies the Ahuna-vairya as the holy lord of the ritual order and describes offerings to the constituent parts of the Gâtha Ahunavaiti.

Visparad XV (Sp. XVIII).

Visparad XV instructs Zarathustrian Mazdayasnians to prepare themselves for lawful deeds in accordance with the sacred Order and to avoid unlawful and evil deeds contrary to the ritual. It directs them to perform good deeds that further husbandry and to render the needy rich. Sraosha is called to be present for the worship and service of Ahura Mazda and for devotion to the Yasna Haptanghâiti. The Yasna Haptanghâiti is to be memorised and recited victoriously, without addition or omission, for the pronunciation of victorious words for Ahura Mazda's Fire.

Visparad XVI (Sp. XIX).

The unit concerns worship of Fire, Ahura Mazda's son, along with Yazads and Rashnus having the seed of fire. It honors Sraosha, the holy man, the entire holy creation, the Blessedness and Fravashi of Zarathustra Spitâma, and the saints and their Fravashis. It also worships the Fravashis of holy men and women within and outside the Province. It sacrifices to fields, waters, lands, plants, and the parts of the Yasna Haptanghâiti.

Visparad XVII (Sp. XX).

Visparad XVII (Sp. XX) concerns striving after the good thoughts, words, and deeds inculcated in the Yasna Haptanghâiti. It states that a blessing is the Right (called) the Best. It says there is weal, including weal for the man who is toward Righteousness Best and right.

Visparad XVIII (Sp. XXI).

The unit concerns worship of Ahura Mazda, the Amesha Spenta, the holy man, and the prior world of the holy and the clean. It presents the state of weal and salvation for the holy man as an object of worship. It also describes the life-long state of blessedness for the holy as the evil man's calamity, along with eternal salvation and the salvation prayer. The text calls for sacrifice to every saint who exists, is now coming into existence, or shall exist in future.

Visparad XIX (Sp. XXII).

Visparad XIX (Sp. XXII) worships Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Âramaiti, and other holy beings and principles. It offers sacrifice to the bountiful saint, anticipative understanding, Âramaiti, holy creatures, shining sun, glorious achievements, glory, herds with Fire and its blessings, holy benefit, and wisdom. The unit connects Âramaiti with bountiful creatures and with the laws of the Righteous Order. It also describes the sun as highest of the high and worships it together with the Bountiful Immortals and the Mãthras with their good ceremonies.

Visparad XX (Sp. XXIII).

Visparad XX (Sp. XXIII) concerns sacrifice and worship offered to the Gâtha Vohu-khshathra, the Vohu-khshathra, and the Khshathra-vairya. It praises the iron-founding and correctly spoken sacrificial words that smite the foe with victory and hold the Daêvas subject. It describes rewards, health, healing, progress, growth, and victorious smiting acquired through the memorised recital of the good thoughts, good words, and good deeds. It also sacrifices to the later Yasna, the heroic Haptanghâiti, which becomes the holy ritual chief as it recurs.

Visparad XXI (Sp. XXIV).

Visparad XXI concerns earnest Yasna and homage offered to the good waters, fertile fruit-trees, Fravashis of the saints, Kine, Gaya (Maretan), Mãthra Spenta, Ahura Mazda, Zarathustra, the lofty lord, and the Bountiful Immortals. It also concerns sacrifice to listening, mercy, hearing of homage, and mercy shown in response to praise. The passage further offers sacrifice to the frârâiti vîdushê, the good praise without hypocrisy or malice, the later Yasna and its offering, and the chapters, metrical lines, words, and word-structure of the later Yasna.

Visparad XXII (Sp. XXV).

Visparad XXII (Sp. XXV) concerns a chant performed for the Bountiful Immortals and the holy Saoshyants. Through the chant and accompanying ceremonial actions, the speakers offer supplications for the Kine. The chant is recognized by the saint as good and fruitful of blessed gifts, but is unknown to the sinner. The speakers pray that the sinner will not outstrip them in thought, words, ceremonies, or offerings when approaching for harm.

Visparad XXIII (Sp. XXVI).

The unit concerns worship offered to Ahura Mazda, the Amesha Spenta, Asha Vahista, Heaven, and other best things. Asha Vahista is identified as Righteousness the Best, while Heaven is described as the best world of the saints, bright and all-glorious. The text also concerns the best approach leading to Heaven and the reward, health, healing, furtherance, increase, and victory associated with the Ahuna-vairya and Airyemâ-ishyô. These benefits are connected with the memorised recital of good thoughts, words, and deeds.
