Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, riches are portrayed as dangerous, destroying many, turning kings' hearts away, and causing wakefulness and anxiety that consume the flesh; love of gold leads fools to ruin and leaves its lover unjustified. In The Good News According to Mark, Jesus says it is difficult for those who have riches, especially those who trust in them, to enter God's Kingdom, though with God all things are possible. In 1 Timothy, the love of money is called a root of all kinds of evil, with the determination to be rich leading to temptation, snares, harmful lusts, ruin, straying from the faith, and sorrows. In The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi, riches are obtained through providence, follow seeking God's kingdom, and serve the needy after hope in Christ. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, prosperity is exceedingly great riches gained through heed, diligence, faithfulness, and security, which are used to provide for those in need. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, great riches caused pride and boastfulness, contributed to distinctions by rank, and gave some access to great learning while poverty left others ignorant.
