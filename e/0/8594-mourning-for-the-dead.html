mourning for the dead

topic · 1 work · 11 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: stay

Abstract written by a model

Mourning for the dead is prescribed in Fargard XII by the deceased person’s relationship to the mourner and by whether the dead are righteous or sinners. It begins with thirty days for righteous parents or children and sixty days for sinners, and reaches six months or a year for the master or mistress of the house. The periods then shorten with more distant kin: grandparents or grandchildren, uncles or aunts, cousins, a cousin’s child, and a cousin’s grandchild. In each stated paired case, sinners receive the longer period; the final summary supplies paired periods for cousins and their descendants.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Fargard XII.

Mourning lasts thirty days for the righteous and sixty days for sinners after the death of a parent or child; six months or a year after the death of the master or mistress of the house; and progressively shorter periods for more distant relatives. The stated periods are twenty-five or fifty days for grandparents and grandchildren, twenty or forty days for uncles or aunts, fifteen or thirty days for cousins, ten or twenty days for a cousin's child, and five or ten days for a cousin's grandchild.
