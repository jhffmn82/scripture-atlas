Abstract written by a model

In Leviticus: must_be kept [by Israel] The LORD commands Israel to do his ordinances and keep his statutes and walk in them, in contrast to the statutes of Egypt and Canaan. In Leviticus: requires observance of all statutes and ordinances The chapter sets out the commandments that express holiness: respecting parents, keeping Sabbaths, avoiding idols, honest dealing, justice without partiality, and loving one's neighbor. In Numbers, the LORD’s commandments given through Moses are to be observed throughout the generations, with offerings for unwitting failure and expulsion for deliberate violation. In Deuteronomy: The law is to be taught by the Levitical priests and followed without turning right or left. In Deuteronomy, commandments, statutes, and ordinances are the unalterable body of law Moses teaches Israel, whose observance brings righteousness, wisdom, blessing, and life. In The Book of Joshua, the book of the law of Moses contains blessing and curse, and Joshua must meditate on and follow it to prosper. In The Book of Ezra, the law of Moses is given by the LORD, held and taught by Ezra, and used to guide inquiry, judgment, and instruction. In The Book of Nehemiah, commandments, statutes, and ordinances given through Moses at Sinai give life to those who obey them and are read aloud to the assembly.

commandments, statutes, and ordinances in Deuteronomy

author unknown · 624 BC · the work’s page · read it where commandments, statutes, and ordinances first appears

Abstract written by a model

The commandments, statutes, and ordinances are the body of law that Moses teaches Israel on the LORD's behalf. They are unalterable: nothing may be added or removed. Keeping them counts as righteousness before the LORD and as Israel's wisdom and understanding in the sight of the peoples, who will see that no other great nation has such righteous law. Their purpose is Israel's good and preservation. The account unfolds in stages. In Chapter 4 Moses teaches them and forbids adding or subtracting. In Chapter 5 he calls Israel to hear them, and the LORD has Moses stay beside him to receive all of them for teaching. Chapter 6 says they are given so Israel may do them in the land, fear the LORD, and live long and well. They are to be taught diligently, bound on hand and forehead, and written on door posts and gates. Chapters 7, 8, and 11 make obedience the condition for the covenant's loving kindness and for blessings: life, multiplying, possessing the land, rain, fruitfulness, and victory. Forgetting the LORD or turning to other gods brings perishing and drought. Chapter 28 sets blessings for keeping them against curses for failing, with fearing the LORD's name as the aim. Chapter 30 declares the commandment not too hard or distant but very near, in the mouth and heart, centered on loving the LORD and walking in his ways. Chapter 31 closes with Moses writing the law in a book, giving it to the priests and elders, and ordering it read to all Israel every seven years at the feast of booths. The Levites place it beside the ark as a witness against Israel.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

The statutes and ordinances are taught by Moses as commanded by the LORD, and Israel is told not to add to or take from them. Keeping them is called Israel's wisdom and understanding in the sight of the peoples, who will see that no other great nation has such righteous law. Obedience is tied to living well and prolonging days in the land.

Chapter 5

Moses opens by calling Israel to hear the statutes and ordinances so they may learn and observe them. The LORD tells Moses to stay beside him so he can receive all the commandments, statutes, and ordinances that Moses is to teach the people for keeping in the land.

Chapter 6

The commandments, statutes, and ordinances are given so Israel may do them in the land, fear the LORD, and have long and good lives. They are to be taught diligently, bound on hand and forehead, and written on door posts and gates. Keeping them is said to be for Israel's good always and counts as righteousness.

Chapter 7

The Israelites are told to keep and do the commandments, statutes, and ordinances commanded that day. The LORD's keeping of the covenant and loving kindness is tied to Israel's listening to and obeying them. Keeping them is presented as the condition for the blessings.

Chapter 8

The commandments are what the Israelites are told to observe so that they may live, multiply, and possess the land. Keeping them means walking in the LORD's ways and fearing him, and the wilderness testing was to see whether they would keep them. Forgetting the LORD means not keeping his commandments, ordinances, and statutes, which leads to perishing.

Chapter 11

The commandments, statutes, and ordinances are to be kept always, so the Israelites may be strong, possess the land, and prolong their days. They are to be laid up in heart and soul, bound as a sign, taught to children, and written on doorposts and gates. the law in Paul’s Letter to the Galatians

author unknown · about AD 48 · the work’s page · read it where the law first appears

Abstract written by a model

The law is a recurring subject in these records, presented as unable to justify anyone: no flesh is justified by its works, but a man is justified through faith in Jesus Christ. In Chapter 2, Paul says he died to the law to live to God, and that righteousness through it would make Christ's death pointless. Chapter 4 says the Son was born under it to redeem others, and that it records Abraham's two sons, read as allegory. Chapter 5 warns that circumcision makes one a debtor to keep all of it and that seeking justification by it means falling from grace. The whole law is fulfilled in loving one's neighbor; those led by the Spirit are not under it, and no law opposes the fruit of the Spirit.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

The text states that no flesh will be justified by the works of the law, and that a man is justified through faith in Jesus Christ. Paul says he died to the law through the law so that he might live to God. He adds that if righteousness were through the law, Christ died for nothing, and that rebuilding what he destroyed would prove him a law-breaker.

Chapter 4

The law is what the Son was born under and what he came to redeem others from. Those who desire to be under it are asked whether they listen to it, since it records the account of Abraham's two sons that the author reads as an allegory.

Chapter 5

The law is something a man who receives circumcision is a debtor to keep entirely, and seeking justification by it means falling away from grace. The whole law is fulfilled in one word, loving one's neighbor as oneself, those led by the Spirit are not under it, and there is no law against the fruit of the Spirit.
