Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Isaiah, Tyre is laid waste and forgotten for seventy years before returning to trade, with its merchandise ultimately becoming holiness to the LORD. In The Book of Ezekiel, Tyre is condemned for gloating over Jerusalem's fall and is declared to be destroyed, impoverished, and never rebuilt. In The Book of Amos, Tyre is condemned for delivering a whole community to Edom and forgetting the brotherly covenant, and its palaces are vowed to be devoured by fire. In The Book of Zechariah, Tyre is condemned despite its wisdom, stronghold, sea power, and wealth, and is dispossessed and devoured by fire.

Tyre in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Tyre first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tyre is an ancient, joyous city of merchant princes, giver of crowns and market of nations, the target of a burden in Chapter 23. It is laid waste, with no house or entrance remaining, and forgotten for seventy years. Then, when the LORD visits her, she returns to her wages and trades with all kingdoms, like a forgotten prostitute seeking remembrance. Finally, her merchandise and earnings become holiness to the LORD.

Chapter 23

Tyre is the target of the burden: a joyous, ancient city of merchant princes, the giver of crowns, and the market of nations, laid waste so that no house or entrance remains. She will be forgotten seventy years, then be compared to a forgotten prostitute who takes up a harp and sings to be remembered. After that the LORD will visit her, she will return to her wages and trade with all the world's kingdoms, and her merchandise and earnings will be holiness to the LORD.

Tyre in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Tyre first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tyre is a seafaring merchant city set at the entry of the sea, in the heart of the seas, renowned as strong and claiming perfect beauty. Its inhabitants trade with the peoples of many islands and enrich the kings of the earth. Chapter 26 condemns it for gloating over Jerusalem's fall; the Lord GOD declares his opposition, and its walls, towers and wealth will fall. Chapter 27 portrays its rich trade and brokenness by the seas. Chapter 28 addresses its prince and king, whose wisdom-gained wealth ends in destruction. Tyre becomes a bare rock for nets, descends to the pit, is never rebuilt, and is no more.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 26

Tyre is condemned for saying 'Aha!' over Jerusalem's breaking and expecting to be replenished by her ruin. The Lord GOD declares he is against it: its walls and towers will fall, its wealth be plundered, its songs cease, and it will become a bare rock for spreading nets, brought down to the pit and never found again.

Chapter 27

Tyre is addressed as the merchant of the peoples to many islands who dwells at the entry of the sea and claims to be perfect in beauty. It is portrayed as a richly built, well-staffed, wealthy trading city that enriched the kings of the earth. It is broken by the seas, brought to silence, and will be no more.

Chapter 28

Tyre is the city whose prince and king are addressed, set in the middle of the seas. Its rulers' wealth was gained through wisdom and commerce, and the judgment spoken against them is that they will be brought down and destroyed.
