The Book of Joshua

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

The Book of Joshua, in 24 chapters, follows Israel's entry into, conquest of, and settlement in the promised land under Joshua, son of Nun, who succeeds Moses. The LORD, Joshua, Rahab, Achan, Caleb, Eleazar the priest, and the tribes matter most. Chapters 1-5 cover the preparation and crossing. The LORD commands Joshua to be strong and keep the law, and the eastern tribes pledge to obey him. Two spies are sheltered in Jericho by Rahab, who is promised protection for her household. The priests carry the ark into the Jordan, the waters stop, and Israel crosses on dry ground. Twelve stones are set up as a memorial. At Gilgal the people are circumcised and keep Passover, the manna ceases, and Joshua meets the commander of the LORD's army. Chapters 6-12 recount the conquest. Jericho's wall falls after a seven-day march, and Rahab's household is spared. Achan takes devoted things, Israel is defeated at Ai, and Achan is stoned. Ai is then taken by ambush, and Joshua reads the law at Ebal and Gerizim. The Gibeonites trick Joshua into a covenant and become servants. A southern coalition is routed, with hailstones and the sun standing still, and a northern coalition under Jabin of Hazor is also defeated. Chapter 12 lists thirty-one defeated kings west of the Jordan alongside Sihon and Og in the east. Chapters 13-21 describe the division of the land. Joshua is old, and much land remains. The eastern tribes already hold their inheritance, and Levi receives cities rather than territory. Caleb, aged eighty-five, receives Hebron, and Othniel wins Debir and Achsah. Lots are cast for Judah, Ephraim, Manasseh, Benjamin, and the remaining tribes, mostly at Shiloh. Zelophehad's daughters receive an inheritance. Some Canaanites are not driven out. Six cities of refuge are set apart, and forty-eight Levite cities are assigned. The text declares that none of the LORD's promises failed. Chapters 22-24 conclude the book. The eastern tribes return home and build an altar by the Jordan. War is averted when Phinehas and the princes accept it as a witness. Joshua gives a farewell charge warning against intermarriage and foreign gods. At Shechem he recounts Israel's history, the people pledge to serve the LORD, and he makes a covenant and sets up a stone. Joshua and Eleazar die and are buried, and Joseph's bones are buried at Shechem.

The Book of Joshua / Chapter 1

After the death of Moses, the LORD commands Joshua, son of Nun, to lead all the people across the Jordan into the land promised to the children of Israel, whose borders stretch from the wilderness and Lebanon to the Euphrates and the great sea. The LORD promises that no one will stand against Joshua and that he will be with him as he was with Moses, and urges him repeatedly to be strong and courageous and to keep and meditate on the law of Moses. Joshua then orders the officers to tell the people to prepare food, since they will cross the Jordan within three days. He reminds the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh of Moses' command that their fighting men must cross armed ahead of their brothers until all have received rest. They pledge to obey Joshua as they obeyed Moses, and declare that anyone who rebels against his commands will be put to death.

Chapter 2

Joshua the son of Nun secretly sends two spies from Shittim to view the land, including Jericho, and they lodge at the house of a prostitute named Rahab. When the king of Jericho demands that she hand them over, Rahab hides them under flax stalks on her roof and sends the pursuers off toward the fords of the Jordan. She tells the spies that Jericho's people are terrified because of the LORD's deeds at the Red Sea and against Sihon and Og, and she asks them to swear to spare her father's house. The spies pledge their lives for hers on condition of her silence, and tell her to tie a scarlet thread in her window and gather her family in the house. She lowers them by a cord from her window in the wall and tells them to hide in the mountain for three days; they then return to Joshua and report that the LORD has delivered the land into their hands.

Chapter 3

Joshua leads the children of Israel from Shittim to the Jordan, where they camp for three days before crossing. The officers instruct the people to follow the ark of the covenant carried by the Levitical priests, keeping about two thousand cubits behind it. Joshua tells the people to sanctify themselves, and the LORD promises to magnify Joshua so Israel will know he is with him as he was with Moses. Joshua announces that the ark will cause the Jordan's waters to be cut off and that the living God will drive out the Canaanite, Hittite, Hivite, Perizzite, Girgashite, Amorite, and Jebusite. When the priests' feet touch the water, the river stops and piles up in one heap at Adam, and all Israel crosses on dry ground near Jericho while the priests stand firm in the middle of the Jordan.

Chapter 4

After all the nation had crossed the Jordan, the LORD told Joshua to have twelve men, one from each tribe, take twelve stones from the middle of the river where the priests' feet stood firm and carry them to the camp as a memorial. Joshua carried this out and also set up twelve stones in the middle of the Jordan, while the priests bearing the ark stood there until everything was finished and the people hurried across. The Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh crossed armed before Israel, about forty thousand men, and the LORD magnified Joshua so that the people feared him as they had feared Moses. At the LORD's command the priests came up out of the Jordan, the waters returned to their place and overflowed their banks, and the people camped at Gilgal on the tenth day of the first month, where Joshua set up the stones and explained that they testify to the LORD's mighty hand and should lead all to fear him.

Chapter 5

The Amorite and Canaanite kings west of the Jordan lose heart upon hearing that the LORD dried up the Jordan for the children of Israel. The LORD tells Joshua to make flint knives and circumcise the sons of Israel again, because those born in the wilderness had not been circumcised while the generation that left Egypt had died there for not listening to the LORD. After the people heal, the LORD declares that he has rolled away the reproach of Egypt, and the place is named Gilgal. The Israelites keep the Passover in the plains of Jericho, eat the produce of the land, and the manna ceases. Near Jericho, Joshua meets a man with a drawn sword who identifies himself as commander of the LORD's army and tells Joshua to take off his sandals because the place is holy.

Chapter 6

Jericho is tightly shut against the children of Israel when the LORD tells Joshua he has given the city into his hand and prescribes a march: armed men and seven priests with ram's horn trumpets circle it once daily for six days, then seven times on the seventh. Joshua commands silence until the day to shout, and the people obey. On the seventh day, at Joshua's command and the priests' trumpet blast, the people shout, the wall falls flat, and Israel takes the city. Everything living in it is destroyed with the sword and the city is burned, while the silver, gold, bronze and iron go into the LORD's treasury. Rahab the prostitute and her household are spared because she hid the messengers, and Joshua pronounces a curse on anyone who rebuilds Jericho, so that the LORD is with Joshua and his fame spreads.

Chapter 7

Achan, of the tribe of Judah, took some of the devoted things from Jericho, so the LORD's anger burned against Israel. Israel's attack on Ai with about three thousand men failed, with thirty-six killed, and Joshua and the elders mourned before the LORD's ark. The LORD told Joshua that Israel had become devoted for destruction and would not stand before its enemies until the devoted things were removed, and he directed a selection by tribe, family, household, and man that singled out Achan. Achan confessed to taking a Babylonian robe, two hundred shekels of silver, and a fifty-shekel wedge of gold hidden in his tent; Joshua and all Israel then took him, his family, his possessions, and the stolen goods to the valley of Achor, stoned and burned them, and raised a heap of stones, after which the LORD turned from his anger.

Chapter 8

The LORD tells Joshua not to fear and instructs him to take all the warriors to Ai, giving it into his hand and permitting Israel to keep its goods and livestock. Joshua sends thirty thousand men to lie in ambush behind the city, then draws the men of Ai out by feigning defeat. When Joshua stretches out his javelin, the ambush takes and burns the city, and Israel turns back and kills the men of Ai caught in the middle, killing twelve thousand in all, capturing the king alive, and later hanging him on a tree. Joshua burns Ai into a permanent heap and Israel takes only the livestock and goods. Joshua then builds an altar of uncut stones on Mount Ebal, writes a copy of Moses' law on stones, and reads all the words of the law, blessing and curse, to all Israel at Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal.

Chapter 9

Kings west of the Jordan hear of Israel's victories and gather to fight Joshua and Israel. The inhabitants of Gibeon instead resort to a ruse, disguising themselves as ambassadors from a far country with old sacks, patched sandals, and moldy bread, and ask Joshua for a covenant. Joshua and the princes of the congregation make peace and swear an oath to them without asking counsel from the LORD. Three days later Israel learns the Gibeonites are neighbors living among them, but the princes refuse to let the congregation strike them because of the oath. Joshua curses the Gibeonites and makes them wood cutters and drawers of water for the congregation and the LORD's altar.

Chapter 10

Adoni-Zedek, king of Jerusalem, alarmed that Gibeon had made peace with Israel, rallies four other Amorite kings (of Hebron, Jarmuth, Lachish, and Eglon) to attack Gibeon. The Gibeonites appeal to Joshua, who marches from Gilgal overnight, and the LORD routs the coalition at Gibeon, killing many with great hailstones, while the sun and moon stand still at Joshua's command. The five kings hide in a cave at Makkedah, are later brought out, humiliated by Israel's commanders, killed, hanged, and thrown back into the cave. Joshua then takes Makkedah, Libnah, Lachish, Eglon, Hebron, and Debir, defeating Horam king of Gezer along the way, and the chapter summarizes that he struck the whole land from Kadesh Barnea to Gaza and Goshen to Gibeon before returning to Gilgal.

Chapter 11

Jabin king of Hazor summons a coalition of northern kings, who gather with a vast army, horses and chariots at the waters of Merom to fight Israel. The LORD tells Joshua not to fear and to hamstring their horses and burn their chariots; Joshua attacks suddenly, routs them as far as great Sidon, and leaves none remaining. Joshua then takes Hazor, kills its king and all inhabitants, and burns it, and goes on to capture and utterly destroy the other cities as Moses commanded, though only Hazor is burned. Joshua makes war a long time, takes the whole land, and removes the Anakim except in Gaza, Gath and Ashdod, and no city but Gibeon of the Hivites makes peace because the LORD hardened their hearts. Joshua gives the land to Israel as an inheritance by tribal divisions, and the land has rest from war.

Chapter 12

Chapter 12 lists the kings defeated by the children of Israel. East of the Jordan, from the valley of the Arnon to Mount Hermon, Moses and the children of Israel struck Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon, and Og king of Bashan, of the remnant of the Rephaim. Moses gave their land to the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh. West of the Jordan, from Baal Gad to Mount Halak, Joshua and the children of Israel struck the kings of the land, and Joshua gave that land to the tribes of Israel according to their divisions. The chapter names the cities of the kings, from Jericho to Tirzah, and totals them at thirty-one.

Chapter 13

Joshua is old, and the LORD tells him that much land remains to be possessed, listing the regions of the Philistines, Geshurites, Canaanites, Sidonians, Gebalites and Lebanon. The LORD promises to drive out the inhabitants and commands Joshua to allocate the land to the nine tribes and the half-tribe of Manasseh. The chapter then recounts the inheritance Moses already gave beyond the Jordan eastward to the Reubenites, the Gadites and the other half-tribe of Manasseh, with detailed border and city lists for each, including the kingdoms of Sihon and Og. It notes that Israel did not drive out the Geshurites and Maacathites, who still live within Israel, and that the tribe of Levi received no land inheritance because the LORD and his offerings are its inheritance.

Chapter 14

Chapter 14 describes how the children of Israel, through Eleazar the priest, Joshua the son of Nun, and the heads of the fathers' houses, distributed the land of Canaan by lot to nine and a half tribes, as the LORD commanded by Moses. The Levites received no land inheritance, only cities with pasture lands, while Moses had already given the two and a half tribes their inheritance beyond the Jordan. At Gilgal, Caleb the son of Jephunneh the Kenizzite reminds Joshua of Moses' oath, made after the Kadesh Barnea spying, that the land he walked would be his. Now eighty-five and still strong, Caleb asks for the hill country with its Anakim and fortified cities, and Joshua blesses him and gives him Hebron. Hebron, formerly Kiriath Arba, becomes Caleb's inheritance, and the land has rest from war.

Chapter 15

Chapter 15 describes the lot given to the tribe of the children of Judah, tracing its south, east, north and west borders from the Salt Sea and the brook of Egypt to the great sea. It then recounts how Caleb the son of Jephunneh received Kiriath Arba (Hebron), drove out the three sons of Anak, and offered his daughter Achsah as wife to whoever took Kiriath Sepher (Debir). Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb's brother, took it and married Achsah, who asked her father for springs of water in addition to the southern land and received the upper and lower springs. The chapter lists Judah's cities by region (the South, lowland, hill country and wilderness) with the counts given for each group. It ends by noting that the children of Judah could not drive out the Jebusites of Jerusalem, who still live with them.

Chapter 16

Chapter 16 describes the lot that came out for the children of Joseph, whose territory ran from the Jordan at Jericho up through the hill country to Bethel, then via Luz, the Archites' border at Ataroth, the Japhletites' border, lower Beth Horon and Gezer, ending at the sea. Manasseh and Ephraim, the sons of Joseph, took their inheritance. The chapter then gives the border of the children of Ephraim by families, running from Ataroth Addar to upper Beth Horon, through Michmethath, Taanath Shiloh, Janoah, Ataroth, Naarah and Jericho to the Jordan, and from Tappuah westward along the brook of Kanah to the sea. Ephraim also received cities with their villages set apart within Manasseh's inheritance. The Ephraimites did not drive out the Canaanites living in Gezer, who remained in Ephraim's territory as servants doing forced labor.

Chapter 17

Chapter 17 describes the lot of the tribe of Manasseh, Joseph's firstborn. Machir received Gilead and Bashan as a man of war, and the other Manasseh families received their shares. Zelophehad's five daughters, who had no brothers, came to Eleazar, Joshua and the princes and received an inheritance among their father's brothers, as the LORD had commanded Moses. The chapter traces Manasseh's border with Ephraim and lists cities in Issachar and Asher that Manasseh could not drive the Canaanites out of, though Israel later put them to forced labor. The children of Joseph complained to Joshua that they had only one lot, and Joshua told them to clear the forest hill country and drive out the Canaanites despite their iron chariots.

Chapter 18

The whole congregation of Israel assembled at Shiloh and set up the Tent of Meeting, as the land lay subdued before them. Joshua rebuked the seven tribes that had not yet received inheritances and directed them to appoint three men from each tribe to survey the land in seven portions and bring a written description back to him. The surveyors recorded the land by cities in a book, and Joshua cast lots for the tribes before the LORD at Shiloh. The first lot to come up was for the tribe of Benjamin, whose borders on the north, west, south, and east are described in detail, lying between Judah and the children of Joseph. The chapter closes by listing Benjamin's twenty-six cities in two groups, twelve and fourteen, with their villages.

Chapter 19

Chapter 19 records the last allotments of land by lot among the remaining tribes. Simeon receives the second lot, taking cities within Judah's territory because Judah's portion was too large. Zebulun, Issachar, Asher, Naphtali, and Dan receive the third through seventh lots, each with borders and cities listed. The Danites expand by capturing Leshem and renaming it Dan. The children of Israel then give Joshua the city Timnathserah in Ephraim's hill country, and Eleazar the priest, Joshua, and the heads of the fathers' houses finish dividing the land by lot at Shiloh before the LORD.

Chapter 20

The LORD tells Joshua to instruct the children of Israel to assign the cities of refuge, as previously spoken of through Moses, so that someone who kills a person accidentally can flee from the avenger of blood. The text sets out the procedure: the man slayer declares his case to the elders at the city gate, is given a place to live, and is not handed over to the avenger of blood. He stays there until he stands before the congregation for judgment and until the death of the high priest, after which he may return to his own city and house. Three cities west of the Jordan (Kedesh, Shechem, Kiriath Arba/Hebron) and three beyond the Jordan (Bezer, Ramoth, Golan) are set apart. These cities serve all the children of Israel and the alien living among them.

Chapter 21

The heads of the Levite families approach Eleazar the priest, Joshua son of Nun, and the tribal heads at Shiloh, reminding them that the LORD commanded through Moses that cities with pasture lands be given to the Levites. The Israelites give cities by lot out of their inheritance: thirteen to the priestly children of Aaron among the Kohathites, ten to the rest of the Kohathites, thirteen to the Gershonites, and twelve to the Merarites. The chapter lists each city by name and tribe, marking the cities of refuge (Hebron, Shechem, Golan, Kedesh, Ramoth in Gilead), and notes that Caleb son of Jephunneh keeps the fields and villages of Hebron. The total comes to forty-eight cities with their pasture lands. The chapter closes by declaring that the LORD gave Israel all the land he swore to their fathers, granted rest on every side, and that nothing he promised failed.

Chapter 22

Joshua commends the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh for keeping all that Moses and he commanded, blesses them, and sends them home to their land beyond the Jordan. After they build a great altar by the Jordan, the children of Israel gather at Shiloh to go to war against them, but first send Phinehas son of Eleazar with ten princes to accuse them of rebellion. The eastern tribes answer that the altar is not for sacrifice but a witness, so that future generations will not say they have no portion in the LORD. Phinehas and the princes are pleased, the war is called off, and the altar is named "A Witness Between Us that the LORD is God."

Chapter 23

After the LORD gave Israel rest from their enemies, the aged Joshua gathered Israel's elders, heads, judges, and officers to give a farewell charge. He reminded them that the LORD fought for them and that the remaining nations were allotted to them as an inheritance, to be driven out by the LORD. He urged them to keep all that is written in the book of the law of Moses, to hold fast to the LORD, to avoid the gods of the remaining nations, and to love the LORD. He warned that intermarriage with these nations would make them a snare and a trap and cause the LORD to stop driving them out. Noting that he was going the way of all the earth, he said that every good promise had been fulfilled and that disobeying the covenant and serving other gods would bring destruction from the good land.

Chapter 24

Joshua gathers all the tribes of Israel, with their elders, heads, judges, and officers, at Shechem, where they present themselves before God. Speaking for the LORD, he recounts the history from Terah and Abraham through the Exodus, the wilderness, the conquest of the Amorites, and the gift of the land. He calls the people to put away foreign gods and serve the LORD, declaring that he and his house will do so. The people pledge to serve the LORD, Joshua warns that the LORD is holy and jealous, and he makes a covenant, writes the words in the book of the law, and sets up a great stone as witness. The chapter closes with the deaths and burials of Joshua at Timnathserah and Eleazar, and the burial of Joseph's bones at Shechem.
