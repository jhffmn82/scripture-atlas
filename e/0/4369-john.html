John in Revelation

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where John first appears

Abstract written by a model

John is a servant of Jesus Christ and the narrator-witness of the Revelation, writing to seven assemblies as their brother and partner in oppression, Kingdom, and perseverance in Christ Jesus. He testifies to God's word and the testimony of Jesus Christ about all he saw, and he was on the isle of Patmos because of God's Word. What matters most is his role as the one who heard and saw the visions and is commissioned to record them. The records unfold in sequence. In chapter 1, in the Spirit on the Lord's day, he hears a voice and sees one like a son of man, falls as dead, is comforted, and is told to write what he has seen, what is, and what will happen hereafter. In chapter 4 he sees a door opened in heaven, is called up, and is immediately in the Spirit, describing the throne, elders, and living creatures. In chapter 5 he weeps because no one is worthy to open the sealed book, until an elder consoles him and the Lamb appears amid universal worship. In chapter 7 he sees the sealed and the great multitude, answering an elder, 'My lord, you know,' and receiving the explanation. In chapter 10 he is forbidden to write what the seven thunders said, eats the little book, finding it sweet and bitter, and is told he must prophesy again over many peoples, nations, languages, and kings. In chapter 17 he is carried in the Spirit into a wilderness, marvels at the woman on the beast, and an angel interprets. In chapter 21 he sees the new heaven and earth and the holy city, is told to write, and is carried to a high mountain to see the city measured. In chapter 22 he identifies himself as the one who heard and saw these things, is stopped from worshiping before the angel, is told not to seal the prophecy, and ends with the cry, 'Yes, come, Lord Jesus!'

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Revelation / Chapter 1

John is the servant to whom the Revelation was made known by Christ's angel, and he testified to God's word and the testimony of Jesus Christ about all that he saw. He writes to the seven assemblies as their brother and partner in the oppression, Kingdom, and perseverance in Christ, and was on the isle of Patmos because of God's Word. In the Spirit on the Lord's day he hears a voice and sees the vision of the one like a son of man, falls as dead, is comforted, and is told to write what he has seen, what is, and what will happen hereafter.

Chapter 4

The narrator looks and sees a door opened in heaven, and hears a voice like a trumpet telling him to come up to be shown what must happen after this. Immediately he is in the Spirit, and he describes the throne, the elders, and the living creatures he sees.

Chapter 5

The narrator sees the sealed book, the mighty angel's proclamation, the Lamb, and the worship around the throne, and he hears the voices of the angels and of every created thing. He weeps much because no one is found worthy to open the book, until one of the elders tells him not to weep.

Chapter 7

The narrator sees the four angels, the angel from the sunrise, the great multitude, and the worship around the throne, and he hears the number of the sealed. When the elder asks who the robed multitude are, he answers "My lord, you know," and receives the explanation from the elder.

Chapter 10

The narrator sees the mighty angel and is about to write what the seven thunders said, but a voice from the sky forbids it. He is told to take the open book from the angel, asks the angel for it, and eats it, finding it sweet in his mouth and bitter in his stomach. He is then told that he must prophesy again over many peoples, nations, languages, and kings.

Chapter 17

The narrator is carried away in the Spirit into a wilderness and sees the woman on the beast. He wonders with great amazement at the sight of her drunk with the blood of the saints, and the angel then interprets the vision for him.

Chapter 21

The narrator, speaking in the first person, sees the new heaven and earth and the holy city and hears the loud voice from heaven. He is told to write because the words are faithful and true. He is carried away in the Spirit to a great and high mountain, where he is shown the city and watches its measuring, and he notes that he saw no temple in it.

Chapter 22

John identifies himself as the one who heard and saw these things. He fell down to worship before the feet of the angel and was told not to do so. He is instructed not to seal up the words of the prophecy, and he ends with the cry "Yes, come, Lord Jesus!"
