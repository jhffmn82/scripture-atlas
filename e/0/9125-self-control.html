Abstract written by a model

Self-control is a religious discipline associated with final liberation. Retained through an illustrious path, it prevents sinful harm toward living bodies through comprehension and renunciation. It unfolds as control that frees a person from attachment despite hardship, then as abstinence through which a sage destroys the effects of works, endures hardships, and brings about scarcity of wants or karman.

Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I / First Book. / First Lecture, Called Knowledge of the Weapon. / First Lesson. .. Seventh Lesson.

Self-control is the conduct that causes no danger and is retained through the illustrious road to final liberation. Those who control themselves do not act sinfully toward living bodies.

Second Lecture, Called Conquest of the World. / First Lesson. .. Sixth Lesson.

Control is presented as a path that frees a person from attachment and leads toward liberation. The hero is not vexed by the hardships caused by control.

Sixth Lecture, Called the Cleaning. / First Lesson. .. Fifth Lesson.

Abstinence enables a sage to destroy the effects of works and to endure cold, heat, grass, gnats, mosquitoes, and other hardships. The unclothed one who excels in abstinence effects scarcity of wants or karman.
