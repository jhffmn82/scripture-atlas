karman

Abstract written by a model

Karman is owned by people through acknowledged acts and arises from the condition of living beings, sinful acts, desires, attachment to pleasures, and violence, especially killing. It causes birth, misery, pain, movement into various families, and pleasant or unpleasant consequences. The records urge understanding its roots and currents, fully knowing and renouncing injurious acts, observing commandments, cutting fetters and worldly connection, and restraining impulses. The wise ascetic subdues passions, endures hardships, remains unattached, practises prescribed methods and contemplation, and through penance, restraint, ascetic exertion, and destruction of sinfulness gets rid of karman.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Third Lecture, Called Hot and Cold. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson.

Karman arises from the condition of living beings and is accumulated through sinful acts and desires. Understanding its root, especially killing, and destroying sinfulness frees a person from karman.

Fourth Lecture, Called Righteousness. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson.

Those who understand karman, observe the commandment, and are unattached subdue and chastise themselves. The desire for the current of karman belongs to a fool who has not cut off the fetters or conquered connection with the world.

Sixth Lecture, Called the Cleaning. / First Lesson. .. Fifth Lesson.

Acts bring beings into various families and produce pleasant and unpleasant consequences. Injurious acts must be perfectly and thoroughly known and renounced.

Seventh Lecture, Called Liberation. / First Lesson. .. Eighth Lesson.

The wise and restrained ascetic wishes for the destruction of karman and continues contemplation. By subduing passions, enduring hardships, becoming unattached, and practising the prescribed methods, he gets rid of karman.

Eighth Lecture, (Called) the Pillow of Righteousness. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson.

Mahâvîra understood that beings become sinners through their actions and that one under the conditions of existence suffers pain. By knowing karman and its currents, he avoided sinful activity and restrained his impulses.
