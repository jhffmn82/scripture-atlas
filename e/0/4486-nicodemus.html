Nicodemus

person · 1 work · 7 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: a man of the Pharisees, a ruler of the Jews, the teacher of Israel

Abstract written by a model

Nicodemus is a Pharisee and ruler of the Jews, addressed by Jesus as a teacher of Israel. In Chapter 3 he visits Jesus by night, calls him a teacher from God because of his signs, misunderstands being born anew, asks 'How can these things be?', and is rebuked for not understanding. In Chapter 7 he asks his fellow Pharisees whether the law judges a man without first hearing him, and they mock him as a Galilean. In Chapter 19 he brings about a hundred pounds of myrrh and aloes and helps bind Jesus' body in linen according to Jewish burial custom.

Nicodemus is in one work on this site, The Gospel According to John. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

Nicodemus is introduced as a Pharisee and a ruler of the Jews who comes to Jesus by night, calling him a teacher from God because of his signs. He misunderstands the idea of being born anew, asking whether a man can enter his mother's womb a second time, and asks 'How can these things be?' Jesus rebukes him as the teacher of Israel who doesn't understand these things.

Chapter 7

Nicodemus, identified as the one who came to Jesus by night and as one of the Pharisees' group, asks whether the law judges a man without first hearing from him and knowing what he does. The others answer by asking if he is also from Galilee and tell him to search and see that no prophet has arisen from there.

Chapter 19

Nicodemus, who first came to Jesus by night, brings about a hundred Roman pounds of myrrh and aloes. He helps take Jesus' body and bind it in linen cloths with the spices according to Jewish burial custom.
