sins in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where sins first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sin is accumulated by ignorant people through overbearing, desirous, cruel, deceitful, and undisciplined conduct. A monk abstains from it by protecting the soul, subduing the senses, and living without ties. Avoiding indulgence, harm, greed, deceit, improper conduct, and dependence makes one pious and victorious in this world and the next.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Fifth Lecture.

Sin is accumulated through overbearing acts and words, desire for wealth and women, and cruel or deceitful conduct. A sinful ascetic does not escape hell.

Fifteenth Lecture.

The monk abstains from sins and always protects his soul from them. He is described as sinless when he subdues his senses and lives without ties.

Seventeenth Lecture.

The sins described include indulgence, neglect of learning and discipline, disrespect, harming living beings, careless inspection and movement, deceit, greed, quarrelling, improper eating, abandoning one's teacher, following heretical ones, fortune-telling, and dependence on householders. One who always avoids these sins is pious among his brethren and conquers this world and the next.
