flattened grains

Also called: agreeable food, alms, food, lump of food

Abstract written by a model

The Eleventh Lesson sets seven rules for mendicants accepting food. Food for a sick mendicant must not be concealed or misrepresented for eating. Acceptance depends on wet or dry hands and vessels, cleaning, moisture, food from the ground, and portions unwanted by others; food ground or winnowed on living material is impure and unacceptable.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Eleventh Lesson.

Food received for a sick mendicant must not be concealed or misrepresented for the purpose of being eaten by the recipient. The unit gives seven rules governing when mendicants may accept food, including rules about wet or dry hands and vessels, cleaning, moisture, food taken from the ground, and portions unwanted by others.
