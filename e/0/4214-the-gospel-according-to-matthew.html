The Gospel According to Matthew

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This is the Gospel According to Matthew, chapters 1 to 28, a narrative of the life, teaching, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. It opens with his genealogy from Abraham and David through Joseph, and his birth to Mary by the Holy Spirit. Wise men seek him, Herod massacres the children of Bethlehem, and the family flees to Egypt before settling in Nazareth. John the Baptizer preaches repentance and baptizes Jesus, and God's voice names him beloved Son. After forty days of fasting and three temptations answered with Scripture, Jesus begins preaching in Galilee and calls Peter, Andrew, James, and John. Chapters 5 to 7 give his long teaching on the mountain: the Beatitudes, fulfilling the law, love of enemies, secret piety, the model prayer, freedom from anxiety, and obedience, ending with the houses on rock and sand. Chapters 8 to 10 show healings, calming the storm, calling Matthew, and the sending of the twelve apostles with warnings of persecution. Chapters 11 to 14 present John's question from prison, growing opposition from the Pharisees, parables of the Kingdom, John's beheading under Herod, the feeding of five thousand, and Jesus walking on the sea. Chapters 15 to 17 include disputes over tradition, the Canaanite woman, the feeding of four thousand, Peter's confession that Jesus is the Christ, the first predictions of suffering, and the transfiguration. Chapters 18 to 20 teach humility, forgiveness, marriage, riches, and serving, through parables such as the unforgiving servant and the vineyard laborers, while Jesus again predicts his death. In chapters 21 to 23 he enters Jerusalem to cries of 'Hosanna', cleanses the temple, is challenged by priests, Pharisees, Sadducees, and Herodians, names the greatest commandment, and pronounces woes on the scribes and Pharisees. Chapters 24 and 25 are his teaching on the end of the age: signs, watchfulness, and the parables of the ten virgins, the talents, and the sheep and goats. Chapters 26 and 27 narrate the Passover meal, Judas's betrayal, Gethsemane, arrest, trial before Caiaphas, Peter's denial, Pilate's judgment, the choice of Barabbas, crucifixion at Golgotha, his death with signs of darkness and an earthquake, and burial in Joseph of Arimathaea's tomb, which is guarded and sealed. Chapter 28 closes with the empty tomb, the angel's announcement to Mary Magdalene and the other Mary, the priests' bribe to the guards, and Jesus's commission to the eleven on a Galilean mountain to make disciples of all nations, with the promise to be with them always.

The Gospel According to Matthew / Chapter 1

Matthew 1 opens with the genealogy of Jesus Christ, son of David and son of Abraham, tracing descent from Abraham through David, the exile to Babylon, and Jacob to Joseph, husband of Mary, and groups it into three sets of fourteen generations. The chapter then recounts the birth of Jesus Christ: Mary, engaged to Joseph, is found pregnant by the Holy Spirit. Joseph, a righteous man, intends to put her away secretly, but an angel of the Lord appears to him in a dream and tells him to take Mary as his wife and name her son Jesus, who will save his people from their sins. The text presents this as fulfilling the prophecy of a virgin bearing a son called Immanuel, 'God with us'. Joseph obeys, takes Mary as his wife, and names the firstborn son Jesus after his birth.

Chapter 2

Wise men from the east come to Jerusalem seeking the newborn King of the Jews, troubling King Herod, who learns from the chief priests and scribes that the Christ is to be born in Bethlehem. Herod secretly sends the wise men to find the child, but after worshiping Jesus and offering gifts, they are warned in a dream and return home another way. Joseph is warned by an angel to flee to Egypt with the child and Mary, while Herod, angered at being mocked, kills all the male children two years old and under in Bethlehem and its surroundings. After Herod's death, an angel tells Joseph to return, and fearing Archelaus in Judea, he settles the family in Nazareth in Galilee, fulfilling what was spoken through the prophets.

Chapter 3

John the Baptizer preaches in the wilderness of Judea, calling people to repent because the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand, and is identified as the voice Isaiah the prophet spoke of. People from Jerusalem, Judea, and the Jordan region go out to be baptized by him in the Jordan, confessing their sins. When Pharisees and Sadducees come, John rebukes them, warns of coming wrath, and says one mightier than he will baptize in the Holy Spirit and judge like a farmer clearing a threshing floor. Jesus comes from Galilee to be baptized; John objects, but Jesus says it fulfills all righteousness and John consents. After the baptism the heavens open, the Spirit of God descends on Jesus like a dove, and a voice from the heavens calls him beloved Son.

Chapter 4

Jesus is led by the Spirit into the wilderness, where after fasting forty days and nights he is tempted three times by the devil and answers each time with Scripture. After the devil leaves and angels serve him, Jesus hears that John was delivered up and withdraws to Galilee, settling in Capernaum in fulfillment of Isaiah's prophecy of light for those in darkness. He begins preaching, 'Repent! For the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand.' By the sea of Galilee he calls the brothers Simon Peter and Andrew, and then James and John, the sons of Zebedee, who immediately leave their nets, boat, and father to follow him. Jesus then goes throughout Galilee teaching, preaching, and healing, and great multitudes from many regions follow him.

Chapter 5

In Chapter 5, Jesus sees the multitudes, goes up a mountain, and teaches his disciples who come to him. He opens with the Beatitudes, blessing the poor in spirit, mourners, the gentle, the merciful, the pure in heart, peacemakers, and the persecuted, and tells his hearers they are the salt of the earth and the light of the world. He declares that he came to fulfill the law and the prophets, not destroy them, and that righteousness must exceed that of the scribes and Pharisees. He then contrasts what was said to the ancient ones with his own teaching on murder and anger, adultery and lust, divorce, oaths, and retaliation. He ends by commanding love of enemies, grounded in the example of the Father in heaven, and calls his hearers to be perfect as the Father is perfect.

Chapter 6

In Chapter 6 the speaker instructs his listeners to practice charitable giving, prayer, and fasting in secret rather than to be seen by people, contrasting them with the hypocrites who seek glory from men and have already received their reward. He gives a model prayer beginning 'Our Father in heaven' and teaches that the Father's forgiveness depends on forgiving others. He then tells them to lay up treasure in heaven rather than on earth, warns that one cannot serve both God and Mammon, and describes the eye as the lamp of the body. Finally he urges them not to be anxious about food, drink, or clothing, pointing to the birds and lilies as evidence of the heavenly Father's care, and to seek God's Kingdom and his righteousness first.

Chapter 7

In Chapter 7, Jesus teaches the multitudes about judging others, prayer, the way of life, and obedience. He warns against judging, using the image of a speck and a beam in the eye, and tells hearers not to give holy things to dogs or pearls to pigs. He urges asking, seeking, and knocking, assuring that the Father in heaven gives good things, and gives the rule to do to others what one wants done to oneself. He contrasts the narrow gate with the broad way, warns of false prophets known by their fruits, and says that saying 'Lord, Lord' is not enough without doing the Father's will. He closes with the wise man who built on the rock and the foolish man who built on the sand, and the multitudes are astonished because he taught with authority, unlike the scribes.

Chapter 8

After coming down from the mountain, Jesus is followed by great multitudes and cleanses a leper by touch, telling him to show himself to the priest. In Capernaum he agrees to heal a centurion's paralyzed servant, and the centurion's confidence in Jesus' word leads Jesus to praise his faith and heal the servant. Jesus then heals Peter's wife's mother of a fever and, that evening, casts out spirits and heals all the sick, which is said to fulfill Isaiah's words. He answers a scribe and another disciple about the cost of following him, calms a violent storm on the sea for his frightened disciples, and in the country of the Gergesenes sends demons from two possessed men into a herd of pigs that rushes into the sea, after which the city begs him to leave.

Chapter 9

Jesus crosses into his own city, where he forgives and heals a paralyzed man, prompting scribes to accuse him of blasphemy while the crowds glorify God. He calls Matthew, a tax collector, and eats with tax collectors and sinners, answering the Pharisees' criticism and John's disciples' question about fasting with sayings about physicians, mercy, the bridegroom, and new wine in fresh wineskins. A ruler asks him to restore his dead daughter; on the way a woman with a twelve-year discharge of blood touches his garment and is made well, and Jesus then raises the girl by taking her hand. He gives sight to two blind men according to their faith, and a mute demon-possessed man speaks after the demon is cast out, while the Pharisees say he casts out demons by the prince of the demons. Jesus goes through the cities and villages teaching and healing, feels compassion for the harassed multitudes, and tells his disciples to pray for laborers for the harvest.

Chapter 10

Jesus calls his twelve disciples, gives them authority over unclean spirits and every disease, and names the twelve apostles, ending with Judas Iscariot who betrayed him. He sends them out to the lost sheep of the house of Israel, not to Gentiles or Samaritans, to preach that the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand, heal, and cast out demons, taking no money or supplies and relying on worthy households. He warns that they will be sent as sheep among wolves, delivered to councils, governors and kings, hated, and betrayed by family, but that the Spirit of their Father will speak through them and those who endure will be saved. He tells them not to fear, since the Father knows every sparrow and hair, and says those who confess him will be confessed before the Father while those who deny him will be denied. He declares he came with a sword, demands love for him above family and taking up one's cross, and promises rewards to those who receive his disciples, even with a cup of cold water.

Chapter 11

After finishing his instructions to the twelve disciples, Jesus goes to teach and preach in their cities. John, imprisoned, sends two disciples to ask whether Jesus is the one who is to come, and Jesus answers by pointing to his works and then praises John to the crowds as a prophet and more than a prophet, identifying him as Elijah and the messenger foretold. Jesus compares this generation to children who complain that no one responds to their flute or mourning, since they rejected both John and the Son of Man. He denounces Chorazin, Bethsaida, and Capernaum for not repenting despite his mighty works, saying Tyre, Sidon, and Sodom will fare more tolerably in judgment. He thanks the Father for revealing things to infants, declares that all things are delivered to him, and invites the weary and burdened to come to him, take his yoke, and find rest.

Chapter 12

In Chapter 12, Jesus is challenged by the Pharisees when his hungry disciples pluck grain on the Sabbath, and he defends them by citing David, the priests, and mercy, declaring himself Lord of the Sabbath. He then heals a man with a withered hand in a synagogue, after which the Pharisees conspire to destroy him, and he withdraws and heals the multitudes, which is said to fulfill Isaiah's prophecy of the chosen servant. After he heals a blind and mute possessed man, the Pharisees claim he casts out demons by Beelzebul, and Jesus answers with warnings about divided kingdoms, blasphemy against the Spirit, and the fruit of words. When scribes and Pharisees ask for a sign, he points to the sign of Jonah and warns of the return of an unclean spirit. Finally, when his mother and brothers wait outside, he says that whoever does the will of his Father in heaven is his brother, sister, and mother.

Chapter 13

Jesus sits by the sea and teaches the gathered multitudes in parables, beginning with a farmer whose seed falls on four kinds of ground. When the disciples ask why he uses parables, he says the mysteries of the Kingdom of Heaven are given to them but not to the crowds, and he explains the sower parable. He then tells parables of the darnel weeds, the mustard seed, the yeast, the hidden treasure, the pearl, and the dragnet, and privately explains the darnel parable to the disciples as a picture of the end of the age. After asking the disciples whether they understood and hearing 'Yes, Lord,' Jesus goes to his own country, where the people in the synagogue are astonished and offended by him, and he does few mighty works there because of their unbelief.

Chapter 14

Herod the tetrarch hears about Jesus and says he is John the Baptizer risen from the dead, recalling that he had imprisoned John for condemning his taking of Herodias, his brother Philip's wife. At Herod's birthday, Herodias's daughter dances, Herod swears to give her what she asks, and prompted by her mother she requests John's head on a platter; Herod, grieved but bound by his oaths, has John beheaded in prison. John's disciples bury the body and tell Jesus, who withdraws by boat, but the crowds follow him on foot and he heals their sick. Jesus feeds about five thousand men, plus women and children, with five loaves and two fish, leaving twelve baskets of leftovers. He then sends the disciples ahead by boat, prays alone on the mountain, and comes to them walking on the sea, where Peter walks toward him, begins to sink, and is saved; the wind ceases, the disciples worship him as the Son of God, and at Gennesaret all who touch his garment's fringe are made whole.

Chapter 15

Pharisees and scribes from Jerusalem accuse Jesus's disciples of breaking the tradition of the elders by not washing their hands before eating. Jesus answers that they void God's commandment for the sake of their tradition, calls them hypocrites, and teaches the multitude and his disciples that what comes out of the heart, not what enters the mouth, defiles a person. He withdraws to the region of Tyre and Sidon, where a Canaanite woman persistently asks him to heal her demon-possessed daughter, and he grants it because of her faith. Back by the sea of Galilee he heals many lame, blind, mute, and maimed people, then feeds four thousand men plus women and children with seven loaves and a few small fish, with seven baskets left over, before sailing to the borders of Magdala.

Chapter 16

In Chapter 16 the Pharisees and Sadducees test Jesus by asking for a sign from heaven, and he rebukes them as hypocrites who can read the sky but not the signs of the times, promising only the sign of Jonah. He then warns his disciples about the yeast of the Pharisees and Sadducees, and they eventually understand he meant their teaching, not bread. At Caesarea Philippi, Simon Peter declares Jesus to be the Christ, the Son of the living God, and Jesus blesses him, calls him Peter, and promises to build his assembly and give him the keys of the Kingdom of Heaven. Jesus then begins to foretell his suffering, death, and resurrection in Jerusalem, rebukes Peter as Satan for objecting, and teaches that following him means denying oneself and taking up a cross, with the Son of Man to come in glory and repay everyone according to their deeds.

Chapter 17

Jesus takes Peter, James, and John up a high mountain, where he is transfigured and Moses and Elijah appear; a voice from a cloud declares him the beloved Son. Coming down, Jesus tells the disciples to keep the vision secret until he has risen, and explains that Elijah has already come in the person of John the Baptizer. A man asks Jesus to heal his epileptic son after the disciples failed; Jesus rebukes the demon and cures the boy, then tells the disciples their unbelief was the cause and speaks of mustard-seed faith. Jesus predicts that he will be delivered to men, killed, and raised on the third day. In Capernaum, collectors of the didrachma approach Peter, and Jesus tells him to take a stater coin from a fish's mouth to pay for them both.

Chapter 18

In Matthew 18, the disciples ask Jesus who is greatest in the Kingdom of Heaven, and he sets a little child among them and says that humbling oneself like the child is the way to greatness. He warns against causing little ones who believe in him to stumble, urges drastic measures against sin, and tells of a shepherd seeking one lost sheep because the Father does not want any little one to perish. He then gives steps for dealing with a brother who sins: speak privately, take witnesses, tell the assembly, and treat the unrepentant one as a Gentile or tax collector, with sayings on binding, releasing, and gathering in his name. When Peter asks how often to forgive, Jesus answers seventy times seven and tells the parable of a king who forgave a servant ten thousand talents, only for that servant to imprison a fellow servant over one hundred denarii, and so be handed to the tormentors.

Chapter 19

Jesus leaves Galilee for Judea beyond the Jordan, where crowds follow and he heals them. Pharisees test him on divorce, and he answers by pointing to creation and the union of husband and wife, saying Moses allowed divorce only because of hardness of heart, and that divorce except for sexual immorality followed by remarriage is adultery. His disciples respond that marriage seems inadvisable, and he speaks of eunuchs and of those able to receive his saying. He then welcomes little children over the disciples' rebuke, and a young man with great possessions asks how to gain eternal life, but goes away sad when told to sell what he has and follow Jesus. Jesus teaches that it is hard for the rich to enter God's Kingdom, and when Peter asks what the disciples will receive, Jesus promises them thrones and a hundredfold reward to all who leave things for his name.

Chapter 20

Jesus tells a parable in which the master of a household hires laborers at various hours of the day for his vineyard and pays all of them a denarius, prompting the first-hired to murmur; he concludes that the last will be first and the first last. On the way up to Jerusalem, Jesus tells the twelve disciples that the Son of Man will be delivered, condemned, mocked, scourged, crucified, and raised on the third day. The mother of the sons of Zebedee asks that her two sons sit at his right and left in his Kingdom, and Jesus says they will drink his cup but that those seats are prepared by his Father. The other ten are indignant, and Jesus teaches that greatness among them comes through serving, as the Son of Man came to serve and give his life as a ransom for many. Leaving Jericho, Jesus stops for two blind men who cry out for mercy despite the crowd's rebuke, and he heals them with compassion.

Chapter 21

Jesus approaches Jerusalem, sending two disciples to fetch a donkey and colt, and enters to crowds shouting 'Hosanna to the son of David!' He drives the sellers and money changers out of the temple, heals the lame and blind, and answers the indignant chief priests and scribes by citing Scripture about children's praise. He curses a fig tree that withers and teaches the disciples about faith and prayer. When the chief priests and elders challenge his authority, he counters with a question about John's baptism, then tells the parables of the two sons and the wicked farmers, which the chief priests and Pharisees perceive to be about them, though they fear the multitudes and do not seize him.

Chapter 22

Jesus tells a parable of a king who gives a wedding feast for his son; the invited guests refuse and kill his servants, so the king destroys them and fills the feast with whomever the servants find, until he casts out a man without wedding clothing. The Pharisees and Herodians then try to trap Jesus over paying taxes to Caesar, and he answers that Caesar's things go to Caesar and God's things to God. The Sadducees pose a puzzle about a woman who married seven brothers in turn, and Jesus replies that in the resurrection people are like angels and that God is the God of the living. A lawyer among the Pharisees asks about the greatest commandment, and Jesus names love of God and love of neighbor. Jesus then asks the Pharisees how the Christ can be David's son if David calls him Lord, and no one can answer him or dare question him further.

Chapter 23

Jesus speaks to the multitudes and his disciples about the scribes and the Pharisees, who sit on Moses' seat. He tells people to observe what they say but not to imitate their works, since they burden others, seek honor and titles, and do everything to be seen by men. He tells his followers not to take the titles Rabbi, father, or master, and says the greatest among them will be a servant. He then pronounces a series of woes on the scribes and Pharisees as hypocrites, condemning their shutting up the Kingdom of Heaven, their proselytizing, their oaths, their neglect of justice, mercy, and faith, and their outward cleanliness hiding inner corruption. He ends by lamenting over Jerusalem, which kills the prophets, and declares its house desolate until it says 'Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord!'

Chapter 24

Jesus leaves the temple and tells his disciples that not one stone of its buildings will be left on another. On the Mount of Olives the disciples privately ask when this will happen and what the sign of his coming and of the end of the age will be. Jesus warns of false christs, wars, famines, and earthquakes as the beginning of birth pains, then describes persecution, the abomination of desolation, and a great suffering that will be shortened for the chosen ones. He says the Son of Man will come visibly on the clouds after those days, gathering the chosen, while no one but the Father knows the day or hour. He urges watchfulness through the days of Noah, the thief, and the faithful and evil servants.

Chapter 25

Chapter 25 presents three teachings about the Kingdom of Heaven and the coming of the Son of Man. In the first, ten virgins await a bridegroom; the five wise ones bring oil and enter the wedding feast, while the five foolish ones lack oil, go to buy it, and are shut out. In the second, a man going into another country entrusts five, two, and one talent to his servants; the first two double their talents and are praised, while the servant who buried his one talent is condemned and cast into the outer darkness. In the third, the Son of Man, as King, gathers all the nations and separates sheep from goats, welcoming the righteous who served the least of his brothers into eternal life and sending those who did not into eternal punishment. The chapter repeatedly stresses watchfulness, readiness, and faithful action.

Chapter 26

In Matthew 26, Jesus tells his disciples that the Passover is two days away and that he will be delivered up to be crucified, while the chief priests, scribes, and elders plot with Caiaphas to seize him by deceit. A woman anoints Jesus in Bethany, and Judas Iscariot agrees to betray him for thirty pieces of silver. At the Passover meal Jesus predicts his betrayal and denial by Peter, gives the bread and cup as his body and blood of the new covenant, and then prays in Gethsemane while the disciples sleep. Judas leads a crowd to arrest Jesus with a kiss, the disciples flee, and the council tries Jesus before Caiaphas, declaring him worthy of death for blasphemy after he affirms he is the Son of Man who will sit at the right hand of Power. Meanwhile Peter follows at a distance, denies Jesus three times, hears the rooster crow, and weeps bitterly.

Chapter 27

The chief priests and elders deliver Jesus to Pontius Pilate, the governor, and Judas, seeing Jesus condemned, returns the thirty pieces of silver, says he betrayed innocent blood, and hangs himself; the priests use the money to buy the potter's field. Pilate questions Jesus, who answers little, and offers the crowd a choice between Barabbas and Jesus; persuaded by the chief priests and elders, the multitude demands Barabbas and the crucifixion of Jesus, and Pilate washes his hands and has Jesus flogged and handed over. Soldiers mock Jesus, Simon of Cyrene is compelled to carry his cross, and Jesus is crucified at Golgotha between two robbers while passersby, priests, and the robbers mock him. Darkness falls, Jesus cries out and yields up his spirit, the temple veil tears, the earth quakes, tombs open, and the centurion says he was the Son of God. Joseph of Arimathaea, a disciple, obtains the body from Pilate and lays it in his new tomb, and the chief priests and Pharisees get Pilate's permission to secure the tomb with a guard and a sealed stone.

Chapter 28

At dawn on the first day of the week, Mary Magdalene and the other Mary come to the tomb, where an earthquake occurs and an angel of the Lord rolls away the stone, leaving the guards like dead men. The angel tells the women that Jesus has risen and instructs them to tell his disciples to go to Galilee, and Jesus himself meets the women and repeats the instruction. Some guards report to the chief priests, who, with the elders, pay the soldiers silver to say the disciples stole the body, a story that spreads among the Jews. The eleven disciples go to a mountain in Galilee, where they worship Jesus though some doubt, and Jesus declares all authority is his and commissions them to make disciples of all nations, baptize, and teach, promising to be with them always.
