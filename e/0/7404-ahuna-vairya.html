Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, it is a veracious word, prayer, and sacred gift. In Yasna, it is a prayer pealed forth in liturgy between heaven and earth. In Visparad, it is a heard, memorised recital recited as a chant and offered wisely with sanctity. In Afrinagan, Gahs and Miscellaneous Fragments, it is a revered prayer chosen by Mazda and counted among victorious sacred words. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), it is a sacred, fiend-smiting and most-healing chanted prayer. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, it is a fiend-smiting, healing prayer and formula.

Ahuna-vairya in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Ahuna-vairya first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Ahuna-vairya is a veracious word, prayer, and sacred gift. Declared before the world’s creation, it contains the seed of righteousness and is worshipped and sacrificed to. Zarathustra first recited it four times, keeping its verses apart and raising his voice. It has five sentences, ending in a concluding fifth sentence, and three stages or measures: good thought, good word, and good deed. Its exact recitation has great stated value; imperfect recitation with additions or omissions has less. It is linked with four classes, five chiefs, and a concluding gift, and is intoned with sanctity, offering saving acts of wisdom and worship.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna VII.

The Ahuna-vairya is among the sacred things sacrificed to, together with the veracious word correctly uttered, the good and pious Prayer for blessings, Haurvatât, Ameretatât, the Kine of blessed gift, Haoma, Haoma-juice, wood-billets, and perfume.

Yasna IX.

The Ahuna-vairya was first recited by Zarathustra, who intoned it four times with verses kept apart and with an increasingly louder voice.

Yasna XIII (Sp. XIV).

The Ahuna-vairya is worshipped.

Yasna XIX.

The Ahuna-vairya is the word pronounced by Ahura Mazda before creation and is said to contain the seed of righteousness. Its correctly recited parts are valued above other stanzas, while recitation with omissions or additions has lesser but still stated effects. It has three measures, good thought, good word, and good deed, and is associated with four classes, five chiefs, and a concluding gift.

Yasna XXVII.

The Ahuna-vairya is intoned with sanctity as a sacred gift offering saving acts of wisdom and worship. It is worshipped at the close of the unit.

Ahuna-vairya in Visparad

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Ahuna-vairya first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ahuna-vairya is a heard, memorised recital recited as a chant and offered wisely with sanctity. It carries many teachings of religious wisdom, is praised and approached, and is worshipped as holy lord of the ritual order, with Ahû and Ratu as constituent titles. As part of the Gâtha Ahunavaiti, it has chapters, metrical lines, words, and word-structure. It culminates as one of two means through which victory and other benefits are found through recital of good thoughts, words, and deeds.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Visparad X (Sp. XI).

The Ahuna-vairya is among the entities the speaker desires to approach with praise.

Visparad XII (Sp. XIV).

The Ahuna-vairya is offered as a wise offering, both when intoned with sanctity and when yet to be intoned. It possesses many teachings of religious wisdom.

Visparad XIV (Sp. XVI).

The Ahuna-vairya is worshipped as the holy lord of the ritual order. It is described as having its Ahû and its Ratu.

Visparad XXIII (Sp. XXVI).

The Ahuna-vairya is one of the two through which victory and other benefits are found by the memorised recital of good thoughts, words, and deeds.

Ahuna-Vairya in Vendidad (Vendîdâd)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Ahuna-Vairya first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ahuna-Vairya is a sacred, fiend-smiting and most-healing chanted prayer expressing that the Lord’s will is the law of holiness. In cleansing it is chanted at changing counts, after furrows are drawn, and two hundred times for Vohu-manô; Zarathustra chants it to repel the Drug as a weapon against Angra Mainyu and to preserve man.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard XI.

The Ahuna-Vairya is chanted five times, eight times, four times, and five times at different stages of the cleansing. Its words include the statement, "The will of the Lord is the law of holiness," and it is said to preserve the person of man.

Fargard XVII.

The Ahuna-Vairya is chanted three, six, or nine times after furrows are drawn around the hole.

Fargard XIX.

The Ahuna-Vairya is chanted by Zarathustra to repel the Drug and is identified as a weapon against Angra Mainyu. It is also recited two hundred times in the cleansing rite for Vohu-manô.

Ahuna Vairya in Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Ahuna Vairya first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ahuna Vairya is a fiend-smiting, healing prayer and formula. Zarathustra sings and repeats it four times with increasing force; its Glory drives the Daêvas beneath the earth and removes sacrifice and prayer from them. Pronouncing it with the word of truth increases strength, victory, and piety in one's soul.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XIX. Zamyâd Yast.

The Ahuna Vairya is sung and repeated four times by Zarathustra with increasing force. Its Glory drives all the Daêvas beneath the earth and removes sacrifice and prayer from them.

XXI. Yast Fragment.

Ahuna Vairya is identified as the formula whose pronouncing, together with the reciting of the word of truth, increases strength and victory in one's soul and piety.
