Ahaz in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Ahaz first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ahaz, son of Jotham, became king of Judah at twenty and reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. Unlike David, he did not do right in the LORD's eyes: he followed Israel's kings, worshipped idolatrously at high places, and made his son pass through the fire. He called himself servant of Tiglath Pileser, paying tribute from the LORD's house and royal treasuries. He had Urijah build a new altar, reserved the bronze altar for himself, and was buried in the City of David, succeeded by Hezekiah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 16

Ahaz became king of Judah at twenty, reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem, and did not do right in the LORD's eyes, making his son pass through the fire and sacrificing at the high places. He bought the help of Assyria with silver and gold from the LORD's house, copied the Damascus altar, set aside the bronze altar, and altered the temple furnishings because of the king of Assyria. He died and was buried in David's city, succeeded by his son Hezekiah.

Ahaz in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Ahaz first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ahaz was king of Judah, son and successor of Jotham, who began reigning at twenty and ruled sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did evil, unlike David: following Israel's kings, making molten images for the Baals, burning his children, and sacrificing at high places. He sought Assyria's help with temple treasure, but it failed him. He also sacrificed to Damascus's gods, cut up the temple vessels, shut the LORD's house, and built altars throughout Judah. He died and was buried in Jerusalem, not in the kings' tombs; his son Hezekiah succeeded him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 27

Ahaz is named as Jotham's son. He reigned in his father's place after Jotham died and was buried in David's city.

Chapter 28

Ahaz began to reign at twenty and reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem, doing evil by following the kings of Israel, making molten images for the Baals, burning his children in the fire, and sacrificing at high places. He sought help from Assyria by giving it treasure from the LORD's house and the royal houses, but it did not help him. He sacrificed to the gods of Damascus, cut up the vessels of God's house, shut its doors, and built altars and high places throughout Judah. He died, was buried in Jerusalem but not in the kings' tombs, and was succeeded by his son Hezekiah.

Ahaz in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Ahaz first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ahaz, king of Judah, son of Jotham and descendant of David's house, trembles when told Syria allies with Ephraim. Though the LORD, through Isaiah, offers a sign, he refuses, saying he won't tempt the LORD. The LORD then foretells trouble, including Assyria's king, for him, his people, and his father's house.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

Ahaz, king of Judah and descendant of David, trembled with his people when told Syria was allied with Ephraim. The LORD told him through Isaiah not to fear and offered him a sign, but Ahaz refused, saying he would not tempt the LORD. The LORD then declared that days of trouble, including the king of Assyria, would come on him, his people, and his father's house.
