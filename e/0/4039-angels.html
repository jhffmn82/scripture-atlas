Abstract written by a model

In The Letter to the Hebrews, angels are lesser spiritual beings who serve as spirits sent to those inheriting salvation. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), the angels question Allah’s appointment of a viceroy, acknowledge His knowledge, and prostrate before Adam except Iblis. In Surah 3: The Family of Imraan (Aal-i-Imraan), the angels bear witness to Allah’s unity, support believers in battle, and give Zachariah and Mary glad tidings. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa): take_in_death oppressed people [while they wrong themselves] Angels take in death those who wrong themselves and ask what they were engaged in. In Surah 13: The Thunder (Ar-Ra'd): Angels guard people before and behind them by Allah's command. In Surah 15: The Rock (Al-Hijr), angels prostrate before the mortal Allah creates, deliver messengers, and punish a guilty people while rescuing Lot’s family. In Surah 16: The Bee (An-Nahl), the angels descend with Allah’s command, cause people to die, prostrate to Allah, fear Him, and obey His commands. In Surah 18: The Cave (Al-Kahf): The angels were commanded to fall prostrate before Adam, and they did so except for Iblis.

In Surah 39: The Groups (Az-Zumar), the angels throng around the Throne, praise their Lord, and witness the completion of judgment. In Surah 41: Explained in detail (Fussilat), the angels descend upon the upright, reassure them, give them glad tidings of Paradise, protect Allah’s friends, and glorify Him. In Surah 43: Ornaments of gold (Az-Zukhruf), the angels are Allah’s slaves, falsely called female by people who will be questioned about that testimony. In Surah 53: The Star (An-Najm), the angels’ intercession is effective only with Allah’s permission, despite disbelievers calling them female. In Surah 74: The Cloaked One (Al-Muddaththir), angels serve as wardens of the Fire, numbering nineteen. In Surah 79: Those who drag forth (An-Naazi'aat), angels hasten and govern the event. In The Second Book of Nephi, angels minister to Nephi and speak the words of Christ through Holy Ghost power. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, angels declare the Lord’s voice and glad tidings, prepare people for the Son’s coming, and speak to the just and holy. In The Book of Helaman, angels of God minister to Nephi, the Lamanites, and about three hundred souls, appearing to wise men and declaring glad tidings. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, angels rejoice over the slain, visit Nephi daily, and minister to little children. In The Book of Moroni, angels minister to people by Christ’s command, call them to repentance, fulfill covenants, and declare Christ’s word.

angels in The Letter to the Hebrews

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where angels first appears

Abstract written by a model

Angels are spiritual beings presented in Hebrews as lesser than the Son. God makes them winds and flames of fire, and they serve as spirits sent to those who will inherit salvation. They are commanded to worship the firstborn, were never called Son, and spoke a word whose transgressions were justly penalized. The world to come was not subjected to them, humans and Jesus were made a little lower than them, and Jesus helps Abraham's offspring, not angels.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

The Letter to the Hebrews / Chapter 1

The angels are shown to be lesser than the Son, who has become as much better than they as his inherited name is more excellent than theirs. God never said to any of them "You are my Son" or "Sit at my right hand," and they are commanded to worship the firstborn. They are described as winds and a flame of fire, and as serving spirits sent out to serve those who will inherit salvation.

Chapter 2

Angels spoke a word that proved steadfast, with every transgression receiving a just penalty. The world to come was not subjected to them, and man and Jesus are described as made a little lower than them. Jesus does not give help to angels but to the offspring of Abraham.
