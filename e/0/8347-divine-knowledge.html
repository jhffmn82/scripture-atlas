divine knowledge

topic · 2 works · 11 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Understanding, highest divine knowledge, intellect, knowledge, knowledge of God, knowledge of the Word, real divine knowledge, true knowledge of God, understanding, wisdom

Abstract written by a model

In Japji, divine knowledge is described as a light in the realm of knowledge and an anvil in spiritual practice, obtained through hearing God's Name, obedience, and the Guru's instruction. In Hymns of Guru Nanak, divine knowledge is described as a rare, peaceful treasure that dispels ignorance, reveals God, supports devotion, and is necessary for emancipation.

divine knowledge in Hymns of Guru Nanak

Guru Nanak · the work’s page · read it where divine knowledge first appears

Abstract written by a model

Divine knowledge is a rare, peaceful treasure recognized by one who inquires of and thinks of the true Guru. Obtained through favourable destiny and the Guru, it makes a person a servant of the True One, dispels mental ignorance, and reveals God’s form through spiritual sight. It is likened to salve for beholding God and material for an enduring lamp of devotion; it brings the benefit of pilgrimage through immortal water. It is necessary for emancipation: outward practices, religious display, learned texts, and appearances cannot reveal the Real Thing without it. Pure knowledge guides understanding, worship, honour, and alms, while wisdom used for wrangling is not true wisdom.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Asa Chhant

Divine knowledge is learned through inquiry of the true Guru and through thinking of the true Guru. A rare person recognizes God through this knowledge.

Rag Suhi .. Suhi ki War

Divine knowledge is presented as the means by which man becomes a servant of the True One and mental ignorance is dispelled. It is obtained through the Guru and is symbolized as salve that enables one to see God's form.

Ramkali .. Ramkali ki War I

Divine knowledge is a good material for the enduring lamp of devotion and is necessary for emancipation. Without it, outward practices and religious displays cannot save a person.

Rag Maru Ashtapadi .. Maru ki War I

Divine knowledge enables a person to see the One who sees all things and to understand God under the Guru's instruction. Without it, religious appearances and learned texts do not reveal the Real Thing.

Sarang ki War

Knowledge has no impurity and is used to understand what is read, worship the Lord, obtain honour, and properly give alms. Wisdom spent in wrangling is not true wisdom.
