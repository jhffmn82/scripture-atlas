Abstract written by a model

The kingdom, gained by King Wu, contains princes, great officers, scholars, and common people, who receive rules of royal ceremonies. Throughout it, carriages, writing, and conduct are standardized. Governed by a Ruler, it can be perfectly ruled: his institutions, acts, and words provide examples, laws, and lessons for ages, inspiring both distant longing and nearby devotion.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

King Wu gained possession of the kingdom. The kingdom included princes, great officers, scholars, and common people to whom the duke of Chau extended the rules of royal ceremonies.

Chapter 28

Throughout the kingdom, carriages have wheels of the same size, writing uses the same characters, and conduct follows the same rules.

Chapter 29

The kingdom is governed by a Ruler whose institutions become examples, laws, and lessons for ages. Those far from the Ruler long for him, while those near him are never weary of him.
