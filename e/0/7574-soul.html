Abstract written by a model

A faithful person’s soul, a spiritual entity departing from the body, remains near the head for three nights. It sings the Ustavaiti Gâtha and tastes immense pleasure, while worshipping good waters and Âtar. At third-night dawn it meets a sweet-scented wind and its own conscience. It then progresses through Good-Thought, Good-Word, and Good-Deed Paradise to Endless Lights, and is guided along the ways to Garô-nmâna.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Yast XXII.

For three nights, the faithful person’s soul sits near the head, sings the Ustavaiti Gâtha, and tastes pleasure. At the third-night dawn it encounters a sweet-scented wind and its own conscience, then enters the Good-Thought Paradise, Good-Word Paradise, Good-Deed Paradise, and Endless Lights.

XXIV. Vîstâsp Yast.

The faithful soul separates from the body and spends the first night in Good Words and the second in Good Deeds before going along the ways to Garô-nmâna. It encounters its own conscience and reaches the Good-Thought, Good-Word, Good-Deed, and Endless Light Paradises.
