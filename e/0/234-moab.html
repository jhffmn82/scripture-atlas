Abstract written by a model

In Numbers, Moab fears Israel, and its king Balak consults the elders and sends princes to Balaam, hoping to defeat Israel and drive them from the land. In Deuteronomy, Moab is a land given by the LORD to Lot's children, so Israel is told not to fight it and passes through its wilderness. In Deuteronomy, the Moabite group is barred from the LORD's assembly to the tenth generation for withholding bread and water from Israel and hiring Balaam to curse them. In The Book of Judges, Moab, ruled by King Eglon, oppresses Israel eighteen years until Ehud's Israelites defeat the Moabites and subdue the land. In The Book of Ruth, Moab is the country where Naomi's family lives during the famine, where the men die, and from which Naomi later departs. In The Second Book of Samuel, David defeats Moab, measures its people, kills two lines and spares one, and makes the Moabites his servants who bring tribute. In The Second Book of Kings, Moab rebels against Israel, is routed while plundering, and is devastated, leaving only Kir Hareseth's stones. In 1 Chronicles, Moab is defeated by David, and the Moabites become his servants and bring tribute.

and 6 more works

In The Second Book of Chronicles, the children of Moab come against Jehoshaphat, then rise against Mount Seir and help destroy one another. In The Book of Isaiah, Moab is a proud nation and land whose cities are destroyed, whose people mourn and flee, and whose pride is humbled. In The Book of Jeremiah, Moab is a place and people whose god is Chemosh; its cities are taken, its people mourn and flee, and its captivity is restored in the latter days. In The Book of Ezekiel, Moab is a nation whose frontier cities are judged and given to the children of the east. In The Book of Amos, Moab is condemned for burning the Edomite king's bones into lime, and its judge and princes are killed amid destruction. In The Book of Zephaniah, Moab is a proud nation accused of reproaching the LORD's people and magnifying itself against their border; it is judged to become a perpetual desolation.

Moab in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · 739 to 680 BC · the work’s page · read it where Moab first appears

Abstract written by a model

Moab is a proud nation and land, with cities such as Ar, Kir Hareseth, Nebo and Medeba, whose destruction is foretold. In Chapter 15 it is laid waste in a single night; people wail, armed men tremble, nobles flee, and the land withers. In Chapter 16 it is likened to scattered birds, mourns ruined vineyards, and its prayers at the high place and sanctuary fail. In Chapter 25 it is trodden down like straw in dunghill water, its pride humbled, its boastings proven worthless, and its high fortress brought to dust, with downfall coming within three years.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 15

Moab is the subject of the burden. Its cities Ar and Kir are laid waste in a night, its people wail over Nebo and Medeba, and its armed men cry aloud with trembling souls. Its nobles flee, its land withers, and a lion is promised against those who escape and the remnant of the land.

Chapter 16

Moab is pictured as wandering birds and a scattered nest, with its daughters at the fords of the Arnon. It wails over its ruined vineyards and fields, and when it goes to its high place and sanctuary to pray, it will not prevail.

Chapter 25

Moab will be trodden down in his place, like straw trodden in the water of the dunghill, while the LORD's hand rests on the mountain. He will spread out his hands like a swimmer, but his pride will be humbled together with the craft of his hands. The high fortress of its walls is brought down to the dust.
