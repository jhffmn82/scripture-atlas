Abstract written by a model

A Philistine woman from Timnah whom Samson asks his parents to get as his wife. Threatened with burning by the thirty companions, she weeps seven days, learns the riddle from Samson and tells her people, then is given to his friend.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

She is a Philistine woman from Timnah whom Samson finds pleasing and marries. Pressured by threats of burning from the thirty companions, she weeps for seven days until Samson tells her the riddle, and she tells it to her people. She is then given to Samson's companion who had been his friend.

Abstract written by a model

Samson's wife, daughter of a father and elder sister of a more beautiful younger sister, is given by her father to Samson's companion, barring Samson from her; the Philistines then burn her to death over Samson's crop burning.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 15

Samson's wife has been given by her father to Samson's companion, and Samson is not allowed to see her. The Philistines later burn her with fire, because Samson's act of burning their crops is blamed on his having had her taken and given away.
