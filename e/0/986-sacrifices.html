Abstract written by a model

In Leviticus, sacrifices must be brought to the Tent of Meeting door and offered to the LORD as peace offerings through the priest, never in open fields or to goat idols; violators are cut off under a permanent statute. In Psalms, sacrifices and offerings of Israel are not rebuked, but God says he does not need their animals and that offerings are not what he ultimately wants. In The Book of Isaiah, sacrifices and offerings are regarded by the LORD as a burden and an abomination when they accompany evil and hands full of blood. In The Letter to the Hebrews, sacrifices offered under the law are repeated by standing priests year by year and day by day, but the blood of bulls and goats cannot take away sins, and Christ's one sacrifice replaces them.

Abstract written by a model

Repeated sacrifices offered under the law by standing priests, year by year and day by day; blood of bulls and goats cannot take away sins, only reminding of them yearly. God did not desire them; Christ's one sacrifice replaces them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10

The sacrifices are offered year by year and day by day by priests who stand serving, repeating the same offerings. They can never take away sins, because the blood of bulls and goats cannot do so, and they serve only as a yearly reminder of sins. God did not desire them, and Christ's one sacrifice replaces them.
