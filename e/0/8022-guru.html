Abstract written by a model

In Japji, Guru teaches that one Bestower sustains all living beings and that God is everywhere, while describing divine knowledge, light, wisdom, and understanding. In Japji, the Guru's instruction reveals God's word and omnipresence and guides people to divine knowledge and understanding of the one Bestower. In So Dar and So Purukh (the Rahiras), the true Guru protects seekers, instructs them in God's Name, enlightens their souls, and blesses those who enter its protection and society. In Asa ki War (Asa di Var), True Guru is a merciful benefactor and giver of life who dispels spiritual darkness, communicates divine instruction, removes pride, reveals God, and leads people to salvation. In Asa ki War (Asa di Var), Guru's instruction gives disciples knowledge that the Kind One saves those upon whom He looks with favor. In Asa ki War (Asa di Var), the Word is communicated by True Guru to the soul after it has wandered through many births. In Hymns of Guru Nanak, Guru gives God's Name, divine knowledge, steadfastness, and God within, breaks pride and doubt, protects people, and leads them to God's court and freedom from transmigration. In Hymns of Guru Nanak, Guru is a spiritual guide and Giver who gives God's name, teaches truth, reveals God, dispels ignorance and suffering, and leads people to salvation and union with God.

and 3 more works

In Hymns of Guru Nanak, the Word is the true Guru's spiritual discourse that cleanses, gives divine knowledge and devotion, implants God's name in the heart, and culminates in salvation and union with God. In Hymns of Guru Nanak, the True Guru's instruction teaches remembrance, truth, contentment, charity, and service, restrains evil inclinations, and leads to salvation and absorption in the True One. In Additional Sloks of Guru Nanak, True Guru protects those who address Him from fear amid life's storms and makes ablution pure for those who regard Him as a river of pilgrimage.

Guru in Japji

Max Arthur Macauliffe · the work’s page · read it where Guru first appears

Abstract written by a model

Guru is a guide who teaches that one Bestower gives to all living beings and that God is everywhere contained. It is identified with Shiv, Vishnu, Brahma, Parbati, Lakhshmi, and Saraswati. In the realm of knowledge, divine light, joy, beauty, incomparable fashioning, and knowledge, wisdom, intellect, understanding, and supernatural skill are described.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Japji .. 36 In the realm of knowledge the light of divine knowledge is resplendent. There are heard songs from which millions of joys and pleasures proceed. Beauty is the attribute of the realm of happiness. There things are fashioned in an incomparable manner. What is done there cannot be described. Whoever endeavoureth to describe it shall afterwards repent. There are fashioned knowledge, wisdom, intellect, and understanding; And there too is fashioned the skill of demigods and men of supernatural power.

The Guru teaches that there is one Bestower on all living beings and that God is everywhere contained. The Guru is described as Shiv, Vishnu, Brahma, Parbati, Lakhshmi, and Saraswati.

the Guru's instruction in Japji

Abstract written by a model

The Guru's instruction reveals God's word and omnipresence. It guides people to hear and acquire divine knowledge, learn that God is everywhere contained, and understand that one Bestower sustains all living beings.

the Word in Hymns of Guru Nanak

Guru Nanak · the work’s page · read it where the Word first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Word is true, the Word of the true Guru, and the highest spiritual discourse: a means of devotion, meditation, and attention wherever one sits. It dwells in the heart, manifests God, moistens and awakens the heart when it has light, restrains the wandering mind, and extinguishes hopes, desires, and pride. Received through the Guru's instruction, accepted as true, taught to the heart, understood, and continually meditated upon, it gives divine knowledge, truth, love, honour, peace, happiness, service, and absorption in God. It cleanses heart and body, removes demerits and doubt, regenerates those who humbly follow it, and implants God's name in the heart. Those imbued or tinctured with it are delivered, united with God, become perfect Bairagis, obtain God's abode and the ambrosial fruit, and cross the terrible ocean into the True One's court. Religious acts, purifications, austerities, devotion, penance, and pilgrimages abide in it. The Word is also contrasted with those who reject it, lack it, or speak and write without fear of God: they wander, are punished on the road of death, or are watched by the god of death. Thus it begins as inner cleansing and discipline, unfolds through Guru-given understanding and devotion, and culminates in salvation, freedom from bondage and transmigration, and union with God.

the True Guru's instruction in Hymns of Guru Nanak

Guru Nanak · the work’s page · read it where the True Guru's instruction first appears

Abstract written by a model

The True Guru's instruction is a path of spiritual teaching and discipline. It calls people to remember the Name, recognize God's will and presence in every heart, speak truth, and practise contentment, charity, and service. It rejects greed, pride, falsehood, worldly love, superstition, and evil inclinations, restraining the mind and giving divine understanding. It adjusts the soul's affairs, reveals God's court at home, brings protection and happiness, and culminates in salvation, emancipation, freedom from transmigration, and absorption in the True One.
