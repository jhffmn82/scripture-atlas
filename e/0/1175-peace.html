Abstract written by a model

Peace is sought for Jerusalem, within its walls, and among those who love the city in Psalms. Peace is spoken by God to his people and pictured as kissing righteousness in Psalms. Peace is the rest of the righteous and the fruit of the lips, while the wicked have none, in The Book of Isaiah. Truth and peace are to be practiced, judged, and loved by the people in The Book of Zechariah. Peace is presented as preferable in relations among believers and within marriage in Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa). Peace is to be established justly between fighting parties and among believing brothers in Surah 49: The Inner Apartments (Al-Hujuraat). Peace would have been like a river for the obedient, while the wicked have no peace, in The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry. Peace is restored after persecution and established throughout the land by Mosiah in The Book of Mosiah.

and 2 more works

Peace is received in the kingdom of God, maintained among the Nephites, and restored through humility and covenants in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma. Peace is established and continues in the land before eventually ending amid wickedness, secret murders, and strife in The Book of Helaman.

peace in Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where peace first appears

Abstract written by a model

Peace is presented as the better course in relations among believers and within marriage. Believers are told to accept those who stand apart, do not fight, and offer peace, and not to deny the belief of someone offering peace for worldly gain. In matters concerning women, orphans, and children, the text calls for just dealing and says Allah knows every good done. When a woman and her husband fear ill treatment or desertion, they may establish peace; the text concludes that peace is better.

Peace is received by people in the kingdom of God at the last day and exists continually among the Nephites. Believing Lamanites bury weapons and suffer rather than sin. Humility after preaching brings four years of peace, while later conflict ends when Morianton’s people covenant to keep peace.

The Lamanites who believe the truth bury their weapons of war for peace. Their refusal to fight and their willingness to suffer rather than commit sin demonstrate their commitment to peace.

Chapter 48

The Nephites enjoy peace and freedom from wars and contentions among themselves for four years because the people humble themselves after the preaching of Helaman and his brethren.

Chapter 50

Peace characterizes the twenty and second and twenty and third years and is restored after the conflict involving Morianton. The people of Morianton covenant to keep the peace before returning to their land.
