nun

person · 1 work · 32 quoted facts · 9 connections
Also called: a nun, sister

Abstract written by a model

A nun is a mendicant and fellow-ascetic governed by detailed rules for clothing, bowls, religious practice, study, and bodily needs. She should possess four raiments of prescribed widths and may beg for specified materials, but should not travel farther than half a yogana for clothes or a bowl. Clothes are worn as received and protected from rain; when seeking alms she must be properly clothed and avoid entering or leaving houses during strong, widespread rain. She may carry one bowl if youthful and may beg for a permitted bottle-gourd, wooden, or clay bowl, but not one bought specifically for her. Before using or carrying it into or out of a householder's abode, she inspects it, removes living beings, and wipes off dust; wet bowls are not rubbed until dry. For religious postures she enters a scot-free town, and for study she accepts only places free of eggs or living beings. If lacking her own broom, she may use or beg a fellow-ascetic's. In easing nature, she avoids ground containing life, appropriated or specially prepared places, uses a chamber-pot, chooses a secluded place free of eggs or living beings, and leaves excrement on ashes in a secluded spot.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Fifth Lecture, Called Begging of Clothes. / First Lesson. .. Second Lesson.

A nun may beg for specified kinds of cloth and should possess four raiments of prescribed widths. She must follow the same rules as a monk concerning acceptance, inspection, wearing, maintenance, airing, drying, travel, and protection of clothes.

Sixth Lecture, Called Begging for a Bowl. / First Lesson. .. Second Lesson.

A nun is subject to the same rules as a monk when begging for and accepting a bowl. She may accept only permitted bowls, must inspect the bowl for living beings, seeds, grass, and dust, and must handle wet bowls and rain with care.

Second Part. / The Seven Lectures. / Eighth Lecture.

A nun who wishes to perform religious postures should enter a village or a scot-free town. The nun should not accept a place infected by eggs or living beings.

Ninth Lecture.

A nun wishing to go to a place for study should not accept one infected by eggs or living beings. She may accept a place free from eggs or living beings.

Tenth Lecture.

A nun should beg for a fellow-ascetic's broom if she lacks her own. She should inspect and clean a suitable ground, avoid places containing life or associated with harm, and ease nature with a chamber-pot in a secluded place.
