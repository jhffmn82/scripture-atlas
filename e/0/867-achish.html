Achish

Also called: Achish the king of Gath, Achish the son of Maoch, king of Gath

Abstract written by a model

Achish, son of Maoch, is king of Gath and a Philistine leader. David first flees to him and feigns madness, so Achish rebukes his servants and refuses to admit him. Later he receives David and his men and gives him Ziklag, believing David's false report of raiding Judah means Israel abhors him and he will serve forever. Finally Achish defends David to the Philistine princes, finding no fault in him and calling him upright, but yields to them and sends David away in peace at first light.

Achish is in one work on this site, The First Book of Samuel. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

Achish is the king of Gath, to whom David fled. His servants identified David as the king of the land and recalled the song about him. Seeing David act insane, Achish rebuked his servants for bringing a madman and refused to let him into his house.

Chapter 27

Achish, son of Maoch and king of Gath, receives David and his household-bearing men, and gives David Ziklag at his request. He believes David's false report of raiding Judah and concludes that Israel abhors David, so David will be his servant forever.

Chapter 29

Achish defends David to the princes, saying he has been with him for years and that he has found no fault in him since he fell away. He tells David he is upright and good in his sight, as an angel of God, but yields to the princes and orders him to leave at first light in peace.
