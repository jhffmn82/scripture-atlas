Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, Jehoshaphat is the king of Judah who does right, allies with Israel, joins Ahab at Ramoth Gilead, loses ships at Ezion Geber, and is succeeded by Jehoram. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Jehoshaphat is the king of Judah who seeks the LORD, strengthens Judah, leads the people in reforms and victory, and dies after a twenty-five-year reign.

Jehoshaphat in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Jehoshaphat first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jehoshaphat, son of Asa and Azubah, was king of Judah, reigning twenty-five years in Jerusalem from the fourth year of Ahab. He did right like Asa and made peace with Israel. He joined Ahab at Ramoth Gilead, insisting on a prophet of the LORD, and was spared when mistaken for Ahab. He lost his ships at Ezion Geber, then died and was buried in David's city, succeeded by Jehoram.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 22

Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, joins Ahab against Ramoth Gilead but asks first to inquire of the LORD and insists on hearing a prophet of the LORD. In battle he is mistaken for Ahab and cries out, and the Syrian captains turn back. His reign of twenty-five years is summarized: he walked in Asa's way, left the high places, made peace with Israel, removed the remaining sodomites, lost his ships at Ezion Geber, refused Ahaziah's offer to sail together, and died, succeeded by Jehoram.

Jehoshaphat in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Jehoshaphat first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jehoshaphat is a king of Judah, son of Asa and Azubah, who became king at thirty-five and reigned twenty-five years in Jerusalem (the text also once calls him king of Israel). He followed his father Asa's example and walked in the first ways of David. He sought the LORD, rejected the Baals, and removed the Asherah poles and high places, though the high places are later said to have remained. His reign begins with strengthening Judah against Israel: he garrisoned the fortified cities and the Ephraim cities Asa had taken, built fortresses and store cities, and kept a large army of mighty men in Jerusalem. He sent princes to teach in Judah's cities and gained great riches and honor. His alliance with Ahab led him to join the war on Ramoth Gilead. He insisted on inquiring of a true prophet of the LORD, was mistaken for Ahab in battle, cried out, and was saved when the LORD helped him. Returning to Jerusalem, he was rebuked by Jehu for helping the wicked, yet Jehu noted he had removed the Asheroth and set his heart to seek God. He then brought the people back to the LORD from Beersheba to the hill country of Ephraim and appointed judges in the fortified cities, charging them to act in the fear of the LORD, with Levites, priests and family heads serving in Jerusalem. When a vast multitude came against him he proclaimed a fast, prayed, urged the people to trust the LORD, and sent singers before the army. Victory brought joy and rest on all sides. Later he allied with Ahaziah in a ship venture to Tarshish, condemned as wicked; the LORD wrecked the ships after Eliezer's rebuke. His acts are recorded in the history of Jehu son of Hanani. He died and was buried in David's city. His son Jehoram, the firstborn, received the kingdom, while his other sons received gifts and fortified cities. Elijah's letter later cites his ways as a standard Jehoram failed to meet.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 17

Jehoshaphat becomes king in place of his father, strengthens himself against Israel, and garrisons the fortified cities of Judah and the cities of Ephraim that Asa had taken. He walks in the first ways of David, seeks the God of his father, rejects the Baals, and removes the high places and Asherah poles. He sends teachers throughout Judah, receives tribute and presents, grows exceedingly great, builds fortresses and store cities, and commands vast numbers of mighty men of valor.

Chapter 18

Jehoshaphat, rich and honored, allied himself with Ahab and went to Samaria, pledging his people to join the war on Ramoth Gilead. He insisted on inquiring for the LORD's word and asked for a true prophet of the LORD, rebuking Ahab's dismissal of Micaiah. In battle, wearing his royal robes at Ahab's urging, he was mistaken for Ahab by the Syrian chariot captains, cried out, and was saved when the LORD helped him and moved them away.

Chapter 19

Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, returned in peace to Jerusalem and was rebuked by Jehu for helping the wicked, though Jehu noted he had removed the Asheroth and set his heart to seek God. He went among the people from Beersheba to the hill country of Ephraim and brought them back to the LORD, then appointed judges in Judah's fortified cities and Levites, priests, and family heads in Jerusalem, charging them to act in the fear of the LORD.

Chapter 20

Jehoshaphat is alarmed by the coming multitude, proclaims a fast, and prays before the assembly, then urges the people to believe the LORD and his prophets and appoints singers to go before the army. After the victory he leads Judah back to Jerusalem with joy and enjoys rest on all sides. His reign of twenty-five years began at thirty-five; he walked in Asa's way, though the high places remained, and he later joined Ahaziah in a ship venture that the LORD wrecked after Eliezer's rebuke.

Chapter 21

Jehoshaphat died and was buried with his fathers in David's city, and his son Jehoram reigned in his place. He had given his other sons great gifts and fortified cities, but gave the kingdom to Jehoram as the firstborn. Elijah's letter later cites his ways as a standard Jehoram failed to follow.
