In The Lamentations of Jeremiah, it is portrayed as the Lord's act in his anger, bringing famine, slaughter, fire, and mourning upon Jerusalem.

Abstract written by a model

The Chaldean king's conquest of Jerusalem: he kills young and old even in the sanctuary, plunders vessels and treasures, burns God's house and palaces, breaks the wall, exiles survivors to Babylon, leaving the land desolate seventy years.

Chapter 36

The king of the Chaldeans kills young and old, even in the sanctuary, and takes all vessels and treasures to Babylon. destruction of the sanctuary in Psalms

author unknown · undated · the work’s page · read it where destruction of the sanctuary first appears

Abstract written by a model

The destruction of the sanctuary is an assault by God's adversaries, who roared in the assembly, set up standards, hacked the carved work with axes, hatchet and hammers, then burned the sanctuary and all worship places, profaning God's Name's dwelling. It leaves perpetual ruins, grounding the speaker's plea.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 74

The destruction is portrayed as an assault in which adversaries roared in the assembly, set up their standards, and cut and broke the carved work with axes, hatchet and hammers. They then burned the sanctuary to the ground, profaned the dwelling place of God's Name, and burned all the places in the land where God was worshiped. It leaves perpetual ruins, and the speaker uses it as the ground for asking why God has rejected his people and for urging him to act.

destruction of Jerusalem in The Lamentations of Jeremiah

author unknown · about 585 BC · the work’s page · read it where destruction of Jerusalem first appears

Abstract written by a model

The destruction of Jerusalem is portrayed as the Lord's act in his anger. Strongholds, palaces, walls, the tabernacle and sanctuary are cast down, with famine, sword slaughter, and a fire devouring Zion's foundations. Prophets' and priests' sins are blamed. Women resort to cannibalism of children, mourning fills Judah, and the people say their end is near and their days fulfilled.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

The destruction includes the casting down of strongholds, palaces, gates, bars, walls, the tabernacle, and the sanctuary, along with famine and slaughter by the sword of youth, old men, virgins, and young men. The text presents it as the Lord's doing in the day of his anger, with no one escaping or remaining, and the speaker grieves it with failing eyes and a troubled heart.

Chapter 4

The destruction is shown through gold dimmed, sanctuary stones poured out at every street corner, and a fire that devoured Zion's foundations. It is attributed to the LORD's wrath and the sins of the prophets and priests. The people say their end is near and their days fulfilled.
