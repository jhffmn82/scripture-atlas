Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Joshua, Jericho is a walled city whose wall falls after the Israelites' shout and which is burned and devoted to destruction. In The Second Book of Kings, Jericho is a stopping point where Elisha heals the city's bad water and barren land.

Jericho in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Jericho first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jericho is a walled city with a king and inhabitants, first viewed by spies sent to it, whose gate is shut at dark. Rahab reports its people's hearts have melted in fear of Israel. The Israelites keep Passover in its plains, and Joshua meets a man with a drawn sword nearby. The city is tightly shut up, the LORD gives it to Joshua, its wall falls flat after the shout, and it is devoted to destruction, burned. Joshua curses anyone who rebuilds it with the loss of firstborn and youngest sons.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Jericho is the city the spies are sent to view, and its gate is shut at dark. Rahab reports that its inhabitants' hearts have melted in fear of Israel, and the spies later report that all the inhabitants of the land melt away before them.

Chapter 5

The Israelites keep the Passover in the plains of Jericho. Joshua is by Jericho when he sees the man with the drawn sword.

Chapter 6

Jericho is tightly shut up, with no one going in or out, because of the children of Israel. The LORD gives it into Joshua's hand, its wall falls flat after the shout, and it is taken, utterly destroyed and burned. Joshua curses anyone who rebuilds it, saying the man will lose his firstborn when laying its foundation and his youngest son when setting up its gates.
