gômêz

substance · 1 work · 8 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: urine

Abstract written by a model

Gômêz is a purifying substance, mixed with ashes and used before or with water. It cleanses clothing, wood, corn, eating vessels, women after still-born delivery, cows that ate corpses, and people who touched or bore corpses. For corpse-bearers it comes from sheep or oxen; human urine is excluded except from the nearest kinsman or kinswoman of the dead. It is used to wash hair and bodies, then water completes cleansing.

gômêz is in one work on this site, Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard VII.

It is used with earth and water to cleanse clothing, wood, corn, eating vessels, a woman with a still-born child, and a cow that has eaten a corpse.

Fargard VIII.

Urine of sheep or oxen is used by corpse-bearers to wash their hair and bodies, while urine of man or woman is excluded except that of the nearest kinsman or nearest kinswoman of the dead. Gômêz is also used in purification before water is applied.

VIII.

Gômêz is used to wash the body of a man who has touched a corpse. The man uses it before rubbing himself dry and, if cleansing by others is unavailable, uses it with water.
