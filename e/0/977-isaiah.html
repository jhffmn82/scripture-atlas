Isaiah in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where Isaiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Isaiah, a prophet and son of Amoz, serves as the LORD's messenger to King Hezekiah. First he reassures Hezekiah's messengers about Assyria, rebukes Sennacherib, and promises the city will be spared. Then he announces Hezekiah's death, returns to declare healing and fifteen added years, applies a fig cake and moves the shadow back. Finally he prophesies that Hezekiah's house will be carried to Babylon.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 19

Isaiah, son of Amoz, receives Hezekiah's messengers and tells them not to be afraid, since the LORD will make the Assyrian king return home and die by the sword. He later sends Hezekiah a longer message of the LORD's answer, including a rebuke of Sennacherib, a sign of recovery over three years, and a promise that Assyria will not take the city.

Chapter 20

Isaiah the prophet, son of Amoz, first delivers the LORD's word that Hezekiah will die, then is sent back to announce healing and fifteen added years. He has a cake of figs applied to the boil and cries to the LORD so the shadow goes back ten steps. He later questions Hezekiah about the Babylonian envoys and prophesies that all in the house will be carried to Babylon and some sons made eunuchs.

Isaiah in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · about 179 BC · the work’s page · read it where Isaiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Isaiah is a great and faithful prophet who, by an excellent spirit, foresaw the future. The Lord delivered the people from Sennacherib's invasion by his hand. He commanded the ways of David that Hezekiah followed, comforted mourners in Zion, and revealed hidden things and events to the end of time. In his days the sun went backward and the king's life was prolonged.

Chapter 48

Isaiah is the prophet by whose hand the Lord delivered the people from Sennacherib's invasion. He is described as great and faithful in his vision, and he commanded the ways of David that Hezekiah followed. By an excellent spirit he saw what would come to pass, comforted those who mourned in Zion, and showed the things that would happen to the end of time and the hidden things before they came; in his days the sun went backward and the king's life was prolonged.

Isaiah in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · 739 to 680 BC · the work’s page · read it where Isaiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Isaiah, son of Amoz, is a prophet and servant of the LORD who saw visions concerning Judah and Jerusalem during the reigns of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah. His story begins with a call: seeing the Lord on a throne, he declares himself undone, a man of unclean lips living among a people of unclean lips. A seraph touches his mouth with a live coal, his iniquity is taken away and sin forgiven, and when asked who will go he volunteers, 'Here I am. Send me!' He is told to deliver a message this people will not understand, and asks 'Lord, how long?' He is then commanded to meet King Ahaz with his son Shearjashub, telling him to keep calm and not fear Rezin and the son of Remaliah, whose plot will not stand. He fathers a son named Maher Shalal Hash Baz, is warned not to walk in the way of this people, seals the law among his disciples, and resolves to wait for the LORD. He and the children given him are signs and wonders in Israel. He also saw the burden of Babylon as a vision. At the LORD's command he walks naked and barefoot for three years as a sign concerning Egypt and Ethiopia. Under Hezekiah he reassures envoys about the Assyrian king, relays the LORD's condemnation of Sennacherib, and promises the city will be defended. He tells Hezekiah he will die, then returns with fifteen more years and a fig poultice for the boil. Finally he questions Hezekiah about visitors from Babylon and announces that his possessions and some of his sons will be taken there. Across the records he is a messenger who obeys, bearing the LORD's word to kings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Isaiah / Chapter 1

Isaiah the son of Amoz is identified as the one who saw the vision concerning Judah and Jerusalem. His vision dates to the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah.

Chapter 6

The narrator sees the Lord on a throne and cries out that he is undone, a man of unclean lips living among a people of unclean lips. After a seraph touches his mouth with a live coal and declares his sin forgiven, he answers the Lord's call with 'Here I am. Send me!' He is told to give this people a message they will not understand and asks 'Lord, how long?'

Chapter 7

Isaiah was commanded by the LORD to meet Ahaz with his son Shearjashub at the end of the conduit of the upper pool, on the highway of the fuller's field. He was to tell Ahaz to keep calm and not fear Rezin and the son of Remaliah, since their plot would not stand.

Chapter 8

The speaker, addressed by the LORD, takes a tablet, has witnesses, goes to the prophetess, and fathers a son who is named Maher Shalal Hash Baz. He is warned with a strong hand not to walk in the way of this people, is told to wrap up the covenant and seal the law among his disciples, and resolves to wait for and look for the LORD. He declares that he and the children the LORD gave him are signs and wonders in Israel.

Chapter 20

Isaiah, son of Amoz, is told by the LORD to loosen the sackcloth from his waist and take off his sandals, and he does so, walking naked and barefoot. The LORD calls him his servant and says he has walked this way three years as a sign and a wonder concerning Egypt and Ethiopia.

Chapter 37

Isaiah, son of Amoz, receives Hezekiah's envoys and tells them not to fear, relaying the LORD's word that the Assyrian king will hear news, return home, and fall by the sword. After Hezekiah prays, Isaiah sends the LORD's longer message condemning Sennacherib, giving a sign of recovery over three years, and promising that the city will be defended.

Chapter 38

Isaiah, the prophet and son of Amoz, delivers the LORD's message to Hezekiah that he will die and should set his house in order. He is then sent back with the LORD's reply that fifteen years will be added to Hezekiah's life. He also directs that a cake of figs be laid as a poultice on the boil so that Hezekiah will recover.

Chapter 39

Isaiah the prophet comes to Hezekiah and asks what the men said, where they came from, and what they saw. He then delivers the LORD's word that all Hezekiah's possessions and some of his sons will be taken to Babylon.
