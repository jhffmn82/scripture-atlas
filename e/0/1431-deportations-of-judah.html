Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, the Babylonian exile/deportation of Judah was the captive removal of Judah’s people from their land by Babylon, with the poorest left behind and Jehoiachin later released from prison. In The Book of Jeremiah, the deportations of Judah were the carrying away of its people by Nebuchadnezzar, after which Jeremiah instructed the captives to settle in Babylon.

Babylonian exile/deportation of Judah in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where Babylonian exile/deportation of Judah first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Babylonian exile of Judah was the captive removal of Judah's people from their land, framed as the LORD's doing. In Nebuchadnezzar's eighth year, after Jehoiachin surrendered, Babylon took temple and palace treasures and deported the king, household, officials, warriors and craftsmen. Nebuzaradan later carried away the remaining people, leaving only the poorest. It closes with Jehoiachin released from prison.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 24

In the eighth year of Nebuchadnezzar's reign, after Jehoiachin surrendered, the king of Babylon took the treasures of the temple and palace and deported the king, his household, officials, mighty men and craftsmen from Jerusalem to Babylon. Only the poorest people remained, and the text frames this as the LORD's doing, as he had said.

Chapter 25

Nebuzaradan carried away the rest of the people left in the city and those who had deserted to Babylon, leaving only some of the poorest of the land. The chapter says Judah was carried away captive out of his land, and it closes with Jehoiachin, in the thirty-seventh year of his captivity, being released from prison.

deportations of Judah in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · 629 to 579 BC · the work’s page · read it where deportations of Judah first appears

Abstract written by a model

The deportations of Judah were the carrying away of its people by Nebuchadnezzar, totaling 4,600: 3,023 Jews in his seventh year, 832 persons from Jerusalem in his eighteenth, and 745 in his twenty-third under Nebuzaradan. Jeremiah instructed the captives to settle in Babylon: build, plant, marry, multiply, pray for the city, and ignore false prophets, diviners, and dreams. The LORD promised to hear them and gather them from all nations.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 29

The captives, including elders, priests, prophets, and people carried off after Jeconiah and the craftsmen departed, are told to build, plant, marry, multiply, and pray for the city. They are warned not to listen to their prophets, diviners, or dreams. They are promised that the LORD will hear them when they seek him with all their heart and will gather them from all nations.

Chapter 52

Three deportations are counted: 3,023 Jews in Nebuchadnezzar's seventh year, 832 persons from Jerusalem in his eighteenth, and 745 in his twenty-third under Nebuzaradan, for a total of four thousand six hundred. The chapter concludes that Judah was carried away captive out of its land.
