Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the Exodus from Egypt is Israel's departure from bondage after 430 years, from Rameses to Succoth, under the LORD's protection and command. In Numbers, the Exodus from Egypt is Israel's bold departure from Rameses after Passover, while the Egyptians buried the firstborn struck by the LORD. In Deuteronomy, the Exodus from Egypt is the LORD's deliverance of Israel through great signs and wonders against Pharaoh, which Israel must remember and recount. In Psalms, the Exodus from Egypt is recalled as the LORD's bringing Israel out of bondage, through the sea and wilderness, with signs, provision, and joy. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), the Exodus from Egypt is Allah's rescue of the Children of Israel from Pharaoh's folk through the sea, where Pharaoh's folk are drowned. In Surah 26: The Poets (Ash-Shu'araa), the Exodus from Egypt begins when Moses is inspired to take Allah's servants away by night while Pharaoh pursues them.

Exodus from Egypt in Exodus

author unknown · about 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Exodus from Egypt first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Exodus from Egypt is Israel's departure from bondage, occurring in the month of Abib at the end of 430 years, to the day. After the firstborn were struck, Israel was thrust out in haste from Rameses to Succoth, carrying unleavened dough, plunder, flocks, herds, and a mixed multitude. It is to be observed as a night to the LORD and remembered with signs. God avoided the Philistine road, leading them by the wilderness toward Etham.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 12

After the striking of the firstborn, Israel is thrust out in haste, taking unprepared dough, plunder, flocks, herds, and a mixed multitude from Rameses to Succoth. It occurs at the end of 430 years, to the day, and is called a night to be much observed to the LORD throughout their generations.

Chapter 13

The departure from Egypt is to be remembered as the day the LORD brought Israel out of the house of bondage by a strong hand, and it is marked by signs on the hand and between the eyes. After Pharaoh let the people go, God avoided the Philistine road and led them around by the wilderness, and they traveled from Succoth to Etham.

Exodus from Egypt in Psalms

author unknown · undated · the work’s page · read it where Exodus from Egypt first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Exodus from Egypt is the LORD's bringing Israel out of bondage, recalled across several chapters. God removed the burden from their shoulders and freed their hands from the basket, and he cites this deliverance as the basis for forbidding foreign gods. It began with striking the Egyptian firstborn and bringing Israel out with a strong hand and outstretched arm. The people left with silver and gold, with no feeble person among the tribes, while Egypt was glad at their departure. The sea was divided, Israel passed through, and Pharaoh's army was overthrown. The LORD then led them through the wilderness with a cloud, fire at night, quails, bread of the sky and water from the rock, bringing them out with joy and singing. Judah became his sanctuary and Israel his dominion, and the sea, Jordan and mountains reacted.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 81

God recalls that he went out over the land of Egypt, removed the burden from his people's shoulder and freed their hands from the basket. He identifies himself as the LORD who brought Israel up out of Egypt, using this as the basis for forbidding foreign gods and inviting Israel to open its mouth for him to fill. The speaker also says he heard a language he did not know when this happened.

Chapter 105

The LORD brought his people out with silver and gold, and not one feeble person was among the tribes. Egypt was glad at their departure, and the LORD covered them with a cloud and gave fire at night. He provided quails, bread of the sky, and water from the opened rock, bringing his chosen out with joy and singing.

Chapter 114

The going out of Israel from Egypt, from a people of foreign language, is the event that begins the chapter. Judah became God's sanctuary and Israel his dominion, and the sea, Jordan, mountains and hills reacted to it.

Chapter 136

The Exodus is told through God striking the Egyptian firstborn and bringing Israel out with a strong hand and outstretched arm. It continues with the division of the Red Sea, Israel's passage, the overthrow of Pharaoh's army, and the leading of the people through the wilderness.
