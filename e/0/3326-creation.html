Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Solomon, creation serves as God's obedient servant and weapon, is made new to protect his servants, and exchanges the roles of its elements and creatures. In Paul’s Letter to the Colossians, creation includes all visible and invisible things, which were created by, through, and for the Son and are held together in him. In Revelation, every created thing offers blessing, honor, glory, and dominion forever to him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb.

creation in The Wisdom of Solomon

author unknown · about 99 BC · the work’s page · read it where creation first appears

Abstract written by a model

Creation is portrayed as God's obedient servant, wholesome and free of destructive poison. In Chapter 5 the whole creation becomes the Lord's weapon, fighting alongside him against his frenzied foes, with sea and rivers overwhelming the wicked. In Chapter 19 it is made new, each part in its kind, complying with God's commands to keep his servants unharmed. Like lute notes changing rhythm, elements swap roles: land and water creatures exchange habitats, fire persists in water, and flames spare creatures and food.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

The whole creation is made the Lord's weapons to punish his enemies, and the universe goes with him to fight against his frenzied foes. The sea is angered against the wicked and rivers sternly overwhelm them.

Chapter 19

The whole creation, each part in its diverse kind, was made new again, complying with God's commandments so his servants would be kept unharmed. The elements are compared to the notes of a lute that change the rhythm while the sound continues: land creatures became water creatures and swimmers moved on land, fire kept its power in water, and water forgot its quenching nature. Flames did not consume the flesh of perishable creatures walking among them, nor melt the ambrosial food that melted easily.
