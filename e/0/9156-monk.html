Abstract written by a model

A monk is an ascetic who enters religious life before old age, renounces wealth, possessions, desire, aversion, and violence, and seeks final liberation. He knows the law and the world with right comprehension, is righteous, self-possessed, wise, just, chaste, and properly aware of sensual perceptions. He leads a mendicant life by collecting alms from all families and preaching the law to all creatures without injuring any being, including himself. He avoids killing and lying, practices carefulness and control, follows the commandment and, if not yet discriminating, follows the âkârya while examining his conduct. He patiently bears unpleasant feelings, heat and cold, pleasure and pain, hardship, abuse, injury, and reviling, regarding mistreatment as connected with former sins. Through equanimity, abstinence, and conquest of obstructions to righteousness, he abandons worldliness.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Second Lecture, Called Conquest of the World. / First Lesson. .. Sixth Lesson.

The wise man should recognize the proper moment for religious life, avoid violence, reject hope and desire, and practice control. He should renounce possessions and worldly attachment while seeking final liberation.

Third Lecture, Called Hot and Cold. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson.

The sage understands the world, the law, the connection of births and sensations, and the misery caused by action. The sage remains equanimous toward heat and cold, pleasure and pain, and conquers obstructions to righteousness.

Fifth Lecture, Called Essence of the World. / First Lesson. .. Sixth Lesson.

A monk should bear unpleasant feelings patiently, avoid killing and lying, practice carefulness, and follow the commandment. A monk who has not reached discrimination should follow the âkârya, examine his conduct, and guard himself against sensuality and harm to living beings.

Sixth Lecture, Called the Cleaning. / First Lesson. .. Fifth Lesson.

A monk who understands the law and leads a life of abstinence controls himself and bears hardships patiently. He may be abused, struck, hurt, or reviled, but he accounts for such treatment by his former sins.
