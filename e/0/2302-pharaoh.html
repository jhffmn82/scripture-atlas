Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Pharaoh rules Egypt, interacts with Abram and Joseph, manages the famine, grants Jacob’s family Goshen, and permits Joseph to bury his father. In Surah 11: Hud, Pharaoh commands and leads his people and leads them to the Fire on the Day of Resurrection. In Surah 12: Joseph (Yusuf), the ruler of Egypt purchases Joseph, investigates the accusation against him, and appoints him over the storehouses of the land. In Surah 54: The Moon (Al-Qamar), Pharaoh’s house denies the revelations and is grasped by the Lord. In Surah 89: The Dawn (Al-Fajr), Pharaoh is described as firm of might, rebellious, and someone who multiplied iniquity in the lands.

Pharaoh in Genesis

author unknown · about 449 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pharaoh first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pharaoh is the king of Egypt, who rules the land and has servants and officers, including a chief cup bearer and chief baker. He ranks above Joseph only in the throne. Early on he takes Sarai into his house, deals well with Abram for her sake, then suffers plagues sent by the LORD, rebukes Abram, and sends him away with an escort. Later he imprisons his cup bearer and baker in anger, then on his birthday restores the cup bearer and hangs the baker. Troubled by two dreams that no magician or wise man can interpret, he summons Joseph, accepts his interpretation, and sets him over Egypt with a signet ring, linen robes, gold chain, new name, and wife Asenath. In the famine he directs Egyptians to Joseph. Pleased that Joseph's brothers have come, he sends wagons for Jacob's family and promises them the good of the land. He grants them Goshen, asks Jacob his age, and is blessed by him. Through Joseph's actions he owns all Egypt's land except the priests' and receives a fifth of harvests. Finally he lets Joseph go bury his father.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 12

Pharaoh takes Sarai into his house after his princes praise her and deals well with Abram for her sake. After the LORD afflicts him and his house with plagues, he rebukes Abram for not saying she was his wife, tells him to take her and go, and commands men to escort Abram away.

Chapter 40

Pharaoh, called king of Egypt, becomes angry with his chief cup bearer and chief baker and puts them in custody. On his birthday he holds a feast for his servants, restores the cup bearer to his position and hangs the baker.

Chapter 41

Pharaoh dreams twice, is troubled, and finds that no magician or wise man can interpret the dreams. He summons Joseph, recounts the dreams, and accepts Joseph's interpretation and advice, then sets Joseph over all the land of Egypt, giving him his signet ring, fine linen robes, a gold chain, a new name, and Asenath as wife. During the famine he tells the Egyptians to go to Joseph and do what he says.

Chapter 45

Pharaoh and his servants are pleased to hear that Joseph's brothers have come. He tells Joseph to have them bring their father and households in wagons, promising them the good and the fat of the land of Egypt, and says they need not worry about their belongings.

Chapter 46

Pharaoh had sent wagons to carry Jacob and his family to Egypt. Joseph plans to tell him that his brothers and father's house have come, and expects Pharaoh to summon them and ask their occupation.

Chapter 47

Pharaoh questions Joseph's brothers about their occupation, grants them the land of Goshen, and tells Joseph to put any able men among them in charge of his livestock. He asks Jacob his age and is blessed by him twice. Through Joseph's actions he comes to own all the land of Egypt except the priests' land, and is entitled to a fifth of the harvests.

Chapter 50

Pharaoh receives Joseph's request through his staff and tells Joseph to go up and bury his father just as he swore. His servants, the elders of his house and the elders of Egypt go up with Joseph.
