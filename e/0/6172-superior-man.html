superior man

Also called: The superior man

Abstract written by a model

The superior man learns deeply by the proper course, preserves benevolence and propriety, loves and respects others, and examines himself when treated perversely. He does not contend with a brute and does not personally teach his son because their relationship forbids it. His life-long anxiety concerns his moral example, not one morning's calamity.

Chapter 18

The superior man is described as not personally teaching his son because the circumstances of the relationship forbid it.

Part II / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 33

The superior man learns deeply and by the proper course, internalizes what he learns, and relies on it firmly. He preserves benevolence and propriety, examines himself when others act wrongly, and is concerned with becoming like Shun rather than with temporary calamity.
