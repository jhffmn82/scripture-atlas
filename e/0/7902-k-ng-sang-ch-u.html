Abstract written by a model

Kêng Sang Ch'u, also called Kêng Saṅg Tzŭ, is Lao Tzŭ’s sole disciple said to have attained TAO. Living in the northern Wei-lei Mountains, he benefits Wei-lei by dismissing clever or conventionally charitable attendants and keeping the useless and incompetent. When inhabitants seek to honour him as Sage or tutelary god, he feels shame before Lao Tzŭ. He teaches Nan Yung Ch'u to preserve life, then directs him to Lao Tzŭ for further instruction.

CHAPTER XXIII.

Kêng Sang Ch'u, who alone among Lao Tzŭ's disciples had attained TAO, benefited the district of Wei-lei by dismissing the systematically clever and conventionally charitable. When the people wished to honour him as a Sage or tutelary god, he felt ashamed, believing this showed that he had departed from Lao Tzŭ's doctrine. He later told Nan Yung Ch'u to preserve his form, secure his vitality, and avoid anxious thoughts, but directed him to Lao Tzŭ for further instruction.
