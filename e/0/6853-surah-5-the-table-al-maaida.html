Abstract written by a model

Surah 5, The Table (Al-Maaida), revealed at Medina, gives believers commands on worship, lawful living, justice, and relations with others. It opens by requiring fulfilment of indentures and permits good foods, the food of those who received the Scripture, and marriage to their virtuous women when marriage portions are given and honour is maintained. It says Allah has perfected the religion and chosen al-Islam, while warning that denial of faith makes works vain. It forbids specified foods, sin, and transgression. It directs believers to purify themselves before prayer by washing, with an accommodation for sickness, travel, or lack of water, emphasizing that Allah seeks purification rather than hardship. The surah recalls covenants with the Children of Israel and with those who say they are Christians, presenting the Torah, Gospel, and Scripture as guidance and light. It recounts Moses and his people refusing to enter the holy land, and the tale of the two sons of Adam. It describes the sending, rejection, and killing of messengers, rejects claims that Allah is the Messiah or one of three, and identifies the Messiah as a messenger and Mary as a saintly woman. Jesus’ signs, his disavowal of worship, and the disciples’ request for a table spread are recounted. Further instructions cover oaths, pilgrimage, hunting, intoxicants, games of chance, testimony, and obedience. The ending contrasts forgiveness and Gardens for believers who do good works with punishment for disbelievers and wrongdoers, while rewarding the truthful.

Surah 5: The Table (Al-Maaida), revealed at Medina, concerns instructions to believers about lawful food, pilgrimage, worship, and conduct toward others. It states that Allah has perfected the religion, completed His favour, and chosen al-Islam as religion. It permits the food of those who received the Scripture and marriage to their virtuous women under stated conditions. It forbids various foods and condemns sin, transgression, and denial of faith.

6 O ye who believe! When ye rise up for prayer, wash you faces, and your hands up to the elbows, and lightly rub your heads and (wash) your feet up to the ankles. And if ye are unclean, purify yourselves. And if ye are sick or on a journey, or one of you cometh from the closet, or ye have had contact with women, and ye find not water, then go to clean, high ground and rub your faces and your hands with some of it. Allah would not place a burden on you, but He would purify you and would perfect His grace upon you, that ye may give thanks.

This unit concerns Allah's commands about purification, prayer, justice, faith, repentance, and conduct toward others. It recounts Allah's covenants with the Children of Israel and those who say they are Christians, and describes the Torah, the Gospel, and the Scripture as sources of guidance and light. It recounts Moses and his people's refusal to enter the holy land, and the tale of the two sons of Adam. It warns the disbelievers and wrongdoers of punishment while promising forgiveness and Gardens to those who believe and do good works.

The text rejects claims that Allah is the Messiah or one of three, identifies the Messiah as a messenger and Mary as a saintly woman, and describes Jesus’ signs and his disavowal of being worshipped. It instructs believers about lawful food, oaths, pilgrimage, hunting, intoxicants, games of chance, testimony, and obedience. It also describes the disciples’ request for a table spread with food, the questioning of Jesus, and the rewards of the truthful and the punishment of disbelievers.
