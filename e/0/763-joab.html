Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Samuel, Joab serves as David’s captain and commander of the army, leading military campaigns and taking decisive actions in David’s conflicts. In The First Book of Kings, Joab serves as captain of the army, supports Adonijah, is condemned for killing Abner and Amasa, and is executed on Solomon’s order. In 1 Chronicles, Joab serves as David’s captain and commander, leading the army, conquering enemies, conducting the census, and fathering Ge Harashim.

Joab in The Second Book of Samuel

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Joab first appears

Abstract written by a model

Joab is a man, son of Zeruiah and brother of Asahel and Abishai, who serves King David as captain and commander of the army, leading David's servants, the mighty men, and eventually all the army of Israel. He carries a sword on a sash and has followers of his own. The records show him as a capable, ruthless, and sometimes defiant commander. His story begins at the pool of Gibeon, where he leads David's servants against Abner's party. After Asahel is killed by Abner, Joab and Abishai pursue Abner, but Joab blows the trumpet to stop the fighting when Abner appeals to him. He buries Asahel and travels all night to Hebron. Later he deceives Abner into returning and strikes him dead in the gate to avenge Asahel, and David curses his house. Sent by David, he defeats the Syrians, then leads the army against the Ammonites. He follows David's letter by placing Uriah where valiant men are, sends word of Uriah's death, and captures Rabbah, the royal city, urging David to come take it so that Joab's name is not given to it. He arranges for the woman of Tekoa to move the king, and brings Absalom back from Geshur. After Absalom has his barley field burned, Joab carries Absalom's plea to the king. In the rebellion, he leads a third of the army and thrusts three darts into Absalom's heart despite the king's command, then stops the pursuit and manages who carries the news. He confronts David over his weeping, after which David says he will make Amasa captain in his place. Joab then kills Amasa with a sword while greeting him as a brother, leads the pursuit of Sheba, besieges Abel of Beth Maacah, ends the siege when the wise woman offers Sheba's head, and is named over all the army of Israel. Finally, he objects to David's census but is overruled, leads the count, and reports 800,000 valiant men in Israel and 500,000 in Judah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

10 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Joab, son of Zeruiah, led David's servants to meet Abner's party at the pool of Gibeon and accepted Abner's proposal for the young men to compete. He, with Abishai, pursued Abner after Asahel's death, then blew the trumpet to stop the fighting when Abner appealed to him. He gathered the people, found nineteen men and Asahel missing, and traveled all night to Hebron after burying Asahel.

Chapter 3

Joab returns from a raid with plunder, tells David that Abner came to deceive him, and secretly sends messengers to bring Abner back. He takes Abner aside in the gate and strikes him dead for the blood of his brother Asahel, and David curses his house.

Chapter 10

Joab was sent by David with the army of mighty men and, seeing the battle set before and behind, took the choicest Israelite men against the Syrians. He urged courage and mutual help, made the Syrians flee, and returned to Jerusalem.

Chapter 11

Joab leads the army against the Ammonites, sends Uriah to David when asked, and on receiving David's letter assigns Uriah to where valiant men are. He then sends a messenger with instructions on how to report Uriah's death to David, and David tells him not to be displeased and to press the attack.

Chapter 12

Joab fights against Rabbah and takes the royal city, the city of waters. He sends messengers urging David to gather the rest of the people and take the city himself, lest Joab's name be given to it.

Chapter 14

Joab perceives the king's heart is toward Absalom and arranges for the woman of Tekoa to speak to the king, putting the words in her mouth. When the king grants the request, Joab thanks him, goes to Geshur, and brings Absalom to Jerusalem. He later ignores Absalom's two summons, has his barley field burned by Absalom's servants, then comes to Absalom and carries his plea to the king.

Chapter 18

Joab led one third of the army and was told by a man that Absalom hung in an oak. He rebuked the man for not killing him, then himself thrust three darts into Absalom's heart despite the king's command. He then blew the trumpet to stop the pursuit, refused Ahimaaz the errand of bearing news that day, and sent the Cushite instead, though he finally let Ahimaaz run too.

Chapter 19

Joab is told of the king's weeping and confronts David, saying he has shamed his servants who saved his life and swearing that no man will stay with him that night unless he goes out to comfort them. David then declares he will make Amasa captain of the army instead of Joab.

Chapter 20

Joab, with Abishai his brother, leads the pursuit of Sheba. He greets Amasa as a brother, takes him by the beard, and kills him with a sword, after which the people follow him. He besieges Abel of Beth Maacah, accepts the wise woman's offer to deliver Sheba's head, blows the trumpet to end the siege, and returns to Jerusalem; he is named over all the army of Israel.

Chapter 24

Joab, captain of the army, objects to the census, asking why the king delights in this thing, but the king's word prevails over him and the captains. He leads the count across the land in nine months and twenty days and reports 800,000 valiant men in Israel and 500,000 in Judah.

Joab in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Joab first appears

Abstract written by a model

Joab, son of Zeruiah and captain of the army, followed Adonijah rather than Absalom and killed Abner and Amasa in peacetime. David cites these killings; Joab flees to the altar, refuses to leave, is killed by Benaiah on Solomon's order, and is buried at home.

Chapter 2

David names Joab's killing of Abner and Amasa as a reason he should not die in peace. Having followed Adonijah, Joab flees to the LORD's Tent and holds the altar's horns, refuses to come out, and is killed there by Benaiah on Solomon's order. He is buried in his own house in the wilderness.

Joab in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Joab first appears

Abstract written by a model

Joab is the son of Zeruiah and brother of Abishai (and Asahel), serving David as captain and commander of the king's army. He first appears going up first against the Jebusites, earning the title of chief as David had promised, and repairing the rest of the city. Later he leads the whole army of mighty men, dividing forces with Abishai, urging courage and trust in the LORD's will; the Syrians flee before him. He then wastes Ammon's country, besieges Rabbah, and overthrows it. When David orders a census, Joab protests, asking why Israel should be made guilty, but the king's word prevails and he carries it out. He reports the totals, omitting Levi and Benjamin because the command was abominable to him. The census is left unfinished, and wrath comes on Israel. He is also the father of Ge Harashim, whose people were craftsmen.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Joab the son of Zeruiah goes up first against the Jebusites and is made chief, as David had promised to whoever struck them first. He repairs the rest of the city. He is also named as the brother of Abishai and of Asahel, and Naharai the Berothite is his armor bearer.

Chapter 19

Joab was sent by David with the whole army of mighty men. Facing battle lines before and behind, he took choice men of Israel against the Syrians and gave the rest to his brother Abishai against the Ammonites, urging courage and trust in the LORD's will. The Syrians fled before him, and he then came to Jerusalem.

Chapter 20

Joab led out the army at the time when kings go out, wasted the country of the children of Ammon, and besieged Rabbah. He struck Rabbah and overthrew it.

Chapter 21

Joab protests the census, asking why David would cause guilt for Israel, but the king's word prevails and he carries it out across Israel. He reports the totals to David, leaving out Levi and Benjamin because the king's word was abominable to him.

Chapter 27

Joab the son of Zeruiah began a census but did not finish it, and wrath came on Israel for this. He is also named as captain of the king's army, and his brother Asahel is captain of the fourth month's division.
