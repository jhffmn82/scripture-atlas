superior man

person · 1 work · 32 quoted facts · 11 connections
Also called: The superior man, a superior man, superior men, the superior man

Abstract written by a model

The superior man is a person whose presence transforms and spiritually influences others toward goodness, often without their knowing the cause. He mends society and, while residing in a country, guides its sovereign toward tranquillity, wealth, and glory; his instruction also shapes the young into filial, obedient, true-hearted, and faithful people. His three delights are family security, freedom from shame before Heaven and people, and finding, teaching, and nourishing talented individuals. Though he may desire wide territory, numerous people, and the ability to tranquillize those within the four seas, his highest enjoyment lies in qualities rooted in his own nature, which endure in poverty and retirement and manifest throughout his body. He has counsels, does not eat the bread of idleness, and cannot be retained through empty demonstrations of respect. His teaching unfolds in five ways: timely influence, perfecting virtue, developing talents, answering inquiries, and encouraging private cultivation and correction. Finally, he models the path: drawing the bow without discharging the arrow, seeming to leap with it toward the mark, standing exactly in the middle for those able to follow.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

Wherever the superior man passes through, transformation follows, and wherever he abides, his influence is spiritual. His influence advances people toward goodness without their knowing who causes it.

Chapter 20

The superior man delights in having both parents alive and brothers without cause for anxiety, being free from shame before Heaven and people, and finding, teaching, and nourishing the most talented individuals in the kingdom.

Chapter 21

The superior man may desire wide territory and a numerous people, and may delight in tranquillizing the people within the four seas. His highest enjoyment lies instead in the qualities rooted in his nature, which remain undiminished in poverty and retirement and manifest throughout his body.

Chapter 32

A superior man, when residing in a country, can guide its sovereign toward tranquillity, wealth, and glory. Through his instructions, the young can become filial, obedient to their elders, true-hearted, and faithful.

Chapter 37

A superior man may not be retained by empty demonstrations of honouring and respecting.

Chapter 40

The superior man teaches through timely influence, perfecting virtue, developing talents, answering inquiries, and encouraging private self-cultivation and correction.

Chapter 41

The superior man draws the bow but does not discharge the arrow, having seemed to leap with it to the mark. He stands exactly in the middle of the path, and those who are able follow him.
