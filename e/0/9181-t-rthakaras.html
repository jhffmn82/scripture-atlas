Tîrthakaras

group · 4 works · 11 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Tîrthakara, former Tîrthakaras, the Tîrthakaras, the first Tîrthakara, the last of the Tîrthakaras

Abstract written by a model

In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I, the Tîrthakaras are great heroes whose teaching is learned through various means, whose commandment must not be broken, and who became perfected through former religious hardships. In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, the Tîrthakaras proclaim Law as a place of safety, serve as the world’s eyes and leaders, teach a salutary path, and declare the world eternal. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, the Tîrthakaras use superior knowledge to establish the Law, introduce outward religious marks, and teach that knowledge, faith, and right conduct lead to final liberation. In Kalpa Sutra, the Tîrthakaras are perfectly enlightened guides and benefactors, unfolding through twenty-three earlier figures to Mahâvîra, their last.

Abstract written by a model

The Tîrthakaras are great heroes whose teaching is learned through an âkârya, innate knowledge, highest instruction, or others. Their commandment must not be broken. In former years they walked long and bore religious troubles, becoming perfected in knowledge and physically lean.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Fifth Lecture, Called Essence of the World. / First Lesson. .. Sixth Lesson.

The teaching of the Tîrthakaras is learned through the teaching of the âkârya, innate knowledge, instruction from the highest, or hearing it from others. The wise person should not break their commandment.

Sixth Lecture, Called the Cleaning. / First Lesson. .. Fifth Lesson.

The Tîrthakaras walked for a long time in former years and bore the troubles of religious life. Endowed with perfect knowledge, they had lean arms and very little flesh and blood.

the Tîrthakaras in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

Hermann Jacobi · the work’s page · read it where the Tîrthakaras first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Tîrthakaras use superior knowledge to establish what carries out the Law, introduce outward marks for religious recognition, and teach knowledge, faith, and right conduct lead to final liberation.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Twenty-Third Lecture.

The Tîrthakaras fixed what is necessary for carrying out the Law by their superior knowledge. They introduced various outward marks so religious people could be recognised, while teaching that knowledge, faith, and right conduct cause final liberation.
