T'ang

person · 2 works · 14 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Emperor T'ang, the Emperor T'ang, your Majesty

Abstract written by a model

In Chuang Tzŭ, T'ang tells Chi of the Leviathan and Rukh to distinguish small from great, consults Pien Sui and Wu Kuang, attacks and defeats Chieh with I Yin, gains the empire, appoints Mên Yin Têng Hêng, and offers the empire to Wu Kuang. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XX (Yao Yueh), T'ang, the child Li, announces before God using a dark-coloured victim, refuses to pardon sinners or conceal God's ministers, assumes responsibility for the people's offences, and refuses to blame them for offences committed by himself.

Abstract written by a model

T'ang tells Chi of the Leviathan and Rukh to distinguish small from great. After consulting Pien Sui and Wu Kuang, he attacks and defeats Chieh with I Yin, gains the empire, appoints Mên Yin Têng Hêng, and offers the empire to Wu Kuang.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER I.

Emperor T'ang tells Chi about the Leviathan and the Rukh to illustrate the difference between small and great.

CHAPTER XXVIII.

T'ang consulted Pien Sui and Wu Kuang before attacking Chieh, then took I Yin into his counsels and defeated Chieh. He later offered the empire to Pien Sui and Wu Kuang, but both declined.

T'ang in The Analects of Confucius, Book XX (Yao Yueh)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where T'ang first appears

Abstract written by a model

T'ang, the child Li, announces before God using a dark-coloured victim. He will not pardon sinners or conceal God's ministers, assumes responsibility for the people's offences, and refuses to blame them for offences committed by himself.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. I. 1. Yao said, 'Oh! you, Shun, the Heaven-determined

T'ang announces before God that he does not dare pardon the sinner and that God's ministers are not kept in obscurity. He says that offences committed by him are not to be attributed to the people, while offences committed by the people rest on his person.
