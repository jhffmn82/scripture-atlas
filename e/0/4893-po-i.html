Po-i

person · 3 works · 6 quoted facts · 3 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book V (Kung-Ye Ch'Ang), Po-i did not keep former wickednesses of men in mind, so few resentments were directed towards him. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XVI (Ke She), Po-i died of hunger at the foot of Shau-yang mountain and remained praised by the people down to the present time. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XVIII: Wei Tsze, Po-i retired to privacy from the world and refused to surrender his will or submit to any taint in his person.
