Abstract written by a model

A woodcutter fells a tree, strips it, makes a useful vessel for life's needs, and burns the scraps to cook and eat. From a discarded crooked, knotty piece he idly carves an idol, paints it red, fixes it in a wall with iron, and shamelessly prays to it for goods, marriage, children, health, journey, and profit.

Chapter 13

The woodcutter saws down a tree, strips its bark, and makes a useful vessel for his life's needs, then burns the scraps to cook and eat his fill. He takes a discarded, crooked, knotty piece, carves it in idleness into the image of a man or worthless animal, paints it red, sets it in a wall with iron, and prays to it without shame.
