King Wu

person · 5 works · 14 quoted facts · 11 connections
Also called: Wu, king Wu

Abstract written by a model

In The Doctrine of the Mean, King Wu continues the enterprises and wishes of King T'ai, King Chi, and King Wan, gains the kingdom and royal throne, establishes ancestral sacrifices maintained by descendants, and embodies far-reaching filial piety through carrying forward his forefathers’ undertakings. In Mencius, Book I (Liang Hui Wang), King Wu, valourous and ashamed of violence and disorder, acts when the people are pleased, and one display of his anger gives repose to all the people of the kingdom. In Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong), King Wu proceeds against those rejecting Zhou, punishes Shang, gives tranquillity to their people by rescuing them from fire and water, and seizes and destroys oppressors. In Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou), Wu is aided by Zhou, whose actions drive the people toward Wu. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), Wu makes benevolence and righteousness his own.
