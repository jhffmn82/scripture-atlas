contention

Also called: a great contention, all the wars, and contentions, anger, contentions, disobedience, disputations, hasty fighting, quarreling, strife, the spirit of contention, the stoutness of the strife, wars and contentions

Abstract written by a model

In Proverbs, strife is portrayed as worse than a feast, begins like breaching a dam, is loved by those who love disobedience, and separates friends through repetition. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, strife is a kind of contention to abstain from, since abstaining diminishes sins; passionate men kindle it, and hasty quarrels spark fires and bloodshed. In The Book of Mosiah, contentions can arise when kings are replaced or dethroned, causing much bloodshed among the people. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, contention is a spirit of the devil among disciples that stirs angry conflict, which Jesus Christ declares is not his doctrine and should be done away.

Abstract written by a model

Strife is portrayed as worse than a feast, since quiet with a dry morsel is better. It begins like breaching a dam, so it should be stopped early; it is loved by those who love disobedience, and repetition separates friends.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 17

Strife is said to be worse than a feast, since a dry morsel with quietness is better than a feasting house full of it. Its beginning is likened to breaching a dam, so contention should be stopped before quarreling breaks out. Those who love disobedience love strife, and repeating a matter separates close friends.
