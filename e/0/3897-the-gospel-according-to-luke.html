The Gospel According to Luke

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This document is the Gospel of Luke, a 24-chapter narrative that opens with a preface to Theophilus and follows Jesus from before his birth to his ascension. Its central figure is Jesus; others who matter most include John the Baptizer, Mary, Peter and the twelve apostles, the Pharisees and scribes who oppose him, Judas, and Pilate. Chapters 1-2 tell of the angel Gabriel announcing the births of John to Zacharias and Elizabeth and of Jesus to Mary. Jesus is born in Bethlehem, welcomed by shepherds, Simeon and Anna, and at twelve is found among the teachers in the temple. Chapters 3-4 present John's baptism of repentance, Jesus' baptism with a voice from the sky calling him beloved Son, his genealogy back to Adam, and his temptation in the wilderness. He is then rejected at Nazareth and begins teaching and healing at Capernaum. Chapters 5-9 show his Galilean ministry: calling Simon, James, John and Levi, healing the leper and the paralytic, disputes over fasting and the Sabbath, choosing the twelve, and teaching blessings and woes. He heals a centurion's servant, raises a widow's son, and forgives a sinful woman. He teaches the parable of the sower, calms a storm, frees the Gadarene man, and raises Jairus' daughter. He sends out the twelve, feeds five thousand, and hears Peter's confession of him as the Christ. He foretells his suffering, is transfigured, and sets his face toward Jerusalem. Chapters 10-18 mostly occur on the journey. Jesus sends out seventy and tells the parable of the good Samaritan. He visits Martha and Mary, teaches prayer, and answers accusations of acting by Beelzebul. He pronounces woes on the Pharisees, warns against covetousness and anxiety, and calls people to repent. He laments over Jerusalem and teaches discipleship and humility. The parables of the lost sheep, coin and son, the shrewd manager, and Lazarus and the rich man follow, as do teachings on faith, the ten lepers, the Kingdom's coming, persistent prayer, the Pharisee and tax collector, and the rich ruler. Chapters 19-21 bring him to Jericho, where he meets Zacchaeus, and then into Jerusalem. He rides in as King, weeps over the city, cleanses the temple, and debates authority, taxes and resurrection with the leaders. He praises the widow's gift and foretells the temple's destruction and the coming of the Son of Man. Chapters 22-24 narrate the climax. Judas betrays Jesus, who shares the Passover meal, prays on the Mount of Olives, is arrested, and is denied by Peter. Pilate and Herod examine him, Pilate yields to the crowd, and Jesus is crucified, dies, and is buried by Joseph of Arimathaea. On the first day of the week the women find the tomb empty. Jesus appears to the Emmaus disciples and the eleven, opens the Scriptures to them, commissions them as witnesses, and is carried up into heaven.

Chapter 1

The chapter opens with a preface addressed to Theophilus, then tells of Zacharias, a priest, and his barren wife Elizabeth, who are told by the angel Gabriel in the temple that they will have a son named John. Because Zacharias doubts, he is made mute until the child's birth. Gabriel is then sent to Mary, a virgin in Nazareth pledged to Joseph, announcing she will conceive Jesus, the Son of the Most High, by the Holy Spirit. Mary visits Elizabeth, who blesses her, and Mary speaks a song of praise to God. John is born, Zacharias writes his name and regains speech, and he prophesies about God's salvation and John's role before the Lord.

Chapter 2

Chapter 2 tells how, under a decree from Caesar Augustus, Joseph and the pregnant Mary travel from Nazareth to Bethlehem, where Mary gives birth to her firstborn son and lays him in a feeding trough. An angel of the Lord announces the birth to shepherds, a multitude of the heavenly army praises God, and the shepherds visit the child and spread the news. After the circumcision and naming of Jesus, his parents present him at the temple in Jerusalem, where Simeon and the prophetess Anna recognize him and Simeon speaks to Mary of a sword piercing her soul. The family returns to Nazareth, and at twelve Jesus stays behind in Jerusalem, where his parents find him after three days among the teachers in the temple and he speaks of being in his Father's house. He returns with them to Nazareth, is subject to them, and increases in wisdom, stature, and favor with God and men.

Chapter 3

In the fifteenth year of Tiberius Caesar, the word of God comes to John, son of Zacharias, in the wilderness, and he preaches a baptism of repentance around the Jordan, in fulfillment of Isaiah's words. John warns the multitudes to produce fruits worthy of repentance and gives practical instructions to crowds, tax collectors, and soldiers. He tells the people that one mightier than he will baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire, and Herod the tetrarch, reproved by John, shuts him up in prison. Jesus is baptized and, while praying, sees the Holy Spirit descend like a dove as a voice from the sky calls him beloved Son. The chapter closes with a genealogy of Jesus, about thirty years old, tracing back through David, Abraham, and Noah to Adam, the son of God.

Chapter 4

Jesus, full of the Holy Spirit, is led into the wilderness, where he fasts forty days and resists the devil's three temptations by quoting Scripture. He returns to Galilee and teaches in synagogues, then reads Isaiah's prophecy in the synagogue at Nazareth and says it is fulfilled in their hearing. The Nazareth crowd, angered by his words about Elijah and Elisha and the rejection of prophets in their hometown, tries to throw him off a cliff, but he passes through them and leaves. At Capernaum he teaches with authority, casts out an unclean spirit from a man in the synagogue, heals Simon's mother-in-law and many others, and silences demons who name him the Christ. When crowds try to keep him, he says he must preach God's Kingdom in other cities as well.

Chapter 5

In Chapter 5, Jesus teaches the crowd from Simon's boat by the lake of Gennesaret and directs Simon to let down his nets, producing a huge catch; Simon, James, and John then leave everything to follow him. Jesus cleanses a man full of leprosy, then heals a paralyzed man lowered through a roof, declaring his sins forgiven, which prompts the scribes and Pharisees to accuse him of blasphemy. He calls the tax collector Levi, who leaves everything and hosts a feast, where the Pharisees and their scribes complain that Jesus eats with sinners. Asked why his disciples do not fast, Jesus answers with the image of the bridegroom and the parables of the garment and wineskins.

Chapter 6

Chapter 6 opens with Jesus' disciples plucking grain on the Sabbath, which draws a challenge from some Pharisees; Jesus answers by citing David and declares the Son of Man lord of the Sabbath. On another Sabbath he heals a man's withered right hand in the synagogue while the scribes and Pharisees watch for an accusation, and they are filled with rage. After a night of prayer on the mountain, Jesus chooses twelve apostles from his disciples and names them, then comes down to a level place where a great crowd gathers to hear him and be healed. There he teaches his disciples with blessings and woes, commands love for enemies, mercy, giving, and not judging, and warns about blind guides, specks and beams, and trees known by their fruit. He closes by asking why people call him 'Lord, Lord' without doing what he says, and contrasts the man who builds on rock with the one who builds without a foundation.

Chapter 7

In Capernaum, Jesus heals a centurion's dying servant from a distance after the centurion, who sent Jewish elders and friends to speak for him, shows faith in Jesus' word, and Jesus praises that faith. At Nain, Jesus raises a widow's only son from his coffin, and the crowd glorifies God and spreads the report throughout Judea. John the Baptizer sends two disciples to ask whether Jesus is the one who is coming, and Jesus points to his works and then tells the crowds that John is a prophet and more than a prophet, while criticizing the generation that rejects both John and himself. At the house of a Pharisee named Simon, a sinful woman weeps on Jesus' feet and anoints them, and Jesus uses a parable of two debtors to rebuke Simon, declare the woman's sins forgiven, and send her away in peace because of her faith.

Chapter 8

Jesus travels through cities and villages preaching God's Kingdom, accompanied by the twelve and several women, including Mary Magdalene, Joanna and Susanna, who support them. He teaches the parable of the sower to a large crowd and explains it privately to his disciples, then speaks of the lamp and of hearing carefully. He calms a storm on the lake, heals a man possessed by a legion of demons in the country of the Gadarenes, and sends the man home to declare what God did for him. Back across the lake, Jairus asks him to heal his dying twelve-year-old daughter; on the way a woman with a twelve-year-old flow of blood touches his cloak and is healed, and Jesus then raises Jairus' daughter, telling her parents to tell no one.

Chapter 9

In Luke 9, Jesus gives the twelve power over demons and disease and sends them out to preach and heal, while Herod wonders who Jesus is. After the apostles return, Jesus withdraws toward Bethsaida, teaches and heals the multitudes who follow, and feeds about five thousand men with five loaves and two fish. Peter confesses Jesus as the Christ of God, and Jesus foretells his suffering, death and resurrection and calls those who would follow him to deny themselves and take up their cross. On the mountain with Peter, John and James, Jesus is transfigured, Moses and Elijah appear, and a voice from a cloud calls him God's beloved Son. Jesus then heals a boy the disciples could not help, corrects the disciples' arguments about greatness, their rebuke of an outside exorcist, and James and John's wish to call down fire on a Samaritan village, and he sets his face toward Jerusalem while answering three would-be followers.

Chapter 10

Jesus appoints seventy others and sends them two by two ahead of him into cities, instructing them to travel without provisions, offer peace to houses, heal the sick, and proclaim that God's Kingdom has come near, while pronouncing woes on Chorazin, Bethsaida, and Capernaum. The seventy return rejoicing that demons are subject to them, and Jesus tells them to rejoice instead that their names are written in heaven. Jesus thanks his Father for revealing these things to little children and says that only the Son reveals the Father. When a lawyer asks what he must do to inherit eternal life, Jesus draws out the command to love God and neighbor and tells the parable of a man attacked by robbers, who is ignored by a priest and a Levite but cared for by a Samaritan. Finally, Martha receives Jesus into her house, complains that her sister Mary sits at his feet instead of helping, and Jesus says Mary has chosen the good part.

Chapter 11

In Luke 11, Jesus, asked by a disciple to teach them to pray, gives a model prayer and the parable of the friend at midnight, urging persistent asking, seeking, and knocking, and assures them the heavenly Father will give the Holy Spirit to those who ask. After he casts out a mute demon, some accuse him of acting by Beelzebul while others demand a sign, and he answers that a divided kingdom cannot stand and that by God's finger the Kingdom of God has come. He calls his generation evil, promises only the sign of Jonah, and says the Queen of the South and the men of Nineveh will condemn it. At a Pharisee's dinner he pronounces a series of woes on the Pharisees and lawyers for hypocrisy, burdening others, killing prophets, and taking away the key of knowledge. The scribes and Pharisees become terribly angry and lie in wait to catch him in his words.

Chapter 12

In Chapter 12, with a vast multitude gathered, Jesus first warns his disciples against the hypocrisy of the Pharisees and tells them to fear God rather than those who kill the body, assuring them of God's care and of the Holy Spirit's help when they are brought before authorities. When a man in the crowd asks him to settle an inheritance, Jesus warns against covetousness and tells the parable of a rich man who stores up goods and loses his life that night. He then tells his disciples not to be anxious about food or clothing, pointing to ravens and lilies, and urges them to seek God's Kingdom, give to the needy, and be watchful like servants awaiting their lord. In answer to Peter, he describes the faithful and wise steward and the greater accountability of those given much, then speaks of bringing fire and division even within households. Finally he rebukes the multitudes as hypocrites for not interpreting the time and urges settling with an adversary before reaching the judge.

Chapter 13

Jesus responds to news of Galileans killed by Pilate and to those killed by a falling tower in Siloam by calling his hearers to repent or perish, and he tells a parable of a fig tree that bore no fruit and was given one more year. On a Sabbath in a synagogue he frees a woman bent over for eighteen years, and the ruler of the synagogue objects; Jesus rebukes him and the crowd rejoices. He compares God's Kingdom to a mustard seed and to yeast, then, while traveling toward Jerusalem, answers a question about how many are saved by urging people to strive to enter the narrow door. Pharisees warn him that Herod wants to kill him; he replies that he must continue his work and that a prophet cannot perish outside Jerusalem, and he laments over Jerusalem, which he says is left desolate.

Chapter 14

Jesus goes to eat at the house of one of the rulers of the Pharisees on a Sabbath, where he is watched. He heals a man with dropsy after asking the lawyers and Pharisees whether healing on the Sabbath is lawful, and they stay silent and cannot answer his question about rescuing a son or an ox. He teaches the invited guests to take the lowest place and tells his host to invite the poor, maimed, lame, and blind rather than those who can repay. In response to a guest's remark about feasting in God's Kingdom, he tells of a man whose great supper is refused by invited guests with excuses, so the poor and others are brought in instead. Turning to the great multitudes, he sets out the demands of discipleship, using the images of a tower builder and a king to teach counting the cost, and ends with the saying about tasteless salt.

Chapter 15

Tax collectors and sinners come near to hear Jesus, and the Pharisees and scribes murmur that he welcomes and eats with sinners. In response Jesus tells three parables: a man who leaves ninety-nine sheep to find one lost sheep, a woman who lights a lamp and sweeps to find one lost drachma coin, and a father with two sons. In the third parable the younger son takes his share of the property, wastes it, falls into famine and feeding pigs, then returns repentant, and the father runs to him, restores him, and holds a feast. The elder son is angry and refuses to go in, and the father begs him and explains that celebrating was appropriate because his brother was dead and is alive, lost and found. The first two parables conclude that there is joy in heaven and among God's angels over one sinner who repents.

Chapter 16

Jesus tells his disciples a parable of a rich man's manager who, accused of wasting possessions and facing dismissal, reduces the debts owed to his lord so that debtors will receive him later; the lord commends his shrewdness. Jesus then teaches about faithfulness in little and much, and says no one can serve both God and Mammon. The money-loving Pharisees scoff, and Jesus says God knows their hearts, that the law and prophets lasted until John, that the law will not fail, and that divorcing and remarrying is adultery. He then tells of a rich man who lived in luxury and the beggar Lazarus; after death Lazarus is comforted at Abraham's bosom while the rich man suffers in Hades, and Abraham refuses his pleas, saying his five brothers have Moses and the prophets.

Chapter 17

In Chapter 17, Jesus teaches his disciples about causing others to stumble, rebuking and forgiving a brother who sins repeatedly, and the power of faith like a mustard seed, then illustrates duty with the image of a servant. On the way to Jerusalem, he meets ten lepers who ask for mercy; he sends them to the priests and they are cleansed as they go, but only one, a Samaritan, returns to thank him and is told his faith has healed him. Asked by the Pharisees when God's Kingdom will come, Jesus says it does not come with observation and is within them. He then tells the disciples that the Son of Man must first suffer and be rejected, and that his coming will be sudden like lightning, comparable to the days of Noah and Lot, with one person taken and another left.

Chapter 18

In Luke 18, Jesus teaches about prayer through two parables: a widow who persistently pleads with an unrighteous judge until he defends her, and a Pharisee and a tax collector who pray in the temple, with the humble tax collector going home justified. Jesus then tells his disciples to let babies and little children come to him, saying God's Kingdom belongs to such as these. A rich ruler asks how to inherit eternal life, is told to sell everything and give to the poor, and leaves very sad; Jesus remarks on how hard it is for the rich to enter the Kingdom and says what is impossible with men is possible with God. Jesus tells the twelve he will be handed over, killed, and rise on the third day, though they do not understand, and near Jericho he heals a blind man who cries out to him as son of David.

Chapter 19

Jesus passes through Jericho, where he sees Zacchaeus, a rich chief tax collector, in a sycamore tree and stays at his house; Zacchaeus pledges half his goods to the poor and fourfold restitution, and Jesus declares salvation has come to the house. Because he is near Jerusalem and the people expect the Kingdom immediately, Jesus tells a parable of a nobleman who gives ten servants a mina each before going away to receive a kingdom, then rewards the faithful, condemns the servant who hid his mina, and orders his enemies killed. Jesus then goes up to Jerusalem, sending two disciples to fetch a colt, and rides it down the Mount of Olives while disciples praise him as King. When the Pharisees ask him to rebuke them, he refuses, then weeps over Jerusalem and foretells its destruction. He enters the temple, drives out the sellers, and teaches daily while the chief priests, scribes, and leaders seek to destroy him but are hindered by the people's attention.

Chapter 20

In the temple, Jesus is questioned by the priests, scribes and elders about his authority, and he counters by asking whether John's baptism was from heaven or from men; they refuse to answer, so he refuses to answer them. He then tells the parable of a man who rented his vineyard to farmers who beat his servants and killed his beloved son, and he cites the rejected stone that became the cornerstone, which the leaders recognize as aimed at them. Spies sent by the leaders ask whether taxes should be paid to Caesar, and Jesus answers by having them identify Caesar's image on a denarius and telling them to give to Caesar what is Caesar's and to God what is God's. Sadducees, who deny the resurrection, pose a case of seven brothers who each married one woman, and Jesus replies that those who attain the resurrection neither marry nor die and that God is God of the living. He then asks how the Christ can be David's son when David calls him Lord, and warns his disciples against scribes who seek honor and devour widows' houses.

Chapter 21

In Chapter 21, Jesus observes rich people giving gifts to the treasury and praises a poor widow who gave two small coins, all she had to live on. When some remark on the temple's beautiful stones, he predicts that not one stone will be left on another, and his listeners ask when this will happen and what the sign will be. Jesus warns them not to be led astray by false claimants, describes wars, earthquakes, famines, plagues, and persecution of his followers, and says Jerusalem will be surrounded by armies, desolated, and trampled by the Gentiles. He foretells signs in the sun, moon, and stars, the coming of the Son of Man in a cloud with power and glory, and teaches the fig tree parable, urging watchfulness and prayer. The chapter closes with Jesus teaching in the temple by day, lodging on Olivet at night, and the people coming early to hear him.

Chapter 22

As the Passover approaches, the chief priests and scribes seek to kill Jesus, and Satan enters Judas Iscariot, who agrees with them to betray Jesus for money. Jesus sends Peter and John to prepare the Passover, then shares the meal with the twelve apostles, giving the bread and cup as his body and the new covenant in his blood, and announcing a betrayer at the table. The apostles dispute who is greatest, and Jesus teaches service and confers a kingdom on them, then warns Peter that he will deny him three times. On the Mount of Olives Jesus prays in agony, Judas leads a crowd to arrest him, and Jesus heals the ear of the high priest's servant. Peter denies Jesus three times in the high priest's courtyard and weeps when the rooster crows, while the elders mock Jesus and question him, and he affirms that he is the Son of God.

Chapter 23

Jesus is brought before Pilate by the whole company of accusers, who charge him with perverting the nation, forbidding taxes to Caesar, and claiming to be Christ, a king. Pilate finds no basis for a charge and sends him to Herod, who mocks him and returns him; Pilate then yields to the crowd and the chief priests, releasing Barabbas and delivering Jesus to their will. Jesus is led out with Simon of Cyrene carrying the cross, warns the mourning daughters of Jerusalem, and is crucified at The Skull between two criminals, one of whom rebukes the other and is promised Paradise. Jesus dies after darkness covers the land and the temple veil is torn, and the centurion calls him righteous. Joseph of Arimathaea, a council member who had not consented to the others' deed, asks Pilate for the body and lays it in a rock-cut tomb, and the women from Galilee watch and prepare spices before resting on the Sabbath.

Chapter 24

Luke 24 begins on the first day of the week, when Mary Magdalene, Joanna, Mary the mother of James, and other women find Jesus' tomb empty and hear two men in dazzling clothing say he is risen. The apostles dismiss their report as nonsense, though Peter runs to the tomb and sees only the linen strips. That day Jesus joins two disciples, one named Cleopas, on the road to Emmaus, explains the Scriptures about himself, and is recognized by them in the breaking of the bread. They return to Jerusalem, where Jesus appears among the eleven and others, shows his hands and feet, eats broiled fish, opens their minds to the Scriptures, and commissions them as witnesses. He then leads them to Bethany, blesses them, and is carried up into heaven, and they return to Jerusalem worshiping with great joy.
