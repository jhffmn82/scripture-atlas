Also called: benevolence and righteousness

Abstract written by a model

Benevolence is a principle arising from commiseration and part of man’s proper nature, not something produced through violence or injury. It subdues its opposite as water subdues fire, though an incomplete amount may fail; its value depends on maturity. It unfolds into a basis for advising rulers and directing government, as superior men lead princes toward benevolence.

Part I / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 16

Benevolence is identified as a principle arising from the feeling of commiseration and as part of man's proper nature. Mencius disputes the claim that it must be fashioned through violence and injury to man's nature.

Chapter 17 .. Chapter 20

Benevolence subdues its opposite as water subdues fire, but a small or incomplete amount may fail and encourage those who are not benevolent. Its value depends on being brought to maturity.

Part II / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 13

Benevolence is presented as the proper basis for advising rulers and directing government. Mencius says a superior man serves his prince by leading him toward benevolence, and that a minister who loves what is good can help govern well.
