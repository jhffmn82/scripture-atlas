Galilee

Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of the Maccabees, Galilee is a land of the Gentiles where Gentiles gather against Jews, whom Simon defeats and brings to Judea. In The Gospel According to Matthew, Galilee is the region where Jesus withdraws after John's arrest, calls disciples, teaches and heals, and commissions the eleven. In The Gospel According to John, Galilee is Jesus' home region, contains Cana, welcomes him after the Jerusalem feast, and provides refuge from Judea's threats.

Galilee in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Galilee first appears

Abstract written by a model

Galilee, called 'Galilee of the Gentiles' in Isaiah's prophecy, is the region where Jesus withdraws after John's arrest, fulfilling the light-dawning prophecy. He calls his first disciples by its sea, teaches and heals throughout it, and later meets the eleven on a mountain there, commissioning them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 4

Jesus withdraws into Galilee after hearing John was delivered up, and Isaiah's words about 'Galilee of the Gentiles' are cited as fulfilled, with light dawning on those who sat in darkness. He calls his first disciples by its sea and goes about all Galilee teaching, preaching, and healing, and multitudes from Galilee follow him.

Chapter 28

Galilee is named by the angel and by Jesus as the place where the disciples will see him. The eleven go there to a mountain where Jesus had sent them and meet him, and he gives them their commission.

Galilee in The Gospel According to John

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Galilee first appears

Abstract written by a model

Galilee is Jesus' home region and contains Cana, where he made water into wine and later heals a nobleman's son. Galileans welcome him after seeing his deeds at the Jerusalem feast, though he notes a prophet lacks honor at home. He stays there to avoid Judea's threats, while Pharisees and some of the crowd dismiss it as no source of prophets or the Christ.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Jesus departs into Galilee after leaving Judea and again after his two days in Samaria. The Galileans receive him because they saw what he did at the feast in Jerusalem, though Jesus testifies that a prophet has no honor in his own country. In Cana of Galilee, where he earlier made water into wine, he heals the nobleman's son.

Chapter 7

Jesus walks in Galilee and stays there to avoid Judea, where the Jews seek to kill him. Some in the multitude doubt he is the Christ because he comes from Galilee, and the others tell Nicodemus that no prophet has arisen out of Galilee.
