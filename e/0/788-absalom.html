Abstract written by a model

Absalom is a son of David, celebrated as the most beautiful man in all Israel, with no defect and heavy hair cut yearly. He has three sons and a daughter, Tamar. His story begins when he hates Amnon for forcing Tamar. After two years of silence he invites the king's sons to a sheep shearing, persuades David to let Amnon come, and has his servants kill Amnon when merry with wine. He flees to Talmai, king of Geshur, and stays three years. Joab brings him back to Jerusalem, but he must live in his own house without seeing the king. After two years he has Joab's field burned to gain attention, says he would rather be dead than kept from the king, and bows before David, who kisses him. Absalom then prepares a chariot, horses and fifty runners, and wins the hearts of Israelites at the gate by promising justice. Under the pretext of a vow he goes to Hebron, sends spies to proclaim himself king, and summons Ahithophel. The people anoint him king. He enters Jerusalem, tests Hushai's loyalty, follows Ahithophel's counsel by going in to his father's concubines in sight of all Israel, then favors Hushai's counsel. He crosses the Jordan, camps in Gilead, and sets Amasa over the army instead of Joab. In battle, riding his mule in the forest, he is caught by his head in an oak. Joab thrusts darts through his heart and ten armor-bearers kill him. He is thrown into a pit under a heap of stones. Having no son to keep his name, he had raised a pillar, still called Absalom's monument. David mourns him bitterly, while Joab rebukes David for loving him over all others.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

Absalom tells the wronged Tamar to keep quiet and takes her into his house, then hates Amnon and speaks to him neither good nor bad for two years. He invites the king's sons to a sheep shearing, persuades David to let Amnon come, and commands his servants to kill Amnon when he is merry with wine. He then flees to Talmai, king of Geshur, and stays there three years.

Chapter 14

Absalom is brought back from Geshur to Jerusalem by Joab but is told to live in his own house without seeing the king's face. He is praised for his flawless beauty, has heavy hair weighed at two hundred shekels each year, and has three sons and a daughter named Tamar. After two years he has Joab's field burned to get his attention, says he would rather be dead than kept from the king, and finally bows before the king, who kisses him.

Chapter 15

Absalom prepared a chariot, horses, and fifty runners, and at the gate won the hearts of Israelites by promising justice and kissing those who bowed to him. He got David's permission to go to Hebron under the pretext of a vow, sent spies to proclaim himself king there, and summoned Ahithophel. At the end of the unit he enters Jerusalem.

Chapter 16

Absalom comes to Jerusalem with the men of Israel and Ahithophel. He questions Hushai's loyalty, accepts his answer, asks Ahithophel for counsel, and follows it by going in to his father's concubines on a tent atop the house in sight of all Israel.

Chapter 17

Absalom was pleased with Ahithophel's counsel but called Hushai the Archite to hear his view as well, and then he and the men of Israel judged Hushai's counsel better. He crossed the Jordan with all Israel, camped in Gilead, and set Amasa over the army instead of Joab. His servants searched for Jonathan and Ahimaaz after a boy told him of them but did not find them.

Chapter 18

Absalom, riding his mule in the forest, was caught by his head in the boughs of a great oak and left hanging between sky and earth. Joab thrust three darts through his heart, ten of Joab's armor-bearers struck and killed him, and he was thrown into a great pit under a heap of stones. The text also notes that he had raised a pillar in the king's valley, since he had no son to keep his name, and it is still called Absalom's monument.

Chapter 19

Absalom is mourned by David, who cries out "My son Absalom, Absalom, my son, my son!" The people also note that he, whom they had anointed over them, is dead in battle, and Joab says David would have been pleased if Absalom had lived and everyone else had died.
