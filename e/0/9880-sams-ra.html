Abstract written by a model

In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I, samsâra is a cycle of rebirth sustained by attachment, ignorance, carelessness, and worldly acts, which the enlightened sage ends. In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, the Circle of Births encompasses animal, human, and divine births, causing repeated suffering until release through the teaching. In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, Samsâra is a boundless flood of repeated birth, death, suffering, and Karman from which practitioners escape through the true Law and Path. In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, worldly ties are attachments to women, property, wealth, relations, honours, and fame from which a sage should remain free. In Sutrakritanga, Book II, Samsâra is a beginningless, endless fourfold cycle of wandering through varied births and deaths, from which the virtuous monk seeks release. In Sutrakritanga, Book II, the fourfold Circle of Births is a beginningless, endless cycle of manifold pains and regeneration that perfected beings end. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, Samsâra is the endless, unstable Wheel of Births driven by Karman, attachment, sin, and carelessness, which spiritual discipline can overcome. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, Samsâra is a beginningless, endless realm of worldly existence from which souls escape through liberation, correct conduct, and destroying Karman.

samsâra in Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where samsâra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Samsâra is a cycle or whirlpool of rebirth: birth and death, pain, worldly attachment, acts, karman, conception, and the misery of the world. Beings undergo manifold births, and those who acquiesce in or prefer worldly pleasures, remain attached, ignorant, or careless turn round and round in it, born again and again. Some do not know whether the soul is reborn, while others know it is. The wise, passionless sage or hero seeks freedom by renouncing desire and acts, discontinuing sin, and becoming enlightened. Crossing samsâra ends this course of births; the Seer has put an end to acts, worldly existence, and samsâra.

Circle of Births in Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Circle of Births first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Circle of Births is a topic encompassing animals, men, and gods as forms of birth. It inflicts former cruelties on people through miserable rebirth and infinite pain. False opinions and attachment to vanities, fame, sensual objects, or subsistence monkhood keep people whirling and suffering again; those exempt or fulfilling the teaching are released from it and mundane existence.

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Fifth Lecture, Called Description of the Hells. / First Chapter. .. Second Chapter.

The Circle of Births inflicts on a person the same cruelty that the person committed in a former birth. Being born in an extremely miserable state, the sufferer experiences infinite pain.

Twelfth Lecture, Called the Creed.

Many men may whirl round in it because of false opinions. Those who are exempt from it do not return to mundane existence.

Thirteenth Lecture, Called the Real Truth.

Attachment to vanities, fame, sensual objects, or using monkhood as a means of subsistence causes a person to suffer again and again in the Circle of Births. A monk who fulfils the teaching should wander released from the Circle of Births.

Samsâra in Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Samsâra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Samsâra is the Circle of Births: a long, impassable, boundless flood or stream in which beings undergo repeated birth, death, disease, old age, pains, and new Karman. Wrong doctrines, sinful conduct, attachment, and seduction by the senses and women keep beings within it and lead to repeated rebirth and danger. It carries along those who remain mixed with the world and prevents them from reaching the opposite shore. Practitioners seek escape by resisting seductions, ceasing from works, annihilating Karman, renouncing everything, and following the true Law and the Path. Mahâvîra and monks cross this difficult flood through knowledge, carefulness, and resistance to seductions; the true Law ends it.

Beings living in the Samsâra acquire new Karman and suffer for their misdeeds. A monk who annihilates Karman should not again mix with the world.

Eleventh Lecture, Called the Path.

Samsâra is a difficult flood or current that beings cross by following the Path. Those who enter it without knowledge and carefulness incur great danger, while one who knows the Law crosses it.

Twelfth Lecture, Called the Creed.

It is compared to a boundless flood of water, impassable and of very long duration because of repeated births. Men seduced by their senses and by women are born in it again and again.

Fourteenth Lecture, Called the Nirgrantha.

Samsâra carries beings along like a stream, and one who remains carried away by it cannot reach the opposite shore.

fourfold Circle of Births in Sutrakritanga, Book II

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where fourfold Circle of Births first appears

Abstract written by a model

The fourfold Circle of Births is an existent, beginningless and endless cycle marked by manifold pains. It unfolds through repeated cutting, piercing, birth, old age, death, conception, and regeneration. Violent beings, those bound by slaughter or acquisition of property, and others described as eating seeds, using cold water, or having intercourse with women remain repeatedly reborn. Beings shift between movable and immovable forms and bodies; wrong teachers cause ruin within it. Perfected beings end the cycle.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Now the explanation of the second subject, viz. merit, is as follows:

The birth and death cycle is described as repeated cutting, piercing, birth, old age, death, conception, regeneration, and other forms of suffering. Violent beings remain subject to it, whereas perfected beings put an end to it.

Fifth Lecture, Called Freedom from Error.

The fourfold Circle of Births exists.

Sixth Lecture, Called .

Those who eat seeds, use cold water, or have intercourse with women are said to be reborn repeatedly and not to end the Circle of Births. Wrong teachers and those who follow wrong doctrines are said to work the ruin of others within it.

Seventh Lecture, Called Nâlandâ.

The Circle of Births contains beings that change between movable and immovable forms. Beings leave one type of body and are reborn in another, so a restricted renunciation cannot ensure that injury is entirely ceased.

Samsâra in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Samsâra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Samsâra is the endless, unstable Wheel of Births: a condition of recurring births inhabited by living beings and full of misery. Beings become many forms, from gods and hell-beings to worms and ants, yet are not disgusted with its misery. Personal actions, good and bad Karman, attachment to bodies and pleasures, sins, wrong actions, and carelessness drive and keep them moving through it, unable to escape their effects. Ignorant and sinful men suffer and wander long within it. Pious ascetics cross it, while a sage who uses spiritual weapons, faith, penance, self-control, patience, zeal, carefulness, content, and truth, defeats and gets rid of Samsâra.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Third Lecture.

Samsâra contains recurring births in which living beings become gods, hell-beings, Asuras, Kshattriyas, Kandâlas, Bukkasas, worms, moths, Kunthu, or ants. Living beings are not disgusted with Samsâra despite its recurring misery.

Fourth Lecture.

A person living in the Samsâra cannot escape the effects of personal actions.

Sixth Lecture.

In the endless Samsâra, ignorant men suffer in many ways. Those who remain attached to their bodies and sins travel a long way in Samsâra.

Eighth Lecture.

Samsâra is unstable, eternal, and full of misery. Pious ascetics (sâdhu) cross it as merchants cross the sea, while sinful beings remain ensnared in it and err through it for a long time.

Ninth Lecture.

Nami teaches that a sage who uses faith, penance, self-control, patience, zeal, carefulness, content, and truth as spiritual weapons can become victorious and get rid of Samsâra.

Tenth Lecture.

Samsâra is the condition through which the careless soul is driven about. Good and bad Karman cause this movement.

Fourteenth Lecture.

Samsâra is feared as the Wheel of Births from which the practitioners seek escape. Attachment to pleasures and wrong actions keep beings within it.

Samsâra in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

Hermann Jacobi · the work’s page · read it where Samsâra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Samsâra is a beginningless, endless, fourfold realm of worldly existence, portrayed as a large and dreadful ocean, a mine of dangers, and a wilderness of old age and death. It contains living beings who have not reached perfection and is marked by repeated births, deaths, misery, dangers, and a long succession of pains. Attachment to pleasure keeps beings wandering within it. Those who long for liberation fear it, while indifference to sensory objects can leave one unaffected by its pains. Souls cross Samsâra through liberation, correct behaviour, religious exercise, beneficial modes of life, abandoning worldly undertakings, destroying Karman, and following the road to perfection. Perfected souls have crossed its boundary and are free from transmigration.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Nineteenth Lecture.

Samsâra is described as a large ocean, a mine of dangers, and a wilderness of old age and death. Mrigâputra says he has repeatedly undergone dreadful births, deaths, misery, and dangers in it.

Twenty-Fifth Lecture.

Samsâra is described as a dreadful ocean whose eddies are dangers. Attachment to pleasure causes beings to continue wandering in it, while freedom from pleasure leads to liberation.

Twenty-Sixth Lecture.

Samsâra is described as an ocean from which Nirgranthas and many souls have crossed through correct behaviour.

Twenty-Seventh Lecture.

The Samsâra is crossed by one who rides, as it were, in the car of religious exercise.

Twenty-Ninth Lecture.

Samsâra is the beginningless and endless fourfold forest of worldly existence. The soul crosses it by abandoning worldly undertakings, destroying Karman, and following the road to perfection.

Thirty-First Lecture.

Samsâra is described as an ocean that many souls have crossed through practising the beneficial mode of life.

Thirty-Second Lecture.

The Samsâra is feared by one who longs for liberation and follows the Law. A person may remain in the Samsâra without being affected by its succession of pains if he is indifferent to sensory objects.

B. Living Beings.

The Samsâra contains living beings that have not reached perfection. Perfected souls have crossed its boundary and are rid of transmigration.

Circle of Births in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

Hermann Jacobi · the work’s page · read it where Circle of Births first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Circle of Births is an endless cycle of repeated pain, like an ocean, in which the soul turns round and round while bound by Karman. A saint seeks escape by becoming a houseless monk and ceasing action. Through thorough, pious practice of the creed of the Ginas, people become pure and free from passions; the omniscient Gina and great sages bring the cycle to an end.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Twentieth Lecture.

The Circle of Births is described as endless and full of recurring pain. The saint resolves to become a houseless monk if he can escape his great pains and thereby cease acting.

Twenty-Third Lecture.

The Circle of Births is compared to an ocean crossed by the great sages. The omniscient Gina and the great sages bring an end to this cycle.

Thirty-Third Lecture.

The Circle of Births is where the soul turns round and round because it is bound by Karman.

B. Living Beings.

Those who love and practise the creed of the Ginas become pure and free from passions and eventually get out of the Circle of Births.
