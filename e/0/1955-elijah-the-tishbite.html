Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, Elijah the Tishbite serves as a prophet and servant of the LORD, confronting Ahab and Baal, performing miracles, and appointing his successors. In The Second Book of Kings, Elijah is a hairy prophet who delivers the LORD’s judgment to Ahaziah, calls down fire, parts the Jordan, and is taken up to heaven in a whirlwind. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Elijah the prophet sends Jehoram a letter delivering the LORD’s judgment for his wickedness. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Elijah is praised as a fiery prophet who brought famine, raised the dead, brought down kings, anointed rulers, and was taken up in a fiery chariot. In The Book of Malachi, Elijah is the prophet promised to be sent before the great and terrible day of the LORD to reconcile fathers and children. In The Gospel According to Matthew, Elijah appears with Moses speaking with Jesus on the mountain, and is identified in prophecy with the one who comes first to restore all things. In The Good News According to Mark, Elijah appears with Moses speaking with Jesus on the mountain, and is described as having come first and being mistreated by people.

Elijah the Tishbite in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Elijah the Tishbite first appears

Abstract written by a model

Elijah the Tishbite is a settler of Gilead, a prophet and servant of the LORD, before whom he stands, with the LORD's word in his mouth. The widow calls him a man of God. He opens by telling Ahab no dew or rain will fall except by his word, then obeys the LORD's commands to live by the brook Cherith and in Zarephath, where he promises the widow that her meal and oil will not run out and prays her dead son back to life. On Mount Carmel he challenges the people to choose between the LORD and Baal, sees the LORD's fire fall, and has the prophets of Baal killed at the brook Kishon. He prays for rain and, with the LORD's hand on him, runs ahead of Ahab to Jezreel. Jezebel, his enemy, threatens his life, so he flees to Beersheba and the wilderness, asks to die, is fed by an angel, and at Horeb twice says he alone is left and jealous for the LORD. He is told to anoint Hazael, Jehu, and Elisha, and puts his mantle on Elisha, who follows and succeeds him. He later confronts Ahab in Naboth's vineyard, announcing judgment on Ahab, his house, and Jezebel.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 17

Elijah, a settler of Gilead, tells Ahab there will be no dew or rain except by his word, then obeys the LORD's commands to live by the brook Cherith and later in Zarephath. He promises the widow that her meal and oil will not run out, and he prays over her dead son, stretching himself on the child three times, until the child revives. The widow concludes that he is a man of God.

Chapter 18

Elijah is told by the LORD to show himself to Ahab, persuades the fearful Obadiah to announce him, and tells Ahab that Ahab and his father's house have troubled Israel by following the Baals. On Mount Carmel he challenges the people to choose between the LORD and Baal, mocks the prophets of Baal, rebuilds the altar, prays, and sees the LORD's fire fall, then has the prophets of Baal killed. He prays for rain with his face between his knees, sends his servant to look at the sea seven times, and, with the LORD's hand on him, runs ahead of Ahab to the entrance of Jezreel.

Chapter 19

Elijah flees Jezebel's threat to Beersheba and then into the wilderness, where he asks the LORD to take his life. Strengthened by food from an angel, he goes to Horeb, twice tells the LORD that he alone is left and is sought for death, and is commanded to anoint Hazael, Jehu, and Elisha. He puts his mantle on Elisha, who then follows and serves him.

Chapter 21

Elijah receives the LORD's word and goes to meet Ahab in Naboth's vineyard. He delivers the charge, "Have you killed and also taken possession?", and announces the coming judgment on Ahab, his house, and Jezebel.

Elijah in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where Elijah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Elijah is a hairy man in a leather belt, called a man of God and proven so by fire from the sky. Sent by the LORD's angel, he tells Ahaziah's messengers the king will die for consulting Baal Zebub. He calls fire on two captains of fifty and their men, spares a third who begs, and delivers the message to the king in person. He then travels with Elisha to the Jordan, parts it with his mantle, and is taken up to heaven by a whirlwind.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

The Second Book of Kings / Chapter 1

Elijah is sent by the LORD's angel to intercept Ahaziah's messengers and tell them the king will die because he sought Baal Zebub. He is identified by the messengers' description of a hairy man in a leather belt. He calls down fire from the sky on two captains of fifty and their men, spares the third captain who begs, and goes with him to tell Ahaziah in person that he will die.

Chapter 2

Elijah travels with Elisha from Gilgal through Bethel and Jericho to the Jordan, telling him each time to wait while the LORD sends him on. He parts the Jordan by striking it with his rolled-up mantle, tells Elisha that seeing him taken will determine whether his request is granted, and is taken up into heaven by a whirlwind after a chariot and horses of fire separate them.

Elijah in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · about 179 BC · the work’s page · read it where Elijah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Elijah is praised as a prophet whose word burned like a torch. By his zeal he brought famine, raised a dead man by the word of the Most High, and brought down kings. He heard rebuke at Sinai and judgments at Horeb, and anointed kings for retribution. He was taken up in a tempest of fire in a fiery chariot, leaving Elisha filled with his spirit. He was recorded as a reprover to pacify anger and restore Jacob's tribes.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 48

Elijah is praised as a prophet like fire whose word burned like a torch; he brought famine, shut up the heavens, brought down fire three times, and raised a dead man by the word of the Most High. He heard rebuke at Sinai and Horeb, anointed kings and prophets to succeed him, and was taken up in a tempest of fire in a chariot of fiery horses. He was recorded for reproofs in their seasons, to pacify anger, turn the heart of the father to the son, and restore the tribes of Jacob.
