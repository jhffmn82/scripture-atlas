Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, the Kine are sacred blessed gifts associated with food, pasture, sacrifice, and religious sanctity. In Yasna, the Kine are holy herds and a sacred living creation whose protection, care, and prosperity support human life and the land. In Visparad, the Kine are blessed cattle for whom confession, homage, praise, supplication, and ceremonial chant are offered.

Kine in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Kine first appears

Abstract written by a model

Kine are sacred, blessed gifts associated with food and pasture, belonging to Ahura Mazda and linked with Asha and religious sanctity. Their flesh is offered in propitiation and sacrifice. The speaker rejects robbery and violence against them and seeks to remove their wandering as they abide steadfastly on land.

Yasna VII.

The flesh of the Kine of blessed gift is offered with Haurvatât and Ameretatât for the propitiation of Sraosha. It is also included among the things sacrificed to the Ahuna-vairya and sacred prayers.

Yasna XII (Sp. XIII).

The Kine belong to Ahura Mazda and are associated with Asha and the blessed religious sanctity. The speaker rejects robbery and violence against them and wishes to remove wandering from them as they abide steadfastly upon the land.

Kine in Yasna

L.H. Mills · the work’s page · read it where Kine first appears

Abstract written by a model

Kine are holy herds: sacred cattle and a pure, joy-creating living creation made by Ahura Mazda. They matter as a source of joy, mental blessings, pleasing homes, lasting strength, peace, and welfare for human life and the land. Their sacred cause is bound to Righteous Order, the waters, plants, Yazads, and the sacred mother-kine. From the beginning, the Kine’s Soul seeks protection from wounding and fearful ravages, along with tillage, pasture, food, comfort, fodder, and a skilled, energetic cattle-chief. Ahura Mazda gives the Kine a path, and she chooses the thrifty husbandman as holy master while rejecting the thieving nomad. Yet evil teachers declare the Kine among the worst things, and wicked men, Grehma, and allies harm or destroy her life. The Kine therefore seek just spiritual leadership, while stint in labour and failure to provide complete pasture are condemned. Their prosperity depends on toil, understanding, merciful care, and the word of the Righteous Order. Worshippers pray to nurture and protect them, providing homes, pastures, and flourishing plants. Ahura Mazda grants joyful meadows of peace, and the Kine remain sought with wealth in herds beside them, supported by waters and plants in the farm-yard.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

12 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna XXIX.

The Kine are described as holy herds whose Soul seeks protection, good tillage, pasture, food, and a capable guardian. Their condition includes wounding and exposure to fearful ravages.

Yasna XXXI.

The Kine receives a path from Ahura Mazda and chooses the husbandman, the thrifty toiler in the fields, as a holy master. Her choice excludes the thieving nomad from the good creed.

Yasna XXXII.

The Kine are treated as sacred, but the rival leader declares them among the worst things visible and is said to destroy the Kine’s life. Grehma and his allies are associated with attempts to conquer or harm the Kine.

Yasna XXXIII.

The Kine seek a spiritual chieftain and are connected with the need for just leadership. Zarathustra abjures the worst care of the Kine’s meadows, described as stint in labour.

Yasna XXXIV.

The Kine is described as a mother whose toil is served by those who work for her. Their work furthers Ahura Mazda’s merciful care through understanding and the word of the Righteous Order.

Yasna XXXV.

The Kine represents the pure creation and is the subject of prayer for comfort and fodder. The worshippers seek to nurture her and to act for her welfare.

Yasna XXXVII.

The Kine (the living creation) is described as made by Ahura Mazda. Ahura Mazda is worshipped as having priority among the Yazads who abide beside the Kine and care for her protection and support.

Yasna XLVI.

The Kine are associated with the sacred cause, the Righteous Order, and the blessings gained through the sacred mother-kine. Wisdom and rewards are sought for their welfare.

Yasna XLVII.

The Kine is the joy-creating Kine made by Ahura Mazda. Ahura Mazda bestows Âramaiti as her joyful meadows of peace because the chosen saint has taken counsel with Good Mind.

Yasna XLVIII.

The Kine are to receive toil and care so that they prosper for human life. They grant pleasing homes and lasting strength, and plants are caused to flourish for their nurture.

Yasna XLIX, 12-L.

The Kine is described as the joy-creating living symbol of peace. The text asks how she may be protected, provided with pastures, and secured for the welfare of the land.

Yasna LI.

The Kine are created along with the waters and plants and are objects of prayer and protection. The tiller of the earth seeks them and the wealth in herds through Asha, while the Karpans are condemned for failing to provide complete pasture for them.

Kine in Visparad

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Kine first appears

Abstract written by a model

Kine are blessed cattle: confession is made for them, their body receives homage and praise, and speakers address supplications to them through chant and ceremonial actions.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Visparad IV (Sp. V).

The Kine is the recipient for whom confession is to be made.

Visparad IX (Sp. X).

The body of the Kine is approached for homage and praises.

Visparad XXII (Sp. XXV).

The Kine are the subject of the speakers' supplications, which they utter through the chant and accompanying ceremonial actions.
