Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, Demon-gods are human-shaped gods whom Zarathustra caused to vanish into the ground. In Yasna, Daêvas are evil, deceitful beings opposed to Mazda-worshippers and wholly abjured by Zarathustra. In Yasna, Demon-gods are spiritual beings who choose the Worst Mind and rush to the Demon of Fury. In Yasna, Daêvas are demon-gods and false religious powers allied with Evil Spirit and opposed by the righteous. In Visparad, Daêvas are held subject by correctly spoken sacrificial words. In Afrinagan, Gahs and Miscellaneous Fragments, Daêvas are commanded to disappear beneath the earth. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII, Daêva describes a man who lies with mankind and becomes an unseen Daêva after death. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Daêvas are wicked spirits that cause corruption, oppose Fire, and flee from Zarathustra.

and 2 more works

In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, Daêvas are evil fiendish beings opposed and ultimately destroyed by Mazda-worshippers and faithful forces. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, Daêvas are evil creations and oppressors resisted and destined to perish under the holy Law.

Daêvas in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Daêvas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Daêvas are evil, deceitful, wicked beings, bereft of good and most like the Demon-of-the-Lie. They are foes of Mazda-worshippers and the Mazdayasnian confession, and the Zarathustrian law is composed against them. Zarathustra seeks H(a)oma’s protection against their hate. They are finally driven away and wholly abjured, including their shelter, headship, thoughts, words, actions, seed, sorcerers, adherents, and associates.

The Daêvas are opposed by the Mazdayasnian confession. The Zarathustrian law is described as composed against them.

Yasna IX.

The D(a)êvas are among the beings whose angry hate Zarathustra asks H(a)oma to overwhelm. They are also named among the enemies against whom H(a)oma’s protection is sought.

Yasna XII (Sp. XIII).

The Daêvas are driven away and abjured as evil, bereft of good, deceitful, wicked, and like the Demon-of-the-Lie. Their shelter, headship, thoughts, words, actions, seed, sorcerers, adherents, and iniquitous associates are all rejected.

Daêvas in Yasna

L.H. Mills · the work’s page · read it where Daêvas first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Daêvas are demon-gods and false religious powers, allied with the Demon of Fury in the great struggle. They are seeds of Evil Mind, ruled by Evil Spirit, linked to the Lie-demon through sacrifice, and cause confusion in human thought. Estranged from Ahura Mazda and Righteousness and forsaken by Good Mind, they are supported by the wicked through evil schemes, words, and perverted Faith and Insight. Their servants possess harmful malice threatening a dwelling and its lord. Yet they are opposed by Mãthra, saints’ good consciences, Sanctity, and Sraosha, who battles and frightens them, destroys them, and hews down the Daêvas of Mazendran.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna XXXII.

The Daêvas are described as seeds from the Evil Mind and as false religious powers. Their worshippers are associated with the Lie-demon, deception, confusion, evil deeds, and separation from the Good Mind, Ahura Mazda, and Righteousness.

Yasna XXXIX.

The Daêvas are opposed by the good consciences of the saints, which have conquered, are conquering, or will conquer them.

Yasna XLIX.

The Daêvas are settled and supported by those whose will and deeds are evil. Their support is identified as the wicked’s perverted Faith and Insight.

Yasna LII (Sp. LI).

The Daêvas and their servants are described as possessing harmful malice that might harm the dwelling and its lord. Sanctity overwhelms that harmful malice.

Yasna LVII (Sp. LVI).

The Daêvas are opposed by Sraosha, who battles them, guards against them, and causes them to turn in fear. He is also said to cleave their skulls and hew down the Daêvas of Mazendran.

Daêvas in Vendidad (Vendîdâd)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Daêvas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Daêvas are wicked, false-speaking evil-doing spirits, produced in the earth when hair or nails are improperly discarded; that deed increases their baleful strength. They cause persistent issue of blood, defile Vohu-manô through bodies they smite, make Âzi, and oppose Fire. They wither improperly clothed people over fifteen. Seeking Zarathustra’s death, they are repelled and flee to Arezûra and into hell.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard XVII.

Daêvas are produced in the earth when hair or nails are dropped into a hole or crack without the lawful rites. The deed is said to increase their baleful strength.

Fargard XVIII.

The Daêvas make Âzi and oppose Fire. They wither a person over fifteen who walks without the sacred girdle or sacred shirt, leaving that person able to destroy the world of the holy spirit like the Yâtus and Zandas.

Fargard XIX.

The Daêvas seek Zarathustra’s death, but they are repelled by him and flee with shouts and evil looks to the head of Arezûra. They run away into the depths of the dark, horrid world of hell.

Daêvas in Yashts (Yasts) I-XII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Daêvas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Daêvas are evil, fiendish beings born of darkness, associated with malice and counted in numberless myriads. They are objects of hatred for Mazda-worshippers and are opposed under the laws of Ahura by worshippers of Mazda, heavenly Yazatas, Ardvi Sûra Anâhita, and other faithful forces. From the beginning, Ahura Mazda’s names are invoked to destroy their malice and afflict them; speakers declare hatred of them and seek their crushing. The Airyaman prayer and Asha-Vahista defeat and smite them, while Haurvatât’s name is invoked against their vast numbers. They appear in conflicts involving the Daêvas of Mâzana and are feared as a threat so great that, if the sun did not rise, they would destroy all things in the seven Karshvares. Though they receive libations after sunset, they are portrayed howling and shouting. Mithra makes them flee in fear, and Sraosha drives them away, forces them to bow, or sends them into darkness. Good prayer, truth, and the Law of Mazda-worshippers ultimately smite or destroy them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

10 sections; each heading opens the text

I. Ormazd Yast.

Daêvas are hated and opposed under the laws of Ahura. Ahura Mazda's names are said to destroy their malice, and the names are invoked to afflict and smite them.

II. Haptân Yast.

The speaker declares hatred of the Daêvas and obedience to the laws of Ahura. Daêvas are included among those whom the Yâtus should crush.

III. Ardibehist Yast.

The Daêvas and their counter-work flee before the Airyaman prayer and are smitten by Asha-Vahista. They are described as among the forces defeated by the prayer.

IV. Khordâd Yast.

The Daêvas are hated by the worshipper of Mazda and opposed by the faithful. Haurvatât’s name is invoked against their thousands, ten thousands, and numberless myriads.

V. Âbân Yast.

The Daêvas are opposed by Ardvi Sûra Anâhita and by those who worship Ahura Mazda. They are described as enemies, with two thirds of the Daêvas of Mâzana being smitten by Haoshyangha, and they are involved in the conflicts and requests made by several figures.

XX.

Daêvas receive libations brought to Ardvi Sûra Anâhita after the sun has set. Six hundred and a thousand of them receive those libations while howling, clapping, hopping, and shouting.

VI. Khôrshêd Yast.

The Daêvas are hated and opposed through the laws of Ahura and through sacrifice to the Sun. They are born of darkness, and if the sun did not rise they would destroy all things in the seven Karshvares.

IX. Gôs Yast.

The Daêvas are hated and opposed by Mazda-worshippers. Haoshyangha seeks power to overcome the Daêvas of Mâzana, while Kavi Vîstâspa seeks to defeat Daêva-worshippers and enemies.

XII.

Daêvas are among the forces whose malice Mithra is asked to smite. They and the Varenya fiends flee in fear from Mithra, and Mithra protects worshippers from the fiendish hordes.

XI. Srôsh Yast Hâdhôkht.

The Daêvas are driven away and made to bow or rush into darkness before Sraosha. Good prayer, the Ahuna Vairya, the word of truth, and the Law of the worshippers of Mazda are described as forces that smite or destroy them.

Daêvas in Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Daêvas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Daêvas are evil creations, fiends, demons, and oppressors opposed to the law of Mazda and Ahura. They assault and oppress the stars, prevent their movement, oppose the faithful Fravashis, and act with worshippers who spill blood and misuse offerings. They are hated by Zarathustra and resisted by the Fravashis, Vayu, Verethraghna, Vîspa-taurvairi, Zarathustra, and the holy Law. Across the records, their malice and strength are broken, they are driven back and beneath the earth by the Ahuna Vairya, deprived of sacrifice and prayer, retire before king Vîstâspa, and are destined to perish with the Drug.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

XIII. Farvardîn Yast.

The Daêvas oppress and assault the stars and prevent them from moving, and they are among the enemies destroyed or smitten by the Fravashis. Their malice and strength are broken in battle by the Fravashis of the faithful.

XXIII.

The Daêvas are described as being driven back and brought to naught. Their creation is declared unworthy of sacrifice and prayer, and their law is opposed by the word and law of Ahura.

XXVI.

The Daêvas are opposed by the Fravashis and powers invoked in this unit. They include the Mâzainya Daêvas, the Daêvas associated with Aêshma, and those whose malice will be destroyed by Vîspa-taurvairi.

XIV. Bahrâm Yast.

Daêvas and their worshippers are described as enemies of the law of Mazda who spill blood, misuse offerings, and have their minds confounded and eyes made giddy. Verethraghna is invoked to destroy their malice and break their columns.

XV. Râm Yast.

The Daêvas are enemies opposed by Vayu and his worshippers. Vayu grants requests to smite or overcome them, and his names are to be invoked against them.

XIX. Zamyâd Yast.

The Daêvas are opposed by Zarathustra and the holy Law. They are conquered, deprived of sacrifice and prayer, driven beneath the earth by the Ahuna Vairya, and are destined to perish with the Drug.

XX. Vanant Yast.

The worshipper identifies himself as one who hates the Daêvas.
