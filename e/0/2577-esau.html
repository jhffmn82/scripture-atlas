Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Esau is Isaac and Rebekah’s elder twin son, a hunter, Jacob’s brother, father of the Edomites, and ancestor of Edom. In 1 Chronicles, Esau is Isaac’s son and the father of Eliphaz, Reuel, Jeush, Jalam, and Korah. In The Book of Malachi, Esau is identified as Jacob’s brother whom the LORD hated, whose mountains were made desolate and heritage given to wilderness jackals.

Esau in Genesis

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Esau first appears

Abstract written by a model

Esau is the elder twin son of Isaac and Rebekah, born red and hairy, who grows into a skillful hunter and man of the field favored by Isaac. Famished, he trades his birthright to his brother Jacob for red stew and lentils, earning the name Edom, and the text says he despised the birthright. When Jacob takes Isaac's blessing, Esau weeps and receives a lesser one of fertile dwelling, living by the sword, and serving his brother. He then hates Jacob and resolves to kill him after Isaac's death. Seeing that Canaanite women displease his father, he marries Mahalath, Ishmael's daughter, in addition to his other wives. Later, living in Seir, the land of Edom, he approaches Jacob with four hundred men, terrifying him. Yet Esau runs to meet him, embraces, kisses, and weeps, says he has enough, and accepts Jacob's gift only after urging, then returns toward Seir. His wives Adah, Oholibamah, and Basemath bear Eliphaz, Reuel, Jeush, Jalam, and Korah. Because their possessions are too great to share a dwelling with Jacob's, he moves his household and livestock to the hill country of Seir. He is identified as Edom and as father of the Edomites.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 25

Esau was born first, red all over like a hairy garment, and grew into a skillful hunter and man of the field loved by Isaac. Famished, he asked Jacob for red stew, gaining the name Edom, and swore away his birthright for bread and lentil stew. The text says he despised his birthright.

Chapter 27

Esau, the elder son, goes to hunt venison at Isaac's request but returns to find that Jacob has already taken the blessing. He cries bitterly, begs for a blessing of his own, and receives one of dwelling in fatness of the earth, living by his sword, and serving his brother. He then hates Jacob and resolves in his heart to kill him after Isaac's death.

Chapter 28

Esau sees that Isaac blessed Jacob, sent him to Paddan Aram, and forbade Canaanite wives, and that Jacob obeyed. Noting that Canaanite women displease his father, he goes to Ishmael and takes Mahalath, Ishmael's daughter and Nebaioth's sister, as a wife in addition to his existing wives.

Chapter 32

Esau, Jacob's brother, lives in the land of Seir, the field of Edom, and receives Jacob's messengers calling him 'my lord.' The messengers report he is coming to meet Jacob with four hundred men, which terrifies Jacob. Jacob plans to appease him with a present and hopes he will accept him.

Chapter 33

Esau comes with four hundred men, runs to meet Jacob, embraces him, falls on his neck, kisses him, and weeps. He asks about the women and children and about the company he met, says he has enough, and accepts the gift only after Jacob urges him. He offers to travel ahead and to leave some of his people with Jacob, and when Jacob declines, he returns that day toward Seir.

Chapter 36

Esau took wives Adah, Oholibamah, and Basemath, who bore Eliphaz, Reuel, Jeush, Jalam, and Korah in Canaan. He gathered his household and livestock and moved to the hill country of Seir, away from Jacob, because their substance was too great to dwell together. He is identified as Edom and as the father of the Edomites.
