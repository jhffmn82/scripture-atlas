Baal

deity · 5 works · 2 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: Baal Berith, Baals, a god, gods, he, him, his altars, his images, other gods, the Baals, the gods, the names of the Baals, the shameful thing

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Judges, Baal is worshiped at an altar and Asherah belonging to Joash's household, and Gideon is named Jerub-Baal. In The Book of Judges, the Baals are followed by the Israelites after Gideon's death, and Baal Berith is made their god. In The First Book of Kings, Baal is the god whose worship is challenged and whose prophets receive no answer when they call on him. In The Second Book of Kings, Baal is deceptively worshiped by Jehu's summoned assembly before his pillar is broken and his worship destroyed in Israel. In The Book of Jeremiah, Baal is a false god followed by prophets and worshiped by Jerusalem and Judah, provoking the LORD's anger. In The Book of Hosea, Baal receives silver, gold, and incense that should belong to the LORD, whose names are promised removal from Israel's mouth.

Baal is a false god whom prophets prophesied by and followed unprofitably, while Israel denied pursuing the Baals. Later, Jerusalem and Judah burned incense to Baal on street altars, provoking the LORD to anger and becoming a named cause of the coming evil.
