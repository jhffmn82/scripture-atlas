sin and plagues

topic · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: All these plagues, great plagues, iniquities, sinned, the plagues, these plagues

Abstract written by a model

A punishment pronounced by the Lord against Israel's leaders and people, written in the law of Moses and unprecedented under heaven, including eating one's own children. The people acknowledge the plagues came as pronounced, killing kings and fathers by famine, sword, and pestilence. They then confess sin, admit their fathers disobeyed God, accept the penalty, and ask that their fathers' iniquities not be remembered.

Chapter 2

The great plagues, such as never happened before under heaven and as occurred in Jerusalem, include eating the flesh of one's own son and daughter, as written in the law of Moses. The people state that all these plagues have come as the Lord pronounced, and that their kings and fathers died in great miseries by famine, sword, and pestilence.

Chapter 3

The people confess that they have sinned before God and that their fathers did not listen to his voice, departing from him. Because of this, plagues cling to them, and they are subject to penalty according to their fathers' iniquities; they ask that those iniquities not be remembered.
