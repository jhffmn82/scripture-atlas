Abstract written by a model

The readers are the addressees of a letter, a community of believers addressed as brothers. They have Jesus as high priest, a confession to hold tightly, hope as refuge, and bold access to the holy place through his blood. The letter unfolds in three movements. In chapter 4 they are urged to enter God's rest, avoid disobedience, and approach the throne of grace for mercy. In chapter 6 the author, though stern, is persuaded of better things for them, notes God will not forget their service to the saints, and urges diligence and imitation of those who inherited the promises. In chapter 10 they are urged to draw near in full faith, encourage love and good works, and keep assembling. They are reminded of past suffering after enlightenment, including compassion for the author in chains and joyful loss of possessions, and are told to keep their boldness, endure, and not shrink back.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Believers are said to enter that rest and are urged to fear falling short, to give diligence to enter it, and to avoid the example of disobedience. They are told to hold tightly to their confession and to draw near with boldness to the throne of grace to receive mercy and find grace for help in time of need.

Chapter 6

The author is persuaded of better things for them, things that accompany salvation, though he warns them sternly. God will not forget their work and labor of love in serving the saints, and they are urged to show diligence to the end and imitate those who inherited the promises through faith and perseverance.

Chapter 10

The readers, addressed as brothers, are urged to enter the holy place by Jesus' blood, draw near with a true heart in full faith, hold fast their confession, and provoke one another to love and good works without forsaking assembling together. They are reminded of their earlier endurance after being enlightened: reproaches, oppressions, compassion for the writer in chains, and joyful acceptance of plundered possessions. They are told not to throw away their boldness, to endure to receive the promise, and are counted among those who have faith to the saving of the soul rather than those who shrink back.
