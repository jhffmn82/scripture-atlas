Artaxerxes in The Book of Ezra

author unknown · 399 to 299 BC · the work’s page · read it where Artaxerxes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Artaxerxes is a Persian king who styles himself king of kings and is advised by seven counselors. In Chapter 4, after receiving Rehum and Shimshai's letter, he searches the records, finds Jerusalem's rebellious past, and orders construction halted until he decrees otherwise. In Chapter 7, in his seventh year, he reverses course for Ezra: permitting Israelites to go, funding them from the treasury, exempting temple personnel from tribute, and enforcing God's law and his own.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Artaxerxes king of Persia receives the letter from Rehum, Shimshai, and their companions. He has the records searched, finds that Jerusalem rebelled in the past and was ruled by mighty kings who received tribute, and decrees that the men cease and the city not be built until he makes a decree.

Chapter 7

Artaxerxes, king of Persia, in his seventh year granted Ezra all his request and wrote a letter calling himself king of kings. He decreed that willing Israelites could go to Jerusalem, supplied silver, gold, and further resources from the royal treasury, exempted temple personnel from taxes, and ordered judgment on any who disobey the law of God or the king. He explained his generosity by asking why wrath should fall on his realm and his sons.

Artaxerxes in The Book of Nehemiah

author unknown · about 399 to 299 BC · the work’s page · read it where Artaxerxes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Artaxerxes is a king with a queen who, in his twentieth year, notices the narrator's sadness, grants his request to go to Judah, and supplies letters to governors, Asaph for timber, and army escorts.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

In his twentieth year, Artaxerxes notices the narrator's sadness and asks his request. He grants the request to send him to Judah, asks how long the journey will be, gives letters to the governors beyond the River and to Asaph for timber, and sends army captains and horsemen with him.
