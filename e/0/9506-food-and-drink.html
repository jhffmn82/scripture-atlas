Abstract written by a model

Food and drink are alms for monks and ascetics, to be collected only when freely given under sanctioned rules and eaten moderately at the proper time. The ascetic must know the permitted quantity, avoid faults, wait apart, and accept food prepared for householders without attachment or disdain, including humble liquids and fare from lowly houses. In Harikêsa-Bala’s case, Brahmanas first refuse their prepared food, then seek forgiveness and offer boiled rice with condiments and other food and drink after his whole-month fast; he accepts it. Discipline in thought, word, and deed remains essential.

Uttaradhyayana Sutra / First Lecture.

A monk should collect freely given alms according to sanctioned rules and eat a moderate portion at the proper time. He should wait alone at a suitable distance from other monks and accept permitted food prepared for somebody else.

Sixth Lecture.

An ascetic should know the permitted quantity of food and drink. He should eat food prepared by householders for their own consumption and receive alms while avoiding faults.

Twelfth Lecture

The Brahmanas initially refuse Harikêsa the food and drink prepared for them. After seeking forgiveness, they offer him boiled rice seasoned with many condiments and other food and drink, which he accepts after fasting for a whole month.

Fifteenth Lecture.

A monk may receive food and drink, including dainties and spices, but he must remain disciplined in thoughts, words, and deeds. He should not despise dish-water, barley-pap, cold sour gruel, or water in which barley has been washed, and should call at the lowliest houses for alms.
