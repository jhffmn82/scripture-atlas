Chu in Mencius, Book I (Liang Hui Wang)

Mencius · the work’s page · read it where Chu first appears

Abstract written by a model

Chu is a great country and power south of Jin, contrasted with the smaller Zou. It disgraces Jin, and its ruler burdens the people through hardship. As Teng lies between kingdoms, Duke Wen considers whether Teng should serve Chu.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Chu inflicted disgrace on Jin. Mencius says Chu's ruler robs the people of their time and drives them into hardship.

Chapter 7

Chu is presented as a great power in comparison with Zou. Mencius uses the example of Zou fighting Chu to show that a small country cannot contend with a great one.

Chapter 13

Chu is identified as one of the kingdoms between which Teng lies. Duke Wen of Teng considers whether Teng should serve Chu.

Chu in Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Chu first appears

Abstract written by a model

Chu is a destination visited by the prince later duke Wen of Teng, and a hostile power that hates and attacks Song; Mencius says Song’s royal government would make Chu less fearful.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong) / Part I / Chapter 1

Chu is the destination to which the prince, afterwards duke Wen of Teng, travels. The prince visits Mencius on the way to Chu and again while returning from it.

Chapter 5

Chu hates and attacks the ruler of Song. Mencius says that if Song truly practised royal government, Chu would not be much to fear.
