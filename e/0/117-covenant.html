Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the covenant was the LORD's agreement with Moses and Israel, promising marvels and driving out the land's inhabitants, while requiring exclusive worship and observance of its laws, whose words were written on tablets as the ten commandments. In Deuteronomy, the covenant at Horeb was made by the LORD with the living Israelites present that day, not their fathers, and contained the commandments spoken from the fire. In 2 Corinthians, the old covenant is a service of death and condemnation, engraved on stone tablets, whose letter kills, with fading glory and a veil removed only in Christ. In The Letter to the Hebrews, the first covenant was a system of divine service ordinances and an earthly sanctuary, imposed as fleshly rules until reformation, dedicated with blood by Moses, yet unable to perfect worshipers' conscience.

covenant in Exodus

author unknown · about 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where covenant first appears

Abstract written by a model

The covenant is the LORD's agreement with Moses and Israel, promising marvels and driving out the land's inhabitants. Its terms forbid covenants with inhabitants and idols, and require exclusive worship, feasts, Sabbath and firstborn laws; the words are written on tablets as the ten commandments.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 34

The LORD makes a covenant, promising marvels before all the people and the driving out of the land's inhabitants, and tells Moses to write the words on which it rests with him and Israel. Its terms include no covenants with the inhabitants, no idols, and keeping the feasts, the Sabbath, and the firstborn laws. The words are written on the tablets as the ten commandments.

old covenant in 2 Corinthians

Paul · AD 55 to 56 · the work’s page · read it where old covenant first appears

Abstract written by a model

The old covenant is a service of death and condemnation, engraved on stone tablets, whose letter kills. It came with fading glory, and a veil still remains over its reading, removed only in Christ.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

The old covenant is described as a service of death and condemnation, engraved on stones, which came with glory that was passing away. The same veil remains at its reading to this day, because the veil passes away only in Christ. The letter, in contrast to the Spirit, kills.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 9

The first covenant had ordinances of divine service and an earthly sanctuary, with fleshly ordinances imposed until a time of reformation. It was dedicated with blood when Moses sprinkled the book and the people, saying 'This is the blood of the covenant which God has commanded you.' Its gifts and sacrifices could not make the worshiper perfect in conscience.
