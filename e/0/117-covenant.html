Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the covenant is the LORD's agreement with Moses and Israel, promising marvels and driving out the land's inhabitants, with terms requiring exclusive worship, feasts, Sabbath and firstborn laws, and the words written on tablets as the ten commandments. In Deuteronomy, the covenant was made by the LORD at Horeb with the living Israelites present that day, consisting of commandments spoken from the fire. In 2 Corinthians, the old covenant is a service of death and condemnation engraved on stone tablets, whose letter kills, with fading glory and a veil removed only in Christ. In The Letter to the Hebrews, the first covenant was a system of divine service ordinances and an earthly sanctuary imposed as fleshly rules until a time of reformation, dedicated with blood by Moses, whose sacrifices could not perfect worshipers' conscience. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), the covenant requires worship of Allah alone, kindness, truthful speech, worship, and payment of the poor-due, while Allah promises fulfillment and warns the Children of Israel against breaking it. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), their covenant is broken by the people, while Allah takes from them a firm covenant and causes the Mount to tower above them.

Chapter 34

The LORD makes a covenant, promising marvels before all the people and the driving out of the land's inhabitants, and tells Moses to write the words on which it rests with him and Israel. Its terms include no covenants with the inhabitants, no idols, and keeping the feasts, the Sabbath, and the firstborn laws. The words are written on the tablets as the ten commandments.
