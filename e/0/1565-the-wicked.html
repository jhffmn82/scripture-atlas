Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Job, the wicked prosper rather than suffer, living long, growing mighty, enjoying safe houses, thriving livestock and children, rejecting God, dying suddenly, going down to Sheol, and being carried to a watched tomb, reserved for the day of calamity and wrath. In Psalms, the wicked witness the blessing and honor of the God-fearing man, grieve and gnash their teeth, then melt away as their desire perishes. In Psalms, the LORD cuts apart the cords of the wicked, depicting them as restrained or freed from their hold by this act. In Psalms, the wicked are bloodthirsty enemies who speak wickedly against God and take his name in vain; David asks God to kill them, bids them depart, and hates them perfectly. In Psalms, the wicked are called workers of iniquity, and David asks that their hand not drive him away; they are ultimately described as fallen, thrust down, and unable to rise. In Psalms, the wicked hate instruction, forget God, cast his words aside while claiming his covenant, side with thieves and adulterers, give their mouths to evil and deceit, slander their own brother, and face God's rebuke and accusation. In Psalms, the wicked are corrupt from birth, wayward, lying, venomous like a deaf cobra, and plotting injustice and violence; the speaker prays for their destruction and disappearance. In Psalms, the wicked are destroyed by the LORD and snared by their own handiwork, finally turned back to Sheol together with all nations that forget God.

the wicked in The Book of Job

author unknown · about 574 BC · the work’s page · read it where the wicked first appears

Abstract written by a model

The wicked, as described in Chapter 21 and observed by Job, are a group who prosper rather than suffer: they live long, grow mighty, enjoy safe houses, thriving livestock and children who dance and sing. They reject God, telling him to depart and seeing no profit in serving him. They die suddenly, go down to Sheol, and are carried to a watched tomb, unchallenged and unrepaid, though reserved for the day of calamity and wrath.

Chapter 21

The wicked are described as living, growing old, and becoming mighty, with children established before their eyes, safe houses, and children who dance and sing. They tell God to depart and ask what profit there is in serving the Almighty. They go down to Sheol in an instant, are carried to the grave and watched over at the tomb, and no one declares their way to their face or repays them.
