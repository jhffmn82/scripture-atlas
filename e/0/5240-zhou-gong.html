Zhou Gong

Abstract written by a model

In Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou), Zhou Gong is described as an ancient sage who continues king Wen's course with king Wu and appoints Guan Shu to oversee Yin's heir, unaware that Guan Shu will rebel. In Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong), Zhou Gong is presented as a sage who assists king Wu in destroying the tyrant Zhou, punishes Yan and other enemies, extinguishes fifty States, removes ferocious animals, and is associated with ancient doctrines and praise-songs.

Chapter 9

Zhou Gong appointed Guan Shu to oversee the heir of Yin without knowing that Guan Shu would rebel. Mencius said that, because Guan Shu was Zhou Gong's elder brother, the appointment was in accordance with what was right.

Zhou Gong in Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Zhou Gong first appears

Abstract written by a model

Zhou Gong is a sage associated with ancient doctrines. He assists king Wu in destroying the tyrant Zhou, then smites Yan and executes its sovereign after three years. He drives Fei Lian to the sea and slays him, extinguishes fifty States, and removes ferocious animals from the country. His doctrines are studied by Chen Liang, and Mencius cites praise-songs describing his punishments of western and northern barbarians, Jing, and Shu.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Zhou Gong's doctrines are among those studied by Chen Liang. Mencius cites the Praise-songs of Lu as saying that Zhou Gong smote the barbarians of the west and north and punished Jing and Shu.

Chapter 9

Zhou Gong assisted king Wu and destroyed Zhou. He smote Yan, put its sovereign to death after three years, drove Fei Lian to the sea and slew him, extinguished fifty States, and drove away ferocious animals.
