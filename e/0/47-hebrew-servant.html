Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the Hebrew servant serves six years, goes free in the seventh, and may serve forever if he declares love for his household. In Deuteronomy, the Hebrew servant serves six years, goes free in the seventh with provisions, and may serve forever if he declares love for his master and household.

Hebrew servant in Exodus

author unknown · about 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hebrew servant first appears

Abstract written by a model

A Hebrew servant is bought by a master and serves six years, going out free in the seventh without payment, taking his wife if he came married. A wife given by the master stays with her children. If he declares love for his household, his ear is bored and he serves forever.

Chapter 21

A bought Hebrew servant serves six years and goes out free in the seventh without payment, taking his wife if he came married. If his master gave him a wife, she and her children stay with the master. A servant who declares love for his master, wife and children has his ear bored with an awl at the door or doorpost and serves forever.

Hebrew servant (the slave sold to a fellow Israelite) in Deuteronomy

author unknown · 624 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hebrew servant (the slave sold to a fellow Israelite) first appears

Abstract written by a model

A Hebrew man or woman sold to a fellow Israelite serves six years and goes free in the seventh, not empty-handed but liberally furnished from the master's flock, threshing floor, and wine press. If the servant says he will not leave because he loves the master and his house, the master pierces his ear with an awl to the door and he serves forever. The master is told the servant has been worth double a hired hand.
