Abstract written by a model

Government is presented as a ruler’s means of practicing benevolence and, in Qi, potentially gaining royal authority quickly. It is injured when evils of the mind are displayed in it, harming the conduct of affairs amid order or disorder. In leisure from external troubles, a prince should digest its principles and legal sanctions, demonstrating understanding of governing.

Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou) / Chapter 1

Government is discussed as something that Guan Zhong directed for a long period and as the means through which a ruler could put benevolent government into practice. Mencius argues that effective government in Qi could quickly produce royal authority.

Chapter 2

Mencius says that evils growing in the mind injure government and that, when displayed in government, they harm the conduct of affairs. He also discusses how sages and rulers would act in times of good government and disorder.

Chapter 4

A prince should use periods of leisure and rest from external troubles to clearly digest the principles of his government with its legal sanctions. Confucius says the cited ode demonstrates understanding of the way of governing.
