The Book of Zechariah

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This document is the book of the prophet Zechariah, son of Berechiah and son of Iddo, a series of visions and oracles dated to the reign of Darius. It centers on the LORD (the LORD of Armies), his jealous love for Jerusalem and Zion, and the restoration of his people and temple. Key figures are Zechariah, the interpreting angel, Joshua the high priest, Zerubbabel, and the promised Branch and humble King. Chapters 1-6 present night visions. After a call to return to the LORD, Zechariah sees horsemen among myrtle trees, and the LORD answers with mercy for Jerusalem after seventy years. Further visions follow: horns and craftsmen, a man measuring Jerusalem, Joshua's filthy garments replaced with clean clothing, a golden lamp stand with two olive trees and the word 'Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit' to Zerubbabel, a flying scroll of curse, a woman called Wickedness carried to Shinar, and four chariots going through the earth. Crowns are made for Joshua, with the Branch promised to build the temple and rule as priest. Chapters 7-8 turn to a question from Bethel about continued fasting. The LORD challenges their motives, recalls the former prophets' demands for justice and compassion, and recounts how the ancestors' hardness led to scattering. He then promises to dwell in Jerusalem, the City of Truth, turning fasts into joyful feasts, and nations will seek him. Chapters 9-14 are oracles. Judgment falls on neighboring cities, while Zion's righteous, lowly King comes on a donkey, speaking peace to nations. The LORD promises to restore Judah and Ephraim and condemns false shepherds. The narrator then acts as a shepherd with two staffs, Favor and Union, is paid thirty pieces of silver thrown to the potter, and breaks both staffs, and a worthless shepherd is announced. Jerusalem is to be besieged, yet the LORD defends it; a spirit of grace is poured out, and the people mourn for one they pierced. A fountain cleanses sin, idols and false prophets are removed, and a refined remnant calls on his name. The book ends with a final day when nations attack Jerusalem, the LORD stands on the split Mount of Olives, becomes King over all the earth, living waters flow, survivors keep the feast of booths, and everything becomes holy to the LORD.

Chapter 1

In the second year of Darius, the LORD's word comes to the prophet Zechariah, son of Berechiah and son of Iddo, first calling the people to return to him and not be like their fathers, who ignored the former prophets yet later admitted the LORD had dealt with them as their ways deserved. On the twenty-fourth day of the eleventh month, Zechariah has a night vision of a man on a red horse among myrtle trees with red, brown, and white horses that have walked through the earth and found it at rest. The LORD's angel asks how long the LORD will withhold mercy from Jerusalem and the cities of Judah after seventy years, and the LORD answers with kind words. Through the interpreting angel the LORD proclaims that he is jealous for Jerusalem and Zion, angry with the nations at ease, and has returned to Jerusalem with mercy so that his house will be built and his cities will overflow with prosperity. Zechariah then sees four horns that scattered Judah, Israel, and Jerusalem, and four craftsmen who come to terrify them and cast down the horns of the nations.

Chapter 2

The narrator sees a man with a measuring line who is going to measure Jerusalem's width and length. The angel who talked with the narrator goes out, and another angel meets him and sends a message for the young man: Jerusalem will be inhabited as unwalled villages because of its many people and livestock, with the LORD as a wall of fire around it and the glory within it. The LORD calls those in the land of the north and those dwelling with the daughter of Babylon to flee, and says he will shake his hand over the nations that plundered Zion's people so they become plunder for their former servants. Zion is told to sing and rejoice because the LORD comes to dwell within her, many nations will join themselves to him, and he will again choose Jerusalem and inherit Judah. All flesh is told to be silent because the LORD has roused himself from his holy habitation.

Chapter 3

In a vision, Joshua the high priest stands before the LORD's angel while Satan stands at his right hand as adversary. The LORD rebukes Satan and calls Joshua a burning stick plucked from the fire. Joshua's filthy garments are removed, his iniquity is declared passed from him, and he is clothed with rich clothing and given a clean turban. The LORD's angel then charges Joshua that if he walks in the LORD's ways and follows his instructions, he will judge and keep the LORD's house and courts and have access among those standing by. The LORD of Armies announces that he will bring out his servant, the Branch, engrave an inscription on a stone with seven eyes, remove the land's iniquity in one day, and that people will then invite neighbors under vine and fig tree.

Chapter 4

The angel who talked with the narrator wakes him and asks what he sees. The narrator describes a golden lamp stand with a bowl, seven lamps, seven pipes to each lamp, and two olive trees beside it. He does not understand the vision, so the angel gives the LORD's word to Zerubbabel: "Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit." The LORD of Armies promises that Zerubbabel, who laid the foundation of the house, will also finish it, and that the great mountain before him will become a plain. The angel explains that the seven are the LORD's eyes running through the whole earth, and that the two olive trees are the two anointed ones who stand by the Lord of the whole earth.

Chapter 5

The narrator sees a flying scroll twenty cubits long and ten wide, which an unnamed speaker explains is a curse going out over the whole land against thieves and those who swear falsely. The LORD of Armies declares he will send it into the houses of such people, where it will remain and destroy the house with its timber and stones. The angel who talked with the narrator then shows him an ephah basket appearing, with a lead cover lifted to reveal a woman sitting inside, whom the angel names Wickedness. The angel throws her back into the basket and weights its mouth with lead. Two winged women, with the wind in their wings like those of a stork, lift the basket between earth and sky and carry it to the land of Shinar, where a house will be built for her and she will be set in her own place.

Chapter 6

In a vision, the narrator sees four chariots, drawn by red, black, white, and dappled horses, come out from between two bronze mountains. The angel who talks with him explains that they are the four winds of the sky, going out from standing before the Lord of all the earth, and they walk back and forth through the earth, with those going north quieting the LORD's spirit there. The LORD's word then tells the narrator to take silver and gold from the exiles Heldai, Tobijah, and Jedaiah, who have come from Babylon to the house of Josiah the son of Zephaniah, and make crowns for Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest. Joshua is to be told of the man named the Branch, who will grow up out of his place, build the LORD's temple, bear glory, and rule as a priest on his throne, and the crowns will be a memorial in the temple. Those far off will come and build, and the promise depends on diligent obedience to the LORD's voice.

Chapter 7

In the fourth year of King Darius, the word of the LORD came to Zechariah on the fourth day of the ninth month, Chislev, after the people of Bethel sent Sharezer, Regem Melech and their men to ask the priests and prophets whether they should keep weeping in the fifth month as they had for years. The LORD of Armies answered through Zechariah, asking the people of the land and the priests whether their fasts of seventy years in the fifth and seventh months were truly for him, and noting that they ate and drank for themselves. He reminded them of the words of the former prophets, who had commanded true judgment, kindness, compassion, and no oppression of the widow, fatherless, foreigner, or poor. Their ancestors refused to listen, turned their backs, stopped their ears and hardened their hearts like flint, so great wrath came from the LORD. He declared that, as they would not listen when he called, he would not listen when they called, and he scattered them among the nations in a whirlwind, leaving the pleasant land desolate.

Chapter 8

In Chapter 8, the LORD of Armies declares his jealous love for Zion and announces his return to dwell in Jerusalem, which will be called the City of Truth. He promises a restored city with old people and playing children, the gathering of his people from east and west, and an end to the poverty and insecurity of former days for the remnant. He turns the house of Judah and house of Israel from a curse into a blessing, commands them to speak truth, judge with truth and peace, and avoid evil plots and false oaths. The fasts of the fourth, fifth, seventh, and tenth months will become joyful feasts. Many peoples and strong nations will come to Jerusalem to seek the LORD, and ten men from every language will grasp the skirt of a Jew because God is with them.

Chapter 9

Chapter 9 opens with the LORD's word against Hadrach, Damascus, Hamath, Tyre and Sidon, announcing that Tyre's wealth and stronghold will not save her from being struck and burned. The Philistine cities Ashkelon, Gaza, Ekron and Ashdod will fear and lose their king and pride, though a remnant will belong to God. The LORD promises to encamp around his house, and the daughter of Zion is told to rejoice because her righteous, lowly King comes riding a donkey, cutting off chariot, horse and bow and speaking peace to the nations. The LORD frees the prisoners of hope, promises them double restoration, and arms Judah and Ephraim like a bow and sword against Greece. He appears with lightning and the trumpet, defends and saves his people like a flock, and the chapter ends with praise of his goodness and the flourishing of young men and virgins.

Chapter 10

Chapter 10 urges the people to ask the LORD for spring rain, contrasting him with the teraphim and diviners who speak vanity and false dreams, leaving the people like sheep with no shepherd. The LORD's anger is kindled against the shepherds and male goats, and he visits his flock, the house of Judah, making them like a majestic war horse. From Judah will come the cornerstone, tent peg, battle bow and every ruler, and they will fight and confound the horse riders because the LORD is with them. The LORD promises to strengthen Judah and save the house of Joseph, bringing them back with mercy, so that Ephraim rejoices like a mighty man. He will signal, gather, redeem and sow them among the peoples, then return them from Egypt and Assyria to Gilead and Lebanon, while Assyria's pride is brought down and Egypt's scepter departs.

Chapter 11

Chapter 11 opens with a lament over the ruin of Lebanon's cedars, the oaks of Bashan, and the shepherds' glory. The LORD tells the narrator to feed the flock of slaughter, whose buyers and sellers exploit them and whose own shepherds show no pity, and declares He will no longer pity the inhabitants of the land. The narrator takes two staffs, Favor and Union, cuts off three shepherds in one month, and then cuts Favor apart to break the covenant with all the peoples. He is paid thirty pieces of silver as wages, which the LORD tells him to throw to the potter in the LORD's house, and he then cuts Union apart to break the brotherhood between Judah and Israel. The LORD commands him to take the equipment of a foolish shepherd, announces a worthless shepherd who will abandon the flock, and pronounces that the sword will wither his arm and blind his right eye.

Chapter 12

Chapter 12 is a revelation from the LORD, who stretches out the heavens, lays the earth's foundation, and forms the human spirit, concerning Israel. He declares that Jerusalem will become a cup of reeling and a burdensome stone to the surrounding peoples, who will gather against it and be wounded, while he strikes their horses and riders with terror, madness, and blindness. The chieftains of Judah will become like a pan of fire and a flaming torch that devour the surrounding peoples, and the LORD will save Judah's tents first and defend Jerusalem's inhabitants, making even the feeble like David. He will seek to destroy all nations that come against Jerusalem and will pour out a spirit of grace and supplication on David's house and Jerusalem's inhabitants. They will look to the one they pierced and mourn for him as for an only son, in a great mourning with each family and their wives apart.

Chapter 13

Chapter 13 describes a coming day when the LORD of Armies opens a fountain to David's house and the inhabitants of Jerusalem for sin and uncleanness. He will cut off the names of idols and remove the prophets and the spirit of impurity from the land. Anyone who still prophesies will be killed by his own parents for speaking lies in the LORD's name, and prophets will be ashamed and deny their calling. The LORD then calls the sword against his shepherd, whose being struck scatters the sheep and turns his hand against the little ones. Two parts of the land will be cut off and die, while the remaining third will be put through fire and refined like silver and gold, call on the LORD's name, and be acknowledged as his people.

Chapter 14

Chapter 14 describes a coming day of the LORD in which all nations are gathered against Jerusalem, which is taken and half its people exiled. The LORD then goes out to fight, stands on the Mount of Olives and splits it, creating a valley of escape. A plague strikes the attacking peoples, and Judah fights at Jerusalem and gathers great wealth. The LORD becomes King over all the earth, living waters flow from Jerusalem, and the land is made like the Arabah while Jerusalem dwells safely. Surviving nations must go up yearly to keep the feast of booths or receive no rain, as Egypt is warned, and everything in Jerusalem and Judah becomes holy to the LORD.
