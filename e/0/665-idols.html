Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Solomon, idols are described as lifeless, hand-made objects that cannot move, help themselves, or endure. In The Book of Isaiah, idols are human-made objects worshipped throughout the land and destined to pass away. In The Book of Ezekiel, idols and images are made from the gold and silver God gave Jerusalem and are associated with abominations, high places, and child sacrifice. In The Book of Ezekiel, idols include Egypt's and Israel's fathers' idols, which Israel followed and to which it offered gifts and sacrifices that profaned God's name. In The Book of Hosea, idols are identified as the work of human hands that the people are told to stop calling their gods. In Surah 21: The Prophets (Al-Anbiyaa), the idols worshipped by Abraham's father and people are broken into fragments by Abraham except for their chief. In Surah 25: The Criterion (Al-Furqaan), other gods are chosen beside Allah but have no power to create, harm, benefit, give life, or raise the dead. In Surah 26: The Poets (Ash-Shu'araa), idols are worshipped by Abraham's folk, but Abraham declares them enemies unable to hear, benefit, or harm.

and 2 more works

In Surah 29: The Spider (Al-Ankaboot), idols are served instead of Allah but provide no provision, and their followers will deny and curse one another on the Day of Resurrection. In Surah 37: Those drawn up in Ranks (As-Saaffaat), gods worshiped beside Allah are carved objects unable to eat, speak, or protect themselves, and Abraham attacks them.

idols (works of men's hands) in The Wisdom of Solomon

Abstract written by a model

Idols are hand-made objects of gold, silver, stone, or carved wood, formed from things God created. They are lifeless, weak, and unable to move or help themselves, so they must be secured in a wall with iron; their hands have no ability. They are accursed together with their makers, an abomination, a stumbling block and snare to the foolish. They did not exist from the beginning and will not last, having entered the world through human boastfulness, so a speedy end was planned and a visitation is coming upon them.

Chapter 13

Idols are described as gold and silver skillfully made, likenesses of animals, or a useless stone from an ancient hand. The carved wooden image is secured in a wall with iron because it cannot help itself, and it is lifeless, weak, without experience, unable to move a step, and has hands with no ability.

Chapter 14

Idols are described as hand-made things, formed from what God created, that were made an abomination, stumbling blocks to souls and a snare to the feet of the foolish. They did not exist from the beginning and will not last forever, because they entered the world through human boastfulness and a speedy end was planned for them. A visitation is coming upon them.
