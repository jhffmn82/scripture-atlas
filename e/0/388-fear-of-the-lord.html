Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Job, it is declared by God to man to be wisdom and is paired with departing from evil as understanding. In Psalms, it is declared the beginning of wisdom, and those who fear the LORD are given food. In Proverbs, it is described as a virtue that prolongs days, aligns with righteousness, and is better than revenues gained through injustice. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, it is presented as the foundation and summit of wisdom, bringing glory, joy, peace, health, and a blessed end. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, it is praised as better than riches and strength and as a garden of blessing.

fear of the Lord in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · about 179 BC · the work’s page · read it where fear of the Lord first appears

Abstract written by a model

The fear of the Lord is presented in Sirach as the foundation and summit of wisdom: its beginning, fullness, crown, and root. It is glory, exultation, gladness, and a crown of rejoicing, giving joy, length of days, peace, perfect health, and a blessed end. Chapter 1 warns against disobeying it or approaching the Lord with a double heart, and says deceitful people lacking it will be cast down. Chapter 23 shows those left behind learning that nothing is better or sweeter than it, alongside heeding the commandments. Chapter 25 calls it the glory of the aged, surpassing all things, with no one greater than one who holds it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus / Chapter 1

The fear of the Lord is described as the beginning, fullness, crown, and root of wisdom, and as glory, exultation, gladness, and a crown of rejoicing. It delights the heart, gives length of days, makes peace and perfect health flourish, and brings a blessed end to those who hold it. Readers are told not to disobey it or to come to the Lord with a double heart, and those who lack it and are full of deceit will be cast down.

Chapter 23

Those left behind after the punishment of the adulterous wife will learn that nothing is better than the fear of the Lord. They will also learn that nothing is sweeter than heeding the Lord's commandments.

Chapter 25

The fear of the Lord is called the glory of the aged and said to surpass all things. No one is greater than the one who fears the Lord, and the question is asked to whom its holder could be compared.
