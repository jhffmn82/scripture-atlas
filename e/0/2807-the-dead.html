the dead

group · 4 works · 8 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: dead, his body, his remembrance, the dead (those in the grave), the departed spirits, the great and the small, the slain, those who go down into the pit

Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the dead are described as the slain lying in the grave, cut off from God's hand and unable to rise to praise the LORD. In Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher, the dead are described as knowing nothing, having no reward, and being forgotten, with their love, hatred, and envy perished. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the dead are people who deserve burial with due honor and whose mourning should end after a day or two. In Revelation, the dead do not live until the thousand years are finished, and later the great and small are given up by the sea, Death, and Hades to be judged.

the dead in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the dead first appears

Abstract written by a model

The dead are people who deserve burial with due honor. They should be mourned with tears and lamentation, wrapped and buried without neglect; after a day or two sorrow should end, mindful of one's own death, and remembrance should rest too.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 38

The dead are to be mourned with tears and lamentation, and their bodies wrapped with due honor and buried without neglect. After a day or two the mourner is to let sorrow go, remembering that there is no returning and that his own end will follow, "yesterday for him, and today for you." When the dead is at rest, his remembrance is to rest too.
