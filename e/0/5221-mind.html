mind in Chuang Tzŭ

Chuang Tzŭ · the work’s page · read it where mind first appears

Abstract written by a model

The mind should guide judgment without fixed opinions, like a tabula rasa and a mirror in the perfect man. Subject to indecision, fear, concealment, passion, and changing perturbations, it receives without keeping, grasps and refuses nothing, comprehends the whole universe, informs the body, rises with life, and sets at death; yet something remains amid change.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

The mind is subject to indecision, fear, concealment, passion, and other changing perturbations. It should be without fixed criteria, like a tabula rasa, so that it can accept things as they are rather than as they appear.

CHAPTER XXI.

The mind is described as what informs the body, rises with life, and sets at death. Its death is more grievous than bodily death, although something that does not pass away remains amid the changing self.

the human mind in Mencius, Book VI (Gaozi)

Mencius · the work’s page · read it where the human mind first appears

Abstract written by a model

The human mind has Heaven-given, proper goodness and senses, yet neglect and harmful circumstances can strip or decay it. Learning seeks its recovery: proper nourishment lets goodness grow, while thinking fulfills its office by gaining the right view of things.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Part I / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 16

The human mind contains proper goodness but can be stripped of it through repeated harmful influences, like the trees of Niu mountain. It is the object that learning must seek to recover, and through thinking it obtains the right view of things.
