Abstract written by a model

In Numbers, the twelve spies explore Canaan for forty days and return with fruit and a report; ten spread an evil report, die by plague, while Joshua and Caleb survive. In Deuteronomy, the twelve spies, one from each tribe, search the land, bring back fruit from Eshcol, and report that it is a good land the LORD gives.

the twelve spies in Numbers

author unknown · 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where the twelve spies first appears

Abstract written by a model

Twelve men, each a prince of his tribe, are sent to explore Canaan and return after forty days with fruit and a report. They agree the land is rich, but ten declare the people too strong, spreading an evil report that makes the congregation murmur against Moses. Those ten die by plague; only Joshua and Caleb survive.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

Twelve men, one prince from each tribe, are sent to explore Canaan from Zin to Rehob and Hebron, and they return after forty days with fruit and a report. They agree the land is rich but ten of them, those who went up with Caleb, declare the people too strong to attack and spread an evil report.

Chapter 14

The men Moses sent to spy out the land return with an evil report that makes the congregation murmur against him. Those who brought the evil report die by plague before the LORD. Only Joshua and Caleb remain alive of the spies.
