Abstract written by a model

Divisions in Corinth arose as groups claimed allegiance to Paul, Apollos, Cephas, or Christ. In Chapter 1, Paul, informed by Chloe's household, urges unity of mind and judgment, rebuking the factions with pointed questions. In Chapter 3, he returns to the Paul-versus-Apollos factions, citing jealousy and strife as proof the believers are fleshly.

Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians / Chapter 1

Paul learned from those of Chloe's household that contentions existed among the Corinthians, with groups saying they follow Paul, Apollos, Cephas, or Christ. Paul begs them to speak the same thing and be perfected in the same mind and judgment, and rebukes the divisions with questions about whether Christ is divided and whether Paul was crucified for them.

Chapter 3

Factions appear among the Corinthians as some say they follow Paul and others Apollos. Paul cites these, along with jealousy and strife, as evidence that the believers are fleshly and walk in the ways of men.
