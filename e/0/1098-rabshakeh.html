Rabshakeh in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Rabshakeh first appears

Abstract written by a model

Rabshakeh is the Assyrian envoy serving Sennacherib's king. Sent with Tartan and Rabsaris, he stands by the upper pool and shouts in the Jews' language to the wall's people, mocking Hezekiah's trust in Egypt and the LORD and offering peace and resettlement. His words defying God alarm Hezekiah; he then rejoins the king at Libnah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

Rabshakeh, sent with Tartan and Rabsaris by the king of Assyria, stood by the upper pool conduit and spoke to Eliakim, Shebnah, and Joah. He refused their request to speak in the Syrian language and instead cried out in the Jews' language to the people on the wall. He mocked Hezekiah's trust in Egypt and the LORD and offered the people peace and resettlement if they surrendered.

Chapter 19

Rabshakeh is the Assyrian envoy whose words defying the living God alarm Hezekiah. He returns to find the king of Assyria warring against Libnah, having heard the king had left Lachish.

Rabshakeh in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Rabshakeh first appears

Abstract written by a model

Rabshakeh is the Assyrian king's envoy, sent from Lachish to Jerusalem with a large army. By the aqueduct he mocks Judah's trust in Egypt and the LORD, refuses Aramaic, and calls out in the Jews' language, offering peace and a good land for surrender while denying any god can deliver from Assyria. Hezekiah's messengers later say his words defy the living God; he returns to find the king fighting at Libnah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 36

Rabshakeh is sent by the king of Assyria from Lachish to Jerusalem with a large army and stands by the aqueduct. He mocks Judah's trust in Egypt and in the LORD, claims the LORD told him to destroy the land, and refuses to speak Aramaic. He calls out in the Jews' language to the people on the wall, offering them peace and a good land if they surrender and denying that any god can deliver from Assyria.

Chapter 37

Rabshakeh is the Assyrian king's envoy whose words Hezekiah's messengers say were sent to defy the living God. He returns and finds the king warring against Libnah, having departed from Lachish. Hezekiah's envoys hope the LORD will rebuke his words.
