Abstract written by a model

In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I, passions are subdued through desirelessness and hard control because desires for wealth, pleasures, life, and worldly goods bring suffering and violence. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, desire originates in ignorance and delusion and ends when greed and desire for sensory pleasures are absent. In The Tao Teh King, or The Tao and Its Characteristics, desires can excite people when displayed, while few desires support attainment and many lead people astray. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), desires for bodily pleasures are natural, but few desires best nourish the mind while many make keeping the heart difficult.

Abstract written by a model

Passions are to be subdued through desirelessness and hard control: desire for wealth, pleasures, life, and worldly goods brings suffering and violence, while lust makes people sensual, cowardly, and attached to life, causing their decline.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Second Lecture, Called Conquest of the World. / First Lesson. .. Sixth Lesson.

Desire for wealth, treasures, pleasures, life, and worldly goods causes people to suffer and commit violence. Desire should be subdued by desirelessness and abandoned with the world.

Sixth Lecture, Called the Cleaning. / First Lesson. .. Fifth Lesson.

Lust causes men to become sensual, cowardly, and attached to life. Those whom lust conquers sink, so they are urged not to shrink from hard control.

Abstract written by a model

Desire originates ignorance and delusion, yet arises from delusion; it ends when greed and desire for sensory pleasures are absent.

Thirty-Second Lecture.

Desire is called an origin of delusion, while delusion is also called an origin of desire. Desire ceases with the absence of greed, and the desire for pleasures becomes extinct when sensory objects are no longer desired.

desires in The Tao Teh King, or The Tao and Its Characteristics

Lao-Tse · the work’s page · read it where desires first appears

Abstract written by a model

Desires can excite people when displayed. The sage avoids showing what would stir them, seeking minds without desire and freedom from disorder. A person with few desires attains them; one with many desires goes astray.

Not showing the people what is likely to excite their desires is presented as a way to keep their minds from disorder. The sage constantly tries to keep the people without desire.
