Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Joshua, the tribe of Benjamin receives its first lot at Shiloh and is allotted twenty-six cities with their villages. In The Book of Judges, Benjamin refuses to hand over Gibeah's wicked men, is defeated after three days of battle, and its survivors receive wives and rebuild their cities. In 1 Chronicles, Benjamin is identified as Saul's tribe, with mighty warrior descendants and later archers and slingers who joined David. In The Book of Nehemiah, the children of Benjamin settle in Jerusalem and in towns from Geba through Michmash, Bethel, Anathoth, Nob, Lod, and Ono.

Benjamin in The Book of Judges

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Benjamin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Benjamin is a tribe of Israel and brother to the other tribes, living in its inherited land alongside Jebusites it did not drive from Jerusalem. It refuses to hand over the wicked men of Gibeah and gathers twenty-six thousand swordsmen. After winning two days, it is crushed on the third, leaving only six hundred men at the rock of Rimmon, and its cities are burned. An oath cuts it off from Israel's daughters, and the people grieve. Offered peace, the survivors receive four hundred women of Jabesh Gilead, seize wives at Shiloh, and rebuild their cities.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 20

Benjamin refuses to heed the other tribes' demand to deliver up the wicked men of Gibeah and gathers twenty-six thousand swordsmen, including seven hundred left-handed chosen men. It wins the first two days but is struck down on the third, losing twenty-five thousand men, with only six hundred surviving at the rock of Rimmon, and its cities, people and livestock are put to the sword and burned.

Chapter 21

Benjamin is the tribe cut off from Israel by the oath, and the people grieve for it. Its remaining men are offered peace at the rock of Rimmon, receive the 400 women of Jabesh Gilead (not enough), then seize wives from the dancing daughters of Shiloh, return to their inheritance, rebuild their cities and live in them.

Benjamin in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Benjamin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Benjamin is a tribe, Saul's own, with sons Bela, Becher, and Jediael, whose lines were counted as mighty warriors (Bela's 22,034). Later it supplied David with ambidextrous archers and slingers; at Hebron three thousand came, though most had kept allegiance to Saul's house until then.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

Benjamin's sons are listed as Bela, Becher, and Jediael. Bela's line was counted at twenty-two thousand thirty-four, Becher's at twenty thousand two hundred, and Jediael's at seventeen thousand two hundred, all mighty men of valor able to go out to war.

Chapter 12

Benjamin, Saul's own tribe, gave David archers and slingers who could use either hand, and some Benjamites came to him at the stronghold. At Hebron three thousand of them came, since most had until then kept their allegiance to Saul's house.
