Abstract written by a model

Tobias is the young man and only son of Tobit and Anna, the dutiful heir whose story is one of obedience, marriage and healing. He is first seen as the son restored to his father at the Pentecost feast, sent to find a poor kinsman for dinner, and he reports a man of their race strangled and cast out in the marketplace. Sarah is destined for him, and it belongs to him to inherit her. Tobit instructs him to bury him, honor his mother, give alms, and marry within the kindred, and tells him of ten talents of silver held by Gabael. Tobias promises obedience, finds a companion, Raphael (whom he does not know is an angel and calls Brother Azarias), and sets out with his dog following. At the Tigris he seizes the leaping fish and keeps its heart, liver and bile. He fears dying like Sarah's seven previous husbands, being an only son, but the angel reassures him, and he comes to love Sarah. At Raguel's house, a kinsman's, he refuses to eat until a covenant is made, then receives Sarah as wife. He burns the heart and liver to drive off the demon, leads his bride in prayer asking mercy and to grow old with her, and takes a wife for truth, not lust. Bound by Raguel's oath to stay fourteen days, he sends Raphael to Gabael for the money, worrying that his father, counting the days, will grieve. After the feast he insists on going home and receives Sarah and half of Raguel's goods. Running ahead to Nineveh with the bile, he anoints Tobit's eyes, saying 'Cheer up, my father,' and restores his sight, then reports events in Media. He proposes giving the companion half of all he brought, and falls on his face in fear when Raphael is revealed; he and Tobit then confess God's works. At the end he receives Tobit's final instructions, buries Tobit and Anna, and moves with his wife and sons to Ecbatana. There he grows old in honor, buries his parents-in-law, inherits their possessions and his father's, and dies at 127 after hearing of Nineveh's destruction.

Tobias in Tobit

author unknown · 224 to 174 BC · the work’s page · read it where Tobias first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

12 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Tobias is the narrator's son, restored to him at the Pentecost feast. His father sends him to find a poor kinsman for dinner, and he returns to report that a man of their race has been strangled and cast out in the marketplace.

Chapter 3

Tobias, son of Tobit, is named as the one to whom Sarah is to be given as wife. The text says it belonged to him to inherit her.

Chapter 4

Tobias is called by his father Tobit and receives a long set of instructions, including to bury Tobit, honor his mother, give alms, and marry within his father's kindred. He is told about the ten talents of silver with Gabael and assured he has much wealth if he fears God and avoids sin.

Chapter 5

Tobias promises to do all his father commands but asks how he can receive the money when he does not know the man. He finds a traveling companion, Raphael, without knowing he is an angel, prepares for the journey, and departs with him, his dog following.

Chapter 6

Tobias, called the young man, seizes the fish that leaps from the Tigris at the angel's command, cuts it open, and keeps its heart, liver and bile. He learns of Sarah and fears dying like her seven previous husbands, since he is his parents' only son, but the angel reassures him. After hearing the angel's instructions he loves Sarah and his soul is strongly joined to her.

Chapter 7

Tobias arrives at Raguel's house, identifies himself as Tobit's son, and urges Raphael (called Azarias) to raise the matter discussed on the road. He refuses to eat until a covenant is made, then receives Sarah as his wife with Raguel's blessing.

Chapter 8

Tobias is brought in to his bride after supper and, remembering Raphael's words, burns the fish's heart and liver on incense ashes to drive off the demon. He then rises and leads his bride in prayer, asking God for mercy and to grow old with her, and they sleep through the night. Raguel swears he must stay fourteen days and will then receive half of Raguel's goods and leave safely for his father.

Chapter 9

Tobias sends Raphael, whom he calls Brother Azarias, to Rages of Media to collect his money from Gabael and bring Gabael to the wedding feast. He says he cannot leave because Raguel has sworn he must not depart, and he worries that his father, who counts the days, will be grieved by a long delay. When Raphael and Gabael arrive at the feast, Tobias blesses his wife.

Chapter 10

Tobias, after the fourteen days of the wedding feast that Raguel had sworn he would spend there, asks to be sent away because his parents look no more to see him. He refuses Raguel's offer to send word to his father instead and insists on going home. Raguel gives him Sarah and half his goods, and Edna asks him not to cause Sarah grief.

Chapter 11

Tobias leaves Raguel and Edna blessing God and travels with Raphael to Nineveh, running ahead with the bile of the fish in hand. He anoints his father Tobit's eyes with it, saying "Cheer up, my father," and is seen by him, then tells his father the great things that happened in Media. His wedding feast is kept for seven days with great gladness.

Chapter 12

Tobias proposes giving the companion half of all that he brought back, recalling that the man led him safely, cured his wife, brought the money, and cured Tobit. He is told privately with Tobit to bless God, falls on his face in fear at Raphael's revelation, and afterward confesses with Tobit the works of God.

Chapter 14

Tobias receives his father's final instructions to take his sons to Media and leave Nineveh. He buries Tobit magnificently, buries Anna with him, and departs with his wife and sons to Ecbatana to Raguel. There he grows old in honor, buries his parents-in-law, inherits their possessions and his father's, and dies at 127 after hearing of Nineveh's destruction.
