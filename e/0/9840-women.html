Abstract written by a model

Women are presented as a temptation for monks, especially difficult for the unwise, and as a source of attachment and sin. The teaching depicts them using pretences, fine clothes, sweet conversation, invitations, and intercourse to win monks’ confidence and hearts. Intimacy then leads to delusion, sin, and loss of discipline. Monks, including female relatives and dependents, are to be avoided; giving up intercourse and adornment establishes control.

Third Lecture, Called the Knowledge of Troubles / First Chapter. .. Fourth Chapter.

Women are presented as a difficult temptation for the unwise and as a source of attachment and sin. Those who give up intercourse with women and stop adorning themselves become firmly established in control.

Fourth Lecture, Called Knowledge of Women. / First Chapter. .. Second Chapter.

Women are described as using clever pretences, fine clothes, sweet conversation, invitations, and other arts to attract monks. The text says that monks should avoid their company because intimacy with women leads to delusion, sin, and loss of discipline.
