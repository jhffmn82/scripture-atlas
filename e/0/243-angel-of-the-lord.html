Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, the angel of the LORD stops Abraham from harming the boy and declares the LORD's oath that Abraham's offspring will be multiplied and bless all nations. In Exodus, the angel sent before Israel guides the Israelites to the prepared place and requires them to listen to his voice without provoking him. In Numbers, the angel of the LORD blocks Balaam's path with a drawn sword, rebukes him for striking his donkey, and tells him to speak only what he is told. In The Book of Judges, the angel of the LORD announces to Manoah's wife that she will bear a Nazirite son, instructs Manoah, directs a burnt offering, and ascends in its flame. In The Book of Judges, the angel of the LORD rebukes Israel at Bochim, announces that the LORD will not drive out the land's inhabitants, and later commissions Gideon and consumes his offering with fire. In The Second Book of Samuel, the LORD's angel stretches out his hand to destroy Jerusalem but withdraws at the LORD's command at Araunah's threshing floor. In The First Book of Kings, the LORD's angel provides Elijah with cake and water and tells him to eat because the journey is too great for him. In The Second Book of Kings, the LORD's angel strikes 185,000 people in the Assyrian camp during the night.

and 7 more works

In 1 Chronicles, the LORD's angel is a destroying messenger sent to Jerusalem during the pestilence and stands at Ornan's threshing floor until David's sacrifice leads him to sheath his sword. In Daniel (Greek), the angel of the Lord carries Habakkuk to Daniel's lions' den and returns him to his own place. In Daniel (Greek), the angel of the Lord enters the furnace with Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, extinguishes the flames, and delivers them. In The Book of Zechariah, the LORD's angel stands among the myrtle trees, intercedes for Jerusalem, presides over Joshua's cleansing, and delivers the LORD's conditional promise. In The Gospel According to Matthew, an angel of the Lord appears to Joseph in a dream, tells him to take Mary as his wife, and instructs him to name her son Jesus. In The Gospel According to Luke, the angel of the Lord announces Jesus' birth to the shepherds and is joined by a multitude of the heavenly army. In The Acts of the Apostles, an angel of the Lord frees Peter from prison and later strikes Herod because he did not give God the glory.

angel of the LORD in The Book of Judges

author unknown · about 1000 BC · the work’s page · read it where angel of the LORD first appears

Abstract written by a model

A messenger of the LORD who appears twice. He first goes from Gilgal to Bochim, recalls the exodus and covenant, rebukes Israel for leaving altars standing and making covenants with the land's inhabitants, and announces the LORD will not drive them out; the people weep. Later he sits under the oak at Ophrah, commissions Gideon to save Israel, consumes an offering with fire from his staff, and vanishes.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

The LORD's angel goes up from Gilgal to Bochim and speaks the LORD's words to all Israel. He recalls the exodus and the covenant, charges Israel with failing to break down the altars and making covenants with the land's inhabitants, and announces that the LORD will not drive those peoples out. The people weep, and the place is named Bochim.

Chapter 6

The LORD's angel sat under the oak in Ophrah, greeted Gideon as a mighty man of valor, and commissioned him to save Israel. He told Gideon to lay the meat and unleavened cakes on a rock and pour out the broth, touched them with his staff so that fire consumed them, and then departed from sight. Gideon recognized him as the LORD's angel.
