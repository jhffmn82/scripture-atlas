sexual immorality in Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where sexual immorality first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sexual immorality is a sin reported among Corinthian believers, of a kind not even named among the Gentiles: a man having his father's wife. Paul clarifies that his earlier warning to avoid sexual sinners meant professing brothers, not the world's. It is later listed among behaviors excluding people from God's Kingdom; readers must flee it, since the body is for the Lord and the immoral person sins against his own body.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Sexual immorality is reported among the Corinthians, of a kind not even named among the Gentiles, namely a man having his father's wife. Paul says he previously wrote not to have company with sexual sinners, clarifying that this means professing brothers rather than the sexual sinners of the world.

Chapter 6

Sexual immorality is listed among behaviors that exclude people from God's Kingdom, and the readers are told to flee it. The writer argues the body is not for it but for the Lord, and that joining with a prostitute makes members of Christ one body with her, so the person sins against his own body.

sexual immorality and uncleanness in Paul’s Letter to the Ephesians

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where sexual immorality and uncleanness first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sexual immorality and uncleanness, with covetousness, are forbidden even to be mentioned among the saints, alongside filthiness and foolish talk. Those who practice them have no inheritance in the Kingdom of Christ and God; God's wrath falls on the disobedient, so readers must not partner with them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Sexual immorality, all uncleanness and covetousness are not even to be mentioned among the saints, and filthiness, foolish talking and jesting are also ruled out. The text says no sexually immoral or unclean person, nor covetous person (an idolater), has an inheritance in the Kingdom of Christ and God. Because of these things God's wrath comes on the children of disobedience, so the readers must not partner with those who practice them.
