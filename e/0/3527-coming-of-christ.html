Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Matthew, the coming of the Son of Man is described as visible like lightning, occurring after suffering, with his arrival on the clouds in power and glory to gather the chosen. In The Good News According to Mark, the coming of the Son of Man follows cosmic disturbances, with his arrival in clouds in great power and glory to gather the chosen. In The Gospel According to Luke, the coming of the Son of Man is described as sudden and unmistakable, preceded by his suffering and rejection and followed by sudden destruction. In 1 Thessalonians, the Lord's coming and the resurrection of the dead involve the Lord descending from heaven, the dead in Christ rising first, and living believers being caught up to meet him. In The Letter from James, the coming of the Lord is said to be at hand, and believers are urged to be patient because the judge stands at the door. In Revelation, Christ's coming is announced as soon, bringing rewards according to each person's work and blessing those who keep the prophecy, with the book ending in a plea for him to come. In The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry, Lehi's testimony and the contents of the book manifest plainly the coming of the Messiah. In The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi, prophets knew of Christ and hoped for his glory many hundred years before his coming, described as his manifesting himself in the flesh.

and 8 more works

In The Words of Mormon, the plates contain prophecies of the coming of Christ, many fulfilled and the remaining prophecies still to come to pass. In The Book of Mosiah, the coming of the Lord is a future event to be believed in until the end of life, requiring the people to remain faithful. In The Book of Mosiah, the coming of Christ is declared by an angel, prophets, signs, wonders, types, and shadows, with Christ coming among humanity in the flesh. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, the coming of Christ is presented as a long-expected, near event centered on salvation and taking away the sins of the world. In The Book of Helaman, the coming of Christ is a foretold event testified of by fathers and prophets, expected after five years with signs and followed by his suffering and death. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, the coming of Christ is a period marked by a sign from which the Nephites reckon time, with fulfilled prophecies and signs showing that Christ had come. In The Fourth Book of Nephi, Who Is the Son of Nephi, One of the Disciples of Jesus Christ, the records mark the passing of years from the coming of Christ. In The Book of Moroni, more than four hundred and twenty years have passed since the sign was given of the coming of Christ.

coming of Christ in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

Abstract written by a model

The coming of Christ is presented as a long-expected, near event: the kingdom of heaven is nigh, priests preach it, and the Son of God is expected to come in glory. Angels will declare the coming to just and holy men, fulfilling the fathers’ words, while Alma awaits it with joy. It is taught that the Son of God will shortly come and later appear after his resurrection. Converted Lamanites look forward to it, though the Zoramites deny any Christ. The coming centers on taking away the sins of the world, declaring salvation, and preparing people and their children to hear the word.
