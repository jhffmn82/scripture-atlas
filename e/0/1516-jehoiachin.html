Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Jehoiachin was a king of Judah who succeeded Jehoiakim, reigned three months, surrendered to Babylon, was captured and carried to Babylon, and was later released and honored by Evilmerodach. In The Book of Jeremiah, Jehoiachin, king of Judah, was held captive in Babylon until Evilmerodach freed him, elevated his throne, and provided him a daily allowance.

Jehoiachin king of Judah in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where Jehoiachin king of Judah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jehoiachin was a king of Judah who succeeded Jehoiakim at eighteen and reigned only three months in Jerusalem. His mother was Nehushta, and he did evil like his father. He surrendered to the king of Babylon with his mother, servants, princes and officers, and was captured by Nebuchadnezzar and carried to Babylon. In the thirty-seventh year of captivity, Evilmerodach released him, spoke kindly, set his throne above other kings, and gave him a daily allowance for life.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 24

Jehoiachin became king at eighteen and reigned three months in Jerusalem; his mother was Nehushta daughter of Elnathan, and he did evil as his father had. He went out to the king of Babylon with his mother, servants, princes and officers and was captured, then carried to Babylon with his wives and officers.

Chapter 25

In the thirty-seventh year of his captivity, on the twenty-seventh day of the twelfth month, Jehoiachin was released from prison by Evilmerodach. He was spoken to kindly, given his prison garments changed, and ate bread before the king continually, receiving a daily allowance for all his life.
