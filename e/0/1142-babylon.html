Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Babylon is the rising distant power that receives Judah’s deportees, takes the temple treasures, carries away Zedekiah, and honors Jehoiachin. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Babylon receives Jehoiakim’s captive party, the temple vessels, Jehoiachin, and Judah’s surviving people and treasures. In Psalms, Daughter of Babylon is addressed as doomed for harming the exiles, with blessing pronounced on whoever repays her. In Psalms, Babylon is the foreign land where the exiles sit weeping by its rivers and hang their harps on the willows. In The Book of Isaiah, Babylon is the proud city and kingdom foretold to be overthrown and left desolate. In The Book of Isaiah, the daughter of Babylon is the proud, pleasure-loving power brought down, shamed, and suddenly bereaved. In The Book of Jeremiah, Babylon is the Chaldean land and kingdom that serves as the LORD’s instrument of judgment, receives Judah’s captives, and is ultimately judged and left desolate. In Baruch, Babylon is the land of exile ruled by Nabuchodonosor, where Jerusalem’s captives remain for generations among idolatrous worshippers.

and 2 more works

In The Book of Ezekiel, Babylon is the Chaldean merchant city where Jerusalem’s king and princes are brought and where the rebel king will die. In The Book of Micah, Babylon is the place where Zion will dwell after leaving the city and where the LORD will rescue and redeem her.

Babylon in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Babylon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Babylon is a distant, rising power whose king first sends envoys to Hezekiah, prompting Isaiah's prophecy that Judah's treasures and some of Hezekiah's sons will be taken there. Under Nebuchadnezzar it takes over Egypt's former territory, receives Jehoiachin and Jerusalem's deportees, and installs Zedekiah, who rebels. Ultimately Zedekiah is carried there in fetters, the temple bronze is taken, and Jehoiachin is honored.

Chapter 20

Babylon is a far country whose king sends envoys to Hezekiah. Isaiah prophesies that all the contents of Hezekiah's house and the treasures stored by his fathers will be carried there, and that some of Hezekiah's sons will be eunuchs in the king of Babylon's palace.

Chapter 24

Babylon, under Nebuchadnezzar, held all that had belonged to the king of Egypt from the brook of Egypt to the Euphrates, so Egypt's king no longer left his land. It became the destination of Jehoiachin, the royal household, and the deported people of Jerusalem, and its king installed Zedekiah, who later rebelled against him.

Chapter 25

Babylon is where Zedekiah was carried in fetters and where the bronze of the LORD's house was taken. It is also where Jehoiachin was held and later honored by its king, who set his throne above the thrones of the kings with him there.

Babylon in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Babylon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Babylon is a city and kingdom called the glory of kingdoms and the beauty of the Chaldeans' pride. The records trace its arc through Isaiah. First, it is foretold to be overthrown like Sodom and Gomorrah, never inhabited again, and left to wild animals, its time near. Next, the LORD of Armies vows to rise against it, cut off its name, remnant, son and son's son, and sweep it with the broom of destruction, leaving it to the porcupine and pools of water. Then a watchman announces it fallen, its gods' engraved images broken to the ground. Hezekiah calls it a far country, and Isaiah prophesies that his treasures and some sons, made eunuchs, will be carried there. Finally, the one whom the LORD loves is named as acting against Babylon and the Chaldeans, and the people are told to flee. Its overall course is from pride to prophesied desolation.

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

Babylon is the subject of the burden Isaiah saw, called the glory of kingdoms and the beauty of the Chaldeans' pride. It will be overthrown as God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah and will never be inhabited again, with only jackals, ostriches, hyenas, and wild goats living there. Its time is near and its days will not be prolonged.

Chapter 14

The LORD of Armies declares he will rise against Babylon and cut off its name, remnant, son and son's son. He will make it a possession for the porcupine and pools of water and sweep it with the broom of destruction.

Chapter 21

Chapter 39

Babylon is described by Hezekiah as a country far from him, from which the envoys came. Isaiah prophesies that Hezekiah's household treasures and some of his sons will be carried there, with the sons becoming eunuchs in the king of Babylon's palace.

Chapter 48

Babylon, along with the Chaldeans, is named as the target of the one whom the LORD loves, who will do what he likes to it, with his arm against the Chaldeans. The people are told to leave Babylon and flee from the Chaldeans.

daughter of Babylon in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where daughter of Babylon first appears

Abstract written by a model

The virgin daughter of Babylon, also called daughter of the Chaldeans, is a proud, pleasure-loving power once titled tender, delicate and mistress of kingdoms. She sat securely, trusted in wickedness, relied on sorceries and trafficking partners from youth, showed Israel no mercy, and claimed no one else besides her. The records unfold her fall: commanded from her throne into dust, silence and darkness, stripped and shamed, grinding flour, then suddenly struck by widowhood and childlessness in one day, with no savior.

Chapter 47

The virgin daughter of Babylon, also called the daughter of the Chaldeans, is told to come down from her throne and sit in the dust and in silence and darkness. She will grind flour, be stripped of her veil, and have her nakedness and shame exposed, and she will no longer be called tender, delicate, or mistress of kingdoms. Widowhood and loss of children will come on her in one day, and no one will save her.

Babylon in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Babylon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Babylon is a land and kingdom of the Chaldeans, on the Euphrates, ruled by King Nebuchadnezzar and worshipping the deities Bel and Merodach. Across the records it is both the LORD's instrument of judgment and the object of that judgment. Its story runs in three stages. First, Babylon is the destination of captivity. Judah's people, treasures, and temple vessels are carried there, including Pashhur, Jeconiah, and Zedekiah, whose sons and princes are killed before he is imprisoned in Babylon. The LORD gives the nations to Nebuchadnezzar, and they serve him seventy years. Jeremiah rejects Hananiah's claim of a quick return, and the exiles are told to seek Babylon's peace and pray for it, since in its peace they will have peace. Babylon's king appoints Gedaliah governor over those left in Judah, besieges Jerusalem, and carries off the bronze of the LORD's house. Later Evilmerodach releases Jehoiachin. Second, after the seventy years the LORD will punish the king and nation for their iniquity. Third, the closing oracles in chapters 50 and 51 declare Babylon taken and fallen. It was a golden cup in the LORD's hand that made the earth drunk and a hammer of the whole earth, now cut apart and broken. It is condemned for pride against the LORD, for plundering his heritage, for devouring Zion, and for mad devotion to engraved images. Its idols are dismayed, its walls, gates, and mighty men are overthrown, and its king grows feeble. Its fate, decreed by the LORD, is desolation without inhabitants, like Sodom and Gomorrah, a dwelling for jackals, forever.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

10 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 20

Babylon is named as the place to which Judah's people and treasures will be carried as captives and spoil. Pashhur, his household, and his friends will go there, and Pashhur will die and be buried there.

Chapter 25

Babylon's king, Nebuchadnezzar, is the instrument of the LORD's judgment, and the nations will serve him seventy years. When the seventy years are complete, the LORD will punish the king of Babylon and his nation for their iniquity and make the land of the Chaldeans desolate forever, as written against it in this book.

Chapter 27

Babylon is the land of the king Nebuchadnezzar, to whom the LORD has given the nations. The remaining temple vessels will be carried there and remain until the LORD visits them and brings them back, and Jeremiah says that the vessels taken earlier will not shortly be returned from Babylon as some prophets claim.

Chapter 28

Babylon is the place where the temple vessels and the captives of Judah, including Jeconiah, were taken. Hananiah prophesied their return from there, while the LORD declared the nations would serve Babylon's king.

Chapter 29

Babylon is the place where the LORD has caused the Judeans to be carried captive. The captives are told to seek its peace and pray for it, since in its peace they will have peace. Its time is limited to seventy years, after which the LORD will visit the exiles.

Chapter 34

Babylon is the place to which Zedekiah will be taken, and its king is the one into whose hand the LORD will give Jerusalem. Its army is said to have gone away from the city but will be commanded by the LORD to return.

Chapter 40

The king of Babylon has made Gedaliah governor over the cities of Judah and committed to him men, women, children and the poorest of the land who were not carried captive. Many were carried captive to Babylon, and Nebuzaradan offers Jeremiah the chance to go there.

Chapter 50

Babylon is declared taken, with Bel and Merodach dismayed and its images disappointed. It is condemned for pride, for plundering the LORD's heritage, and for devotion to idols, and is to be repaid as it has done, left desolate and uninhabited forever like Sodom and Gomorrah. Its king grows feeble at the news of the coming enemy, and the earth trembles at its capture.

Chapter 51

Babylon was a golden cup in the LORD's hand that made all the earth drunk, but is now declared fallen, unhealed, and doomed to desolation. Its walls, gates, mighty men, princes, and idols are to be overthrown, and its land left without inhabitant like heaps and a dwelling for jackals.
