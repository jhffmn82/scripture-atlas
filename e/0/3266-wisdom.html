Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Solomon, Wisdom is a personified spirit who loves humanity, guides and protects the righteous, and embodies divine understanding. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Wisdom is a personified feminine figure from the Lord who disciplines seekers, rewards perseverance, and dwells in Israel.

Wisdom in The Wisdom of Solomon

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Wisdom first appears

Abstract written by a model

Wisdom is presented as a personified spirit who loves humanity yet will not enter a soul that devises evil or a body enslaved by sin, and who does not hold a blasphemer guiltless. The records open by setting this moral condition for her presence, then turn to how she is found. She is radiant and unfading, easily seen by those who love her, and she anticipates and graciously meets those who seek her and are worthy. Her beginning is desire for instruction, which leads to love, observance of her laws, immortality and a kingdom. Envy has no fellowship with her. The middle of the records describes her nature. She is the architect of all things, a pure and subtle spirit more mobile than any motion, a breath of God's power, a reflection of everlasting light, and fairer than the sun. Though one, she can do all things and renews them while remaining in herself. She passes into holy souls to make them friends of God and prophets, and evil does not prevail against her. The narrator prefers her to thrones, riches, gems, health and beauty, since all good came with her, and he sees her as a bride, counselor and companion who must be given by God. Her relation to God is central. She lives with him, is loved by him, sits by him on his thrones, knows his works, and was present at creation. She understands what pleases him, chooses his works, and teaches soberness, understanding, righteousness and courage. She knows the past and foresees the future. A prayer asks God to send her from the heavens to work with and guide the speaker. The final movement is a historical sequence of rescue. Wisdom guards, delivers, guides and prospers the righteous, beginning with the first formed father, whom she delivers from his transgression and strengthens to rule. She saves a righteous man in the flood, preserves another blameless, delivers one from the fire on the five cities, guides a fugitive in straight paths and shows him God's kingdom, and goes with a sold and imprisoned man into the dungeon and gives him eternal glory. She delivers the holy people from oppressors, enters a servant of the Lord, leads the people through the Red sea, drowns their enemies, and gives speech to the mute and to babes. The records close by noting she prospered the works of the righteous through a holy prophet, framing the desert journey that follows.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

Wisdom is described as a spirit who loves man, but she will not enter a soul that devises evil nor dwell in a body enslaved by sin. She does not hold a blasphemer guiltless for his lips. A holy spirit of discipline is said to flee deceit and thoughts without understanding.

Chapter 6

Wisdom is described as radiant and unfading, easily seen by those who love her and found by those who seek her. She anticipates those who desire her, sits at the gates of those who rise early to seek her, and goes about meeting the worthy graciously. Her beginning is desire for instruction, which is love, which is observance of her laws, leading to immortality and a kingdom; the speaker promises to explain what she is and how she came into being, and says envy has no fellowship with her.

Chapter 7

Wisdom came to the narrator when he asked, and he preferred her to thrones, riches, gems, health, beauty and light, since all good things came with her. She is described as the architect of all things, a quick, holy, subtle and pure spirit that pervades everything, a breath of God's power, a reflection of everlasting light and an image of his goodness. Though one, she can do all things and renews them, passes into holy souls to make them friends of God and prophets, and is fairer than the sun, for evil does not prevail against her.

Chapter 8

Wisdom reaches from one end to the other with full strength and orders all things well. She lives with God, is loved by the Sovereign Lord, is initiated into his knowledge, chooses his works, and is described as the maker and architect of things that exist. She teaches soberness, understanding, righteousness, and courage, knows the past and foresees the future, and is sought by the speaker as a bride, counselor, and companion, though he says she must be given by God.

Chapter 9

Wisdom is described as sitting by God on his thrones, being with him, knowing his works, and being present when he made the world. She understands what is pleasing in God's eyes and right according to his commandments. The speaker asks God to send her from the holy heavens so she can work with him, guide him prudently and guard him in her glory, and the prayer says people on earth were saved through wisdom.

Chapter 10

Wisdom guards, delivers, guides and prospers the righteous throughout the chapter, from the first formed father to the holy people. She saves from the flood and the fire, stays with a righteous man in the dungeon, enters the soul of a servant of the Lord, brings the people over the Red sea, drowns their enemies, and opens the mouth of the mute and makes babes' tongues speak clearly.

Chapter 11

Wisdom is said to have prospered the works of the righteous in the hand of a holy prophet. This opens the chapter and frames the journey through the desert that follows.

Wisdom in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Wisdom first appears

Abstract written by a model

Wisdom is a personified, feminine figure in the Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach. She comes from the Lord, is with him forever, and was created before all things and before the ages, coming out of the mouth of the Most High. She is given freely to those who love the Lord and is tied to keeping the commandments. Her duration is eternal, and no first or last man has fully explored her; her thoughts are filled from the great deep. The records show her relationship to seekers as demanding but rewarding. She exalts her sons, takes hold of those who seek her, and is loved by the Lord. She first walks with a follower in crooked ways, bringing fear, dread and discipline until she trusts his soul. Then she returns him to the straight way and reveals her secrets, but she forsakes and hands over to his fall anyone who goes astray. She is harsh to the unlearned and like a mighty stone of trial to those without understanding, and she is manifest to few. For those who persevere, her fetters become a covering of strength, a robe of glory and a crown of rejoicing, and she yields rest and gladness at the last. She can be gathered from youth and found with gray hair, and cultivating her takes little toil before her fruit is eaten. She is also pictured as a house with windows, doors, walls and branches, offering shelter from heat, good things and glory. For the one who fears the Lord she is like a mother and a wife, feeding him bread of understanding and water of wisdom, exalting him above his neighbors and opening his mouth in the congregation. She is far from pride and is not obtained by foolish men, sinners and liars. The account culminates in her self-praise. After seeking rest among all peoples, she was told by the Creator to dwell in Jacob and take her inheritance in Israel. She ministered in the holy tabernacle before the Most High, was established in Zion, and was given rest in Jerusalem, rooted in the honored people, the Lord's own portion. She compares herself to cedar, cypress, palm, olive, plane, cinnamon, myrrh, terebinth and vine, and to rose bushes in Jericho. Her memory is sweeter than honey. Her stream grew from a canal into a river and then a sea, watering the garden, and she labors not for herself only but for all who seek wisdom.

The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus / Chapter 1

Wisdom comes from the Lord, is with him forever, and was created before all things. She is with all flesh according to the Lord's gift, was given freely to those who love him, and fills her house with desirable things. Those who desire her are told to keep the commandments, and the Lord will give her to them freely.

Chapter 4

Wisdom is personified as one who exalts her sons, takes hold of those who seek her, and gives life, gladness, glory and security to those who love and heed her. She first walks with a follower in crooked ways, bringing fear, dread and discipline until she trusts and tests his soul, then returns him to the straight way and reveals her secrets, but she forsakes and hands over to his fall anyone who goes astray. Her presence is known through speech and instruction, so she should not be hidden.

Chapter 6

Wisdom is portrayed as a woman who is harsh to the unlearned and a stone of trial to those without understanding, who cast her off, and she is not manifest to many. To those who persevere, her fetters, chains, and bands become a covering of strength, a robe of glory, and a crown of rejoicing, and she brings rest and gladness. She can be gathered from youth, found even at gray hair, and her cultivation requires little toil before her fruit is eaten.

Chapter 14

Wisdom is portrayed as a house with windows, doors, walls, and branches, whose ways and secrets can be learned by those who pursue her. Those who seek her are to hunt her like prey and lodge near her, and they find in her a dwelling where good things are, shelter from heat, and glory.

Chapter 15

Wisdom meets the one who fears the Lord like a mother and receives him like a wife married in her virginity. She feeds him bread of understanding and water of wisdom, exalts him above his neighbors, and opens his mouth in the congregation. She is far from pride and is not obtained, seen, or remembered by foolish men, sinners, and liars.

Chapter 24

Wisdom praises herself, saying she came from the mouth of the Most High, was created before the ages, and will never cease to exist. After seeking rest among all peoples, she was told to dwell in Jacob, ministered in the tabernacle, and took root in Zion and Jerusalem. She likens herself to trees and perfumes, invites all who desire her to be filled, and says her stream grew into a river and a sea and that she labors for all who seek wisdom.
