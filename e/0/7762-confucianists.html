Confucianists

Also called: Confucianists and the Mihists, the Confucianists

Abstract written by a model

The Confucianists arise after interference with natural goodness has troubled the empire. They preach charity and duty amid cangues and fetters, though their teachings are presented as helping produce those restraints. As one of five schools, they dispute moral standards and rightness without attaining a governing harmony, and are said to hold false principles.

CHAPTER XI.

The Confucianists arise after the empire has already become troubled by interference with natural goodness. They preach amid cangues and fetters, although the unit presents their teachings as contributing to the conditions that produced those restraints.

CHAPTER XXIV.

The Confucianists are one of the schools disputing Hui Tzŭ over moral standards and rightness. Chuang Tzŭ places them among competing positions that have not attained a single governing harmony.
