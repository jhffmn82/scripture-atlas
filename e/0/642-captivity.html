Abstract written by a model

In Baruch, captivity is the condition of Israel's scattered children, brought on by their sins and ending with Jerusalem mourning those carried away. In The Book of Ezekiel, Israel's captivity results from its iniquity and trespass, but the Lord GOD promises mercy, reversing it and gathering Israel from enemy lands. In The Book of Amos, captivity is prophesied for Israel, with notable men going captive first and feasting and lounging ending.

captivity of Israel's children in Baruch

author unknown · about 299 BC · the work’s page · read it where captivity of Israel's children first appears

Abstract written by a model

Captivity is the condition of Israel's scattered children, brought upon them by God as penalty for their sins and their fathers' iniquities. It begins with the people reproached and cursed in enemy land, yet promising praise. It closes with Jerusalem mourning her children, carried off like a flock, and calling Zion's neighbors to remember.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

Captivity is the condition in which the people now live, scattered by God in their enemies' land as a reproach and a curse and subject to penalty for their fathers' iniquities. In it they nonetheless promise to praise the Lord and call on his name, having called to mind their fathers' sins.

Chapter 4

Jerusalem's sons and daughters were carried off like a flock taken by enemies and traveled rough roads, a captivity brought on by God because of their sins. Jerusalem called on those near Zion to remember it and mourned the sending away of her children with weeping.

Israel's captivity in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · about 592 to 570 BC · the work’s page · read it where Israel's captivity first appears

Abstract written by a model

Israel's captivity resulted from the house of Israel's iniquity and trespass against the Lord GOD, who hid his face, delivering them to adversaries and the sword. He promises mercy, reversing the captivity, gathering them from enemy lands, and leaving none captive.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 39

The Lord explains that the house of Israel went into captivity for their iniquity and trespass, as he hid his face and gave them into the hand of their adversaries, and they fell by the sword. He promises to reverse the captivity of Jacob, gather them from the peoples and enemy lands, and leave none of them captive any more.
