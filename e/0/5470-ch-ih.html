Ch'ih

person · 3 works · 6 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Kung-hsi Hwa, Tsze-hwa

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book V (Kung-Ye Ch'Ang), Ch'ih might be employed to converse with visitors and guests in a court while standing with his sash girt, though the Master does not know whether he is perfectly virtuous. In The Analects of Confucius, Book VI (Yung Yey), Tsze-hwa is employed on a mission to Ch'i and travels with fat horses to his carriage and wearing light furs. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XI (Hsien Tsin), Kung-hsi Hwa asks Confucius to explain why different advice was given to Tsze-lu and Zan Yu, and says he would study ancestral temple ceremonies and sovereign audiences and act as a small assistant.
