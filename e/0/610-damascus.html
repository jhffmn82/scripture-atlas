Damascus

place · 5 works · 10 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: Syria, the city of my joy, the city of praise, the people of Syria

Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Damascus is taken by the king of Assyria, who carries its people captive to Kir and kills Rezin; Ahaz goes there to meet Tiglath Pileser and sees the altar there. In The Book of Isaiah, Damascus is declared to lose its kingdom, cease being a city, become a ruinous heap, and be forsaken, left to flocks. In The Book of Jeremiah, Damascus is foretold to fall, growing feeble, fleeing, and being seized by anguish; its young men and warriors die in its streets, and the LORD's fire devours Ben Hadad's palaces. In The Book of Amos, Damascus is condemned for threshing Gilead with iron instruments; its gate bar is broken, Hazael's house and Ben Hadad's palaces are burned, and Syria's people are exiled to Kir. In The Acts of the Apostles, Damascus is the destination of Saul's journey to arrest believers, where he is stopped by Jesus, healed and baptized, preaches Christ, and escapes a plot by being lowered over the wall in a basket.
