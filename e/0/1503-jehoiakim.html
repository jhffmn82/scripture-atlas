Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Jehoiakim, formerly Eliakim son of Josiah, is made king of Judah by Pharaoh Necoh, reigns eleven years, rebels against Nebuchadnezzar after serving him three years, and is succeeded by his son Jehoiachin. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Jehoiakim, formerly Eliakim, is installed as king of Judah by Egypt's king, reigns eleven years, does evil, is fettered by Nebuchadnezzar, and is succeeded by his son Jehoiachin. In The Book of Jeremiah, Jehoiakim is portrayed as an unjust and defiant king of Judah who opposes the LORD's word and is condemned to an ignominious end.

Jehoiakim in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where Jehoiakim first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jehoiakim, formerly Eliakim, son of Josiah and Zebidah, is made king of Judah by Pharaoh Necoh, who renames him. At twenty-five he begins an eleven-year reign in Jerusalem, taxing the people to pay Pharaoh's tribute and doing evil in the LORD's sight. He serves Nebuchadnezzar three years, then rebels; the LORD sends raiding bands against Judah. He dies and his son Jehoiachin succeeds him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 23

Jehoiakim, formerly Eliakim and son of Josiah and Zebidah, is made king by Pharaoh Necoh, who changes his name. He is twenty-five when he begins to reign and rules eleven years in Jerusalem. He taxes the people of the land to pay Pharaoh's tribute and does evil in the LORD's sight.

Chapter 24

Jehoiakim served Nebuchadnezzar for three years and then rebelled against him. The LORD sent raiding bands against Judah in his time, and he died and was succeeded by his son Jehoiachin. His other acts are said to be in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah, and Zedekiah is later said to have done evil like all that he did.

Jehoiakim in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · 629 to 579 BC · the work’s page · read it where Jehoiakim first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jehoiakim is a king of Judah and son of Josiah, portrayed as unjust and defiant toward the LORD's word. He is first condemned for building a lavish cedar house with his neighbor's unpaid labor, and for covetousness, shedding innocent blood, oppression, and violence. He had Uriah fetched from Egypt and killed with the sword for prophesying like Jeremiah. He then cut up and burned the scroll read to him, showing no fear, and ordered Jeremiah and Baruch arrested. The LORD pronounces his end: no mourning, burial like a donkey cast beyond Jerusalem's gates, his body exposed to heat and frost, no heir on David's throne, and punishment for his offspring and servants.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 22

Jehoiakim, son of Josiah, is condemned for building a wide house with cedar and red paint using his neighbor's unpaid labor, and for covetousness, shedding innocent blood, oppression, and violence. He is told no one will lament him and that he will be buried like a donkey, drawn and cast out beyond Jerusalem's gates.

Chapter 26

Jehoiakim is king of Judah, the son of Josiah, at the time of this word from the LORD. He sought to kill Uriah for prophesying like Jeremiah, sent Elnathan son of Achbor and others to fetch him from Egypt, and killed him with the sword.

Chapter 36

Jehoiakim hears the scroll read, cuts it with a penknife and burns it in the brazier, showing no fear and ignoring the intercession of Elnathan, Delaiah, and Gemariah. He orders Jeremiah and Baruch arrested. The LORD declares he will have no one to sit on David's throne, his body will be cast out to heat and frost, and he, his offspring, and his servants will be punished.
