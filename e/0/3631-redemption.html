Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, abundant redemption is with the LORD, giving Israel reason to hope that he will redeem them from all their sins. In The Book of Isaiah, redemption is achieved for Jerusalem by the LORD, who redeems his people without money and reveals salvation to the ends of the earth. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, reconciliation is received through Jesus Christ after believers are reconciled to God through the death and life of his Son. In Paul’s Letter to the Colossians, redemption and reconciliation give believers forgiveness of sins, deliverance from darkness, and reconciliation to God through Christ’s blood and death. In The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry, redemption is deliverance from captivity and oppression by the Lord, the Redeemer of Israel. In The Words of Mormon, redemption of Christ is the knowledge Mormon prays his brethren may regain so they may become a delightsome people. In The Book of Mosiah, redemption is necessary for salvation, satisfies justice, and saves the Lord’s people through Christ’s coming, sufferings, death, resurrection, and ascension. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, redemption is the great and eternal plan that provides resurrection, reclaims mankind from spiritual death through the Son of God’s sacrifice, and offers mercy to those who repent and believe.

and 3 more works

In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, redemption of Christ reclaims mankind from spiritual death through the Son of God’s death, sufferings, and blood atonement. In The Book of Helaman, redemption comes to the people through the order of God’s Son, as Abraham and others knew and the people were shown before his coming. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, salvation includes Jerusalem’s redemption without money and the people’s proclamation of peace and salvation.

redemption in The Book of Mosiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where redemption first appears

Abstract written by a model

Redemption is necessary for salvation: without it, all mankind would perish. Prepared from the foundation of the world, it satisfies justice and saves the Lord’s people from a lost and fallen state. It is made possible by Christ’s coming and comes through his power, sufferings, death, resurrection, and ascension. Though the children of Israel did not understand it because of hard hearts, Alma preached repentance and redemption. Alma (son) described being redeemed after repentance and tribulation, rescued from bitterness, iniquity, darkness, and eternal torment.

redemption in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where redemption first appears

Abstract written by a model

Redemption is the great and eternal plan laid from the foundation of the world. It provides the resurrection of the dead and, through the Son of God’s sacrifice, repentance, and atonement, reclaims mankind from spiritual death and offers mercy to those who repent and believe on his name. It was anticipated in preparatory redemption, taught as people looked forward to the Son, and proclaimed among the Lamanites. It does not destroy justice: the wicked and those who do iniquity remain as though no redemption had been made, except for the loosing of the bands of death.

Chapter 11

The redemption made by the Son of God benefits those who believe on his name, while the wicked remain as though there had been no redemption made except for the loosing of the bands of death.

Chapter 12

The plan of redemption was laid from the foundation of the world and brings to pass the resurrection of the dead. It provides a way for repentant people to receive mercy through the Only Begotten Son, while it has no power to destroy the works of justice for those who do iniquity.

Chapter 13

A preparatory redemption made the holy calling possible for those who were called according to faith. The people are taught to look forward to the Son for redemption.

Chapter 17

The sons of Mosiah seek to bring the Lamanites to know the plan of redemption.

Chapter 34

The great plan of redemption is brought about for those who repent and do not harden their hearts. It is made possible through the infinite and eternal sacrifice of the Son of God.

Chapter 42

The plan of redemption provides the means to reclaim mankind from spiritual death through repentance and the atonement. Without it, the souls of the dead would remain miserable and cut off from the Lord’s presence.
