Hui of Liu Xia

person · 4 works · 21 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: Hui, Liu Hsia Chi

Abstract written by a model

In Chuang Tzŭ, Liu Hsia Chi is a virtuous Lu official and Robber Chê’s much older elder brother who warns Confucius against visiting Robber Chê and later confirms Confucius was rebuffed. In Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou), Hui of Liu Xia is portrayed as unashamed to serve an impure prince or hold inferior office, carrying out his principles when employed and remaining self-possessed when neglected or poor. In Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang), Hui of Liu Xia is a sage whose steady character endures impure princes, low office, dismissal, and poverty without shame, concealment, complaint, or grief, and whose character makes the mean generous and the niggardly liberal. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), Hui of Liu Xia maintains a firm purpose of life and would not change it for three highest offices.

Hui of Liu Xia in Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou)

Mencius · the work’s page · read it where Hui of Liu Xia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hui of Liu Xia is a person portrayed as unashamed to serve an impure prince or hold inferior office. When employed he carries out his principles; when neglected or poor he neither murmurs nor grieves, remaining self-possessed because others cannot defile him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

Hui of Liu Xia was not ashamed to serve an impure prince or to be an inferior officer. He carried out his principles when employed, did not murmur when neglected, did not grieve in poverty, and believed that others could not defile him.
