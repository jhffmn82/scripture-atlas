husbands

Also called: Husbands, You husbands, each man, her husband, his wife, husband, men, men (husbands), the husband, the man, their own husbands, unbelieving husband, your husbands, your own husbands

Abstract written by a model

In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, husbands owe their wives affection, lack authority over their own bodies, are commanded not to leave their wives, and worry about pleasing them. In Paul’s Letter to the Colossians, husbands are told to love their wives and not be bitter against them. In 1 Peter, husbands are instructed to live with their wives according to knowledge, honor them as joint heirs of grace, and avoid hindering their prayers. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), husbands are instructed how to respond to wives’ rebellion, desertion, and unequal treatment, including admonishing, separating beds, and scourging, while not seeking a way against obedient wives.

husbands in Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians

Abstract written by a model

Husbands, as described in Chapter 7, owe their wives affection and lack authority over their own bodies, which belong to the wife. They are commanded by the Lord not to leave their wives, even unbelieving ones content to stay, and married men worry about pleasing their wives.

Chapter 7

Husbands are told to give their wives the affection owed them and are told they have no authority over their own bodies, but the wife does. They are commanded not to leave their wives, including unbelieving wives who are content to stay. A married man is concerned about how to please his wife.

men (husbands) in Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where men (husbands) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Men who are husbands are instructed to admonish women from whom they fear rebellion, banish them to beds apart, and scourge them. If the women obey, the men must not seek a way against them. Husbands are addressed concerning ill treatment, desertion, and unequal treatment of wives. They cannot deal equally between their wives, however much they wish, and they are told not to turn altogether away from one wife.
