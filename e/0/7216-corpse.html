corpse

Also called: body, carcases of dogs or corpses of men, corpse of a dog, corpse of a man, dead, dead body, dead matter, dead one, lifeless body, unclean remains, whole body of a dead dog

Abstract written by a model

A corpse is kept in a house for the dead, then laid on the Dakhma with its eyes toward the sun, where rain and birds act upon it and birds may eat it after death. It may be carried by dogs, birds, wolves, winds, or flies without sin. A corpse found in running water, ponds, wells, snow, or hail must be removed and laid on dry ground; until removal and purification, it makes portions of water unclean. It contaminates wood and corn, though these may be cleansed by separation and sprinkling. A dead body is removed from a house when possible, or the house is removed; it may remain in a grave until signs allow removal. After over a year dried and dead, man may touch it without impurity.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard V.

A corpse may be carried by dogs, birds, wolves, winds, or flies without causing sin to the person where it arrives. Corpses are kept in houses for the dead and then laid on the Dakhma, where rain and birds act upon them.

Fargard VI.

A corpse found in running water must be removed and laid on dry ground, even when it is falling to pieces and rotting. Corpses in ponds, wells, snow or hail, and running streams render specified portions of water unclean until removed and the required purification is completed.

Fargard VII.

It can pollute wood and corn, which may be cleansed by setting them apart at prescribed distances and sprinkling them with water, depending on their condition.

Fargard VIII.

A dead body is removed from a house when possible, or the house is removed while the body lies on the spot. It may remain in a grave for two nights, three nights, or a month until birds, plants, floods, and wind indicate that it can be carried to a corpse-bearing building.
