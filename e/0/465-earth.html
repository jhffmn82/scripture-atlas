Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, earth emerges from the flood as waters recede, becomes dry, receives living creatures from the ship, and continues with seed time, harvest, seasons, and day and night. In Genesis, earth begins formless and water-covered, becomes dry land as God gathers the waters, yields vegetation, produces living creatures, and receives light from the sky lights. In Genesis, earth is corrupted and filled with wickedness and violence, so God resolves to destroy it and all flesh with a flood. In The Book of Job, earth has foundations, measured dimensions, a stretched line, and a cornerstone laid by the LORD; its ends are shaken at dawn and its wilderness receives rain and grass. In Psalms, earth was founded by the LORD, emerged from waters above the mountains, and is watered and filled with the fruit and riches of his works. In Psalms, earth is called to praise the LORD through its creatures and features, while his glory is above it. In Psalms, earth belongs to the LORD together with its fullness and inhabitants, having been founded by him upon the seas and established on the floods. In Psalms, earth changes, mountains shake into the seas, and it melts when God lifts his voice; it is where God makes desolations, ends wars, and is exalted.

and 14 more works

In Psalms, earth is visited, watered, enriched, and blessed by God, producing abundant grass, pastures, flocks, grain, and joyful singing. In Psalms, all the earth is commanded to sing to the LORD, tremble before him, rejoice, and await his judgment. In Psalms, earth is commanded to rejoice, trembles at the LORD's lightning and presence, and witnesses the mountains melting like wax. In Proverbs, earth and its deeps, springs, mountains, hills, fields, and dust are described as not yet existing when wisdom was born; wisdom was present as the LORD established the heavens, bounded the sea, and marked earth's foundations. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, creation is boundless yet trembles with heaven, the deep, and earth when the Lord visits, and is filled with his blessings and living things. In The Book of Isaiah, earth is polluted by its inhabitants, devoured by a curse, shaken and emptied by the LORD, and laid waste under disobedience. In The Book of Zephaniah: The LORD vows to utterly sweep away everything from the surface of the earth. In 1 John, the world is a moral realm opposed to the Father, containing worldly lusts and pride, hating believers, passing away with its lusts, while God's doer endures forever. In Revelation: The earth receives the fire thrown from the censer, followed by thunders, sounds, lightnings, and an earthquake. In Revelation, earth receives the dragon and his angels, is warned of woe with the sea, and helps the woman by swallowing the river cast out by the dragon. In The Doctrine of the Mean, Earth is large, substantial, far-reaching, and enduring, sustaining mountains, containing rivers and seas, supporting all things, and nourishing them without injury. In Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou), Earth affords advantages of situation, though they are inferior to the union arising from the accord of Men. In Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong), the world has a wide house, correct seat, and great path where a great man dwells, stands, and walks. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), Earth is used as a comparison for the broad, flowing influence of the superior man.
