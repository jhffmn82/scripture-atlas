Abstract written by a model

Felix is a governor at Caesarea, married to Drusilla, long a judge of the Jews and knowing the Way more exactly. He receives Paul and Lysias's letter, keeps Paul in Herod's palace, and defers the case. Terrified by Paul's talk of judgment, he still hopes for a bribe. After two years Porcius Festus succeeds him, and Felix leaves Paul in bonds to win favor with the Jews.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 23

Felix, the governor at Caesarea, receives Paul and the letter from Claudius Lysias. He asks Paul's province, learns he is from Cilicia, promises to hear him when his accusers arrive, and commands that he be kept in Herod's palace.

Chapter 24

Felix, the governor, hears the accusation against Paul and defers the case until Lysias comes down, having more exact knowledge of the Way. He orders Paul kept in custody with privileges, and is terrified when Paul reasons about righteousness, self-control, and judgment to come. He hopes for money from Paul, and when succeeded by Porcius Festus after two years, leaves Paul in bonds to gain favor with the Jews.
