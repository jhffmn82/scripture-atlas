Abstract written by a model

Living beings are all forms of life and existence described as earth, water, fire, wind, plants, grass, trees, corn, animals, and creatures that move or do not move. They suffer and experience pleasure and pain according to their own Karman, owe their present existence to it, and remain subject to birth, old age, death, and repeated rebirth. All desire happiness, hate pain, and will meet death. Yet people harm them through killing, injury, cutting, dismembering, and sciences or spells intended for destruction, often for comfort, pleasure, or amusement. The lectures repeatedly direct wise people and monks to stop such harm: no being should be killed or injured, directly or through others, by hand or foot, in thought, word, or act. Restraint and tender treatment toward every class of being, wherever it is found, are presented as protection of the soul and as part of the peace called Nirvâna.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

10 sections; each heading opens the text

Second Lecture, Called the Destruction of Karman. / First Chapter. .. Third Chapter.

Living beings suffer individually for their own deeds and experience pleasure and pain according to their Karman. They are subject to birth, old age, and death, and wise people are instructed to avoid injuring them and to regard them as themselves.

Third Lecture, Called the Knowledge of Troubles / First Chapter. .. Fourth Chapter.

A monk should cease to injure living beings, whether moving or stationary, above, below, or on earth. Abstaining from such injury is part of the peace called Nirvâna.

Fifth Lecture, Called Description of the Hells. / First Chapter. .. Second Chapter.

Living beings are harmed by sinners who kill or injure them for their own comfort. A wise man hearing about the hells should not kill any living being in the whole world.

Seventh Lecture, Called Description of the Wicked.

Living beings include earth, water, fire, wind, grass, trees, corn, and movable beings such as oviparous, viviparous, dirt-born, and fluid-born creatures. All desire happiness, and harming them harms the soul; every being will meet death.

Eighth Lecture, Called on Exertion.

Living beings are harmed by those who learn sciences or spells for killing and by those who kill, cut, or dismember them for comfort, pleasure, or amusement.

Ninth Lecture, Called the Law.

Living beings include earth, water, fire, wind, grass, trees, corn, animals, beings engendered in fluids and dirt, and plants. A wise man should treat all six classes tenderly in thoughts, words, and acts.

Tenth Lecture, Called Carefulness.

Living beings suffer pain and are afflicted. They should not be injured by a person's hands or feet, nor harmed directly or through others.

Eleventh Lecture, Called the Path.

Living beings include those that move and those that do not, on high, below, and on earth. They hate pains, so a wise man should study them and not kill or injure them.

Twelfth Lecture, Called the Creed.

They are among those who will be born again and again, together with Râkshasas, dwellers in Yama's world, Asuras, Gandharvas, and spirits that walk the air.

Fourteenth Lecture, Called the Nirgrantha.

Living beings are to be protected in thought, word, and act. A monk should remain restrained toward movable and immovable beings on high, below, and on earth, without hostile thoughts.
