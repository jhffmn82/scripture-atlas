Baruch the son of Neriah in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · 629 to 579 BC · the work’s page · read it where Baruch the son of Neriah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Baruch, son of Neriah (and of Mahseiah), is a scribe who serves Jeremiah. He receives the sealed and open deeds of purchase before witnesses and is commanded to store them in an earthen vessel so they last many days. He writes Jeremiah's dictated words on a scroll, reads it in the LORD's house and before the princes, is told to hide, is targeted by the king, is hidden by the LORD, and writes a second scroll. The LORD speaks of sorrow added to his pain, tells him not to seek great things for himself, and promises he will escape with his life. Later he is accused of turning Jeremiah against the people and is taken into Egypt, arriving at Tahpanhes.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 32

Baruch, son of Neriah, son of Mahseiah, receives the sealed and open deeds of purchase from Jeremiah before Hanamel, the witnesses, and the Jews in the court of the guard. He is commanded to put them in an earthen vessel so they will last many days.

Chapter 36

Baruch writes Jeremiah's dictated words on a scroll and reads it in the LORD's house, and later before the princes, explaining that he wrote with ink from Jeremiah's mouth. He is told to hide, is targeted for arrest by the king, is hidden by the LORD, and writes the second scroll.

Chapter 43

Baruch is accused by Azariah, Johanan, and the proud men of turning Jeremiah against them in order to deliver them to the Chaldeans to be killed or carried captive to Babylon. He is among those taken into Egypt and arrives at Tahpanhes.

Chapter 45

Baruch writes Jeremiah's words in a book and is the recipient of the LORD's message in the fourth year of Jehoiakim. He is told not to seek great things for himself, and is promised that he will escape with his life wherever he goes.
