strange woman (the seductress)

Abstract written by a model

The strange woman, also called the foreigner or adulteress, is a figure of seduction who appears across four chapters and is set against Wisdom. In Chapters 2 and 5 she flatters with words, forsakes the friend of her youth, and forgets the covenant of her God. Her lips drip honey and her mouth is smoother than oil, but in the end she is bitter as wormwood and sharp as a two-edged sword. Her house, feet and steps lead down to death and Sheol; none who go to her return or reach the paths of life. Her ways are crooked without her knowing it, and the son is told to be delivered from her and not to come near her house. In Chapter 7 she meets a young man in the street dressed as a prostitute, crafty, loud and defiant, lurking in streets and squares. She kisses him, claims to have paid her vows, and invites him to her perfumed bed since her husband is away until the full moon. Her flattering words lead him astray, and the speaker says she has thrown down many wounded and all her slain are a mighty army. In Chapter 8 the contrast is made through Wisdom, who cries out publicly, speaks truth, hates evil, and offers counsel, power, riches and honor. Kings and rulers reign by her; the LORD possessed her before creation. She was his craftsman and delight, and whoever finds her finds life, while those who hate her love death.

Chapter 2

The strange woman, also called the foreigner, flatters with her words, forsakes the friend of her youth, and forgets the covenant of her God. Her house leads down to death and her paths to the departed spirits, and none who go to her return or attain the paths of life. The son is to be delivered from her.

Chapter 5

The adulteress is described as having lips that drip honey and a mouth smoother than oil, yet in the end she is as bitter as wormwood and as sharp as a two-edged sword. Her feet go down to death and her steps lead to Sheol; she gives no thought to the way of life, and her ways are crooked without her knowing it. The son is warned not to come near her house or be captivated by her.

Chapter 7

The strange woman meets the young man in the street dressed as a prostitute and with crafty intent. She is described as loud, defiant, and always lurking in streets and squares. She kisses him and, claiming to have paid her vows, invites him to her perfumed bed because her husband has gone on a long journey and will return at the full moon. Her flattering words lead him astray, and the speaker says she has thrown down many wounded and that all her slain are a mighty army.

Chapter 8

Wisdom cries out in public places, at high places, crossroads, and city gates, calling the simple and fools to understanding. She declares her words true and righteous, says she hates evil, pride, and arrogance, and claims counsel, power, riches, and honor, with kings and rulers reigning by her. She says the LORD possessed her before creation, that she was present as a craftsman and a delight by his side, and that she delighted in the sons of men; whoever finds her finds life, and those who hate her love death.
