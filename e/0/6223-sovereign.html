Abstract written by a model

A sovereign has duties and should imitate Yao to discharge them perfectly. Benevolence is essential: it preserves the throne, while severe oppression leads to death and lesser oppression endangers life and weakens the kingdom. Effective government also requires the confidence of those in inferior positions, founded on friends’ trust. The sovereign must rectify faults in his mind, because character determines whether acts are benevolent, righteous, and correct.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

A sovereign should perfectly discharge the duties of a sovereign by imitating Yao. A sovereign who rules by severely oppressing the people will be slain, and a sovereign who stops short of the highest pitch of oppression will still endanger his life and weaken his kingdom.

Chapter 3

A sovereign who is not benevolent cannot preserve the throne from passing from him.

Chapter 12

The sovereign's confidence must be obtained by those occupying inferior situations if they are to succeed in governing the people. That confidence depends on being trusted by friends.

Chapter 20

The sovereign should have what is wrong in his mind rectified. His character determines whether his acts are benevolent, righteous, and correct.
