Abstract written by a model

In Leviticus, leprosy makes a person unclean until a priest examines and finds him healed, with a two-bird cleansing ritual marking the day of cleansing. In The Second Book of Kings, leprosy afflicts Naaman, is cleansed after he dips seven times in the Jordan, and is transferred to Gehazi as punishment for his greed and lying. In The Second Book of Chronicles, leprosy breaks out on Uzziah's forehead as a strike from the LORD, leaving him a leper until his death and living and buried apart.

leprosy in Leviticus

author unknown · 539 to 331 BC · the work’s page · read it where leprosy first appears

Abstract written by a model

Leprosy is a plague making a person unclean until a priest examines and finds him healed. A two-bird cleansing ritual marks the day of cleansing. The law, grouped with itch, mildew, swelling, scab, and bright spot, teaches distinguishing clean from unclean.

Chapter 14

Leprosy is treated as a plague that makes a person unclean until the priest examines him and finds it healed. The chapter's closing summary names it with itch, mildew of a garment or house, swelling, scab, and bright spot, as conditions the law teaches to distinguish as clean or unclean.
