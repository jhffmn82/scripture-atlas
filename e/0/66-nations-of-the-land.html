Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the inhabitants of the land are peoples who may not dwell among the Israelites lest they cause sin; they are to be driven out gradually, and Israel must make no covenant with them. In Leviticus, the nations cast out of the land defiled themselves with abominations, causing the land to be punished and to vomit out its inhabitants as a warning to Israel. In Deuteronomy, the seven nations of Canaan are greater and mightier than Israel, and the LORD gradually expels and destroys them, delivering their kings to Israel, which must make no covenants, marriages, or show mercy toward them. In Deuteronomy, the nations of the land are mightier and greater than Israel, marked by fortified cities, tall Anakim, wickedness, and abominations; the LORD promises to destroy and drive them out, and Israel must destroy their worship sites and avoid their gods. In The Book of Joshua, the nations remaining among Israel are allotted to Israel as an inheritance, but the LORD will drive them out only if Israel stays faithful; Israel must not mingle with them or serve their gods. In The Book of Judges, the inhabitants of the land remain as a snare to Israel because Israel did not obey the LORD's command to drive them out, and the LORD leaves them to test Israel. In The Book of Ezra, the peoples of the lands are listed among various nations that follow abominations, and their land is described as unclean and filled with filthiness; Israel is forbidden to seek their peace or prosperity.

nations of the land in Deuteronomy

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where nations of the land first appears

Abstract written by a model

The nations of the land are peoples mightier and greater than Israel, with heavily fortified cities and tall Anakim among them. They are marked by wickedness and abominations, including child sacrifice by fire and heeding sorcerers and diviners. Early on, the LORD promises to destroy them and drive them out before Israel. Later, Israel is told to destroy their worship sites and avoid their gods, and finally the nations are again shown driven out because of their abominations.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

They are described as nations greater and mightier than Israel, with great cities fortified up to the sky, and a great and tall people, the sons of the Anakim. The LORD will destroy them and drive them out before Israel because of their wickedness.

Chapter 12

The nations will be dispossessed and cut off by the LORD before the Israelites, who will dwell in their land. Their worship sites are to be destroyed, and the Israelites are warned not to inquire after their gods, because the nations did every abomination the LORD hates, even burning their sons and daughters in fire.

Chapter 18

The nations Israel will dispossess practice abominations and listen to sorcerers and diviners. Because of these abominations, the LORD drives them out from before Israel.

The remaining nations are allotted to Israel as inheritance, and the LORD will drive them out if Israel stays faithful. Israel must not mingle with them or mention, swear by, serve, or bow to their gods; clinging to them would make them snares, traps, scourges, and thorns.

Chapter 23

The remaining nations were allotted to Israel as an inheritance, and the LORD would thrust them out and drive them from Israel's sight. If Israel held fast to them, they would become a snare, a trap, a scourge in the sides, and thorns in the eyes.
