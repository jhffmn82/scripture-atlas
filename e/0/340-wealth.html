Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, wealth cannot buy life or redemption and is left to others at death, when a man carries nothing away and his glory does not descend after him. In Proverbs, wealth and riches ransom a man's life yet are deceptive; dishonest gain dwindles, steady gathering grows, riches lack profit in wrath, and a sinner's wealth is stored for the righteous. In Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher, riches and wealth are vanity because silver never satisfies, goods multiply consumers, hoarded wealth perishes, and the dead carry nothing away. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, riches and poverty come from the Lord; diligence may bring wealth, but death may leave it to others, and the Lord can swiftly make a poor man rich. In The Letter from James, riches are corrupted, garments are moth-eaten, and gold and silver are corroded, testifying against and consuming the oppressors who stored treasure in the last days. In The Great Learning, wealth is the result while virtue is the root, and its scattering or accumulation affects the people and does not constitute the state's true prosperity.

Abstract written by a model

Riches are a topic of vanity: love of silver never satisfies, growing goods merely multiply consumers, hoarded wealth perishes through misfortune, and at death the owner carries nothing away, leaving as naked as he came.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 5

Love of silver and abundance is never satisfied and is called vanity. As goods increase, those who eat them increase, and the owner gains only the sight of them. Wealth kept to its owner's harm perishes by misfortune, and nothing can be carried away at death.
