Ahab

Also called: Ahab king of Israel, Ahab king of Samaria, Ahab the son of Omri, Ahab’s house, his father, king of Israel, my lord, the king, the king of Israel, the whole house of Ahab, your father, your lord

Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, Ahab is king of Israel who reigns from Samaria, worships Baal, persecutes prophets, covets Naboth’s vineyard, and dies in battle. In The Second Book of Kings, Ahab is the ruler whose house is doomed for Naboth’s blood, with his seventy sons and whole house destroyed by Jehu. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Ahab is the king of Israel who persuades Jehoshaphat to join him at Ramoth Gilead and dies in battle after being struck by a stray arrow.

Ahab in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ahab first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ahab, son of Omri, is king of Israel who reigns twenty-two years from Samaria, where he is buried. He marries Jezebel daughter of Ethbaal, serves and worships Baal, builds a house and altar for Baal in Samaria, and makes the Asherah, doing more evil than any earlier king and provoking the LORD. He also builds an ivory house and cities. In the records he first appears sending Obadiah to find grass for his horses and mules during famine, and searching for Elijah, whom he calls a troubler of Israel; Elijah answers that Ahab and his father's house forsook the LORD's commandments, and the contest at Mount Carmel follows. Against Ben Hadad he wins two victories after prophetic words, but calls him brother, makes a covenant, and lets him go; a prophet says his life will replace Ben Hadad's, and he goes home sullen and angry. He then covets Naboth's vineyard and lets Jezebel arrange Naboth's death. Confronted by Elijah, he tears his clothes and fasts in sackcloth, so the LORD delays the evil to his son's day. Finally he hates and imprisons Micaiah for prophesying evil, disguises himself in battle at Ramoth Gilead, is struck by a random arrow, and dies at evening. His son Ahaziah succeeds him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 16

Ahab begins to reign in Asa's thirty-eighth year and reigns twenty-two years in Samaria. He does evil above all before him, marries Jezebel daughter of Ethbaal, serves and worships Baal, raises an altar for Baal in Samaria, and makes the Asherah, provoking the LORD more than any earlier king of Israel.

Chapter 18

Ahab sends Obadiah to search the springs and brooks for grass for the animals, and had sent to every nation and kingdom seeking Elijah. He calls Elijah a troubler of Israel, is rebuked as the one who forsook the LORD's commandments, and gathers Israel and the prophets to Mount Carmel. After the contest Elijah tells him to eat and drink, and he rides to Jezreel in the rain.

Chapter 20

Ahab, king of Israel, first submits to Ben Hadad's demands, then refuses the second on the elders' advice, and wins two victories after prophetic words. He calls Ben Hadad his brother, makes a covenant with him and lets him go, and is then told by a prophet that his life will take the place of Ben Hadad's life; he goes home sullen and angry.

Chapter 21

Ahab asks Naboth for his vineyard, sulks and refuses to eat when refused, and lets Jezebel arrange Naboth's death before going down to take possession. Elijah confronts him with the LORD's judgment, and when Ahab tears his clothes and fasts in sackcloth, the LORD delays the evil to his son's day.

Chapter 22

Ahab, king of Israel, proposes taking Ramoth Gilead from Syria, hates Micaiah for prophesying evil about him, and imprisons him. He disguises himself in battle but is struck by a random arrow and dies at evening, and is buried in Samaria. His reign is closed out with a reference to the ivory house and cities he built, and Ahaziah his son succeeds him.

Ahab in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ahab first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ahab is a ruler whose house is doomed by the LORD for the blood of Naboth and his sons. He has seventy sons in Samaria. Jehu destroys his whole house, cutting off every male, fulfilling Elijah's word and echoing the fates of Jeroboam's and Baasha's houses.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

Ahab's whole house is declared to be doomed, with every male cut off, and is compared to the houses of Jeroboam son of Nebat and Baasha son of Ahijah. Jehu recalls riding after Ahab with Bidkar and the LORD's burden laid on Ahab for the blood of Naboth and his sons.

Chapter 10

Ahab is the master whose house Jehu destroys, fulfilling the LORD's word spoken by Elijah. Jehu says Ahab served Baal a little, as part of his ruse to the people.

Ahab in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ahab first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ahab is a king of Israel who persuades Jehoshaphat to join him at Ramoth Gilead, trusts his four hundred prophets, and imprisons Micaiah for prophesying evil. Disguised in battle, he is struck by a stray arrow and dies at sunset.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

Ahab, king of Israel, feasted Jehoshaphat and persuaded him to go with him to Ramoth Gilead. He trusted his four hundred prophets, said he hated Micaiah for never prophesying good about him, and had him imprisoned on bread and water after hearing a prophecy of evil. He disguised himself in battle, was struck by a randomly shot arrow between the joints of his armor, propped himself up in his chariot until evening, and died at about sunset.
