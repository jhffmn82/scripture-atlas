Judah in Genesis

author unknown · about 449 BC · the work’s page · read it where Judah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judah is a son of Jacob (Israel) and brother of Joseph and Benjamin. The records follow him from a flawed early role to leadership and honor. In Chapter 37 he proposes selling Joseph to the Ishmaelites instead of killing him, and his brothers listen. In Chapter 38 he settles near his friend Hirah the Adullamite, marries Shua's daughter, and fathers Er, Onan, and Shelah. After Er and Onan die, he withholds Shelah from Er's widow Tamar, then unknowingly sleeps with her, leaving his signet, cord, and staff as a pledge. When she shows the pledge, he acknowledges she is more righteous than he. She bears twins, Perez and Zerah. In Chapter 43 he persuades Israel to send Benjamin to Egypt, offering himself as collateral and promising to bear the blame forever if he fails. In Chapter 44 he speaks for the brothers before Joseph, admits God has found out their iniquity, and offers to stay as a slave in Benjamin's place because of that pledge to his father. In Chapter 49 Jacob blesses him: his brothers will praise him and bow before him, he is a lion's cub, and the scepter will not depart from him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 37

Judah asks what profit there is in killing their brother and concealing his blood. He proposes selling Joseph to the Ishmaelites, since Joseph is their brother and flesh, and his brothers listen to him.

Chapter 38

Judah settles near Hirah the Adullamite, marries Shua's daughter, and fathers Er, Onan, and Shelah. After his sons Er and Onan die, he sends Tamar away to wait for Shelah but does not give her to him, and later unknowingly sleeps with her, leaving his signet, cord, and staff as a pledge. When she shows the pledge, he acknowledges she is more righteous than he, and he does not know her again.

Chapter 43

Judah reminds his father that the man said they would not see his face unless their brother came with them. He urges Israel to send the boy so that all may live, and offers himself as collateral, promising to bear the blame forever if he does not bring Benjamin back. His appeal persuades Israel.

Chapter 44

Judah spoke for the brothers before Joseph, saying God had found out their iniquity and offering that all of them be slaves. He then made a long plea recounting their father's love for the boy and fear of losing him, and offered to stay as Joseph's slave instead of the boy, because he had become collateral for him to their father.

Chapter 49

Jacob says Judah's brothers will praise him, his hand will be on the neck of his enemies, and his father's sons will bow before him. He is called a lion's cub, and the scepter will not depart from him until he comes to whom it belongs, to whom the obedience of the peoples will be given.

Judah in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · 449 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Judah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judah is a man with five sons: Er, Onan and Shelah by Shua's daughter, and Perez and Zerah by his daughter-in-law Tamar. Er, the firstborn, was wicked and killed. Later records list Perez, Hezron, Carmi, Hur and Shobal, tracing many descendant families, including Shelah's line. Judah's children multiplied, unlike the Simeonites' families.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Judah had sons Er, Onan and Shelah by Shua's daughter, and Perez and Zerah by his daughter-in-law Tamar, five sons in all. Er, his firstborn, was wicked in the LORD's sight and was killed. Judah's descendants through Perez and Zerah form the main line of the chapter.

Chapter 4

Judah's sons are listed as Perez, Hezron, Carmi, Hur, and Shobal, and many descendant families are traced from them, including Zorathites, craftsmen, and potters. His son Shelah's line is also recorded. The Simeonites are said not to have multiplied like the children of Judah.
