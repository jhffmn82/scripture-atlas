moon

celestial body · 5 works · 40 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: Moon, Moon that keeps in it the seed of the Bull, The Moon (Al-Qamar), The moon, the Moon, the moon

Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the moon marks seasons, declares times, serves as a sign, names the month, and completes its course. In Surah 54: The Moon (Al-Qamar), the Moon is described as having been rent in twain when the Hour drew nigh. In Yasna, Moon is a holy lord of ritual order, approached in worship, and contains seeds of Kine and cattle in its beams. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, the Moon keeps the seed of the Bull, waxes and wanes, gives blessings, and receives offerings. In Nyayish, the Moon keeps the only-created Bull’s seed, is hailed and watched, waxes and wanes, and receives sacrifices.

Moon that keeps in it the seed of the Bull in Yashts (Yasts) I-XII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Moon that keeps in it the seed of the Bull first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Moon, holy and master of holiness, keeps the seed of the Bull. It waxes for fifteen days, warming its light so golden-hued plants grow, then wanes for fifteen days. Bright and glorious, it gives water, warmth, wisdom, wealth, healing, and prosperity, and receives libations, Haoma, meat, baresma, holy spells, speech, and deeds.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

VII. Mâh Yast.

The Moon is hailed and worshipped as holy and master of holiness. It waxes for fifteen days and wanes for fifteen days; its light causes golden-hued plants to grow in spring, and offerings are made to it.

III. Mâh Nyâyis.

The Moon is described as keeping in it the seed of the Bull and is associated with the only-created Bull and the Bull of many species. It is hailed, observed as it looks upon those who look at it, asked about as it waxes and wanes, and worshipped through sacrifices for its brightness and glory.
