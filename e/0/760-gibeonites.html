Gibeonites in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Gibeonites first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Gibeonites were Hivites living in Gibeon, Chephirah, Beeroth, and Kiriath Jearim. Fearing for their lives after Joshua's victories at Jericho and Ai, they tricked Israel into a covenant. When exposed, they cited the LORD's command to Moses, submitted to Joshua's judgment, and were made wood cutters and drawers of water. Later, at peace with Israel, they appealed to Joshua at Gilgal as his servants, and he came with his army to rescue them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

The inhabitants of Gibeon, whose cities are Gibeon, Chephirah, Beeroth, and Kiriath Jearim, fear for their lives after hearing what Joshua did to Jericho and Ai and trick Israel into a covenant. When exposed, they explain they knew of the LORD's command to Moses to destroy the land's inhabitants, submit to Joshua's judgment, and are made wood cutters and drawers of water.

Chapter 10

The men of Gibeon, having made peace with Israel, sent to Joshua at Gilgal begging him not to abandon his servants and to come quickly to save them. Their appeal brought Joshua and his army to their rescue.

Abstract written by a model

The Gibeonites, a remnant of the Amorites bound by Israel's oath, were targeted by Saul. They refuse silver or gold, request seven of Saul's sons, and hang them before the LORD in Gibeah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 21

The Gibeonites are described as a remnant of the Amorites to whom Israel had sworn an oath, whom Saul sought to kill. They refuse silver or gold, ask that seven of Saul's sons be delivered to them, and hang them on the mountain before the LORD in Gibeah of Saul.
