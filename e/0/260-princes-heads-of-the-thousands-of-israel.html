Abstract written by a model

The princes are twelve tribal leaders, each head of his fathers' house, also called heads of the thousands of Israel. In Chapter 1, one man from each tribe, from Elizur of Reuben to Ahira of Naphtali, is named to stand with Moses and Aaron and assist in the counting. In Chapter 7, as overseers of those counted, they bring six covered wagons and twelve oxen, then each gives an identical altar dedication offering on successive days: silver platters, silver bowls, golden ladles, and sacrificial animals.

Chapter 1

One man from each tribe, each head of his fathers' house, is named to stand with Moses and Aaron: Elizur of Reuben, Shelumiel of Simeon, Nahshon of Judah, Nethanel of Issachar, Eliab of Zebulun, Elishama of Ephraim, Gamaliel of Manasseh, Abidan of Benjamin, Ahiezer of Dan, Pagiel of Asher, Eliasaph of Gad, and Ahira of Naphtali. They are called the princes of the tribes and heads of the thousands of Israel, and they assist in the counting.

Chapter 7

The princes, heads of their fathers' houses and over those who were counted, brought six covered wagons and twelve oxen, one wagon per two princes and one ox each. They then each gave an offering for the altar's dedication on successive days, in the same amounts, totaling twelve silver platters, twelve silver bowls, and twelve golden ladles with animals for sacrifice.
