day of the LORD in The Book of Joel

author unknown · about 399 to 299 BC · the work’s page · read it where day of the LORD first appears

Abstract written by a model

The day of the LORD is a prophesied event in the Book of Joel, announced as imminent destruction from the Almighty. It is great, very awesome, and a day of darkness and gloominess that no one can endure. Joel cries 'Alas for the day!', then describes the earth quaking, heavens trembling, and sun, moon, and stars darkened, the sun turning to darkness and the moon to blood. It culminates in judgment on the nations in the valley of Jehoshaphat, with repayment on Tyre, Sidon, Philistia, and desolation for Egypt and Edom.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Joel / Chapter 1

Joel cries 'Alas for the day!' and declares that the day of the LORD is at hand. It will come as destruction from the Almighty.

Chapter 2

The day of the LORD is described as close at hand, a day of darkness, gloominess, clouds and thick darkness, great and very awesome, such that no one can endure it. The earth quakes, the heavens tremble, and the sun, moon, and stars are darkened. Later it is called the great and terrible day, preceded by the sun turning to darkness and the moon to blood.

Chapter 3

The LORD will execute judgment on the nations in the valley of Jehoshaphat, imaged as a sickle put to a ripe harvest and a full wine press. Tyre, Sidon, and Philistia will have their repayment returned on their own heads, and Egypt and Edom will become desolate for shedding innocent blood.

day of the LORD's judgment in The Book of Zephaniah

author unknown · 639 to 608 BC · the work’s page · read it where day of the LORD's judgment first appears

Abstract written by a model

The day of the LORD is announced as near and hurrying greatly, a day of sacrifice, wrath, distress, ruin, darkness and gloom, when trumpet and alarm sound against fortified cities and silver and gold cannot save anyone. It then appears as an appointed time of fierce anger on a shameless nation, passing like chaff, while the humble may be hidden. It culminates with the LORD rising to the prey, gathering nations to pour out indignation, devouring all the earth with the fire of his jealousy, then purifying the peoples' lips and removing the proud from his holy mountain.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Zephaniah / Chapter 1

The day of the LORD is announced as at hand, near, and hurrying greatly, and is called the day of the LORD's sacrifice. It is described as a day of wrath, distress, anguish, trouble, ruin, darkness, gloom, clouds, blackness, trumpet and alarm against fortified cities, in which silver and gold cannot deliver anyone.

Chapter 2

The day of the LORD's anger is an appointed time, when the day passes like chaff, and his fierce anger comes on the shameless nation. The humble are told they may be hidden on that day.

Chapter 3

The LORD tells those who wait for him that it is the day he rises up to the prey, determined to gather the nations and kingdoms and pour on them his indignation and fierce anger. On it all the earth will be devoured by the fire of his jealousy, and it leads into the purifying of the peoples' lips and the removal of the proud from his holy mountain.

day of the LORD's coming in The Book of Malachi

author unknown · 459 to 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where day of the LORD's coming first appears

Abstract written by a model

The day of the LORD's coming is a great and terrible day of judgment and purification. In Chapter 3, no one can easily endure it, as he is like refiner's fire and launderers' soap, purifying the sons of Levi, judging wrongdoers, and claiming those who fear him. In Chapter 4, it burns like a furnace, consuming the proud and wicked to ashes beneath the righteous, with Elijah sent beforehand.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

The day of the LORD's coming is described as one that no one can easily endure or stand through, as he is like a refiner's fire and launderers' soap. On that day he purifies the sons of Levi, brings judgment on wrongdoers, and makes those who fear him his own possession.

Chapter 4

The day is described as burning like a furnace and as coming to burn up the proud and all who work wickedness, leaving them neither root nor branch. It is called the day that the LORD makes, on which the wicked become ashes under the feet of the righteous. It is also named the great and terrible day of the LORD, and Elijah is to be sent before it comes.
