Abstract written by a model

The whole duty is the complete prescribed discipline for a monk or nun: rules on food, alms, conduct, and continual exertion. Stated in the Eighth Lesson, it requires understanding all meanings, bliss, rejecting specified impure or raw substances, and indifference to pleasant scents.

Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book II, translated by Hermann Jacobi / Second Book / First Part. / First Lecture, Call

The whole duty of a monk or a nun is presented as the prescribed discipline concerning food, alms, conduct, and exertion. One should be instructed in all its meanings and endowed with bliss while always exerting oneself.

Eighth Lesson.

The whole duty is identified as the complete set of rules stated in the Eighth Lesson. It includes rejecting specified impure or raw substances and remaining indifferent to pleasant scents.
