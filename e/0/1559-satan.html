Abstract written by a model

In 1 Chronicles, Satan stands against Israel and moves David to take a census of Israel. In The Book of Job, Satan accuses Job and brings about the loss of his possessions and children before afflicting him with painful sores. In The Book of Zechariah, Satan stands at Joshua the high priest's right hand as his adversary and is rebuked twice by the LORD. In The Gospel According to Matthew, Satan tempts Jesus three times with bread, the temple pinnacle, and worldly kingdoms. In The Good News According to Mark, Satan is alleged to be a ruler of demons and is described by Jesus as a strong man whose house can be plundered only after he is bound. In The Gospel According to Luke, Satan tempts Jesus, claims authority over the world's kingdoms, and is linked to Beelzebul, prince of the demons. In The Gospel According to John, the devil is described by Jesus as a murderer, liar, and father of lies. In Revelation, Satan is the great red dragon, deceiver, accuser, persecutor, and giver of authority to the first beast, ultimately cast into the lake of fire.

Satan in The Book of Job

author unknown · about 574 BC · the work’s page · read it where Satan first appears

Abstract written by a model

Satan appears among God's sons presenting themselves before the LORD, reporting that he has been traveling back and forth on the earth. He accuses Job of fearing God only because of protection and blessings. Given power over Job's possessions but not Job himself, he brings about the loss of Job's goods and children. Appearing again, he argues Job would renounce God if his flesh were touched, and, given Job with his life spared, strikes him with painful sores.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

Satan comes among God's sons before the LORD, says he has been going back and forth on the earth, and challenges Job's piety, claiming Job fears God only because he has been protected and blessed. Given power over all that Job has, though not over Job himself, he goes out from the LORD's presence, and Job's possessions and children are then lost.

Chapter 2

Satan comes among God's sons to present himself before the LORD, saying he has been going back and forth on the earth. He argues that Job will renounce God if his bone and flesh are touched. Given Job into his hand with his life spared, he strikes Job with painful sores.

Satan in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where Satan first appears

Abstract written by a model

Satan, called the devil and linked to Beelzebul, prince of the demons, claims authority over the world's kingdoms. In Chapter 4 he tempts Jesus for forty days with bread, kingdoms for worship, and a temple leap, then departs after rebuffs. In Chapter 11, a crowd accuses Jesus of casting out demons by Beelzebul.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

The devil tempts Jesus for forty days, telling him to turn a stone into bread, offering him all the kingdoms of the world if he will worship him, and urging him to throw himself from the temple pinnacle while quoting Scripture. He claims the authority over the kingdoms has been delivered to him. After Jesus rebuffs each temptation, he departs until another time.

Chapter 11

Some in the crowd claim Jesus casts out demons by Beelzebul, the prince of the demons. Jesus replies that a divided kingdom falls, asks by whom their own children cast out demons, and says he acts by God's finger.

Satan in Revelation

author unknown · about AD 95 · the work’s page · read it where Satan first appears

Abstract written by a model

Satan is a great red dragon with seven heads, ten horns, and seven crowns, also called the old serpent and the devil. He is the deceiver of the whole world and the accuser of the brothers before God day and night. He is first seen drawing a third of the stars down and threatening the woman's child. Defeated in war, he is thrown down to earth with his angels, furious because his time is short. He persecutes the woman and her offspring, and gives the first beast power, throne, and authority, so people worship him. Finally he is bound in the sealed abyss for the thousand years, then thrown into the lake of fire, tormented forever.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 12

A great red dragon with seven heads, ten horns, and seven crowns, he draws a third of the stars to the earth with his tail and stands ready to devour the woman's child. He is identified as the old serpent, the devil and Satan, the deceiver of the whole world, and is defeated in war and thrown down to earth with his angels. Furious and knowing his time is short, he persecutes the woman, spews a river after her, and then goes to make war on the rest of her offspring.

Chapter 13

The dragon gives the first beast its power, throne, and great authority. People worship the dragon because he gave his authority to the beast. The second beast speaks like a dragon.
