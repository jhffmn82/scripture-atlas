meditation in Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where meditation first appears

Abstract written by a model

Meditation is a monk’s duty when not subdued by wicked pleasures. It unfolds as solitary, guarded, restrained practice, then as the wise inward withdrawal that covers sins, alongside religious practices governed chiefly by forbearance.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Second Lecture, Called the Destruction of Karman. / First Chapter. .. Third Chapter.

Meditation is presented as a duty for those who are not subdued by wicked pleasures. A monk should meditate alone, guarded in words and restrained in thoughts.

Eighth Lecture, Called on Exertion.

Meditation is used by the wise man to cover his sins, as a tortoise draws its limbs into its body. A monk should meditate and perform religious practices while regarding forbearance as the paramount duty.
