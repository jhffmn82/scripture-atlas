Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, Asa is king of Judah who reigns forty-one years, removes idols and Maacah, makes an alliance against Baasha, suffers diseased feet, and is succeeded by Jehoshaphat. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Asa is king of Judah who begins by doing right, reforms worship, leads Judah in battle and covenant renewal, but later relies on foreign alliances, imprisons a seer, suffers diseased feet, and dies.

Asa in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Asa first appears

Abstract written by a model

Asa was king of Judah, reigning forty-one years in Jerusalem. Son of Maacah's line, he did right in the LORD's eyes like David, and his heart stayed perfect with the LORD all his days. He removed idols and deposed Maacah for making an abominable image, though he left the high places. Constantly at war with Baasha, he secured a treaty with Ben Hadad and used Ramah's stones and timber to build Geba and Mizpah. In old age his feet were diseased. He was buried in Jerusalem, succeeded by his son Jehoshaphat.

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 15

Asa reigned forty-one years over Judah and did right in the LORD's eyes like David: he put away the sodomites and idols, removed his mother Maacah from being queen for making an image for an Asherah, and burned the image at the Kidron. He left the high places, though his heart was perfect with the LORD all his days. Facing Baasha's building of Ramah, he bribed Ben Hadad with temple and palace treasure, then used Ramah's stones and timber to build Geba and Mizpah; in old age he was diseased in his feet, and Jehoshaphat his son succeeded him.

Asa in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Asa first appears

Abstract written by a model

Asa is a king of Judah, son of Abijah and Maacah's descendant line, who succeeds his father and begins by doing what is good and right in the LORD's eyes. He removes foreign altars, high places, pillars and Asherah poles, commands Judah to seek the LORD, and builds fortified cities during ten quiet years. He fields an army of 580,000 and, when Zerah attacks, cries to the LORD and pursues the fleeing Ethiopians to Gerar. Encouraged by prophecy, he renews the altar, leads a covenant gathering in his fifteenth year, and removes his mother Maacah as queen mother, though the high places remain. Later, facing Baasha, he pays Ben Hadad with temple and royal treasure instead of relying on the LORD. Rebuked by Hanani, he imprisons the seer and oppresses some people, and is told wars will follow. In his thirty-ninth year his feet are diseased; he seeks only physicians, dies in his forty-first year, and is buried in his own tomb in David's city.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

Asa succeeds his father Abijah as king and does what is good and right in the LORD's eyes, removing foreign altars, high places, pillars, Asherah poles and sun images. He builds fortified cities during ten quiet years, fields an army of 580,000 men, and when Zerah attacks he cries to the LORD for help. He pursues the fleeing Ethiopians to Gerar, plunders the area, and returns to Jerusalem.

Chapter 15

Asa took courage on hearing the prophecy, put away the abominations from Judah, Benjamin, and the cities taken in Ephraim, and renewed the LORD's altar. He led the covenant gathering at Jerusalem in his fifteenth year, removed his mother Maacah as queen mother and destroyed her image, and brought dedicated silver, gold, and vessels into God's house. His heart was perfect all his days, though the high places were not removed.

Chapter 16

Asa, king of Judah, paid Ben Hadad with silver and gold from the temple and royal treasuries to break his treaty with Baasha, then used Ramah's stones and timber to build Geba and Mizpah. He was rebuked by Hanani for relying on Syria instead of the LORD, responded by imprisoning the seer and oppressing some people, and was told he would have wars from then on. He was diseased in his feet in his thirty-ninth year, sought only physicians, died in his forty-first year, and was buried in his own tomb in David's city.
