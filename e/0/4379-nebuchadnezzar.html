Abstract written by a model

Nebuchadnezzar is the king of the Assyrians, reigning from Nineveh, who styles himself the great king and lord of all the earth. The records open with him making war on Arphaxad and summoning western peoples, who refuse him. Angered, he swears vengeance by his throne and kingdom. He then defeats Arphaxad in the seventeenth year, captures Ecbatana, kills Arphaxad in the mountains of Ragau, and returns to Nineveh to feast with his vast multinational army for one hundred twenty days. He next convenes his great men, who decree destruction of all flesh that did not follow his word, and charges Holofernes with a campaign against the west. His aims grow explicitly divine: destroying the land's gods so all nations worship him alone and call upon him as a god. Later chapters show him through Holofernes, who calls him lord of all the earth and the only god, says his words will not be in vain, and promises that those who serve him will not be hurt. He remains offstage, a distant, absolute power.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Judith / Chapter 1

Nebuchadnezzar, king of the Assyrians in Nineveh, makes war on Arphaxad and sends summons to many western peoples, who refuse to join him. Angered, he swears by his throne and kingdom to avenge himself on them, then defeats Arphaxad in the seventeenth year, takes Ecbatana, and kills Arphaxad. He returns to Nineveh and feasts with his army for one hundred twenty days.

Chapter 2

Nebuchadnezzar convenes his servants and great men, recounts the wickedness of the land, and they decree the destruction of all flesh that did not follow his word. He charges Holofernes with a campaign against the west, swearing by his life and kingdom that he will carry out his threats, including covering the earth with the feet of his army.

Chapter 3

Nebuchadnezzar is called the great king, and the messengers describe their people as his servants. Holofernes has been given the task of destroying all the gods of the land so that all nations will worship Nebuchadnezzar only and all tongues and tribes will call upon him as a god.

Chapter 6

Nebuchadnezzar is called by Holofernes the lord of all the earth and the only god, who will send forth his might to destroy Israel. Holofernes quotes him as saying his spoken words will not be in vain.

Chapter 11

Nebuchadnezzar is called king of all the earth, and Holofernes says those who serve him will not be hurt. Judith claims that even the beasts and birds live through his power and Holofernes' strength. Holofernes offers her a home in his palace and renown throughout the earth.
