A poor brother is an impoverished fellow Israelite protected by law in Chapter 25. He may have sold possessions, which his nearest kinsman or he himself, if prosperous, can redeem. He is upheld like a temporary resident, spared interest and profit, and serves as a hired servant, not a slave. He and his children go free at Jubilee; if sold to a foreigner, redemption is priced by remaining years.

Chapter 25

A poor brother who sells part of his possessions may have them redeemed by his nearest kinsman, or he may redeem them himself if he prospers. He is to be upheld, given no loans at interest or food for profit, and treated as a hired servant rather than a slave. He and his children go free at the Jubilee, and if sold to a foreigner he may be redeemed by relatives or himself at a price based on the years remaining.
