Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Pekah was king of Israel who killed Pekahiah, reigned twenty years, allied with Rezin against Jerusalem, and was killed by Hoshea. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Pekah the son of Remaliah killed 120,000 valiant men in Judah in one day. In The Book of Isaiah, Pekah the son of Remaliah was king of Israel and co-plotted with Rezin against Jerusalem and Judah.

Pekah in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pekah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pekah, son of Remaliah, was a king of Israel who rose as captain of Pekahiah, killed him, and reigned twenty years in Samaria, doing evil like Jeroboam. Assyria's Tiglath Pileser seized northern lands in his reign. He allied with Rezin of Syria to besiege Ahaz in Jerusalem without success, and Hoshea finally conspired against and killed him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 15

Pekah son of Remaliah, captain of Pekahiah, killed him and reigned twenty years in Samaria, doing evil. Tiglath Pileser of Assyria took many northern lands during his reign. Hoshea conspired against and killed him, and the LORD began sending him with Rezin of Syria against Judah.

Chapter 16

Pekah son of Remaliah, king of Israel, is named as the reference point for the start of Ahaz's reign, in his seventeenth year. He joined Rezin king of Syria in coming up to Jerusalem to wage war and besieging Ahaz, but they could not overcome him.
