In The Second Book of Chronicles, Huram serves Solomon as king of Tyre, supplying materials, skilled workers, bronze work for God's house, cities, ships, and servants.

Hiram in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hiram first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hiram is the king of Tyre who had always loved David. On hearing of Solomon's anointing he sent servants, blessed the LORD for David's wise son, and agreed to supply cedar and cypress timber floated down from Lebanon, receiving yearly wheat and oil and making a treaty. Later he furnished cedar, cypress and gold, received twenty Galilean cities, disliked them, calling them Cabul, yet sent 120 talents of gold and experienced sailors for Solomon's Ophir fleet.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Hiram, king of Tyre, sent servants to Solomon on hearing of his anointing, having always loved David. He rejoiced at Solomon's message, blessed the LORD for giving David a wise son, and agreed to supply cedar and cypress timber, bringing it down from Lebanon to the sea and floating it in rafts to the place Solomon specified. In return he received yearly wheat and oil for his household, and he made a treaty with Solomon; his builders also helped cut stones and prepare timber.

Chapter 9

Hiram king of Tyre has furnished Solomon with cedar, cypress and gold, and receives twenty cities in Galilee in return. He is displeased with them, calling them Cabul, yet sends Solomon 120 talents of gold and sends experienced sailors with Solomon's servants on the fleet to Ophir.

Huram in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Huram first appears

Abstract written by a model

Huram is the king of Tyre who blesses the LORD, God of Israel, and serves Solomon. In Chapter 2 he replies in writing, agrees to send Lebanon wood by sea in rafts to Joppa, asks for promised wheat, barley, oil and wine, and sends the skilled Huram-abi. In Chapter 4, Huram, also called Huram-abi, makes the bronze work for God's house: two pillars with capitals, bowls, networks and four hundred pomegranates, bases with basins, pots, shovels, forks, and the sea with twelve oxen. He also gives cities to Solomon and sends ships and servants.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Huram, king of Tyre, replies in writing to Solomon, saying the LORD loves his people and made Solomon king over them, and he blesses the LORD, the God of Israel. He sends the skilled man Huram-abi, agrees to cut wood in Lebanon and bring it by sea in rafts to Joppa, and asks that the promised wheat, barley, oil, and wine be sent to his servants.

Chapter 4

Huram, also called Huram-abi, made the pots, shovels, forks, and basins, and finished the work he did for King Solomon in God's house. This included the two pillars, their bowls and capitals, the two networks, the four hundred pomegranates, the bases with their basins, and the sea with its twelve oxen.
