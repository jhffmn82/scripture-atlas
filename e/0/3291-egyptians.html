Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, the Egyptians fear Abram, trade their wealth and land for food during famine, become Pharaoh's servants, and avoid eating with Hebrews. In Exodus, the Egyptians enslave Israel, suffer the plagues and death of their firstborn, pursue Israel into the sea, and drown. In Psalms, the Egyptian enemies have their hearts turned to hate God's people, conspire against his servants, suffer plagues, and rejoice when Israel departs. In The Wisdom of Solomon, the Egyptians oppress God's people, suffer plagues and darkness, lose their children and army, and drown while pursuing the holy ones. In The Wisdom of Solomon, the enemies of God's people oppress them and foolishly treat lifeless idols as gods. In The Book of Isaiah, the Egyptians turn to idols and wizards, suffer under a fierce king, then come to know and worship the LORD before stumbling with those they help. In The Book of Ezekiel, the Egyptians are scattered for forty years, gathered back to Pathros as the lowest of kingdoms, and come to know the LORD. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), Pharaoh's folk afflict the Children of Israel, slay their sons, spare their women, and drown in the sea.

Egyptians in Genesis

author unknown · about 449 BC · the work’s page · read it where Egyptians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Egyptians are a people portrayed first as a perceived threat to Abram, who fears they will kill him for Sarai's beauty, which Pharaoh's princes praise. Later, in famine, they trade money, livestock, bodies and land for bread and seed, are moved to cities by Joseph, give Pharaoh a fifth, and become his servants. They avoid eating with Hebrews.

Chapter 47

When their money fails, the Egyptians give Joseph their livestock for bread, and in the second year offer their bodies and lands to Pharaoh in exchange for food and seed. They are moved to the cities, receive seed, agree to give a fifth of the harvest to Pharaoh, and say Joseph has saved their lives and that they will be Pharaoh's servants.

Egyptians in Exodus

author unknown · about 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Egyptians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Egyptians are the people who dominate and oppress Israel in these records, and their story is one of escalating conflict ending in their ruin. They begin by dreading the multiplying Israelites, enslaving them ruthlessly with hard labor, and Pharaoh orders every Hebrew son thrown into the river. God promises deliverance and says the Egyptians will favor Israel, so Israelite women ask them for jewels and clothing. A series of plagues follows: the river turns to blood and cannot be drunk, livestock die, boils and hail strike, and some of Pharaoh's servants who fear the LORD's word shelter their servants and livestock while others do not. The LORD distinguishes them from Israel. The death of their firstborn causes a great cry; they urge Israel to leave in haste, saying "We are all dead men," and give silver, gold, and clothing. Pharaoh's heart is hardened and they pursue Israel into the sea, where they are confused, acknowledge the LORD fights for Israel, and are covered by the returning waters, seen dead on the shore.

Chapter 14

The Egyptians pursue Israel after the LORD hardens Pharaoh's heart and overtake them encamped by the sea. They follow Israel into the middle of the sea, are confused, and say the LORD fights for Israel against them. The returning waters cover them, and Israel sees them dead on the seashore.

Egyptians in The Wisdom of Solomon

author unknown · about 99 BC · the work’s page · read it where Egyptians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Egyptians are portrayed as ungodly oppressors who refused to know God and enslaved his people, the holy ones and children of God, along with guest benefactors whom they hated. Their punishment is presented as deserved and fitting. They are afflicted by vermin, locusts and flies, then by strange rains, hail, storms and fire, and are imprisoned in darkness, losing sight. After plotting to kill the holy ones' babes, they lose their children and whole army in a flood. Terrified by dreams, and having disbelieved because of enchantments, they confess the people to be God's children. They then foolishly pursue those they had driven out, meeting a strange death.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 16

The oppressors are deservedly punished through vermin, locusts and flies, with no healing found, and suffer inescapable lack. They are pursued by strange rains, hails and storms and consumed by fire, so that they see they are chased by God's judgment and recognize him as the one who delivers from every evil.

Chapter 18

The Egyptians were deprived of light and imprisoned by darkness, as they deserved for imprisoning God's children. After plotting to kill the holy ones' babes, they lost their multitude of children and their whole army in a mighty flood. Terrified by dreams, and having disbelieved because of enchantments, they confessed the people to be God's children.

Chapter 19

The Egyptians, still lamenting at the graves of their dead, made a foolish decision to pursue as fugitives those whom they had begged to leave and driven out. Their doom made them forget what had happened, so they would fill up their remaining punishment and find a strange death. They are also condemned for making slaves of benefactor guests, afflicting those who had shared their rights, and being struck with loss of sight in yawning darkness, each looking for the passage through his own door.

Egyptians in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · 739 to 680 BC · the work’s page · read it where Egyptians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Egyptians are a people shown in two stages. In Chapter 19 they fall into infighting, lose their spirit, turn to idols and wizards, are handed to a fierce king, and fear Judah. Then, in that day, they cry to the LORD, know him, worship, are healed, and worship with the Assyrians. In Chapter 31 they are declared men, not God, with horses of flesh, and will stumble and be consumed with those they help.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 19

The Egyptians are stirred to fight brother against brother and city against city, lose their spirit, and turn to idols, charmers, and wizards. They are given to a fierce king, become like women trembling at the LORD's shaking hand, and fear Judah. In that day they cry to the LORD, know him, worship with sacrifice and offering, keep their vows, return to him, are healed, and worship with the Assyrians.

Chapter 31

The Egyptians are declared to be men and not God, and their horses flesh and not spirit. When the LORD stretches out his hand, the helper will stumble and all will be consumed together with the one helped.
