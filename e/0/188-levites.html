Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the sons of Levi gather to Moses, take the LORD’s side, and execute judgment throughout the camp. In Leviticus, the priests eat and retain designated portions of offerings, sprinkle blood, burn altar fat, and perform atonement. In Numbers, the Levites are set apart to serve the LORD, carry and guard the Tabernacle, receive tithes and cities, and perform assigned sanctuary duties. In Deuteronomy, Levi carries the ark, ministers and blesses in the LORD’s name, teaches the law, offers incense and sacrifices, and receives the LORD as inheritance. In Deuteronomy, the Levites receive no land inheritance, minister at the chosen place, share in offerings and tithes, and declare the curses. In The Book of Joshua, the tribe of Levi receives the LORD and offerings made by fire as its inheritance instead of land. In The Book of Joshua, the Levites receive forty-eight pastured cities instead of a land inheritance. In 1 Chronicles, the Levites descend from Levi, carry the ark, serve the tabernacle and temple, guard vessels and gates, sing, and minister before the LORD.

Levites in Numbers

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Levites first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Levites are a tribe set apart by the LORD and deliberately excluded from the census of the children of Israel, because they receive no inheritance of land among them. Their central significance is that they belong to the LORD, taken in place of Israel's firstborn, and are given to Aaron and his sons as a gift dedicated to the LORD to serve in the Tent of Meeting. The records first establish their appointment as custodians of the Tabernacle of the Testimony: they carry it with its furnishings, take it down, set it up, and encamp around it so no wrath falls on the congregation. They camp in the middle of the camps, and the Tent of Meeting sets out with them. They are then set before Aaron, and 22,000 males from a month old are counted across the three clans of Gershon, Kohath, and Merari. Those aged thirty to fifty who enter service number 8,580, each assigned his service and burden. Wagons and oxen are distributed by service: Gershon receives two wagons and four oxen, Merari four wagons and eight oxen under Ithamar, while Kohath receives none because they carry the holy things on their shoulders. The Levites are cleansed and offered as a wave offering, serving from age twenty-five and retiring at fifty, after which they assist their brothers. Their duties carry limits and costs: they make atonement so no plague strikes when Israel approaches the sanctuary, but they must not approach the altar or the sanctuary vessels on pain of death, and they bear their own iniquity. In place of land they receive Israel's tithe as inheritance by a statute forever, and they give a tithe of the tithe to Aaron the priest. A later count lists the families, including Libnites, Hebronites, Mahlites, Mushites, and Korahites, totaling 23,000 males from a month old. The records end with the provision of forty-eight cities with pasture lands, six of them cities of refuge, given out of Israel's inheritance for the Levites to dwell in.

Chapter 1

The tribe of Levi is not counted among the children of Israel in the census, by the LORD's command. The Levites are instead appointed over the Tabernacle of the Testimony and its furnishings, to carry it, care for it, take it down and set it up, and encamp around it.

Chapter 2

The Levites encamp in the middle of the camps, and the Tent of Meeting sets out with them. They are not counted among the children of Israel, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Chapter 3

The Levites are set before Aaron to minister to him and to keep the requirements of the congregation and the tabernacle. They are taken as the LORD's in place of Israel's firstborn, and twenty-two thousand males from a month old are counted in the three clans of Gershon, Kohath, and Merari.

Chapter 4

The Levites are divided into the Kohathite, Gershonite, and Merarite clans, and those aged thirty to fifty who enter service are counted. The total is 8,580, each assigned according to his service and burden.

Chapter 7

The Levites received the wagons and oxen from Moses according to their service. The sons of Gershon got two wagons and four oxen, and the sons of Merari got four wagons and eight oxen under Ithamar son of Aaron. The sons of Kohath received none because they carried the sanctuary's holy things on their shoulders.

Chapter 8

The Levites are taken from among the children of Israel, cleansed, and offered as a wave offering, becoming the LORD's in place of the firstborn. They then serve in the Tent of Meeting before Aaron and his sons, working from age twenty-five and retiring from service at fifty, after which they assist their brothers.

Chapter 18

The Levites are joined to Aaron and his sons as a gift dedicated to the LORD to serve the Tent of Meeting, but they may not come near the vessels of the sanctuary or the altar on pain of death. They bear their own iniquity, receive all of Israel's tithe as their inheritance with no land, and must give a tithe of the tithe to Aaron the priest; they may eat the rest anywhere as their reward.

Chapter 26

The Levites are counted separately by family: Gershonites, Kohathites, and Merarites, along with the Libnites, Hebronites, Mahlites, Mushites, and Korahites. They number 23,000, every male from a month old and upward. They are not counted among the children of Israel because no inheritance is given them among them.

Chapter 35

The Levites are to receive forty-eight cities to dwell in, together with pasture lands for their livestock, possessions, and animals. Six of these cities are the cities of refuge.

The Levites are a chosen group, set apart by the LORD to stand before him, minister and burn incense. Across the records they serve as ark-bearers, singers and musicians with David's instruments, teachers of all Israel who are holy to the LORD, guards of the king, officers before the judges, and helpers in sacrifice. Early on they take up the ark and the singers stand at the altar's east end. Later, named Levites (Shemaiah, Nethaniah, Zebadiah, Asahel, Shemiramoth, Jehonathan, Adonijah, Tobijah, Tobadonijah) are sent with princes and priests to teach the book of the LORD's law in Judah's cities. Under Jehoiada they are gathered to Jerusalem as gatekeepers in three groups, surrounding the king with weapons and keeping both Sabbath shifts on duty. Under Hezekiah they sanctify themselves and carry uncleanness out of the house to the brook Kidron, play instruments and sing, and help skin burnt offerings, being more upright in heart than the priests in sanctifying themselves. Some Levites were ashamed, then sanctified themselves, and killed Passovers for those not clean, handing blood to the priests. Under Josiah they return the ark to Solomon's house, prepare by divisions, kill and roast the Passover, and provide offerings for themselves, the priests, singers and gatekeepers, while their chiefs donate five thousand small livestock and five hundred cattle. Their role thus runs from carrying and teaching to guarding and sacrificial service.

Levites in The Book of Nehemiah

Abstract written by a model

The Levites are a group of temple servants and teachers in Israel who appear throughout the records as religious leaders, singers, and recipients of sacred provisions. Named leaders such as Jeshua, Bani, Kadmiel, and Sherebiah recur. Early on they teach the people the law, reading distinctly and giving the sense, and calming the people on the holy day. They then lead the assembly in crying out, confession, and long prayer, and join the princes and priests in sealing the covenant. The covenant establishes their support: they receive tithes of the ground in farming villages and must bring the tithe of the tithes to the treasure house in the house of God. Later 284 Levites live in Jerusalem, under leaders including Uzzi of the sons of Asaph, while other divisions settle in Judah's cities and Benjamin's territory. Singers have settled provision by the king's commandment. Chiefs praise according to David's commandment, serve as gatekeepers at storehouses, are summoned for the dedication, purify themselves and the people, and set apart portions for Aaron's sons. The account ends with a lapse: the Levites and singers fled to their fields because portions were unpaid. Nehemiah gathers them back, appoints faithful treasurers, and restores their appointed duties.
