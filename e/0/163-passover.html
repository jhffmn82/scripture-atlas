Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, Passover is a perpetual ordinance and feast to the LORD commemorating the Exodus from Egypt, eaten in haste with unleavened bread and bitter herbs. In Leviticus, the LORD's Passover occurs on the fourteenth day of the first month in the evening. In Numbers, Passover is a LORD-appointed observance kept on the fourteenth day of the first month, with a second-month alternative and the feast of unleavened bread following. In Deuteronomy, Passover is an observance kept to the LORD in Abib, with its animal sacrificed at the chosen place and eaten there. In The Second Book of Kings, Passover is a covenant observance Josiah commands the people to keep in Jerusalem in his eighteenth year. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Israel's Passover under Hezekiah was delayed to the second month because too few priests were sanctified and the people had not gathered. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Josiah's Passover was kept to the LORD in Jerusalem on the fourteenth day of the first month, followed by seven days of unleavened bread. In The Book of Ezra, the children of the captivity keep Passover on the fourteenth day of the first month, followed by seven days of the feast of unleavened bread.

and 3 more works

In The Book of Ezekiel, Passover is a seven-day feast requiring unleavened bread and specified offerings from the prince. In The Gospel According to Matthew, Passover is prepared by the disciples on the first day of unleavened bread and eaten by Jesus with the twelve before his crucifixion. In The Gospel According to Luke, Passover is the approaching feast of unleavened bread, which Jesus directs Peter and John to prepare and desires to eat with the apostles before his suffering.

Passover in Exodus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Passover first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Passover is a perpetual ordinance and feast to the LORD, kept through generations, commemorating the Exodus from Egypt. Named for the LORD passing over Israelite houses, it is eaten in haste in one house with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, explained to children. Foreigners and hired servants are excluded; circumcised servants and strangers may eat; nothing remains until morning.

It is to be eaten in haste, in one house, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, and its meaning is to be explained to children; foreigners and hired servants may not eat it, but circumcised servants and strangers may.

Passover in Numbers

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Passover first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Passover is a LORD-appointed observance kept on the fourteenth day of the first month at evening, under statutes forbidding leaving any until morning or breaking a bone. Israel keeps it in the wilderness of Sinai. A second-month alternative is allowed, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs. A clean man not on a journey who neglects it is cut off, while a foreigner may keep it by the same statute. The feast of unleavened bread follows on the fifteenth.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

The Passover is to be kept in its appointed season, on the fourteenth day of the first month at evening, according to all its statutes and ordinances, and Israel keeps it so in the wilderness of Sinai. The LORD adds that it may be kept in the second month with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, leaving none until morning and breaking no bone. A clean man not on a journey who fails to keep it is cut off and bears his sin, and a foreigner may keep it under the same statute.

Chapter 28

The LORD's Passover falls on the fourteenth day of the first month. The feast of unleavened bread follows on the fifteenth day.

Abstract written by a model

Passover is a observance kept to the LORD in the month of Abib. Its animal is sacrificed from flock and herd at the chosen place, never within the people's own gates, at evening at sunset, recalling leaving Egypt. It is roasted and eaten there, with no meat left until morning, then the people return to their tents.
