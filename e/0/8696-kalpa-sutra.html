Kalpa Sutra

Abstract written by a model

The Kalpa Sûtra presents the lives, eras, lineages, and monastic rules associated with the Ginas and Tîrthakaras, culminating in regulations for religious communities. It begins with Mahâvîra, the last Tîrthakara: his embryo first enters Devânandâ’s womb in Kundagrâma, then, by Sakra’s order, Harinegamesi transfers it to the Kshatriyânî Trisalâ. Both women’s dreams signal an extraordinary son; Trisalâ’s fourteen auspicious dreams are identified as those seen by every mother of a Tîrthakara. Siddhârtha and dream interpreters understand them to foretell either a universal emperor or a Gina. Mahâvîra remains still in Trisalâ’s womb to protect her from anxiety, later moves, and resolves not to become houseless while his parents live. After nine months and seven and a half days he is born, named Vardhamâna by his parents and Mahâvîra by the gods. He renounces property and household life, practises austerity for more than twelve years, attains Kevala in his thirteenth year, and dies at Pâpâ, becoming Siddha, Buddha, and Mukta; Indrabhûti attains Kevala that same night. The work notes differing chronological reckonings since his death and closes his life. It next recounts Pârsva’s life, from birth in Benares through renunciation with three hundred men, Kevala after eighty-four days, and liberation on mount Sammeta; then Arishtanemi’s life, liberation on mount Girnâr, followers, and chronology. The epochs of intermediate Tîrthakaras calculate immense intervals since Nami, Munisuvrata, Malli, Sîtala, and others. Rishabha’s life follows: he teaches arts and occupations, installs his hundred sons as kings, renounces, attains Kevala, establishes a fourfold community, and is liberated on mount Ashtâpada. A list of Sthaviras traces Mahâvîra’s nine Ganas, eleven Ganadharas, and later spiritual succession from Ârya-Sudharman through named teachers. Finally, the Rules for Yatis prescribe Paggusan observance: residence, travel, alms, diet, clothing, cleanliness, speech, disputes, and care for small living beings. Mahâvîra proclaims the Paryushanâkalpa, ending both the rules and the Kalpa Sûtra.

Kalpa Sutra / Lives of the Ginas. / Life of Mahâvîra.

This unit concerns the descent, conception, and transfer of the embryo of the Venerable Ascetic Mahâvîra, the last of the Tîrthakaras. Mahâvîra first took the form of an embryo in the womb of Devânandâ in the brahmanical part of Kundagrâma, but Sakra ordered Harinegamesi to transfer him to the womb of Trisalâ in the Kshatriya part. Devânandâ saw fourteen great dreams, and Rishabhadatta interpreted them as signs that she would bear a remarkable son. The text also states that Mahâvîra's descent, conception, transfer, birth, renunciation, and attainment of Kevala occurred under Uttaraphalgunî, while his final liberation occurred under Svâti.

In that night in which the embryo of the Venerable Ascetic Mahâvîra was removed

This unit concerns the removal of the embryo of the Venerable Ascetic Mahâvîra from the womb of the Brâhmanî Devânandâ and its transfer to the womb of the Kshatriyânî Trisalâ. Afterward, Trisalâ sees fourteen auspicious dreams, including an elephant, bull, lion, goddess Srî, garland, moon, sun, flag, vase, lotus lake, milk-ocean, celestial abode, heap of jewels, and fire. She awakens joyful after seeing them. The text states that every mother of a Tîrthakara sees these fourteen dreams when the famous Arhat enters her womb.

Kshatriyânî Trisalâ tells Kshatriya Siddhârtha about fourteen illustrious great dreams, and he interprets them as foretelling the birth of a son who will become either a universal emperor or a Gina. Interpreters of dreams confirm this interpretation from their dream-books before Siddhârtha and Trisalâ. The Venerable Ascetic Mahâvîra remains motionless in the womb to spare his mother anxiety, later moves, and resolves not to enter the state of houselessness during his parents’ lives. After nine months and seven and a half days, Trisalâ gives birth to a healthy boy, Mahâvîra.

This unit recounts the birth, naming, renunciation, ascetic practice, attainment of Kevala, and final liberation of the Venerable Ascetic Mahâvîra. His parents named him Vardhamâna, while the gods gave him the name Venerable Ascetic Mahâvîra. He abandoned his property, entered houselessness, endured hardships for more than twelve years, and attained Kevala during his thirteenth year. He later died in Pâpâ, became a Siddha, Buddha, and Mukta, and his disciple Indrabhûti attained Kevala that same night.

(147) Since the time that the Venerable Ascetic Mahâvira died, &c.

The unit states that nine centuries have elapsed since the Venerable Ascetic Mahâvira died. It adds that the eightieth year of the tenth century has been reached, though another redaction gives the ninety-third year. The Fifth Lecture ends, as does the Life of Mahâvîra.

Life of Pârsva.

This unit recounts the Life of Pârsva, including his descent and conception, birth, renunciation, attainment of Kevala, and final liberation. Pârsva was born in Benares, lived thirty years as a householder, then entered houselessness with three hundred men. He attained Kevala after eighty-four days and later died on mount Sammeta after a hundred-year life, becoming freed from all pains. The asterism Visâkhâ marked the five most important moments of his life.

Life of Arishtanemi.

The Life of Arishtanemi recounts the major events in the life of the Arhat Arishtanemi, whose five most important moments occurred under the asterism Kitrâ. It describes his descent, birth, renunciation, attainment of Kevala, community, lifespan, death on mount Girnâr, and final liberation. It also records the numbers of his followers and the time elapsed since his death.

Epochs of the Intermediate Tîrthakaras.

The unit describes the epochs of the Intermediate Tîrthakaras and calculates elapsed years from the deaths of Arhat Nami, Munisuvrata, and Malli. It states that 584,979 years have elapsed since Arhat Nami died, that the current year is 1,184,980 since Munisuvrata’s death, and that it is year 6,584,980 since Malli’s death. It places Sîtala’s death a krore of Sâgaropamas less 42,003 years and eight and a half months before Vîra’s death. It also gives increasingly remote intervals separating earlier Tîrthakaras from Sîtala and Malli.

Life of Rishabha.

This unit recounts the life of the Arhat Rishabha, the Kosalian, from his descent from heaven and conception to his birth, reign, renunciation, attainment of Kevala, death, and final liberation. Rishabha taught sciences, accomplishments, arts, and occupations during his reign, then anointed his hundred sons as kings before entering houselessness. He attained Kevala after prolonged austerities and meditation, established a large fourfold community, and ultimately died on mount Ashtâpada after fasting. The unit ends by relating the elapsed time from Rishabha’s death to the death of Mahâvîra.

List of the Sthaviras.

This unit concerns the lineage of the Venerable Ascetic Mahâvîra, beginning with his nine Ganas and eleven Ganadharas. It describes the deaths of the Ganadharas and states that the present Nirgrantha Sramanas descend spiritually from Ârya-Sudharman. It then lists successive Sthaviras and their disciples, Ganas, Sâkhâs, and Kulas in short and detailed redactions. The unit concludes with reverences to later teachers, including Gambû, Nandita, Desiganin, Sthiragupta, Dharma, and Devarddhi.

Rules for Yatis.

This unit explains when the Venerable Ascetic Mahâvîra and later religious communities commence the Paggusan, and gives rules for conduct during it. It regulates residence, travel, alms collection, food and drink, permissions, clothing and utensils, cleanliness, lodging, speech, disputes, and movement. It also describes special dietary restrictions, precautions concerning small living beings and moisture, and practices intended to support monastic control. The unit concludes with Mahâvîra proclaiming the Lecture called Paryushanâkalpa and identifies itself as the end of the Rules for Yatis and of the Kalpa Sûtra.
