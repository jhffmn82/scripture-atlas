Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, sin in Chapter 21 is portrayed as a snake and a two-edged sword whose wounds are deadly and unhealing. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, sin in Chapter 27 is pictured as a nail between stones in commerce and as a lion lurking for evildoers, with many sinning for profit. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, sin must not be minimized or compounded, because sinners face the Lord’s indignation and unrighteous gains will not profit in calamity. In The Book of Hosea, sin is the reason Israel has fallen, and forgiveness is sought so the LORD may heal Israel’s waywardness and turn away his anger. In The Gospel According to John, sin enslaves those who commit it, brings death to unbelievers in their sins, and is the charge Jesus challenges his accusers to establish. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, sin is a universal power that entered through one man, affects all people, reigns in death, enslaves as a master, and is surpassed by grace. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, sin dwells in the narrator’s flesh, produces coveting through the commandment, deceives and kills him, and wars against his mind to hold him captive. In The Letter to the Hebrews, sins are not removed by animal sacrifices, but Christ’s single offering deals with them permanently; willful sin after knowing the truth leaves no further sacrifice.

and 7 more works

In The Letter from James, lust conceives and bears sin, full-grown sin produces death, and knowing good without doing it is also sin. In 1 John, sin is lawlessness and unrighteousness, bringing self-deception when denied but forgiveness and cleansing when confessed, and it is distinguished as leading to death or not leading to death. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), sin is linked with evil, mischief, transgression, disobedience, wrongful consumption, aggression, disbelief, and preferring worldly life, with accountability for what hearts earn. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), sin is committed against oneself, includes grave wrongdoing such as deceit and associating partners with Allah, and can be forgiven when one seeks Allah’s pardon. In Surah 5: The Table (Al-Maaida), sin is a cause of punishment, though Allah remits sins for those who fulfill the covenant and relents when people repent and amend. In Surah 6: The Cattle (Al-An'aam), sin causes destruction and punishment, while those who repent after doing evil through ignorance and then do right receive Allah’s forgiveness. In Surah 29: The Spider (Al-Ankaboot), disbelievers bear their own sins and additional loads from those they mislead, despite claiming they will bear those others’ sins.

sin in Paul’s Letter to the Romans

author unknown · about AD 57 · the work’s page · read it where sin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sin is presented as a universal power affecting the world: all, Jews and Greeks alike, have sinned and fall short of God's glory, and the law brings knowledge of it. It entered through one man, bringing death to all; it existed before the law but is not charged without law. It reigns in death and enslaves as a master, paying wages of death. Grace abounds more, so believers, who died to sin, are not under its dominion.

Sin entered the world through one man, and all sinned, so death passed to all. Sin was in the world before the law but is not charged when there is no law, and the law came in so that the trespass might abound. Sin reigned in death, but grace abounded more where sin abounded.

It holds people in bondage as a master, and serving it leads to death. Its wages are death, and it will not have dominion over believers because they are under grace.

sin in 1 John

author unknown · undated · the work’s page · read it where sin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sin is lawlessness and includes all unrighteousness. The letter first says denying sin is self-deception and makes God a liar, while confessing it brings God's forgiveness and cleansing through the blood of Jesus Christ. It then says whoever sins has not seen or known him, those born of God do not sin because his seed remains, and the Son was revealed to take sins away. Finally it distinguishes sin leading to death from sin not leading to death, for which one should ask and God gives life.

Disbelievers cannot bear the sins of those they mislead, although they claim they will do so. They will bear their own loads and other loads beside their own, and will be questioned about what they invented.
