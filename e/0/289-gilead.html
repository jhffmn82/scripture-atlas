Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Gilead is the mountain toward which Jacob travels, where Laban overtakes him and where they camp, receive God's warning, and make a covenant. In Numbers, Gilead is a place suited to livestock that Reuben and Gad plan to leave to their families, flocks, and cities, and that Moses grants to them and Machir. In Deuteronomy, Gilead is taken with the cities of the plain and Bashan and divided among the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh, with Machir receiving it. In The Book of Judges, Gilead is the land of Jair's thirty cities, where the Amorites lived, Israelites were oppressed, and the Ammonites encamped. In The First Book of the Maccabees, Gilead is the land where Jews are besieged, Dathema serves as a stronghold, Judas conquers cities, and the Jews are removed to Judah.
