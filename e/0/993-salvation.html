Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Isaiah, salvation is identified with God and pictured as wells from which people joyfully draw water. In The Second Book of Nephi, salvation is a free, everlasting gift available to all through the merits, mercy, and grace of the Holy Messiah, inspiring trust and joy and offered with a command to repent.

salvation in The Second Book of Nephi

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where salvation first appears

Abstract written by a model

Salvation is a free, everlasting gift prepared from the fall of man, available alike to all people and never abolished. No flesh can dwell in God's presence except through the merits, mercy, and grace of the Holy Messiah. A promised seer’s work brings Joseph’s people to it through the Lord’s power. Identified with God, it inspires trust without fear and joy drawn from its wells. The Lord offers it freely and commands persuasion of all men to repentance.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Salvation is free and is prepared from the fall of man. No flesh can dwell in God's presence except through the merits, mercy, and grace of the Holy Messiah.

Chapter 3

The work brought forth by the promised seer will bring Joseph's people unto salvation through the power of the Lord.

Chapter 8

The Lord's salvation has gone forth and will be forever, from generation to generation. His salvation will not be abolished even as the heavens vanish away and the earth waxes old.

Chapter 22

Salvation is identified with God, and people trust in it without fear. People draw water with joy from the wells of salvation.

Chapter 26

Salvation is offered freely to all people, and none are forbidden from partaking of it. The Lord commands his people to persuade all men to repentance.
