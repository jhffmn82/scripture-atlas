Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Isaac is Abraham and Sarah’s long-awaited son, the recipient of God’s covenant, the husband of Rebekah, and the father of Esau and Jacob. In 1 Chronicles, Isaac is listed as a son of Abraham and father of Esau and Israel. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Isaac receives from God the covenant and blessing established for Abraham’s sake. In Paul’s Letter to the Galatians, Isaac is presented as the son of the free woman, born through promise and according to the Spirit, who will inherit.

Isaac in Genesis

author unknown · about 449 BC · the work’s page · read it where Isaac first appears

Abstract written by a model

Isaac is the long-awaited son of Abraham and Sarah, named at God's direction before his birth, with God promising an everlasting covenant with him and his offspring. He is born at the set time, circumcised on the eighth day, and weaned amid a great feast. Called Abraham's only, beloved son, he is commanded to be offered as a burnt offering; he carries the wood, asks about the lamb, and is bound on the altar, but is spared when the angel intervenes and a ram is offered instead. A wife is sought for him from Abraham's homeland's kin rather than taking him back there; he meets Rebekah, brings her into his mother Sarah's tent, loves her, and is comforted after Sarah's death. He inherits all Abraham had, and after Abraham's death God blesses him and he lives by Beer Lahai Roi. Married at forty, he entreats the LORD for barren Rebekah, and at sixty twins Esau and Jacob are born. He favors Esau because he ate his venison. During a famine he goes to Gerar, is told not to go to Egypt, and out of fear claims Rebekah is his sister. He sows and reaps a hundredfold, becomes very great, is sent away by Abimelech, reopens his father's wells, digs new ones, and settles at Beersheba, where he builds an altar and makes a covenant with Abimelech. Old and blind, he tries to bless Esau but is deceived into blessing Jacob; he trembles, affirms the blessing, and gives Esau a lesser one of living by the sword and serving his brother. He then blesses Jacob again, forbids a Canaanite wife, sends him to Laban's daughters in Paddan Aram, and wishes him the blessing of Abraham. His displeasure with Canaanite daughters prompts Esau's new marriage. At Mamre (Kiriath Arba, Hebron), Jacob comes to him. Isaac dies at 180, old and full of days, and is buried by Esau and Jacob.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 17

Isaac is the name God tells Abraham to give to the son Sarah will bear to him at the set time next year. God says he will establish his everlasting covenant with Isaac and with Isaac's offspring after him.

Chapter 21

Isaac is born to Sarah and Abraham at the set time God had spoken of, and is circumcised at eight days. He grows and is weaned, and Abraham holds a great feast that day. God says Abraham's offspring will be named through him.

Chapter 22

Isaac is called Abraham's only son, whom he loves, and is commanded to be offered as a burnt offering. He carries the wood, asks where the lamb for the offering is, and is bound and laid on the altar by Abraham. He is spared when the angel stops Abraham, and a ram is offered in his place.

Chapter 24

Isaac is the son for whom a wife is sought and is not to be taken back to Abraham's homeland. He goes out to meditate in the field at evening, sees the camels coming, receives Rebekah into his mother Sarah's tent, takes her as wife, loves her, and is comforted after his mother's death.

Chapter 25

Isaac received all that Abraham had, and after Abraham's death God blessed him and he lived by Beer Lahai Roi. At forty he married Rebekah, entreated the LORD because she was barren, and was sixty when the twins Esau and Jacob were born. He loved Esau because he ate his venison.

Chapter 26

Isaac goes to Gerar during a famine, is told by the LORD not to go to Egypt, and claims Rebekah is his sister out of fear. He sows and reaps a hundredfold, becomes very great, is sent away by Abimelech, reopens his father's wells, digs new ones, and settles at Beersheba, where he builds an altar and makes a covenant with Abimelech.

Chapter 27

Old and blind, Isaac asks Esau to bring venison and savory food so he can bless him. He questions the person who comes, feels his hands, smells his clothing, and is deceived into blessing Jacob. On learning the truth he trembles violently, affirms that Jacob will be blessed, and gives Esau a lesser blessing of living by the sword and serving his brother until he breaks loose.

Chapter 28

Isaac calls Jacob, blesses him, and commands him not to take a Canaanite wife but to take one from Laban's daughters in Paddan Aram. He prays that God Almighty would make Jacob fruitful and give him the blessing of Abraham and the land, then sends him away. His displeasure with Canaanite daughters prompts Esau's new marriage.

Chapter 35

Isaac is at Mamre, Kiriath Arba (Hebron), when Jacob comes to him. He lives one hundred eighty years, dies, is gathered to his people old and full of days, and is buried by his sons Esau and Jacob.
