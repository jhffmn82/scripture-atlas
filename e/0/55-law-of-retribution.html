law of retribution

topic · 4 works · 6 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth, don't resist him who is evil, eye for eye, fracture for fracture, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, lex talionis (eye for eye), life for life, life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot, one kind of law, retaliation, tooth for tooth

Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, lex talionis is a retributive principle requiring equal-for-equal penalties when harm follows an injury to a pregnant woman. In Leviticus, the law of retribution requires life for life for killing a man, restitution for killing an animal, and equal penalties for bodily injuries, applying equally to foreigner and native-born. In Deuteronomy, the law of retribution requires a false witness to suffer what he planned for his brother, stating life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, and foot for foot without pity. In The Gospel According to Matthew, retaliation is rejected by Jesus, who replaces eye for an eye with teachings not to resist the one who is evil.

Abstract written by a model

Lex talionis is a retributive principle in Chapter 21: if a pregnant woman is hurt in a fight and harm follows, life is taken for life, followed by a list of equal-for-equal penalties from eye to bruise.

law of retribution in Leviticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where law of retribution first appears

Abstract written by a model

The law of retribution, stated by the LORD in Chapter 24, requires life for life for killing a man and restitution for killing an animal, and prescribes fracture for fracture, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, applying equally to foreigner and native-born.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 24

The LORD states that one who strikes a man mortally shall be put to death and one who kills an animal shall make it good, life for life. An injurer is to suffer what he has done: fracture for fracture, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, under one law for foreigner and native-born.
