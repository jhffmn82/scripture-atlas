Udaka, the son of Pêdhâla

Abstract written by a model

Udaka, son of Pêdhâla, is a Nirgrantha follower of Pârsva of the Mêdârya Gôtra. Questioning Gautama about renouncing injury, he first defends a restricted view, then understands and respects Gautama’s teaching. In Gautama’s presence, he leaves the four-vow creed for Mahâvîra’s five great vows and Pratikramana.

Seventh Lecture, Called Nâlandâ.

Udaka, the son of Pêdhâla, a Nirgrantha and follower of Pârsva, questions Gautama about renunciation of injury to movable and immovable beings. He initially defends a restricted renunciation but accepts Gautama’s teaching after their discussion. He then changes from the creed which enjoins four vows to the creed which enjoins the five great vows and the Pratikramana.
