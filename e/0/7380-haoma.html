Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, Haoma is a sacred, holy plant and complete sacred offering central to praise, sacrifice, worship, healing, victory, and ritual benefits. In Yasna, Haomas are plants offered in celebrations to Ahura Mazda and good waters, accompanying Zaothras and used in Haoma-water. In Yasna, Haoma is a golden-flowered healer sacrificed to restore, advance the world, drive death afar, and worship Sraosha on high Haraiti. In Visparad, Haomas are offerings presented to Ahura Mazda, Verethraghna, the Bountiful Immortals, good waters, souls, and the holy creation, producing abundance when properly pressed and offered. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII, Haoma touched by a corpse can be cleansed, remains uncorrupted when strained for sacrifice, and has a defiled stem portion that must be buried when unstrained. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, Haoma is an enlivening, healing, fair, lordly, golden-eyed being who offers sacrifices to Drvâspa and Mithra while seeking Franghrasyan’s binding. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, Haoma is a victorious, protective sacrifice offered to Vayu and drunk with Asha and Ashi Vanguhi, granting protection and battle victory.

Haoma in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Haoma first appears

Abstract written by a model

Haoma is a sacred, holy plant and complete sacred offering, with a counterpart, Parahaoma, and an associated Haoma-juice. It is Mazda-made, inherently good, righteous, healing, victorious, golden-hued and beautiful; it has bending sprouts, flesh, and branch. It grows widely on mountains, heights, gorges, abysses, snow-peaks, Haraiti, and Peaks-above-the-eagles, in many kinds and forms of milky whiteness or gold. Its healing liquors inspire the pious, and it is called the springs of Righteousness and a source of ritual. Haoma is central to praise and sacrifice. It and its juice are worshipped, offered, propitiated, and approached with punctilious sanctity, including for the Fravashi of Spitâma Zarathustra, Mazda-made waters, and the praise of Ahura Mazda. It accompanies Zaothras, is lifted with sanctity, and is pressed, mixed with milk, poured from a silver cup into a golden chalice, and offered ritually. Zarathustra praises it, its plants, Gâthas, songs, teachings, and health-imparting, victory-giving ritual words. In Yasna IX, H(a)oma comes to Zarathustra as he serves the sacred Fire, calling itself holy and death-driving. It grants inspiration, strength, victory, health, healing, wisdom, prosperity, good husbands for unwed maidens, and other benefits; it lowers Keresâni and is invoked against destroyers of righteousness. In Yasna X its healing, friendship-making, prosperity-giving, and protective power is praised. In Yasna XI it condemns a drinker who withholds its full outpouring, declares itself no head-smiter but far from death, receives an offering, and is credited with binding Frangrasyan in iron. Later it continues to be worshipped with Zaothras, beneficial waters, flesh, and branch.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna III.

Haoma and Parahaoma are approached with praise for the propitiation of the Fravashi of Spitâma Zarathustra. Haoma is also approached with praise for the propitiation of the good waters which Mazda created, and both Haoma and Haoma-juice are approached with a Yast.

Yasna VI.

Haoma and the Haoma-juice are worshipped in the sacrifice. Haoma is named alongside the sacred Fravashi of Zarathustra Spitâma, the wood-billets, perfume, and Fire.

Yasna VII.

Haoma is offered with a complete and sacred offering for the propitiation of the Fravashi of Zarathustra Spitâma and for the good waters which are Mazda-made.

Yasna VIII.

Haoma and its juice are offered for the praise of Ahura Mazda and other sacred powers. Haoma is also propitiated for sacrifice, homage, and praise as bringing Righteousness.

Yasna IX.

H(a)oma comes to Zarathustra while he serves the sacred Fire and identifies himself as holy and driving death afar. He grants or is asked for inspiration, strength, victory, health, healing, prosperity, wisdom, protection, good offspring, knowledge, husbands for maidens, and success against enemies. He lowers Keresâni from his throne and is invoked to hurl his mace at dragons, tyrants, and other destroyers of righteousness.

Yasna X.

H(a)oma grows on mountains and is praised for its healing, victorious, health-bringing, friendship-making, and prosperity-giving powers. It is pressed, mixed with milk, poured between vessels, offered in ritual, and invoked to drive away wasting, death, curses, malice, and harmful beings. Zarathustra praises H(a)oma as Mazda-made, along with its plants, songs, teachings, and truthful ritual words.

Yasna XI.

H(a)oma curses the drinker who withholds its full outpouring and says it is not a head-smiter but holy and far from death. H(a)oma receives an offering of tongue and left eye, is praised as effective and Mazda-made, and is asked to grant the best world of the saints. H(a)oma is also said to have bound Frangrasyan fast in iron.

Yasna XXII.

Haoma is approached with praise and sanctity. It accompanies the Zaothras, and its water is also approached with praise for the beneficent waters.

Yasna XXV.

Haoma is worshipped as flesh and branch, together with the Zaothras and beneficial waters. Haoma-water is worshipped for the beneficial waters.
