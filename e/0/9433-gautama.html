Gautama in Sutrakritanga, Book II

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Gautama first appears

Abstract written by a model

Gautama stays in park Hastiyâma and debates Udaka, the son of Pêdhâla, over renunciation and injury to living beings. He rejects Udaka’s limiting interpretation, teaches that injury to movable beings is sinful, persuades Udaka, and is taken to the Venerable Ascetic Mahâvîra.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Seventh Lecture, Called Nâlandâ.

Gautama debates Udaka, the son of Pêdhâla, about the correct interpretation of vows concerning injury to living beings. He teaches that injury to movable beings is sinful, that renunciation must not be limited in a way that permits injury to other beings, and that Udaka’s interpretation is incorrect. Gautama’s explanations persuade Udaka, and he takes him to the Venerable Ascetic Mahâvîra.

Gautama in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

Hermann Jacobi · the work’s page · read it where Gautama first appears

Abstract written by a model

Gautama is Vardhamâna’s famous, enlightened disciple, complete in sciences, right conduct, and knowledge of the twelve Aṅgas. Residing in Kôshthaka park, he conquers foes, fetters, and love of existence through discipline. He arrives at Srâvastî and answers Kêsi’s questions about two Laws and overcoming spiritual dangers.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Twenty-Third Lecture.

Gautama is a famous disciple of Vardhamâna who has mastered the sciences and right conduct, knows the twelve Aṅgas, and arrives at Srâvastî. He answers Kêsi’s questions, explaining the differences between the two Laws and the means of overcoming spiritual dangers.
