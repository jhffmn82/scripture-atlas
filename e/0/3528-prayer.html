Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the psalmist’s prayer is asked to rise before the LORD like incense and evening sacrifice and is set against evil deeds. In Psalms, David’s prayer asks the LORD to hear his petitions, relieve him, guide and revive him, deliver him from enemies, and answer quickly. In The Gospel According to Matthew, prayer should be offered secretly without being seen or using vain repetitions, since the Father knows believers’ needs beforehand. In The Gospel According to Luke, prayer is taught as an address to the Father seeking holiness, the Kingdom, daily bread, forgiveness, and deliverance, with persistence in asking, seeking, and knocking. In The Gospel According to Luke, prayer is taught as persistent and humble rather than self-righteous, illustrated by the persistent widow and the tax collector’s plea for mercy. In The Letter from James, prayer is prescribed for the suffering and sick, with the prayer of faith bringing healing and forgiveness and the righteous person’s insistent prayer proving effective. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), prayer is worship and a means of seeking help and Allah’s companionship, performed with devotion and guarded especially as the midmost prayer. In Surah 13: The Thunder (Ar-Ra'd), prayer belongs truly to Allah, while prayer directed to others receives no response, and the righteous are regular in prayer and promised reward.

In Surah 21: The Prophets (Al-Anbiyaa), prayer is made to Allah in distress and need, and Allah hears and answers the prayers of Noah, Job, Dhu'n-Nun, and Zachariah. In The Table-Talk of the Prophet Mohammad (Part 1), prayer is a witnessed duty whose congregational performance receives greater reward, and it is linked with ablution, purity, charity, and making up missed prayers. In The Second Book of Nephi, prayers are persistently offered to the Lord God, with Nephi crying out, receiving visions, and praying continually for his people. In The Second Book of Nephi, prayer must be offered to the Father in Christ’s name before performing anything unto the Lord. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, prayer is continual turning and calling to God for mercy, strength, comfort, wisdom, and success, offered in diverse settings and contrasted with selfish separation. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, prayer is offered secretly to the Father without vain repetition, seeking his will, forgiveness, and deliverance from evil. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, prayer is an appeal for strength and deliverance, commanded continually in families and worship, with disciples praying to Jesus and Jesus praying to the Father. In The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record, prayer is offered to the Father in Jesus Christ’s name for whatever the saints need, including the restoration of the brethren to knowledge of Christ. In The Book of Moroni, prayer is practiced as the church meets often to fast and pray, remaining continually watchful while relying on Christ’s merits. In Yasna, Prayer is offered to Ahura Mazda with praise, seeking grace, protection, righteous lands, guidance, and help. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, prayer is worshipful ritual utterance offered with sacrifice, glorification, hymns, blessings, and invocations to Ahura Mazda and holy beings.
