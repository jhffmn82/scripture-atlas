disciples (Jesus's disciples) in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where disciples (Jesus's disciples) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The disciples are the followers of Jesus, a group that grows from learners into commissioned messengers. Early on they follow him, practice no fasting while the bridegroom is with them, and are taught on the mountain, where they are called the salt of the earth and the light of the world and told to let their good works shine so the Father is glorified. They are defended when they pluck grain on the Sabbath, and Jesus calls them his mother and brothers. They are given the mysteries of the Kingdom, receive explanations of the parables, and say they understand. They distribute the loaves Jesus blesses, then worship him in the boat as the Son of God. Their understanding is repeatedly imperfect: they misread the warning about yeast, are told to tell no one he is the Christ, are taught to deny themselves and take up the cross, and fail to cast out a demon because of lack of faith. Peter, James, and John fall in fear at the voice from the cloud. They are sorry at the prediction of his death, react to teachings on divorce, rebuke those bringing children, and are astonished about the rich. Jesus promises they will sit on twelve thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel. On the Mount of Olives they ask privately about the end of the age and are warned of hatred and told to watch. At the end the eleven go to the Galilee mountain, bow to Jesus though some doubt, and receive the commission to make disciples of all nations, baptize, and teach all he commanded.

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

The disciples come to Jesus after he sits down on the mountain, and he opens his mouth to teach them. They are told they are the salt of the earth and the light of the world, and that they should let their light shine so people see their good works and glorify the Father.

Chapter 12

Jesus's disciples, hungry, pluck and eat heads of grain in the grain fields on the Sabbath, and the Pharisees call this unlawful. Jesus defends them as guiltless. Later he stretches his hand toward them and calls them his mother and brothers.

Chapter 13

The disciples ask why Jesus speaks in parables and are told the mysteries of the Kingdom of Heaven are given to them, and that their eyes and ears are blessed. They receive explanations of the sower and darnel parables, and answer 'Yes, Lord' when Jesus asks if they have understood.

Chapter 14

At evening the disciples ask Jesus to send the crowds away to buy food, are told to feed them themselves, and distribute the loaves Jesus blesses and breaks, then gather twelve baskets of leftovers. Jesus makes them go ahead by boat, where they are distressed by wind, frightened by the sight of him on the sea, and after he enters the boat they worship him, saying he is truly the Son of God.

Chapter 16

The disciples forgot to bring bread and misunderstood Jesus's warning about yeast, until he reminded them of the loaves for the five thousand and four thousand and they understood he meant the teaching of the Pharisees and Sadducees. They reported what men say about Jesus, were commanded to tell no one he was the Christ, and were taught that following him requires denying oneself and taking up one's cross.

Chapter 17

Peter, James, and John fall on their faces in fear at the voice from the cloud, and Jesus touches them and tells them to rise. The disciples ask about Elijah, are unable to cure the epileptic boy, and are told it was because of their unbelief. They are exceedingly sorry on hearing Jesus's prediction of his death.

Chapter 19

The disciples react to Jesus's teaching on divorce by saying it is not expedient to marry, and they rebuke those bringing little children to him. They are exceedingly astonished at his saying about the rich and ask who can be saved. Jesus promises that they will sit on twelve thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel.

Chapter 24

The disciples point out the temple buildings to Jesus, then come to him privately on the Mount of Olives to ask when these things will be and what the sign of his coming and the end of the age is. They are warned not to be led astray, told they will be hated and delivered up, and urged to watch and be ready.

Chapter 28

The eleven disciples are told through the women to go to Galilee, and they go to the mountain where Jesus had sent them. There they bow down to Jesus, though some doubt, and receive his commission to make disciples of all nations, baptize them, and teach them to observe all he commanded.

disciples (Jesus's disciples) in The Good News According to Mark

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where disciples (Jesus's disciples) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The disciples are the twelve followers who accompany Jesus throughout the records, appearing as a group whose closeness to him is repeatedly matched by their lack of understanding. Early on they eat with tax collectors and sinners, do not fast while the bridegroom is with them, and pluck grain on the Sabbath, with Jesus defending them. They are then sent out in pairs with authority over unclean spirits, distribute loaves and fish, and gather twelve baskets, then seven baskets, of leftovers. Yet they are terrified when they see Jesus walking on the sea, and their hearts are described as hardened because they had not understood about the loaves. Jesus repeatedly asks whether they yet understand, including over the warning about yeast. They are taught about his coming suffering and told to tell no one about him. They fail to cast the spirit out of the boy, which Jesus says comes out only by prayer and fasting. They do not grasp his predictions of his death, are afraid to ask, argue about who is greatest, forbid an outsider casting out demons, rebuke those bringing children, and the ten become indignant at James and John. Peter says they have left all to follow him. Two fetch the donkey as instructed, and they hear the fig tree cursed. Peter, James, John, and Andrew privately ask about the temple's fate and are warned of deception, persecution, and the need to endure, watch, and pray. At the Passover the twelve each ask 'Surely not I?' when betrayal is foretold by Jesus. All vow not to deny him, but they all leave him and flee when he is arrested.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Jesus' disciples are at the table with the tax collectors and sinners and are questioned about it by the scribes and Pharisees. They are not fasting, which Jesus explains while the bridegroom is with them. They pluck ears of grain on the Sabbath, and Jesus defends them.

Chapter 6

The disciples follow Jesus, and the twelve are sent out in pairs with authority over unclean spirits. After returning to report, they suggest sending the crowd away, distribute the loaves and fish, and gather twelve baskets of pieces. They are terrified when they see Jesus walking on the sea, supposing him a ghost, and the text says they had not understood about the loaves and their hearts were hardened.

Chapter 7

Jesus' disciples are criticized for eating bread with unwashed hands. Later, in a house, they ask him about the parable, and he asks whether they too are without understanding before explaining that food goes into the stomach and makes all foods clean.

Chapter 8

The disciples ask how so many could be satisfied with bread in a deserted place, then serve the multitude and gather seven baskets of leftovers. They misunderstand Jesus' warning about yeast, thinking it concerns having no bread, and Jesus asks whether they yet understand, recalling the twelve and seven baskets. They are later asked who people say Jesus is and are taught about his coming suffering, and are commanded to tell no one about him.

Chapter 9

The disciples fail to cast the spirit out of the boy, and Jesus tells them privately that this kind comes out only by prayer and fasting. They do not understand Jesus's prediction of his death and are afraid to ask, and they argue on the way about who is greatest. disciples (Jesus' disciples) in The Gospel According to John

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where disciples (Jesus' disciples) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The disciples are the group of followers chosen and appointed by Jesus, taken out of the world to bear lasting fruit. They are first seen invited with Jesus to a wedding, where they believe in him after he reveals his glory. Their faith grows unevenly: they recall Scripture at the temple, and only after his resurrection do they fully believe the Scripture and his word. They baptize on his behalf, misunderstand his talk of food while he speaks with a woman, and are sent to reap what others labored for. They distribute loaves and fish and see him walk on water, yet many followers find his teaching hard and turn back while the twelve remain, including Judas, who would betray him. At the foot-washing they are told they are clean, except one. Jesus commands them to love one another so everyone will know they are his disciples. He promises a prepared place, the Counselor, and his peace, though Thomas, Philip, and Judas (not Iscariot) show incomplete understanding. He calls them branches who must remain in him, and friends rather than servants, warning of the world's hatred, persecution, expulsion, and death. They will testify because they have been with him from the beginning. They claim to believe because he speaks plainly, but he foretells they will scatter and leave him alone, promising their sorrow will become joy and that in him they may have peace.
