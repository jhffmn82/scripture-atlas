Book of Poetry

object · 7 works · 18 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: The Book of Poetry, the Book of Poetry

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book XVII (Yang Ho), Confucius urges his children to study the Book of Poetry, saying it provides knowledge of the Odes and acquaintance with the names of birds, beasts, and plants. In The Great Learning, the Book of Poetry is cited to teach resting, self-culture, reverence, remembrance of former kings, family order, and the proper conduct of rulers, while describing rulers as parents of the people and warning that the great decree is not easily preserved. In The Doctrine of the Mean, Book of Poetry presents images of hawks, fishes, family, spirits, Heaven, rulers, and prudent virtue, moving through natural and practical patterns, union, spiritual and heavenly ordinances, and praise of rulers before teaching that true virtue is silent, unseen, brilliant, and unostentatious. In Mencius, Book I (Liang Hui Wang), the Book of Poetry recounts King Wen’s marvellous tower and pond and cites poetry on misery, solitude, Gong Liu, and king Tai. In Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou), the Book of Poetry contains an ode illustrating sincere submission through people of every direction accepting it, and uses preparing a nest before rain to urge study, harmony with God’s ordinances, protection from insult, and much happiness. In Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou), the Book of Poetry contains ancient statutes and teachings urging fidelity to old rules, vigilance as Heaven produces Zhou’s overthrow, remembrance that Yin’s beacon is near in the last sovereign of Xia, and studious harmony with God’s ordinances, while warning against transgression and ease and cautioning that otherwise the kingdom goes to ruin. In Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang), the Book of Poetry contains the principle of informing parents before marriage and is cited concerning informing parents before marriage, the sovereign’s domain and service, filial thoughts, and the people of Zhou, with a warning that its individual terms and sentences must not be interpreted against the general scope.

Book of Poetry in Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou)

Mencius · the work’s page · read it where Book of Poetry first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Book of Poetry contains ancient statutes and teachings cited in Mencius. It urges fidelity to old rules, vigilance as Heaven produces Zhou’s overthrow, remembrance that Yin’s beacon is near in the last sovereign of Xia, and studious harmony with God’s ordinances. It moves from warning against transgression and ease toward happiness through diligence, while cautioning that otherwise the kingdom goes to ruin.

The Book of Poetry is cited for teachings about following ancient statutes without transgression or forgetfulness. It is also cited as warning people not to remain at ease when the overthrow of Zhou is being produced by Heaven.

The Book of Poetry says that the beacon of Yin is not remote and places it in the time of the last sovereign of Xia.

Chapter 4
