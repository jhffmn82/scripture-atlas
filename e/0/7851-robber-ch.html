Robber Chê

person · 1 work · 21 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Captain, Chê, your captain

Abstract written by a model

Robber Chê is Liu Hsia Chi’s younger brother, a powerful leader of nine thousand followers and a scourge of the empire. He ravages the empire, plunders nobles, steals wives and daughters, and disregards kinship and ancestor worship. Described as imposing and eloquent, he has passions, whirlwind thoughts, and strength to defy foes. He defines Tao through successful thieving, teaching its five qualities and exploiting the Sages’ doctrine for profit. When Confucius proposes reform, he angrily rejects it, attacks Confucius and ancient rulers, and favors preserving life and satisfying natural desires.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER X.

Robber Chê teaches that thieving contains wisdom, courage, heroism, shrewdness, and justice in dividing spoil. The unit says that the doctrine of the Sages is indispensable to him and that doubling the Sages' wisdom would double his profits.

CHAPTER XXIX.

Robber Chê commands nine thousand followers, ravages the empire, and commits widespread theft and violence. He angrily rejects Confucius’s proposed reform, attacks Confucius’s teachings and the reputations of ancient rulers, and argues that ambition should give way to preserving life and satisfying natural desires.
