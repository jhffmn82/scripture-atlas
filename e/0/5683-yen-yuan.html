Yen Yuan

person · 7 works · 25 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: Hui, Yen Hui

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book V (Kung-Ye Ch'Ang), Yen Yuan says he wishes neither to boast of his excellence nor display his meritorious deeds. In The Analects of Confucius, Book V (Kung-Ye Ch'Ang), Hui understands an entire subject after hearing one point, surpassing Tsze-kung in the Master's comparison. In The Analects of Confucius, Book VI (Yung Yey), Yen Hui loved learning, did not transfer anger or repeat faults, and died after a short appointed time. In The Analects of Confucius, Book VI (Yung Yey), Hui maintains a mind free from anything contrary to perfect virtue for three months and lives joyfully despite poverty. In The Analects of Confucius, Book IX (Tsze Han), Hui is described as never flagging when the Master sets forth anything to him. In The Analects of Confucius, Book IX (Tsze Han), Yen Yuan admires and studies the Master's doctrines, advances constantly despite difficulty, and never stops. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XI (Hsien Tsin), Yen Yuan is described as a disciple who loved learning, nearly attained perfect virtue, died young, and was mourned exceedingly by Confucius. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XII (Yen Yuan), Yen Yuan asks how to practice perfect virtue and resolves to follow the lesson despite deficient intelligence and vigour.

and 2 more works

In The Analects of Confucius, Book XV (Wei Ling Kung), Yen Yuan asks how the government of a country should be administered. In The Doctrine of the Mean, Hui chooses the Mean, firmly holding what is good whenever he finds it and never letting it go.
