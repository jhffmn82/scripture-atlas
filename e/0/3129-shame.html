Shame is treated as a moral topic in Chapter 41: it is fitting only for particular wrongs, not everything. Listed examples include sexual immorality before parents, lying before a prince, offense before a judge, iniquity before the congregation, unjust dealing with partner or friend, theft, and revealing secrets. Being ashamed of the right things earns favor in everyone's sight, while retaining every kind of shame is not good.

Chapter 41

Shame is presented as appropriate for particular wrongs, not for everything: sexual immorality before parents, lying before a prince, offense before a judge, iniquity before the congregation, unjust dealing with a partner or friend, theft, and revealing secrets, among others. By being ashamed of the right things a person finds favor in the sight of every man, and the text notes that it is not good to retain every kind of shame.
