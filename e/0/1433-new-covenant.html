Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Jeremiah, the new covenant is declared as the LORD's promise to Israel and Judah to write his law on their hearts, make him known to all, and forgive iniquity. In 2 Corinthians, the new covenant is described as the Spirit's life-giving service of righteousness, surpassing the earlier service in glory and remaining. In The Letter to the Hebrews, the new covenant is presented as a better covenant established on better promises, mediated by Christ, and providing eternal inheritance and forgiveness.

new covenant in The Book of Jeremiah

Abstract written by a model

The new covenant is a promise declared by the LORD in Chapter 31, made with the houses of Israel and Judah. Unlike the broken covenant made when their fathers left Egypt, it places his law in their inward parts, writes it on their hearts, lets all know him, and forgives iniquity, remembering sin no more.

Chapter 31

The LORD declares he will make a new covenant with the houses of Israel and Judah, not like the one made with their fathers when he brought them out of Egypt, which they broke. In it he will put his law in their inward parts and write it on their hearts, they will all know him from least to greatest, and he will forgive their iniquity and remember their sin no more.

new covenant in The Letter to the Hebrews

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where new covenant first appears

Abstract written by a model

The new covenant is a better covenant, established on better promises and made by the Lord with the house of Israel and the house of Judah. Its provisions: God's laws put in minds and written on hearts, all knowing him from least to greatest, and mercy with sins remembered no more. Chapter 8 introduces it, with the high priest as mediator, and sets out these promises. Chapter 9 identifies Christ as mediator, whose death redeemed transgressions under the first covenant, so the called receive the eternal inheritance; it is compared to a will, in force after death. Chapter 10 repeats the quoted promise through the Holy Spirit's testimony and concludes that, with remission given, no further offering for sin remains.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

The new covenant is the better covenant of which the high priest is mediator, given on better promises. The Lord promises to make it with the house of Israel and the house of Judah after those days, putting his laws in their minds and hearts, so that all will know him from least to greatest, and showing mercy by remembering their sins no more.

Chapter 9

Christ is the mediator of a new covenant, since a death occurred for the redemption of transgressions under the first covenant. Through it those who have been called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance. The text compares it to a will, which is in force only after the death of the one who made it.

Chapter 10

The new covenant is quoted from the Lord through the testimony of the Holy Spirit: after those days God will put his laws on their heart and write them on their mind. Under it God will remember their sins and iniquities no more. Because remission is given, there is no more offering for sin.
