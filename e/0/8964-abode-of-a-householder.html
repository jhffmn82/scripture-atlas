abode of a householder

Also called: house, the abode of a householder

Abstract written by a model

The abode of a householder is a place where food may be offered to monks or nuns collecting alms. They must inspect and reject impure food, enter with a complete outfit, and avoid entering or leaving with it during strong rain, mist, dust, or many flying insects.

Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book II, translated by Hermann Jacobi / Second Book / First Part. / First Lecture, Call

A monk or nun entering the abode of a householder to collect alms should examine the food offered there and reject food that is impure or unacceptable.

Third Lesson.

A monk or nun wishing to enter the abode of a householder should do so with the complete outfit. They should not enter or leave it with the complete outfit when strong rain, mist, wind-blown dust, or many flying insects are present.
