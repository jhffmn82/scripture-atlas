the virtue according to the Mean in The Doctrine of the Mean

James Legge · about 449 BC · the work’s page · read it where the virtue according to the Mean first appears

Abstract written by a model

The virtue according to the Mean belongs to nature and is perfect, though rare among the people for a long time. Its greatest power unfolds through sincerity, reverence, concealment, and freedom from display, culminating in comparison with supreme Heaven’s soundless, scentless doings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

The virtue according to the Mean is identified as perfect, but it is said to have been rare among the people for a long time.

Chapter 33

Virtue is described as most powerful when it is sincere, reverential, concealed, and free from display. Perfect virtue is compared to the doings of supreme Heaven, which have neither sound nor smell.
