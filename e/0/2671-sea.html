Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the sea flees when it sees the Exodus from Egypt and is asked why it fled. In Psalms, the sea, the great waters, writhes and convulses at seeing God, who passes through it by paths whose footsteps remain unknown. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the sea is a body of water the Lord calmed by his counsel and planted with islands, where sailors recount dangers and where strange, wondrous works, varied living things, and huge creatures are found. In Jonah, the sea is where the LORD sends the storm; it grows increasingly stormy until the mariners throw Jonah into it, after which it ceases raging. In The Gospel According to Matthew, the disciples' boat is distressed by waves in the middle of the sea when Jesus and Peter walk on its waters, and the wind ceases when they enter the boat. In The Good News According to Mark, the sea is where Jesus teaches the multitude from a boat and later commands the storming waters to become calm. In Revelation, the sea is struck by judgment: a burning mountain turns one third to blood, killing a third of its creatures and ships, and it later becomes entirely blood.
