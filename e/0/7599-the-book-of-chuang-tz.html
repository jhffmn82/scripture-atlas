the book of Chuang Tzŭ

Abstract written by a model

Chuang Tzŭ's work is an extraordinary, largely allegorical, literary, and dialectical production. Praised for marvellous literary beauty and much originality of thought, it destructively criticizes the Confucian and Mihist schools. Though once fifty-three chapters, its present thirty-three chapters include inside, outside, and miscellaneous sections, sayings of Confucius, interpolations, and spurious chapters. Long classed as heterodox, it is not sacred and was unusable to rulers and ministers.

_Introduction._

Chuang Tzŭ's work is largely allegorical, literary, and dialectical, and it has suffered from interpolations and spurious chapters. It now consists of thirty-three chapters, divided into inside, outside, and miscellaneous sections, with Kuo Hsiang's edition serving as the surviving principal version. The work is praised for its literary beauty and originality but is not considered sacred.
