Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Hezekiah's prayer is offered in the LORD's house, asking the LORD for salvation from Sennacherib so all kingdoms know the LORD alone is God. In The Book of Isaiah, Hezekiah's prayer is offered in the LORD's house, asking the LORD to hear Sennacherib's words and grant salvation so all kingdoms know the LORD.

Abstract written by a model

Hezekiah's prayer, offered in the LORD's house, addresses the LORD as God of Israel enthroned above the cherubim and maker of heaven and earth. He asks salvation from Sennacherib so all kingdoms know the LORD alone is God; the LORD answers through Isaiah that he heard.

Hezekiah's prayer in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Hezekiah's prayer first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hezekiah prays in the LORD's house, addressing the LORD of Armies as sole God of all kingdoms. He asks him to hear Sennacherib's words, admits Assyria destroyed nations with wooden and stone gods, and requests salvation so all kingdoms know the LORD. Isaiah then relays that the LORD heard and will act.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 37

Hezekiah prays in the LORD's house, addressing him as LORD of Armies, enthroned among the cherubim, and sole God of all the kingdoms who made heaven and earth. He asks the LORD to hear Sennacherib's words, admits that Assyria destroyed nations whose gods were only wood and stone, and asks for salvation so all kingdoms may know the LORD alone. Isaiah relays that the LORD has heard this prayer and will act against Sennacherib.
