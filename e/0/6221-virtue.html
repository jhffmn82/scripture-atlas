Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book XV (Wei Ling Kung), virtue is a topic known by only a few and a vital personal responsibility that must be preserved even at the cost of life. In Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou), virtue is one of two courses Confucius says can be pursued, spreading over four seas like rushing water.

virtue in The Analects of Confucius, Book XV (Wei Ling Kung)

author unknown · 474 to 220 BC · the work’s page · read it where virtue first appears

Abstract written by a model

Virtue is a topic known by only a few, yet more important to man than water or fire. Every man bears personal responsibility for it and must not yield its performance even to a teacher. The determined scholar and man of virtue preserve it complete, even at the cost of life. It enables a man to hold fast what he has gained; without enough, he loses it. Specious words confound virtue, and no one has been seen to love it as much as beauty. Though vital, its course has not been seen to cause a man’s death.
