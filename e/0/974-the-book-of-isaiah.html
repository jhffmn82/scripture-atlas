The Book of Isaiah

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This is the Book of Isaiah, 66 chapters of prophecy by Isaiah son of Amoz concerning Judah and Jerusalem, set from the reigns of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah onward. It moves from judgment to comfort to restoration. Chapters 1-12 open with the LORD accusing a rebellious, bloodstained people, offering cleansing if they obey, and promising a purged Zion. Isaiah's call comes in the year Uzziah died, when he sees the Lord enthroned and is sent to a people who will not understand. Under Ahaz, the Immanuel sign is given amid the Syrian-Israelite threat, and Assyria is foretold as a flood. Promises follow of a child with the names Wonderful Counselor and Prince of Peace and a shoot from Jesse's stock bringing universal peace, ending in a song of thanksgiving. Chapters 13-27 are oracles against nations: Babylon, Moab, Damascus, Egypt, Tyre and others, with Assyria also broken. Egypt and Assyria are eventually joined in worship with Israel. The oracles widen into judgment on the whole earth, a feast on the LORD's mountain, death swallowed up, and songs of trust. Chapters 28-35 pronounce woes on scoffers and on those who rely on Egypt rather than the Holy One of Israel. They promise a righteous king, Assyria's fall, and a wilderness that blossoms with a Holy Way for the redeemed, while Edom is laid waste. Chapters 36-39 are narrative. Sennacherib's envoy Rabshakeh taunts Jerusalem, Hezekiah prays, and the LORD's angel strikes 185,000 Assyrians. Hezekiah then recovers from illness with fifteen added years, but shows Babylon's envoys his treasures and is told they will be carried to Babylon. Chapters 40-55 shift to comfort for exiles. The LORD, incomparable Creator, mocks idols and names Cyrus as his anointed to free Israel. Babylon is condemned. A servant is called as a light to the nations, suffers, and bears the sins of many. The LORD invites all to come and promises his word will accomplish what he pleases. Chapters 56-66 call for justice, true fasting and Sabbath-keeping, and welcome foreigners and eunuchs. They confess the people's sin, and Zion is told to arise and shine as nations bring wealth. An anointed speaker proclaims good news, and the LORD comes in vengeance. The people pray for his return, and he promises new heavens and a new earth where weeping ends and wolf and lamb feed together. The book closes with all flesh worshipping before him and the fate of transgressors, whose worm and fire never end.

The Book of Isaiah / Chapter 1

Isaiah the son of Amoz records his vision concerning Judah and Jerusalem during the reigns of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah. The LORD accuses Israel of rebelling against the children he raised, calls the nation sinful and wounded, and describes its land as desolate and its cities burned, with only a small remnant spared from being like Sodom and Gomorrah. The LORD rejects the people's sacrifices, feasts, and prayers because their hands are full of blood, and commands them to cease evil, seek justice, and defend the fatherless and widow. He offers to make their scarlet sins white as snow if they are willing and obedient, but threatens the sword if they refuse. The chapter laments that the faithful city has become a prostitute with rebellious princes, and promises that the LORD will purge her dross, restore her judges, and redeem Zion with justice while the transgressors are consumed.

Chapter 2

Isaiah the son of Amoz records a vision concerning Judah and Jerusalem. In the latter days the mountain of the LORD's house will be raised above all hills, nations will stream to it to learn the LORD's ways, and he will judge between them so that they beat swords into plowshares and stop learning war. The text then turns to the house of Jacob, which is accused of being filled with foreign divination, wealth, horses, chariots, and idols made by their own hands. A day of the LORD of Armies is announced against everything proud and lofty, including cedars, mountains, towers, walls, and ships of Tarshish, so that the LORD alone is exalted. Men will flee into rocks and caves in terror and cast away their silver and gold idols, and the chapter ends by urging people to stop trusting in man.

Chapter 3

Chapter 3 describes the Lord, GOD of Armies, removing from Jerusalem and Judah all supply and support, including food, water, and capable leaders such as the mighty man, judge, prophet, and elder. Boys and children are given to rule, the people oppress one another, and the situation is so ruinous that no one will accept leadership. Jerusalem and Judah fall because their words and deeds defy the LORD and they flaunt their sin like Sodom, while the righteous are told it will go well for them and the wicked are told disaster awaits. The LORD stands to judge the elders and leaders for devouring the vineyard and plundering and crushing the poor. He then condemns the arrogant daughters of Zion, announcing sores and baldness, the loss of their finery, and a reversal into rottenness, rope, sackcloth, and branding, while their men fall by the sword and Jerusalem sits desolate on the ground.

Chapter 4

Chapter 4 describes a future 'day' in which seven women take hold of one man, asking to be called by his name to remove their reproach while providing their own bread and clothing. The LORD's branch and the land's fruit will be beautiful and glorious for the survivors of Israel. Those left in Zion and Jerusalem, written among the living, will be called holy after the Lord washes away the filth of the daughters of Zion and purges Jerusalem's blood by the spirit of justice and burning. The LORD will then create over Mount Zion and its assemblies a cloud and smoke by day and a flaming fire by night, forming a canopy of glory. This serves as a pavilion giving shade from heat and refuge from storm and rain.

Chapter 5

Chapter 5 opens with a song about a beloved's vineyard on a fruitful hill that was carefully cultivated yet yielded wild grapes, and the inhabitants of Jerusalem and men of Judah are asked to judge between him and it. He announces he will remove its hedge and wall, leave it a wasteland of briers and thorns, and withhold rain. The vineyard is identified as the house of Israel and the men of Judah, from whom the LORD of Armies looked for justice and righteousness but found oppression and a cry of distress. A series of woes follows against land-grabbers, drunken revelers, those who mock God's work, those who call evil good, the self-wise, and corrupt judges who take bribes. The chapter ends with the LORD's anger stretched out against his people and a distant nation summoned by a banner, swift and tireless, roaring like lions and leaving darkness and distress.

Chapter 6

In the year King Uzziah died, the narrator sees the Lord enthroned in the temple, attended by six-winged seraphim who proclaim the holiness of the LORD of Armies. The narrator is overwhelmed by his own uncleanness and that of his people, until one of the seraphim touches his mouth with a live coal from the altar and declares his iniquity taken away. When the Lord asks whom he should send, the narrator volunteers, and is told to go to this people with a message that they will hear and see without understanding, their hearts made fat, ears heavy and eyes shut so they do not turn and be healed. Asked how long, he is told until cities, houses and land are utterly waste and the people removed far away, with even a surviving tenth to be consumed, leaving the holy seed as a stump like that of a terebinth or oak.

Chapter 7

In the days of King Ahaz of Judah, Rezin of Syria and Pekah son of Remaliah of Israel went up against Jerusalem but could not prevail, and Ahaz and his people trembled when told Syria was allied with Ephraim. The LORD sent Isaiah and his son Shearjashub to meet Ahaz and tell him not to fear, because the plot to tear Judah apart and set up the son of Tabeel as king would not stand, and Ephraim would be broken within sixty-five years. The LORD then told Ahaz to ask for a sign, but Ahaz refused, saying he would not tempt the LORD. The LORD himself therefore gave a sign: the virgin will conceive and bear a son named Immanuel, and before the child knows to refuse evil and choose good, the land of the two kings will be forsaken. The chapter ends by foretelling that the LORD will bring the king of Assyria, along with flies and bees, on Judah, leaving the land desolate and overgrown with briers and thorns.

Chapter 8

The LORD instructs the speaker to write 'For Maher Shalal Hash Baz' on a large tablet before witnesses Uriah the priest and Zechariah son of Jeberechiah, and a son is born to the speaker and the prophetess and given that name as a sign that Damascus and Samaria will be plundered by the king of Assyria. Because this people refused the waters of Shiloah and rejoiced in Rezin and Remaliah's son, the LORD will bring the king of Assyria like a mighty flood of the River that sweeps into Judah, reaching to the neck, though the peoples who plot against it are told they will be shattered because God is with us. The LORD warns the speaker not to walk in the way of this people or fear what they fear, but to hold the LORD of Armies as holy, who will be a sanctuary to some and a stumbling stone, trap and snare to both houses of Israel and the people of Jerusalem. The speaker is told to bind up the covenant and seal the law among his disciples, and he resolves to wait for the LORD who hides his face from the house of Jacob. He and his children are signs in Israel, and the people are urged to turn to the law and covenant rather than consult mediums and wizards, or they will have no morning and will be driven into thick darkness, hungry and cursing their king and their God.

Chapter 9

Chapter 9 opens with a promise that gloom will end for Zebulun, Naphtali, and Galilee of the nations, where people who walked in darkness see a great light. The nation is given increased joy, and the yoke, staff, and rod of the oppressor are broken as in the day of Midian. A child is born and a son given, bearing the names Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, and Prince of Peace, whose government on David's throne will have no end, upheld by the zeal of the LORD of Armies. The chapter then turns to judgment: the Lord sends a word against Jacob and Israel, and Ephraim and Samaria's proud boasts of rebuilding are answered by adversaries such as the Syrians and Philistines devouring Israel. Because the people do not turn to the LORD of Armies, he cuts off head and tail, wickedness burns like fire, people turn on one another, and Manasseh and Ephraim devour each other and together oppose Judah, while his anger is not turned away.

Chapter 10

Chapter 10 opens with woe pronounced on those who write unrighteous decrees that rob the needy, widows, and fatherless of justice, and asks what they will do in the day of visitation. The Lord then calls Assyria the rod of his anger, sent against a profane nation to plunder it, though Assyria does not intend to serve God and boasts of its own strength, wisdom, and conquests, comparing Jerusalem to the cities and idols it has already taken. God declares that after finishing his work on Mount Zion and Jerusalem he will punish the king of Assyria's arrogance, and compares Assyria's boasting to an ax or saw exalting itself over the one who wields it. The light of Israel will burn Assyria's forest like fire, leaving so few trees that a child could count them, while a remnant of Jacob will return to the mighty God and lean on the LORD in truth. The Lord tells his people in Zion not to fear Assyria, promises that its yoke will be broken, traces Assyria's advance town by town to Nob near Jerusalem, and ends with the Lord cutting down the lofty like the forest, with Lebanon falling by the Mighty One.

Chapter 11

Chapter 11 describes a shoot that will come from the stock of Jesse, on whom the LORD's Spirit rests with wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge and the fear of the LORD. He will judge the poor and humble with righteousness and equity, strike the wicked, and wear righteousness and faithfulness as his belt. Peace will extend to the animal world, where predators and prey, including the wolf, lamb, leopard, lion and cobra, live together with a little child leading them, since the earth will be full of the knowledge of the LORD. In that day the nations will seek the root of Jesse, and the Lord will a second time recover the remnant of his people from Assyria, Egypt and other lands, ending the rivalry of Ephraim and Judah. The LORD will dry up obstacles such as the Egyptian sea and the River, making a highway for the remnant from Assyria as there was for Israel out of Egypt.

Chapter 12

Chapter 12 presents a short song of thanksgiving that the people will sing "in that day." In the first part, the speaker thanks the LORD because his anger has turned away and he now comforts them, and declares trust in God as salvation, strength, and song. The people will joyfully draw water from the wells of salvation. In the second part, they are called to give thanks to the LORD, call on his name, declare his deeds among the peoples, and sing of the excellent things he has done so it is known in all the earth. The chapter closes by urging the inhabitant of Zion to cry aloud and shout because the Holy One of Israel is great among them.

Chapter 13

Chapter 13 is a prophecy about Babylon, seen by Isaiah the son of Amoz. The LORD of Armies musters an army from far countries to carry out his anger, and the coming day of the LORD is described as cruel, with cosmic darkening and the shaking of heaven and earth. The people face terror, slaughter, and plunder, and the LORD says he will stir up the Medes against them. Babylon, called the glory of kingdoms, is to be overthrown like Sodom and Gomorrah and left permanently uninhabited, home only to desert animals. Its time is said to be near.

Chapter 14

Chapter 14 promises that the LORD will have compassion on Jacob, choose Israel again, and set them in their own land, where foreigners will join them and their former oppressors will serve them. Israel is told to take up a parable against the king of Babylon, who is mocked as a fallen oppressor: Sheol stirs the dead rulers to greet him, and the shining one who vowed to ascend to heaven and make himself like the Most High is brought down to the pit. He is denied honorable burial, his offspring are marked for slaughter, and the LORD of Armies vows to cut off Babylon's name and remnant and make it a possession for the porcupine and pools of water. The LORD also swears to break the Assyrian in his land and declares his plan for all nations, which no one can stop. In a burden dated to the year King Ahaz died, Philistia is warned not to rejoice over a broken rod, since worse will come out of the north, while Zion is affirmed as founded by the LORD as a refuge for the afflicted.

Chapter 15

Chapter 15 delivers a burden, or oracle, against Moab. Its cities Ar and Kir are laid waste in a single night. The people go to high places such as Bayith and Dibon to weep, shave their heads and beards, and wear sackcloth, while cries ring from Heshbon, Elealeh, Jahaz, and across Moab's borders. Nobles flee toward Zoar and Horonaim, the waters of Nimrim dry up, and survivors carry off their stored goods over the brook of the willows. The speaker laments for Moab, yet declares the waters of Dimon full of blood and promises still more on Dimon, a lion against those of Moab who escape and the remnant of the land.

Chapter 16

Chapter 16 continues the oracle concerning Moab. Moabite daughters are pictured as scattered birds at the fords of the Arnon, and an appeal is made for Zion to hide the outcasts and fugitive from the destroyer, with the promise that oppression will end and a throne will be established in loving kindness in the tent of David. The speaker then reports Moab's pride, arrogance and wrath, which come to nothing, and describes Moab wailing over the ruined vineyards and fields of Heshbon, Sibmah, Jazer, Elealeh and Kir Hareseth. The speaker weeps and mourns for Moab, and Moab's prayers at the high place and sanctuary will not prevail. The LORD concludes that within three years the glory of Moab and its great multitude will be held in contempt, leaving a very small and feeble remnant.

Chapter 17

Chapter 17 delivers the LORD of Armies' burden against Damascus, which is to become a ruinous heap while its cities are abandoned to flocks. The fortress of Ephraim and the kingdom of Damascus, along with the remnant of Syria, will cease. The glory of Jacob will grow thin like a field stripped by the harvester, leaving only a few gleanings like olives left on a shaken tree. In that day people will look to their Maker, the Holy One of Israel, and not to altars, Asherah poles or incense altars, because they forgot the God of their salvation and planted foreign seedlings whose harvest flees in grief. The chapter ends with the roar of many peoples and nations, whom God rebukes so that they flee like chaff and dust, and with the plunderers of the speaker's people vanishing before morning.

Chapter 18

Chapter 18 opens with an address to a land of the rustling of wings beyond the rivers of Ethiopia, which sends ambassadors by sea in papyrus vessels to a nation tall and smooth. All the world's inhabitants are called to look and listen when a banner is raised on the mountains and a trumpet is blown. The LORD tells the speaker that he will remain still and watch from his dwelling place, like clear heat in sunshine or a cloud of dew in harvest heat. Before the harvest, he will cut off the sprigs and spreading branches of the vine with pruning hooks, leaving them for ravenous birds and animals to eat through summer and winter. At that time a present will be brought to the LORD of Armies from the tall and smooth people to Mount Zion.

Chapter 19

Chapter 19 delivers the burden of Egypt. The LORD comes on a swift cloud, shaking Egypt's idols, stirring Egyptians to civil strife, and giving them over to a cruel lord and fierce king. The Nile and its streams dry up, ruining fishermen, flax workers, and weavers, while the princes of Zoan and Memphis, and Pharaoh's counselors, are shown to be foolish and misled by a perverse spirit. In that day Egypt will tremble, five Egyptian cities will speak the language of Canaan and swear to the LORD, and an altar and pillar will mark his presence. The LORD will strike and heal Egypt, a highway will link Egypt and Assyria so they worship together, and Israel will be a third with them as a blessing, each named as the LORD's own.

Chapter 20

In the year Tartan, sent by Sargon king of Assyria, came to Ashdod and captured it, the LORD told Isaiah son of Amoz to loosen the sackcloth from his waist and remove his sandals, and Isaiah walked naked and barefoot. The LORD explained that Isaiah's three years of walking this way were a sign and a wonder concerning Egypt and Ethiopia. The king of Assyria would lead away the captives of Egypt and exiles of Ethiopia, young and old, naked and barefoot with buttocks uncovered, to Egypt's shame. Egypt and Ethiopia would be dismayed and confounded, since Ethiopia was their expectation and Egypt their glory. The inhabitants of the coast land would then say that those they had fled to for help against the king of Assyria were captured, and ask how they themselves would escape.

Chapter 21

Chapter 21 gives three burdens. The first, the burden of the wilderness of the sea, has the speaker describing a grievous vision in which Elam is told to attack and Media's sighing is stopped, leaving him in anguish and trembling. The Lord has him set a watchman, who reports a troop of horsemen in pairs and announces that Babylon has fallen and its engraved images are broken. The second burden, of Dumah, has a voice from Seir asking the watchman about the night, and he answers that morning and night both come and tells the caller to inquire again. The third burden, on Arabia, speaks of Dedanite caravans lodging in thickets, Tema's people giving water and bread to fugitives from war, and the Lord's word that within a year all the glory of Kedar will fail and its archers will be few.

Chapter 22

Chapter 22 is a burden against the valley of vision, a tumultuous city whose rulers fled and were captured, while the speaker weeps over the destruction of his people. The inhabitants prepared for siege by looking to the armor, gathering waters, counting and breaking down houses, and building a reservoir, but did not look to the one who planned this long ago. When GOD of Armies called for weeping and mourning, they instead feasted, saying 'let's eat and drink, for tomorrow we will die,' and were told this iniquity will not be forgiven until they die. The text then turns to the treasurer Shebna, who cut himself a tomb on high and is told he will be hurled away to a large country, die there, and be thrust from his office. Eliakim son of Hilkiah is to be called, clothed with Shebna's robe and belt, made a father to Jerusalem and Judah, and given the key of David's house, but the nail fastened in a sure place is finally said to give way and be cut down.

Chapter 23

Chapter 23 is a burden, or oracle, against Tyre, a wealthy merchant city whose trade drew on the Nile harvest and made it the market of nations. The ships of Tarshish, the coast dwellers, and Sidon are told to howl, be still, and be ashamed because Tyre is laid waste and Egypt will be in anguish at the report. The LORD of Armies is said to have planned this to stain the pride of all glory and to bring the honorable of the earth into contempt. He stretched out his hand over the sea, shook the kingdoms, and ordered the destruction of Canaan's strongholds. Tyre will be forgotten seventy years, after which the LORD will visit her and she will return to her wages, but her merchandise and earnings will become holy to the LORD, providing food and durable clothing for those who dwell before him.

Chapter 24

Chapter 24 describes the LORD emptying and laying waste the earth, scattering its inhabitants because they broke the laws, statutes, and everlasting covenant. The judgment falls on all classes alike, from priest and people to master and servant, and brings a curse, mourning, and the end of mirth and wine. Cities are broken and shut up, and few people remain, like gleanings after a vintage. A remnant lifts voices in praise of the LORD's majesty from the east and the islands of the sea, while the speaker laments the treachery he sees. Fear, the pit, and the snare overtake the inhabitants, the earth is shaken and falls, and the LORD punishes the high ones on high and the kings of the earth, imprisons them, and reigns on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem before his elders.

Chapter 25

Chapter 25 is a song of praise addressed to the LORD, who is exalted for his faithful, long-planned wonders, including turning a fortified city into a ruin that will never be rebuilt. Because he has been a stronghold and refuge to the poor and needy, strong peoples and awesome nations will glorify and fear him, while the noise of strangers and the song of the dreaded ones are brought low. On this mountain the LORD of Armies will prepare a feast of choice meat and wines for all peoples, destroy the covering and veil over all nations, swallow up death forever, and the Lord GOD will wipe away tears and remove his people's reproach. In that day his people will declare they waited for him and rejoice in his salvation. Moab, however, will be trodden down like straw in a dunghill, its pride and craft humbled, and the high fortress of its walls brought down to the dust.

Chapter 26

Chapter 26 presents a song to be sung in the land of Judah, celebrating a strong city whose walls are God's salvation and calling for the gates to open to the righteous nation that keeps faith. The LORD is praised as an everlasting Rock who keeps the steadfast in perfect peace, brings down the lofty city to the dust, and ordains peace for his people. The speakers, the people of Judah, say they have waited for the LORD, desire him by night, and acknowledge only his name, though they confess their suffering produced no deliverance, likened to a woman giving birth to wind. The wicked do not learn righteousness even when shown favor, and the LORD's adversaries will be consumed by fire. The chapter contrasts the dead who will not rise with the LORD's dead who will live, and ends with the people told to hide in their rooms until the indignation passes while the LORD comes out to punish the earth's inhabitants.

Chapter 27

Chapter 27 describes "that day," when the LORD will punish leviathan, the fleeing and twisted serpent, and kill the dragon in the sea. The LORD then calls for a song about his pleasant vineyard, which he keeps and waters night and day, and which he will defend against briers and thorns unless they seek peace with him. Jacob will take root and Israel will blossom, filling the world with fruit, and Jacob's iniquity will be forgiven when the altar stones are crushed like chalk and the Asherah poles and incense altars rise no more. The fortified city is left desolate, and its withered boughs are burned by women because its people have no understanding and the one who made them shows no compassion. On that day the LORD will thresh from the Euphrates to the brook of Egypt, gather the children of Israel one by one, and a great trumpet will bring the perishing in Assyria and the outcasts in Egypt to worship him on the holy mountain at Jerusalem.

Chapter 28

Chapter 28 opens with a woe against the drunkards of Ephraim, whose proud crown will be trampled by a mighty one sent by the Lord, while the LORD of Armies becomes a crown of glory to his remaining people. The priest and the prophet also reel with strong drink, err in vision and stumble in judgment, and the people mock the message as childish precept on precept, so it will come to them as stammering speech that leads to their fall. The scoffers who rule in Jerusalem claim a covenant with death and shelter in lies, but the Lord says he lays in Zion a tried cornerstone and will make justice the measuring line, sweeping away their refuge and annulling the covenant. The overflowing scourge will trample them repeatedly, and the Lord will do his unusual work, with a warning not to scoff lest their bonds be strengthened. The chapter ends with a farming analogy in which the farmer plows, sows and threshes each crop appropriately, taught by God, showing that the wonderful counsel of the LORD of Armies applies measured judgment.

Chapter 29

Chapter 29 opens with a woe against Ariel, the city where David encamped, which the LORD of Armies will distress with siege, mourning, and lamentation, bringing it low until its voice whispers from the dust. Then the LORD will visit Ariel with thunder, earthquake, and fire, and the multitude of nations fighting against it will vanish like a dream. The prophets and seers are struck with a spirit of deep sleep, so that all vision is like a sealed book no one can read. The LORD condemns this people for honoring him with their lips while their hearts are far away, and promises a marvelous work in which the wisdom of their wise men perishes. The chapter ends with a reversal in which the deaf hear, the humble rejoice, oppressors are cut off, and Jacob is no longer ashamed but sanctifies the Holy One of Jacob, while the erring come to understanding.

Chapter 30

In Chapter 30 the LORD denounces the rebellious children of Judah for seeking an alliance with Egypt and Pharaoh instead of consulting him, warning that this refuge will bring only shame and confusion. The people reject the prophets, demand pleasant deceits, and refuse the LORD's offer of salvation through returning, rest, quietness and confidence, so their iniquity will collapse like a broken wall and they will flee. Yet the LORD promises to wait and be gracious, so that the people will dwell in Zion, hear a guiding voice, discard their idols, and receive rain, abundant harvests, and healing. The chapter ends with the LORD's fiery, angry coming against the nations and the Assyrian, who will be dismayed and struck with the rod and consumed in a prepared burning place for the king.

Chapter 31

Chapter 31 pronounces woe on those who go down to Egypt for help, trusting in horses, chariots, and horsemen rather than looking to the Holy One of Israel. The LORD is described as wise and unchanging in his words, and he will rise against evildoers and those who help them, so that both helper and helped will fall. The LORD of Armies is likened to a lion undaunted by shepherds and to hovering birds, promising to fight on Mount Zion and to protect, deliver, and preserve Jerusalem. The children of Israel are called to return to him and to cast away their idols of silver and gold. The Assyrian will fall by a sword not of man, his young men will be put to forced labor, and his princes will fear the banner.

Chapter 32

Chapter 32 opens with a promise that a king will reign in righteousness and princes in justice, with a man serving as shelter from wind and storm. Perception and speech will be restored, and the fool and scoundrel, who speak folly, plot iniquity and use lying words to destroy the humble and deprive the hungry and thirsty, will no longer be honored, while the noble devises noble things. The prophet then addresses the women at ease, warning them to tremble because the vintage will fail, the harvest won't come, and thorns and briers will overrun my people's land and the joyous city, leaving the palace and city deserted and turned into dens for wild donkeys and pasture for flocks. This desolation lasts until the Spirit is poured on us from on high, when the wilderness becomes a fruitful field and justice and righteousness dwell there, bringing peace, quietness and confidence forever. My people will live in peaceful habitations even though hail flattens the forest and the city is leveled, and those who sow beside all waters are called blessed.

Chapter 33

Chapter 33 opens with a woe against a destroyer and betrayer who will in turn be destroyed and betrayed. The people pray to the LORD for grace and strength, and the LORD declares he will now arise and be exalted, turning the plans of his opponents into chaff and stubble and burning peoples like lime and thorns. The sinners in Zion tremble at the devouring fire, and the text answers who can live with it: the one who walks righteously, speaks blamelessly, and refuses bribes and bloodshed. That person will dwell on high with secure bread and water, and will see the king in his beauty. The chapter ends with Zion pictured as a quiet, unmovable city where the LORD is judge, lawgiver, and king, enemy ships are helpless and the lame take plunder, and the inhabitants are forgiven their iniquity.

Chapter 34

Chapter 34 summons the nations and the earth to hear that the LORD is enraged against all the nations and their armies and has given them over to slaughter. The sky's army dissolves and the sky is rolled up like a scroll, and the LORD's sword, having drunk its fill in the sky, comes down on Edom for judgment. The LORD holds a sacrifice in Bozrah and a great slaughter in Edom, on a day of vengeance and a year of recompense for the cause of Zion. Edom's land becomes burning pitch and sulfur and lies waste forever, with no one passing through. Pelicans, porcupines, owls, ravens, jackals, ostriches, wolves, wild goats, the night creature, the arrow snake and kites possess it, each with its mate, by the LORD's command, lot, and measuring line.

Chapter 35

Chapter 35 describes the joyful transformation of the wilderness and desert, which blossom like a rose and receive the glory of Lebanon, Carmel and Sharon. The people are urged to strengthen weak hands and feeble knees and to tell the fearful that their God will come with vengeance and save them. When God comes, the blind, deaf, lame and mute will be healed, and water will break out in the desert. A highway called The Holy Way will appear, closed to the unclean and to wicked fools and free of lions and ravenous animals, and the redeemed will walk on it. The LORD's ransomed ones will return to Zion singing, crowned with everlasting joy, while sorrow and sighing flee away.

Chapter 36

In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah, Sennacherib king of Assyria captured all the fortified cities of Judah and sent Rabshakeh with a large army from Lachish to Jerusalem. Rabshakeh stood by the aqueduct and delivered a taunting message to Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah, mocking trust in Egypt and in the LORD and claiming the LORD had told him to destroy the land. When the officials asked him to speak Aramaic rather than the Jews' language, he instead called out loudly in the Jews' language to the people on the wall, urging them not to trust Hezekiah and promising them a land of grain and wine if they surrendered. He argued that no gods of other nations, such as those of Hamath, Arpad, Sepharvaim, and Samaria, had saved their lands from Assyria. The people stayed silent as the king commanded, and the three officials returned to Hezekiah with torn clothes to report Rabshakeh's words.

Chapter 37

King Hezekiah of Judah, on hearing Rabshakeh's taunts, tears his clothes, puts on sackcloth, and sends Eliakim, Shebna, and the priestly elders to Isaiah, who relays the LORD's promise that the Assyrian king will hear news, return home, and fall by the sword. Sennacherib, through letters carried by messengers, again insists that no god has saved any nation he has conquered, and Hezekiah spreads the letter before the LORD and prays for deliverance so that all kingdoms will know the LORD alone is God. Isaiah answers with the LORD's message mocking Sennacherib's arrogance, promising a sign of recovery and renewed growth for the remnant, and declaring that Assyria will not shoot an arrow at Jerusalem. The LORD's angel strikes 185,000 men in the Assyrian camp, Sennacherib returns to Nineveh, and his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer kill him with the sword in the house of his god Nisroch, after which Esar Haddon reigns in his place.

Chapter 38

Hezekiah, king of Judah, falls sick and near death, and the prophet Isaiah tells him on the LORD's behalf to set his house in order because he will die. Hezekiah turns to the wall, prays, and weeps bitterly, and the LORD sends Isaiah back to say that He has heard the prayer and seen the tears, and will add fifteen years to Hezekiah's life and deliver him and the city from the king of Assyria. As a sign, the LORD causes the shadow on the sundial of Ahaz to go back ten steps. Hezekiah's writing then recounts his anguish at facing death, his praise for being delivered from the pit and having his sins cast behind the LORD's back, and his vow to sing in the LORD's house. The chapter closes with Isaiah's instruction to apply a cake of figs as a poultice on the boil, and Hezekiah's question about the sign that he will go up to the LORD's house.

Chapter 39

Merodach-baladan, king of Babylon, sends letters and a gift to Hezekiah after hearing that Hezekiah had been sick and recovered. Hezekiah is pleased and shows the Babylonian envoys everything in his house and dominion, including his silver, gold, spices, oil, and armor. The prophet Isaiah questions Hezekiah about the visitors and then delivers the word of the LORD of Armies: everything in Hezekiah's house will one day be carried to Babylon, and some of his sons will be taken to serve as eunuchs in the Babylonian king's palace. Hezekiah answers that the LORD's word is good, adding that there will be peace and truth in his own days.

Chapter 40

Chapter 40 opens with God commanding that his people, and Jerusalem in particular, be comforted because her warfare is over and her iniquity pardoned. A voice calls for the way of the LORD to be prepared in the wilderness, so that his glory is revealed to all flesh, and human frailty is contrasted with the word of God, which stands forever. Messengers to Zion and the cities of Judah are told to announce 'Behold, your God!', who comes with might yet shepherds his flock gently. The text then exalts the LORD as the incomparable Creator, before whom the nations are like a drop in a bucket and the idol made by a workman is no likeness of him. Finally, Jacob and Israel are rebuked for saying their way is hidden from the LORD, and are told that the unwearying God gives strength to the weak and renews those who wait for him.

Chapter 41

In Isaiah 41, the LORD summons the islands and peoples to a judgment and asks who raised up one from the east to subdue nations; he answers that he is the first and the last. The nations respond with fear and busy themselves making idols, while the LORD reassures Israel, his servant Jacob, the offspring of Abraham his friend, that he will help and uphold them and make them a threshing instrument against their enemies. He promises to answer the poor and needy by turning the wilderness into pools, springs and groves of trees so that all will know his hand did it. Finally, the LORD challenges the idols to declare the former and coming things, concludes that they and their works are nothing, and says he has raised up one from the north and will send good news to Zion and Jerusalem.

Chapter 42

Chapter 42 opens with the LORD presenting his chosen servant, on whom he has put his Spirit, to bring justice to the nations gently and without failing. The LORD, creator of the heavens and earth, calls the servant in righteousness to be a covenant for the people and a light for the nations, opening blind eyes and freeing prisoners. The LORD declares that he will not share his glory with engraved images and announces new things, prompting a call for all the earth, the sea, the islands, the wilderness, Kedar and Sela to sing him a new song. The LORD then says he will break his long silence like a man of war and a travailing woman, lead the blind on unknown paths, and turn back those who trust in idols. The chapter ends by rebuking blind and deaf Israel, a plundered and imprisoned people whom the LORD gave to robbers because they disobeyed his law, and on whom he poured his anger without their taking it to heart.

Chapter 43

In Chapter 43 the LORD tells Jacob and Israel not to fear, because he created and redeemed them, will be with them through water and fire, and will gather their sons and daughters from east, west, north, and south. He calls them his witnesses and declares that he alone is God and Savior, that no one can deliver from his hand, and that he will bring down Babylon and the Chaldeans for their sake. He then tells them to forget former things because he will do a new thing, making a way and rivers in the wilderness so his chosen people may declare his praise. He rebukes Jacob and Israel for not calling on him, for neglecting offerings, and for burdening him with sins, yet says he blots out transgressions for his own sake. The chapter ends with an invitation to plead their case, a charge that their first father and teachers sinned, and a pronouncement that Jacob will become a curse and Israel an insult.

Chapter 44

The LORD addresses Jacob, also called Israel and Jeshurun, as his chosen servant, promising to pour out water, his Spirit, and blessing on his descendants. He declares himself the first and the last, with no other God or Rock, and calls Israel his witnesses. The chapter then mocks idol makers, especially the blacksmith and carpenter, who burn part of a tree for warmth and food and then bow to the rest as a god. The LORD assures Israel that he has blotted out its transgressions and redeemed it, and creation is called to sing. He closes by declaring himself creator who frustrates diviners, promises that Jerusalem and Judah's cities will be rebuilt, and names Cyrus as his shepherd who will carry out his pleasure.

Chapter 45

In Chapter 45, the LORD addresses Cyrus, his anointed, promising to go before him, break open doors of bronze and bars of iron, and give him hidden treasures so that he will know the LORD calls him by name, for the sake of Jacob and Israel. The LORD declares that there is no other God, that he forms light and darkness and makes peace and calamity, and warns that no one may strive with their Maker. He says Cyrus will rebuild his city and free his exiles without price or reward, and that Egypt, Ethiopia, and the Sabeans will come over to Cyrus in chains confessing that God is with him. Idol makers will be confounded, while Israel will be saved with an everlasting salvation. The LORD calls all the ends of the earth to look to him and be saved, swearing that every knee will bow to him and that all Israel's offspring will be justified and rejoice.

Chapter 46

Chapter 46 contrasts the helpless idols of Bel and Nebo with God, who carries his people. Bel and Nebo bow down, and their idols are loaded onto animals as a burden, going into captivity because they cannot deliver it. God then addresses the house of Jacob and the remnant of the house of Israel, saying he has carried them from birth to old age and will deliver them. God asks to whom he can be compared, mocking those who pay a goldsmith to make a god that must be carried, cannot move, and cannot answer or save. He declares that he is God, that there is no other, that he declares the end from the beginning, and that his counsel will stand. He calls a ravenous bird from the east to carry out his plan, tells the stubborn-hearted that his righteousness and salvation are near, and promises salvation to Zion and glory to Israel.

Chapter 47

Chapter 47 is a pronouncement against the virgin daughter of Babylon, the daughter of the Chaldeans, who is ordered down from her throne to sit in the dust, do menial work, and suffer public shame. The LORD of Armies, called Redeemer and Holy One of Israel, declares that he will take vengeance and spare no one. He says he gave his angry people into her hand, but she showed no mercy and laid a heavy yoke on the aged. She is condemned for her pride, her pleasure-seeking security, her claim 'I am, and there is no one else besides me,' and her trust in wickedness, sorceries, and enchantments. Sudden disaster, loss of children and widowhood will come on her in one day, and her astrologers and former trading partners will be unable to save her.

Chapter 48

The LORD addresses the house of Jacob, who swear by his name but not in truth, and reminds them that he declared former things long ago so they could not credit an idol. He now announces new, hidden things and says he withholds his anger for his own name's sake, having refined them in the furnace of affliction. He declares himself the first and the last and the creator of earth and heavens, and says that the one he loves will act against Babylon and the Chaldeans. He laments that Israel did not heed his commandments, which would have brought peace like a river, and then calls them to leave Babylon and proclaim that he has redeemed his servant Jacob. The chapter closes with the reminder of water from the rock in the deserts and the statement that there is no peace for the wicked.

Chapter 49

In Isaiah 49, a speaker called by the LORD from the womb and named his servant, Israel, says he has labored in vain but trusts the LORD for his reward. The LORD answers that restoring Jacob's tribes is too light a task and makes him a light to the nations and a covenant for the people, so that kings will rise and worship. The LORD promises to free the bound, lead them to pasture and springs, and gather them from afar, and the heavens and earth are called to sing. Zion says the LORD has forsaken her, but he replies that he will not forget her, since he has engraved her on his palms, and he promises her children will return and crowd her. The LORD will lift his banner to the nations so they carry her sons and daughters, and he will rescue the captives of the mighty and judge her oppressors so that all flesh knows he is Savior and Redeemer.

Chapter 50

In Chapter 50, the LORD challenges the people by asking where the divorce certificate for their mother is and to which creditor he sold them, declaring that they were sold for their iniquities and their mother put away for their transgressions. He asks why no one answered when he came and called, insisting that his hand is not shortened and that he can redeem, as shown by his power to dry up the sea and clothe the heavens with blackness. A speaker, the servant of the Lord GOD, then describes being given the tongue of the taught to sustain the weary, and having his ear opened so that he was not rebellious. He tells of giving his back to those who beat him and not hiding his face from shame and spitting, trusting that the Lord GOD will help him and that his adversaries will wear out like a moth-eaten garment. The chapter closes with a call for those who fear the LORD to trust his name while walking in darkness, and a warning that those who kindle their own fires will lie down in sorrow.

Chapter 51

In Isaiah 51, the LORD calls on those who pursue righteousness to remember Abraham and Sarah, and promises to comfort Zion and turn her wastes into Eden. He declares that his righteousness and salvation are near and will last forever, though the heavens and earth will wear out, so his people should not fear the insults of men. A voice then calls on the arm of the LORD to awake as in the days when he cut Rahab in pieces and dried up the sea, and the ransomed are promised a joyful return to Zion. The LORD rebukes the fear of mortal men and the oppressor, and promises the captive exile freedom. He then addresses Jerusalem, which has drunk the cup of his wrath and has no one to guide her, and announces that he has taken the cup from her hand and will put it into the hands of her oppressors.

Chapter 52

Chapter 52 summons Zion and Jerusalem to awake, put on strength and beautiful garments, and shake off the dust and bonds of captivity, since the unclean will no longer enter the holy city. The LORD declares that his people, who went down into Egypt and were oppressed by the Assyrian, were sold for nothing and will be redeemed without money, so that they will know his name. A herald on the mountains brings good news of peace and salvation, proclaiming 'Your God reigns,' and the watchmen and the waste places of Jerusalem sing because the LORD has comforted and redeemed his people and bared his holy arm before all nations. The people are told to depart, cleanse themselves, and leave without haste, with the LORD going before them and as their rear guard. The chapter closes with the LORD's servant, who will deal wisely and be exalted, whose appearance was marred and who will cleanse many nations while kings shut their mouths in astonishment.

Chapter 53

Chapter 53 describes a servant who grew up like a tender plant, was unattractive, despised and rejected, and acquainted with suffering and disease. The speakers ("we") confess that they wrongly thought God had struck him, when in fact he was pierced and crushed for their transgressions and iniquities, bringing them peace and healing. The LORD laid the iniquity of all on him; he was oppressed and silent like a lamb led to slaughter, cut off from the land of the living, and given a grave with the wicked and a rich man despite his innocence. It pleased the LORD to bruise him and make his soul an offering for sin, after which he will see his offspring, prolong his days, see the light, and be satisfied. The LORD promises him a portion with the great and the strong, because he poured out his soul to death, was counted with transgressors, bore the sins of many, and interceded for them.

Chapter 54

Chapter 54 is the LORD's address to a barren, desolate woman, a figure for Israel, urging her to sing and enlarge her tent because her children will outnumber those of the married wife and will possess the nations. The LORD promises she will forget the shame of her youth and widowhood, since her Maker is her husband and the Holy One of Israel is her Redeemer. He admits forsaking her for a moment in overflowing wrath, but vows everlasting loving kindness, comparing his oath to the waters of Noah. He says the mountains may depart before his loving kindness and covenant of peace do. He promises to rebuild the afflicted city with sapphires, rubies, and precious stones, to have her children taught by himself, and to establish her in righteousness. No weapon formed against her will prevail, and this is declared the heritage of the LORD's servants.

Chapter 55

In Chapter 55 the LORD invites everyone who thirsts to come to the waters and receive food, wine, and milk without money or price, asking why they spend labor on what does not satisfy. He promises an everlasting covenant, the sure mercies of David, and says he has given a witness, leader, and commander to the peoples; a nation unknown to the addressee will run to it because the LORD has glorified it. The people are urged to seek the LORD while he is near, and the wicked man is told to forsake his way and thoughts and return, for the LORD will freely pardon. The LORD declares that his thoughts and ways are higher than human ones as the heavens are higher than the earth, and that his word, like rain and snow that water the earth, will not return void but will accomplish what he pleases. The chapter ends with a promise of going out with joy and peace, singing mountains and clapping trees, and cypress and myrtle replacing thorn and brier as an everlasting sign making a name for the LORD.

Chapter 56

In Chapter 56 the LORD calls his people to maintain justice and do what is right, because his salvation is near and his righteousness will soon be revealed. He blesses those who keep the Sabbath without profaning it and keep their hands from evil. He assures foreigners who join themselves to him and eunuchs who keep his Sabbaths and hold fast his covenant that they will not be separated from his people: eunuchs will receive an everlasting name better than sons and daughters, and foreigners will be brought to his holy mountain, where their offerings will be accepted in his house, called a house of prayer for all peoples. The Lord GOD, who gathers Israel's outcasts, promises to gather still others in addition to those already gathered. The chapter ends by summoning the animals of the field and forest to devour, and condemns Israel's watchmen as blind, ignorant, mute dogs who are greedy, lack understanding, pursue their own gain, and plan to indulge in wine and strong drink, expecting tomorrow to be like today.

Chapter 57

Chapter 57 opens by lamenting that the righteous and merciful perish unnoticed, though they enter peace and rest. The LORD then summons the 'sons of a sorceress', offspring of adulterers and prostitutes, and condemns their mockery and their idolatrous practices: burning with passion among oaks, killing children in the valleys, offering drink offerings to smooth stones, setting a bed on high mountains, and seeking alliances with a king and sending ambassadors as far as Sheol. He says their works will not benefit them, that the idols they gathered will be carried off by the wind, while whoever takes refuge in him will inherit the land and his holy mountain. The high and holy One who dwells with the contrite and humble promises to revive them, says he will not contend forever, and declares that he will heal and lead the backsliding one and give peace to those far and near. The chapter closes by comparing the wicked to a troubled sea and stating that there is no peace for them.

Chapter 58

In Chapter 58 the LORD commands a prophetic voice to cry aloud and declare to his people, the house of Jacob, their disobedience. The people seek God daily and complain that their fasting goes unnoticed, but the LORD answers that they pursue pleasure and oppress their laborers while fasting amid strife and contention. He defines the fast he has chosen as releasing the bonds of wickedness, freeing the oppressed, sharing bread with the hungry, sheltering the poor, and covering the naked. For such obedience he promises light, healing, guidance, strength, restoration, and the name Repairer of the Breach. He also calls them to honor the Sabbath as a delight rather than pursuing their own pleasure, promising delight in the LORD and the heritage of Jacob their father.

Chapter 59

Chapter 59 declares that the LORD's hand is not too short to save nor his ear too dull to hear, but that the people's iniquities have separated them from God. The passage details their bloodshed, lies, and violence, and the people confess that justice and righteousness are far from them and that they grope in darkness. The LORD sees that there is no justice and no intercessor, so his own arm brings salvation, and he puts on righteousness, salvation, vengeance, and zeal as armor and clothing. He will repay adversaries according to their deeds, and people from west to east will fear his name. The chapter ends with the promise that a Redeemer will come to Zion and to those in Jacob who turn from disobedience, and with the LORD's covenant that his Spirit and words will remain with the people and their descendants forever.

Chapter 60

Chapter 60 addresses Zion, told to arise and shine because the LORD's light and glory have come upon her while darkness covers the earth and peoples. Nations and kings are drawn to her brightness, and her sons and daughters return from far away. Peoples from places such as Midian, Ephah, Sheba, Kedar, Nebaioth, Lebanon, and Tarshish bring gold, frankincense, flocks, ships, silver, and timber to honor the LORD and beautify his house. Foreigners rebuild her walls, former oppressors bow to her, and nations that refuse to serve her perish. The LORD promises to turn her from forsaken to an eternal excellency, to replace violence with peace and righteousness, to be her everlasting light instead of sun and moon, and to make her people righteous and numerous.

Chapter 61

In Chapter 61, a speaker whom the Lord GOD's Spirit rests upon and who has been anointed announces good news to the humble, healing for the broken hearted, liberty for captives, and the year of the LORD's favor alongside the day of vengeance. Those who mourn in Zion receive a garland, the oil of joy, and the garment of praise, and are called trees of righteousness. The people will rebuild old ruins, while strangers and foreigners tend their flocks and fields, and they will be called the LORD's priests and servants of God, eating the wealth of the nations and receiving double for their shame. The LORD declares that he loves justice, hates robbery and iniquity, and will make an everlasting covenant, so that their offspring are acknowledged among the nations as blessed. The speaker rejoices, clothed with salvation and righteousness like a bridegroom or bride, and says the Lord GOD will make righteousness and praise spring up before all nations.

Chapter 62

Chapter 62 has a speaker vowing not to stay silent for the sake of Zion and Jerusalem until her righteousness and salvation shine out. The LORD promises that the nations and kings will see her glory and that she will receive a new name, becoming a crown in his hand. She will no longer be called Forsaken or Desolate but Hephzibah and Beulah, because the LORD delights in her and her land will be married. Watchmen are set on Jerusalem's walls to pray without rest until the LORD establishes her as a praise on the earth. The LORD swears that her grain and wine will no longer go to enemies and foreigners, and the people are told to prepare the highway and raise a banner because salvation and reward are coming. The chapter ends with new names for the people: The Holy People, The LORD's Redeemed, and Sought Out, A City Not Forsaken.

Chapter 63

Chapter 63 opens with a dialogue in which a figure coming from Edom and Bozrah, with garments stained red, declares himself mighty to save; he says he trod the peoples alone in his anger as the day of vengeance and the year of his redeemed arrived. The speaker then recounts the LORD's loving kindnesses to the house of Israel, whom he called his people and became their Savior, sharing in their affliction and carrying them. Israel rebelled and grieved his Holy Spirit, so he became their enemy, yet he remembered the days of Moses and the leading through the sea and wilderness. The chapter ends with a prayer asking the LORD to look down from heaven, to show his zeal and compassion, and to return for his servants' sake, since he is their Father and Redeemer. The people lament that adversaries have trodden down the sanctuary and that they have become like those never ruled by him.

Chapter 64

In Chapter 64 the speakers plead with the LORD to tear the heavens and come down, so that the mountains quake and the nations tremble before his name as they did when he did awesome things they did not expect. They affirm that no one has ever seen a God besides him who works for those who wait for him. They confess that the LORD was angry and they sinned, that they have all become unclean, that their righteousness is like a polluted garment, and that their iniquities carry them away like the wind. They appeal to the LORD as Father and potter, with themselves as clay and the work of his hand, and ask him not to be furious or remember iniquity forever. They lament that the holy cities, Zion, Jerusalem and the burned house where their fathers praised him are wasted, and ask whether he will stay silent and punish them severely.

Chapter 65

In this chapter the LORD speaks of a rebellious people who provoke him with idolatrous sacrifices, burning incense on bricks and mountains, sitting among graves, eating pig's meat, and claiming to be holier than others. He promises to repay their iniquities and those of their fathers, and to destine those who forsake him and prepare a table for Fortune and Destiny to the sword. He will spare a remnant, bringing offspring out of Jacob and Judah, so that his chosen servants inherit his mountains while Sharon and the valley of Achor become places for flocks and herds. The LORD contrasts his servants, who will eat, drink, rejoice and be called by another name, with the wicked, who will hunger, thirst, wail and leave their name as a curse. He then announces the creation of new heavens and a new earth and of Jerusalem as a delight, where weeping ends, lives are long, labor is not in vain, prayers are answered before they are spoken, and the wolf and lamb feed together on his holy mountain.

Chapter 66

The LORD declares that heaven is his throne and earth his footstool, and that he looks to the poor and contrite who tremble at his word, while rejecting the sacrifices of those who choose their own ways and delight in abominations. He promises that Zion will give birth to her children suddenly and that Jerusalem will be a source of comfort, peace, and glory, with the LORD comforting his people like a mother. The LORD will come with fire, whirlwind, and sword to execute judgment on all flesh, especially those who practice idolatrous rites and eat pig's meat, the mouse, and abominable things. He will gather all nations to see his glory, send survivors to distant nations such as Tarshish, Pul, Lud, Tubal, and Javan to declare it, and bring back the brothers of his people to Jerusalem, selecting priests and Levites among them. The chapter closes with the promise that his people's offspring and name will endure like the new heavens and new earth, that all flesh will worship before him, and that they will look on the corpses of transgressors, whose worm and fire never end.
