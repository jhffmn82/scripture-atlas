the Branch

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Zechariah, the coming King of Zion arrives righteously and lowly on a donkey's colt, speaks peace to the nations, and rules from sea to sea and to the earth's ends. In The Book of Zechariah, the Branch is the LORD's servant who grows up, builds the LORD's temple, bears glory, and rules as priest on his throne with peace between both roles. In The Second Book of Nephi, the rod and branch from Jesse grows out of Jesse, receives the Spirit of the Lord, judges righteously, reproves equitably, smites the earth, slays the wicked, and stands as an ensign of the people.

the Branch in The Book of Zechariah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the Branch first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Branch is the LORD's servant, promised in Chapter 3 with Joshua and his fellows as a sign. In Chapter 6 he grows up from his place, builds the LORD's temple, bears glory, and sits and rules on his throne as priest, with peace counseled between both roles.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

The Branch is named as the LORD's servant whom the LORD says he will bring out. Joshua and his fellows are described as men who are a sign in connection with this promise.

Chapter 6

The Branch is a man who will grow up out of his place and build the LORD's temple. He will bear the glory, sit and rule on his throne, and be a priest on his throne, with the counsel of peace between them both.

the rod and branch from Jesse in The Second Book of Nephi

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the rod and branch from Jesse first appears

Abstract written by a model

The rod and branch grows out of Jesse and receives the Spirit of the Lord: wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, and fear. With quick understanding, it judges the poor righteously, reproves the meek equitably, smites the earth, and slays the wicked. Clothed in righteousness and faithfulness, it is the root of Jesse, an ensign of the people, with glorious rest.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

The rod and branch receive the Spirit of the Lord, including wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, and fear of the Lord. They judge the poor with righteousness, reprove the meek with equity, smite the earth with the rod of their mouth, and slay the wicked with the breath of their lips.
