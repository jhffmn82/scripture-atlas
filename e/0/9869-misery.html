Abstract written by a model

Misery arises from sin, wicked deeds, possessions, violence, passions, and wrong understanding. It is produced by one’s own works: living beings suffer the consequences of their deeds and cannot escape them before experiencing them. Its forms include hardships such as cold, hunger, insults, blows, temptation, attachment, and the continuous bodily and mental pain of the hells. Those who do not know its origin cannot know its prevention, and false or wrong paths lead to misery and destruction. The wise understand its influx and annihilation, endure suffering patiently without passions, and follow restraint, right knowledge, and the Path toward liberation from all misery.

Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I / First Book. / First Lecture, Called the Doctrine. / First Chapter. .. Fourth Chapter.

Misery arises from wicked deeds, possessions, violence, passions, and wrong understanding. Those who fail to know its origin cannot know its prevention, and those who follow false doctrines remain subject to it.

Second Lecture, Called the Destruction of Karman. / First Chapter. .. Third Chapter.

All living beings suffer for their own deeds, and no one escapes the consequences before experiencing them. A wise person should endure suffering patiently without giving way to passions.

Third Lecture, Called the Knowledge of Troubles / First Chapter. .. Fourth Chapter.

Suffering and hardships include cold, heat, hunger, begging, insults, bites, blows, hair-plucking, sexual temptation, and attachment. Weak men retreat from them, while disciplined monks endure them for liberation.

Fifth Lecture, Called Description of the Hells. / First Chapter. .. Second Chapter.

Pain and suffering in the hells are described as insupportable, continuous, and accompanied by cries of agony. Sinners experience both bodily and mental pain and cannot find relief or escape.

Eleventh Lecture, Called the Path.

The Path is said to lead to liberation from all misery. Those who enter upon a wrong path go to misery and destruction.

Twelfth Lecture, Called the Creed.

It is produced by one's own works, not by those of somebody else. Its influx and annihilation are understood by the wise.
