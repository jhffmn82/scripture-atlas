Also called: Man, Man!, a man, man, some have exerted themselves, some men, that man, this man, ye men

Abstract written by a model

Men may control themselves or only pretend to be houseless. Sinful acts toward earth, water, fire, plants, animals, and wind cause loss of happiness and perfect wisdom. Attached to sense objects and lust, they suffer, fail to reach liberation, and come to destruction through frail bodies; when benighted, they are subject to old age and death. Seeing living beings suffer, they should lead a religious life: restrain themselves, recognize friendship, understand truth, overcome Mâra, and renounce causes of sin. Carelessness returns a man to life, but circumspect practice may bring liberation from death and make men reward-knowing sages.

Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I / First Book. / First Lecture, Called Knowledge of the Weapon. / First Lesson. .. Seventh Lesson.

Men may control themselves or merely pretend to be houseless. They act sinfully toward earth, water, fire, plants, animals, and wind, or cause or allow others to do so, and thereby lose happiness and perfect wisdom.

Third Lecture, Called Hot and Cold. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson.

Man is taught to restrain himself, recognize his own friendship, understand the truth, and overcome Mâra. A man who is careless returns to life, while one who leads a circumspect religious life may be liberated from death.

Sixth Lecture, Called the Cleaning. / First Lesson. .. Fifth Lesson.

Men attached to the objects of the senses cry bitterly and do not reach liberation because of their sinfulness. Men given to lust come to destruction through their weak and frail bodies.
