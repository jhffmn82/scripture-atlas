vanity

Abstract written by a model

Vanity is the central theme of the Preacher's verdict in Ecclesiastes, summed up as 'Vanity of vanities, all is vanity,' and equated with a chasing after wind. It characterizes all things, all that comes, and all human and animal fate. The records trace it from beginning to end. Chapter 1 declares it over all works under the sun, including the pursuit of wisdom, madness and folly. Chapter 2 applies it to mirth, great works, the lack of profit, the shared fate of wise and fool, and labor left to a successor who did not work for it, called a great evil. Chapter 3 applies it to man's lack of advantage over animals, who share one breath and fate, after noting wickedness in places of justice. Chapter 4 ties it to envy-driven labor, the solitary man's endless toil, and a popular young king's fading succession. Chapter 6 links it to unenjoyed riches, the stillborn child, the wandering of desire, and the many words that multiply it. Chapter 11 extends it to youth and the dawn of life, and chapter 12 restates the verdict after the description of aging and death.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Ecclesiastes / Chapter 1

The Preacher declares "Vanity of vanities, all is vanity." He repeats the verdict after seeing all the works done under the sun, calling them vanity and a chasing after wind, and says that his pursuit of wisdom, madness and folly was likewise a chasing after wind.

Chapter 2

Vanity, along with a chasing after wind, is the narrator's verdict on mirth, his great works, the lack of profit under the sun, the shared fate of wise and fool, his labor left to a successor, a man's sorrowful days, and God's allotment to the sinner. It is repeated throughout the chapter, and the narrator once calls the inheritance of labor by one who did not work for it also a great evil.

Chapter 3

The narrator calls all vanity when explaining that man has no advantage over the animals, since both share one breath and one fate. This follows his observation that wickedness occupies even places of justice and righteousness.

Chapter 4

Vanity is applied to labor driven by a neighbor's envy, to the solitary man's endless toil, and to the succession of a popular young king whom later generations will not rejoice in. It is called a striving after wind and a chasing after wind.

Chapter 6

Vanity is attached to the unenjoyed riches, to the stillborn child's coming, to the wandering of desire, which is also called a chasing after wind, and to the many words that multiply it. The speaker also calls man's whole life vain and asks what such words profit him.

Chapter 11

The unit says that all that comes is vanity, in the context of a man living many years and remembering the many days of darkness. It also calls youth and the dawn of life vanity.

Chapter 12

Vanity is the Preacher's verdict on everything: 'Vanity of vanities! All is vanity!' It is stated in the passage after the description of aging and death.
