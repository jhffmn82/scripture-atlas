Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Sennacherib is king of Assyria who captures Judah's fortified cities, takes silver and gold from Hezekiah, sends an army to Jerusalem, defies the LORD, departs to Nineveh, and is killed by his sons. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Sennacherib invades Judah, attacks Lachish, mocks Hezekiah and the LORD, and is killed by his sons after the LORD destroys his army. In Tobit, Sennacherib is the son and successor of Enemessar who rules in troubled times, kills Israelites, seeks Tobit for execution, and is murdered by two of his sons. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Sennacherib is an arrogant Assyrian invader who sends Rabshakeh against Jerusalem, boasts against Zion, and departs after the Lord destroys his camp. In The Book of Isaiah, Sennacherib is the king of Assyria who captures Judah's fortified cities, sends an army to Jerusalem, writes to Hezekiah against Jerusalem's God, is turned back by the LORD, and is killed by his sons. In The Book of Nahum, the king of Assyria is addressed as a cruel ruler whose shepherds and nobles slumber, whose people are scattered, and whose fatal wound is incurable.

Sennacherib in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Sennacherib first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sennacherib is king of Assyria. In Hezekiah's fourteenth year he captured Judah's fortified cities, took silver and gold from Hezekiah, and sent Tartan, Rabsaris and Rabshakeh with a great army from Lachish to Jerusalem. He then sent a letter defying the LORD, who rebuked his arrogance and promised to turn him back. He departed to Nineveh and was killed by his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer while worshiping Nisroch.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

Sennacherib, king of Assyria, came up in Hezekiah's fourteenth year against all of Judah's fortified cities and took them. He appointed three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold from Hezekiah and received the temple and palace silver and gold. He then sent Tartan, Rabsaris, and Rabshakeh with a great army from Lachish to Jerusalem.

Chapter 19

Sennacherib, king of Assyria, sends messengers with a letter defying the LORD and claiming no god has saved nations from Assyria. The LORD rebukes his arrogance and promises to turn him back. He departs, lives at Nineveh, and is killed by his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer while worshiping in the house of Nisroch his god.

Sennacherib in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Sennacherib first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sennacherib is the king of Assyria. He captures Judah's fortified cities and sends Rabshakeh with an army from Lachish to Jerusalem. While warring against Libnah, he writes to Hezekiah that Jerusalem's God cannot save it. The LORD denounces his pride and turns him back; his army is struck and he returns to Nineveh, where his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer kill him and Esar Haddon reigns.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 36

Sennacherib, king of Assyria, attacks and captures all the fortified cities of Judah. He sends Rabshakeh with a large army from Lachish to Jerusalem, and his words are presented by Rabshakeh as those of 'the great king.'

Chapter 37

Sennacherib, king of Assyria, is warring against Libnah and sends a letter to Hezekiah claiming that the gods of conquered nations could not save them and that Jerusalem's God cannot either. The LORD denounces his pride, promises to put a hook in his nose and turn him back, and after his army is struck he returns to Nineveh. There his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer kill him with the sword while he worships in the house of Nisroch, and his son Esar Haddon reigns in his place.
