Abstract written by a model

Tobit is a righteous man of the tribe of Naphtali, son of Tobiel and descended from Asiel, carried captive from Thisbe to Nineveh in the days of Enemessar. He walks in truth and righteousness, gives alms, tithes and offerings, and alone of his tribe goes to Jerusalem for the feasts. He is married to Anna, father of Tobias, refuses the Gentiles' bread, and becomes Enemessar's purchasing agent, with favor granted by the Most High. He leaves ten talents of silver in trust with Gabael at Rages of Media. His habit of burying slain kinsmen brings an informer's charge, flight, and the loss of all goods except his wife and son, until his nephew Achiacharus helps him return to Nineveh. After burying a strangled kinsman at a Pentecost feast, he is mocked by neighbors, sleeps by his courtyard wall, and loses his sight to sparrow dung, at age fifty-eight. Poor and dependent on Achiacharus, he quarrels with Anna over a kid she brought home. Grieved, he prays for death, confessing God's righteous judgments, and his prayer is heard as Raphael is sent to heal him. He instructs Tobias in righteousness, alms and fearing God, hands over the handwriting for the money, and hires a companion, whom he accepts as kindred, promising wages and assuring Anna a good angel will go with their son. While Tobias is delayed he worries but insists the boy is well. On his return Tobias rubs bile on his father's eyes, the white films peel away, and Tobit sees, blesses God, and publicly gives thanks. He insists the companion be paid, then falls in fear when Raphael is revealed, and is told to bless God and write down what happened. He writes a prayer of rejoicing, exalting God as everlasting King and urging sinners to repent. In old age he charges Tobias and his six grandsons to leave Nineveh, keep the law, and bury him and Anna. He dies at 158 and is buried magnificently, with Anna, by Tobias. Overall his story moves from faithful suffering and affliction to mercy, restored sight, and thanksgiving.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

11 sections; each heading opens the text

Tobit / Chapter 1

Tobit, of the tribe of Naphtali, was carried captive to Nineveh, yet walked in truth and righteousness, going alone to Jerusalem for the feasts and giving tithes while his tribe sacrificed to Baal. He married Anna, fathered Tobias, refused the Gentiles' bread, and became Enemessar's purchasing agent. After burying the dead under Sennacherib he was informed on, fled, and lost all his goods save his wife and son, until Achiacharus enabled his return to Nineveh.

Chapter 2

The narrator sits down to a Pentecost feast but leaves it to take in a strangled kinsman, then buries him at sunset after eating in heaviness and recalling Amos's prophecy. Mocked by neighbors, he sleeps polluted by his courtyard wall and loses his sight to sparrow dung. He later accuses Anna of bringing home a stolen kid and is ashamed of her, and she turns his own righteousness back on him.

Chapter 3

Tobit is grieved and weeps, then prays to the Lord, confessing the righteousness of God's judgments and asking not to be punished for his own sins and those of his fathers. He asks to be released from life by death because of false reproaches and sorrow. His prayer is heard, and Raphael is sent to scale the white films from his eyes. At that time he returns and enters his house.

Chapter 4

Tobit remembers the money left with Gabael and, saying he has asked for death, decides to instruct his son Tobias before he dies. He gives Tobias commands about honoring his mother, righteousness, alms, marriage, wages, drunkenness, and counsel, and explains the ten talents of silver. He reassures Tobias that poverty is no cause for fear if he fears God.

Chapter 5

Tobit gives Tobias the handwriting and tells him to find a man to go with him, offering wages. He questions the hired man's tribe and family, accepts him as kindred, agrees to pay a drachma a day plus necessities and more if both return safe, and blesses the journey. He comforts Anna by saying a good angel will go with their son and bring him back safely.

Chapter 7

Tobit is described by Raguel as his cousin and brother, and the travelers confirm he is alive and in good health. It is learned that he has lost his sight, which grieves Raguel and his household.

Chapter 10

Tobit counts the days of his son's journey and, when Tobias does not return, worries that he is detained or that Gabael has died and cannot give him the money. He is very grieved, but tells his wife to hold her peace and not worry, saying Tobias is in good health.

Chapter 11

Tobit goes toward the door and stumbles, but his son takes hold of him and rubs bile on his eyes. The white films peel away, he sees Tobias, weeps, and blesses God and his holy angels. He then goes out to meet Sarah at the gate of Nineveh, gives thanks publicly, and blesses her, and those who see him marvel that he has received his sight.

Chapter 12

Tobit calls Tobias and insists that the man who went with him be paid his wages and more. He agrees that half of what was brought back is due, and has the angel told to take it. After Raphael's revelation, Tobit falls on his face in fear, is told to bless God and write down what happened, and afterward confesses God's wonderful works.

Chapter 13

Tobit writes a prayer of rejoicing blessing God as the everlasting King. He gives thanks in the land of his captivity, shows God's strength and majesty to a nation of sinners, and urges them to turn and do righteousness. He also exalts God and calls on his own soul to bless the great King.

Chapter 14

Tobit, who lost his sight at fifty-eight and regained it after eight years, grows very old and gives alms and thanks to God. He charges Tobias and his grandsons to leave Nineveh, keep the law, and bury him and Anna, warning with the example of Aman and Achiacharus. He dies in bed at 158 and is buried magnificently by Tobias.
