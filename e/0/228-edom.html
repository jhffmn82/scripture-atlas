Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Edom is another name for Esau, whose descendants inhabit Seir, where chiefs and kings are recorded before Israel has a king. In Numbers, Edom is a kingdom ruled by a king that refuses Israel passage and confronts it with force. In Deuteronomy, Esau's descendants live in Seir, which the LORD gave them, and Israel is told not to contend with them or take their land. In The Second Book of Samuel, Edomites become David's servants after he places garrisons throughout Edom. In The Second Book of Kings, Edom's king joins Israel and Judah's expedition, but later Edom revolts from Judah and remains free. In 1 Chronicles, Edom has kings and chiefs before Israel, is defeated by Abishai, garrisoned by David, and made subject to him. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Edom revolts from Judah, appoints its own king, and remains independent after Jehoram's attack. In Judith, Esau's descendants advise Holofernes to starve Israel and join the forces camped near Bethulia.

and 8 more works

In Psalms, Edom is named in connection with Joab's killing of twelve thousand and is portrayed as receiving God's sandal. In The Book of Isaiah, Edom is cursed by the LORD, slaughtered, and left as a perpetual wasteland inhabited by wild creatures. In The Book of Jeremiah, Edom is a proud nation judged by the LORD, whose wisdom fails and cities become uninhabited, while its orphans and widows are preserved. In The Lamentations of Jeremiah, Edom's daughter is told that the cup of judgment will pass to her and expose her sins. In The Book of Ezekiel, Edom is judged for vengeance against Judah and becomes wholly desolate with Mount Seir. In The Book of Amos, Edom is condemned for pursuing its brother with perpetual wrath, and fire is promised against Teman and Bozrah. In The Book of Obadiah, Edom is condemned for pride and violence against Jacob, and its people, allies, wise men, and warriors are destroyed. In The Book of Malachi, Edom is called The Wicked Land after the LORD repeatedly throws down what it builds.

Abstract written by a model

Edom is a land placed under the LORD's curse, its people called his cursed people. It suffers slaughter, including at Bozrah, then its streams become pitch and its dust sulfur, burning unquenched with smoke rising forever. Nobles come to nothing, thorns overgrow its fortresses, and it lies waste and impassable across generations, inhabited only by jackals, ostriches and other wild creatures.
