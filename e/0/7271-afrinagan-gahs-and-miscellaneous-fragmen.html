Abstract written by a model

This document collects Zoroastrian ritual texts: Âfrînagân services, the five Gâhs, and miscellaneous fragments. Âfrînagân blesses Hâvani, seasonal lords, the Gâthas, and Rapithwina; it requires Mazdayasnians to bring Myazda or substitutes, imposes expulsion and penance for failure, and seeks rulers’ strength, victory, health, long life, and Heaven. The Gâhs organize sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise across five ritual times, invoking Ahura Mazda, Zarathustra, fires, Fravashis, and divine powers while rejecting the Daêvas. The fragments broaden this ritual order through instructions, confessions, prayers, and moral teaching: good thoughts, words, and deeds lead to Heaven, evil ones to Hell. They praise sacred beings and powerful words, and call for the defeat of Angra Mainyu, the Daêvas, and the Drug(k).

Âfrînagân. / I. Âfrîn[-Agân] Gahanbâr. .. III. Âfrîn[-Agân] Rapithvin.

Âfrînagân presents ritual services and blessings for Hâvani, the seasonal lords, the Gâthas, and Rapithwina. It instructs Mazdayasnians to offer Myazda or, if unable, equivalent liquor or wood, and prescribes expulsion and penance for those who fail to bring the required offerings. It blesses rulers with strength, victory, long life, health, and Heaven. Âfrîn[-Agân] Rapithvin teaches that performing the Rapithwina office with the prescribed ritual preparations earns a reward like the wind that advances and blesses the material world.

The Gâhs. / I. The Gâh Hâvan. .. V. Gâh Ushahin.

The Gâhs are five ritual sections, Gâh Hâvan, Gâh Rapithvin, Gâh Uziren, Gâh Aiwisrûthrima, and Gâh Ushahin, devoted to sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise within the ritual order. They invoke Ahura Mazda, Zarathustra, the sacred fires, divine beings, Fravashis, and other lords associated with different times, virtues, places, and functions. The sections repeatedly affirm Mazda-worship, opposition to the Daêvas, and devotion to the lore of the Lord. They also bless or sacrifice to powers connected with righteousness, waters, fire, prosperity, strength, obedience, justice, and the advancement of settlements.

Miscellaneous Fragments. / I. .. IX.

The Miscellaneous Fragments contain ritual instructions, praises, confessions, prayers, and teachings concerning Mazda-worship, sacrifice, moral conduct, and cosmic order. Zarathustra Spitama is instructed to declare words for worship, while Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, waters, plants, Fravashis, and Yazads receive praise or sacrifice. The unit teaches that good thoughts, words, and deeds lead to Heaven, while evil ones lead to Hell, and it calls for the defeat of Angra Mainyu, the Daêvas, and the Drug(k). It also presents prayers and sacred words, including the Airyemâ-ishyô, Ahuna-vairya, Khshathra-vairya, Yâ daêna, and the Mãthra-spenta word, as powerful and victorious.
