Josiah's religious reforms in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Josiah's religious reforms first appears

Abstract written by a model

Josiah's religious reforms are a sweeping purge of idolatry, aimed at confirming the words of the law in the book Hilkiah found. He removes Baal, Asherah and sky-army vessels, idolatrous priests and shrines, defiles Topheth and the high places, burns the sun chariots, destroys the Bethel altar, and extends the purge to Samaria, yet the LORD's wrath against Judah is not turned.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 23

Josiah removes the vessels for Baal, the Asherah, and the army of the sky from the LORD's temple, removes idolatrous priests, and breaks down shrine prostitutes' houses, high places, and altars. He defiles Topheth and the high places Solomon built, destroys the Bethel altar, and extends the purge to Samaria's cities. He also removes mediums, wizards, teraphim, and idols, yet the LORD's wrath against Judah is not turned.

purge of idolatry in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where purge of idolatry first appears

Abstract written by a model

Josiah's purge of idolatry began in his twelfth year: he removed high places and images from Judah and Jerusalem, burned priests' bones on their altars, extended it to Manasseh, Ephraim, Simeon, Naphtali, returned to Jerusalem, before temple repair.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 34

Beginning in his twelfth year, Josiah purged Judah and Jerusalem of high places and images, burned the bones of the priests on their altars, and extended the work to the cities of Manasseh, Ephraim, Simeon, and Naphtali. He then returned to Jerusalem. The work preceded the temple repair and the finding of the book.
