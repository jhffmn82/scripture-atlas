Abstract written by a model

In Surah 3: The Family of Imraan (Aal-i-Imraan): Some people are condemned for wrongfully slaying the prophets. In Surah 3: The Family of Imraan (Aal-i-Imraan), messengers are sent by Allah, though disbelievers deny and sometimes slay them. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), messengers are sent to mankind with good cheer and warning, and believers are commanded to believe in all of them without distinction. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), the prophets receive Allah's favour and revelation, belong to the company of those who obey Him, and some are slain wrongfully. In Surah 5: The Table (Al-Maaida), messengers bring glad tidings, warnings, clear proofs, and revelations, convey Allah's message without compulsion, and are questioned about mankind's response. In Surah 6: The Cattle (Al-An'aam), the messengers bear good news and warn humankind of Judgment while patiently awaiting Allah's succour. In Surah 7: The Heights (Al-A'raaf): Messengers convey Allah's messages and counsel their peoples, but many are denied; they will be questioned along with those to whom they were sent. In Surah 10: Jonas (Yunus), messengers are sent to nations with clear proofs, and judgment is made fairly between each messenger and his people.

In Surah 11: Hud, the messengers bring Abraham good news, warn Lot of destruction, protect him, and instruct him to depart by night. In Surah 13: The Thunder (Ar-Ra'd), messengers are sent before Muhammad, and no messenger brings a portent except by Allah's leave. In Surah 14: Abraham (Ibrahim), a messenger is sent in the language of his folk to make the message clear and call people to Allah. In Surah 15: The Rock (Al-Hijr): Messengers were sent among earlier peoples, but those peoples mocked them. In Surah 18: The Cave (Al-Kahf), the messengers are sent as bearers of good news and warners while disbelievers dispute and mock them and the revelations. In Surah 21: The Prophets (Al-Anbiyaa), the messengers before Muhammad are men who receive inspiration, are mocked, and are ultimately vindicated against their mockers. In Surah 21: The Prophets (Al-Anbiyaa), the prophets receive inspiration and guide people by Allah's command, establishing worship, doing good deeds, and giving alms. In The Believers (Al-Muminoon), messengers are sent successively to nations that deny them, and they are told to eat good things and do right. In Surah 26: The Poets (Ash-Shu'araa), the messengers call their peoples to ward off evil, obey Allah and them, and seek no wage except from the Lord of the Worlds. In Surah 28: The Stories (Al-Qasas), the messengers are questioned about their peoples' responses, and they are raised before townships are destroyed to give warning. In Surah 35: The Originator (Faatir), messengers of Allah come with clear proofs, the Psalms, and Scripture giving light, while every nation has a warner. In Surah 40: The Forgiver (Al-Ghaafir), the messengers bring clear proofs, face disbelief and mockery, and receive Allah's help along with believers. In Surah 41: Explained in detail (Fussilat): The messengers before Muhammad also received statements like those given to him. In Surah 43: Ornaments of gold (Az-Zukhruf), prophets are sent among earlier peoples who mock them, and earlier messengers affirm that Allah appointed no gods beside the Beneficent. In Surah 46: The Dunes (Al-Ahqaf), the messengers include earlier warners, and Muhammad is told to be patient like the stout-hearted among them. In Surah 64: Mutual Disillusion (At-Taghaabun), Allah's messengers bring clear proofs of Allah's sovereignty and convey the message plainly. In Surah 65: Divorce (At-Talaaq): Communities that revolt against the ordinance of Allah and His messengers are called to a stern account and punished with dire punishment.

messengers in Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where messengers first appears

Abstract written by a model

Messengers are messengers of Allah, sent to mankind with good cheer and warning. No messenger is sent except to be obeyed by Allah’s leave, and believers in Allah and the Last Day obey them by that leave. Their coming leaves mankind with no argument against Allah, whose might and wisdom frame their mission. People are told to believe in Allah’s messengers, while disbelievers may wrongly distinguish among them by believing in some and disbelieving in others. Those who believe in Allah and all His messengers without making distinctions are promised their wages from Allah, who is Forgiving and Merciful. The account closes by presenting belief in the messenger who has come with Truth from the Lord as better for mankind, while affirming that Allah possesses all that is in the heavens and earth and is Knower and Wise.

the prophets in Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the prophets first appears

Abstract written by a model

The prophets are people whom Allah has shown favour and to whom He inspires revelation. They belong to the best company alongside those who obey Allah and the messenger. The records also place them within a wider account of people receiving clear proofs, forgiveness, authority, disbelief, wrongdoing, and guidance. Most gravely, some prophets were slain wrongfully.
