almsgiving

Also called: Almsgiving, Give, Kindness, alms, almsgiving and doing good, charity, do good, generosity, generosity and almsgiving, good deeds, good thing ye spend, kindness, spend

Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, almsgiving is charitable giving commanded as a way to keep the Most High's commandments and glorify the Lord, rewarding the godly and lowly while being withheld from sinners and the ungodly, profiting more than gold, delivering from affliction, fighting enemies, enduring forever, and rescuing in affliction. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), almsgiving is a good deed for Allah’s pleasure that helps the poor, with multiplied reward, while injury, reproach, ostentation, and inferior gifts diminish or invalidate it.

almsgiving in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where almsgiving first appears

Abstract written by a model

Almsgiving is a recurring theme of charitable giving, presented as a commanded act of keeping the Most High's commandments and glorifying the Lord with generosity, equivalent to a thank offering. Early, it is to be given knowingly to the godly and lowly, earning thanks and reward, and withheld from sinners and the ungodly. Next, it is said to profit more than gold and, stored up, to deliver from affliction and fight enemies better than shield or spear. Finally, it endures forever and rescues in affliction better than relatives and helpers.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 12

Doing good is to be done knowingly, toward the godly and lowly, and it then earns thanks and reward. The text says no good comes to one who continues in evil or who gives no alms. Giving is withheld from the sinner and ungodly man, whom it would not benefit and who would repay it with evil.

Chapter 29

Almsgiving is commended as keeping the commandments of the Most High, and it is said to profit more than gold. Stored up in one's store-chambers, it will deliver from all affliction and fight against an enemy better than a mighty shield and a ponderous spear.

Chapter 40

Almsgiving is said to endure forever, and kindness is likened to a garden of blessings. It is also said to rescue in time of affliction better than relatives and helpers.

Abstract written by a model

Almsgiving is a good deed for Allah’s pleasure, encouraged through giving good things to help the poor. Allah multiplies its reward, while injury, reproach, ostentation, and inferior gifts diminish or invalidate it.
