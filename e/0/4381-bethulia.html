Abstract written by a model

Bethulia is a hill-country city on a mountain, inhabited by the children of Israel and ruled by Ozias, Chabris, and Charmis. It has gates guarded by watchmen, and springs flow from the foot of the mountain beneath it. Its men first repel Holofernes' servants with slingers and receive the freed Achior in assembly. The enemy host then surrounds the city and seizes the springs and ascents. Over thirty-four days the cisterns empty and people collapse from thirst. The leaders swear to surrender after five days, but Judith, the city's widow of Manasses, warns that its fall would mean the capture of all Judea and the plundering of the sanctuary. Ozias and the elders open the gate for her and her maid, and the men watch her descend the mountain and cross the valley. She returns, calls the watchmen to open the gates, and the people run together, light a fire, worship God, and bless her after she shows the head. The enemy then flee, and Bethulia's people plunder the camp of Asshur, becoming exceedingly rich.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

Bethulia is the city whose men, seeing Holofernes' servants on the hilltop, take up weapons and use slingers to keep them from coming up. It receives the freed Achior and gathers its elders, young men, and women in assembly, led by the rulers Ozias, Chabris, and Charmis.

Chapter 7

Bethulia is surrounded by the enemy host, which seizes the springs below it and the ascents to it. Over thirty-four days its cisterns empty and its people collapse in the streets and gate passages from thirst. It is brought very low, with its walls and towers still manned.

Chapter 8

Bethulia is the city of Judith and of her late husband Manasses, whose people are fainting from lack of water. Its leaders have sworn to surrender it to the Assyrians after five days, and Judith warns that its capture would mean the capture of all Judea and the plundering of the sanctuary.

Chapter 10

Bethulia is a city with a gate, at which Ozias and the elders Chabris and Charmis stand. They open it for Judith and her maid, and the men of the city watch her until she goes down the mountain and passes the valley out of sight.

Chapter 13

Judith and her maid go up the mountain to Bethulia and reach its gates, where Judith calls to the watchmen to open. The men of the city hurry down, call the elders, and everyone, small and great, runs together, finding her return unbelievable. They open the gate, light a fire, and surround the women, then worship God and bless Judith after she shows the head.

Chapter 15

The enemy who had encamped in the hill country round about Bethulia flee away. The rest of the people who lived at Bethulia fall upon the camp of Asshur and plunder it, becoming exceedingly enriched.
