Abstract written by a model

Job's integrity and oath of innocence is his steadfast claim to blamelessness. In Chapter 27 he vows never to abandon his integrity or righteousness while he lives, and his heart will not reproach him. In Chapter 31 he swears a long series of conditional oaths denying lust, deceit, adultery, neglect of the poor, idolatry and more, accepting curses if guilty, signing his statement and offering to wear any indictment as a crown.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 27

Job declares that until he dies he will not put away his integrity and will hold fast to his righteousness without letting it go. He says his heart will not reproach him as long as he lives.

Chapter 31

Job swears a long series of conditional oaths ("if I have...") denying lust, deceit, adultery, mistreatment of servants, neglect of the poor, trust in wealth, idolatry of the sun and moon, gloating over an enemy, inhospitality, concealed sin, and exploiting his land. Each is paired with a curse he accepts if guilty, and he signs his statement and offers to bear any indictment as a crown. This forms his final assertion that he is blameless.
