justice of man before God

topic · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: be just with God, clean, human justice before God, just with God, justice, matter of strength

Abstract written by a model

The question of how a man can be just before God is raised in two chapters. In Chapter 9, Job asks it and concludes contending with God is hopeless: no one could answer once in a thousand times, his own mouth would condemn him even if blameless, God is mighty and cannot be summoned, no umpire exists, and washing would leave him in the ditch. In Chapter 25, Bildad repeats the question, implying man cannot be clean, being a worm.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

Job opens by asking how man can be just with God, and concludes it is hopeless to contend with him. A person could not answer God once in a thousand times, and even if righteous or blameless, Job says his own mouth would condemn him and prove him perverse. Job argues that if the matter is one of strength, God is mighty, and if of justice, no one can summon him. He says there is no umpire to lay a hand on both parties, and that even washing with snow and lye would leave him plunged into the ditch.

Chapter 25

Bildad questions whether man can be just with God, and whether one born of a woman can be clean. The implied answer is that he cannot, since even the moon and stars are not pure in God's sight and man is a worm.
