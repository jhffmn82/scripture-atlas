Esther

Also called: Esther the blameless consort of our kingdom, Esther the queen, Queen Esther, Queen Esther the daughter of Aminadab, This lady, queen, queen Esther, the lady, the queen, your handmaid

Abstract written by a model

Esther is a beautiful orphan, daughter of Aminadab and foster daughter of Mordecai, who becomes queen of King Ahasuerus, replacing Vashti. Brought into the care of Hegai, keeper of the women, she receives purification items and seven maidens. Following Mordecai's charge, she conceals her family and kindred, keeps her manner of life, and fears God. She enters the king in the twelfth month of his seventh year, wins his love beyond all other virgins, and is crowned. She later reports Mordecai's discovery of a conspiracy. When her people, the Jews, are threatened, she is troubled, notes that entering uncalled means death and that she has not been called for thirty days, then resolves to go in despite the law. She asks the Jews to fast, humbles herself in ashes and mourning, and prays to the Lord, her only helper, professing that she abhors her royal crown and has not eaten at Haman's table. On the third day she goes with two maids to the king, faints, is comforted, and invites the king and Haman to two banquets. At the second she asks for her life and her people's life, names Haman as the enemy, and he begs her for mercy. She receives Haman's possessions, appoints Mordecai over them, pleads to reverse Haman's letters, and is authorized to write in the king's name. She asks that the Jews may act the next day and that Haman's sons be hanged. With Mordecai she establishes Purim forever. In Mordecai's interpretation, she is the little spring that became a river, the woman the king married and made queen.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Esther, the foster daughter of Mordecai and daughter of Aminadab, was beautiful and was brought to Hegai, who favored her and gave her purification items and seven maidens. Following Mordecai's command she told no one her family or country and kept her manner of life. She went in to the king in the twelfth month, the seventh year of his reign, won his love beyond all the other virgins, and was crowned queen; she later reported Mordecai's discovery of the conspiracy to the king.

Chapter 4

Esther is deeply troubled by the news, sends clothes to Mordecai, and dispatches Hathach to learn the truth. She first points out that entering uncalled means death, and she has not been called for thirty days, but after Mordecai's reply she resolves to go in to the king contrary to the law, even if she must die. She asks the Jews to fast for her, then humbles herself in mourning garments and ashes and prays that the Lord help her, asking for harmonious speech before the lion and professing that she hates the glory of transgressors and has not eaten at Haman's table.

Chapter 5

Esther ceases praying on the third day, puts on her glorious apparel, and goes with two maids before the king, fearful in heart but beautiful in appearance. She faints before him, is comforted, and tells him she saw him as an angel of God. She invites the king and Haman to a feast, and at the banquet she defers her request by asking them to come again the next day.

Chapter 7

At the second banquet, Esther asks the king to grant her life and her people's life, saying they have been sold for destruction, pillage, and genocide. She names Haman as the enemy, and Haman then begs her for mercy.

Chapter 8

Esther receives all that belonged to Haman, appoints Mordecai over it, and again pleads with the king, falling at his feet, to reverse Haman's letters. She says she cannot bear to see the affliction of her people and kindred. The king authorizes her to write in his name.

Chapter 9

Esther, asked by the king what more she wanted, requested that the Jews be allowed to do the same the next day and that Haman's sons' bodies be hanged. She is called the daughter of Aminadab. With Mordecai she wrote and confirmed the letter about Purim and established it by a command forever.

Chapter 10

In Mordecai's interpretation, Esther is the little spring that became a river, the woman whom the king married and made queen.
