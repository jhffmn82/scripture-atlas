Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Egypt is a refuge and land of abundance where Joseph rises to authority and Jacob's family settles. In Exodus, Egypt is the place of Israel's affliction and the stage for the plagues and their release. In Numbers, Egypt is the place the people wish to return to despite the LORD's signs worked there. In Deuteronomy, Egypt is Israel's house of bondage from which the LORD delivers them with signs and wonders. In Psalms, Egypt is the land of Ham where God sends plagues against Pharaoh before Israel departs. In The Book of Isaiah, Egypt is an unreliable refuge that faces judgment before later receiving healing and blessing. In The Book of Jeremiah, Egypt is a refuge Israel wrongly seeks and a nation punished by Nebuchadnezzar. In The Book of Ezekiel, Egypt is judged, its pride humbled, its people scattered, and its land later restored.

and 7 more works

In The Book of Hosea, Egypt is a refuge, a place of derision and burial, and the origin from which Israel was called. In The Book of Zechariah, Egypt is a nation from which the LORD brings back his people and whose scepter departs. In The Gospel According to Matthew, Egypt is where Joseph and Mary shelter the child Jesus from Herod until his return. In The Acts of the Apostles, Egypt is where Joseph governs, Jacob's family settles, Moses grows up, and Israel is oppressed before departing. In Surah 10: Jonas (Yunus), Egypt is where Moses and Aaron are sent to Pharaoh and where houses are appointed for worship. In Surah 12: Joseph (Yusuf), Egypt is where Joseph is purchased, established, gains authority, and receives his family. In Surah 12: Joseph (Yusuf), the land of Egypt is where Allah establishes Joseph and entrusts him with its storehouses.

Egypt in Genesis

author unknown · about 449 BC · the work’s page · read it where Egypt first appears

Abstract written by a model

Egypt is a land ruled by Pharaoh, described as good and fat, with grain available for purchase and a region called Goshen. Its people regard shepherds as an abomination. It first appears as a refuge: Abram lives there as a foreigner during a severe famine, Sarai is taken into Pharaoh's house, which is plagued, and Abram is sent away with his wife and possessions. Later Joseph is brought there by merchants and sold to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh. After Pharaoh's magicians fail to interpret his dreams, Joseph is set over the whole land. It produces abundantly for seven years, stores food, then endures famine yet has bread, and people from all countries come to buy. Jacob sends his sons to buy grain; Joseph rules and reveals himself. Pharaoh offers the best land, and Jacob's household of seventy comes, settling in Goshen. As famine continues, all land except the priests' becomes Pharaoh's, with a fifth owed to him, and Jacob's family multiplies exceedingly.

Egypt in Exodus

author unknown · about 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Egypt first appears

Abstract written by a model

Egypt is a land on the Nile, described as the house of bondage, where the Israelites multiplied, were feared by a new king, and built the storage cities Pithom and Raamses for Pharaoh. Joseph was already there and Jacob's household came to join him. What matters most is that Egypt is the place of Israel's affliction and the stage for God's wonders leading to their release. The account unfolds as God announces he will bring his people out and strike Egypt with all his wonders. Moses, whose brothers remain there, returns after the men seeking his life are dead. Signs follow: the waters turn to blood; frogs, lice and flies afflict the land, though its magicians can imitate only the frogs; livestock pestilence, boils, and a hail with lightning worse than any since it became a nation strike it, while Goshen alone is spared. Locusts devour what the hail left, and thick darkness covers the land for three days. Pharaoh's servants call Egypt destroyed. The final plague comes at midnight, when all firstborn die, leaving no house without a dead, and an unprecedented great cry rises throughout the land. After Pharaoh stubbornly refused to release them, Israel leaves after 430 years, setting out from Rameses, brought out by the LORD's strength of hand.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

10 sections; each heading opens the text

Exodus / Chapter 1

Egypt is where Joseph already was and where the sons of Israel came with Jacob's household. Its land was filled with the multiplying Israelites, and its new king viewed them as a threat. Israelites built the storage cities Pithom and Raamses for Pharaoh there.

Chapter 3

Egypt is the land where God's people are afflicted and from which God will bring them up. God says he will strike it with all his wonders, after which its king will let the people go.

Chapter 4

Egypt is where Moses' brothers, the Israelites, remain, and where he asks Jethro to let him return to see if they are alive. The LORD tells him to go back because the men who sought his life are dead, and Moses travels there with his wife and sons.

Chapter 7

Egypt is the land where the LORD will stretch out his hand and multiply his signs and wonders. Its waters, including rivers, streams, pools and ponds, turn to blood, and blood is throughout the whole land, even in vessels of wood and stone.

Chapter 8

The land of Egypt is covered with frogs, which die in heaps and make the land stink, then is afflicted with lice on man and animal, and finally corrupted by swarms of flies in the houses of Pharaoh and his servants. Its magicians can imitate the frogs but not the lice, and the Egyptians are said to hold certain sacrifices as an abomination.

Chapter 9

The land of Egypt is struck by the livestock pestilence, boils throughout the land, and a hail with lightning worse than any since it became a nation. The hail breaks every tree and every herb of the field. Only Goshen is spared.

Chapter 10

Egypt is described by Pharaoh's servants as destroyed, and is covered by locusts that eat everything the hail left, leaving nothing green. It is then covered in thick darkness for three days, and the Egyptians do not see one another or rise from their places.

Chapter 11

Egypt is to suffer one more plague, in which the LORD goes into its midst at midnight and all its firstborn die. A great cry, unlike any before or after, will rise throughout the land.

Chapter 12

Egypt is the land where the LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron and where he strikes all the firstborn, so that there is not a house without one dead. It is the land Israel leaves after 430 years, setting out from Rameses.

Chapter 13

Egypt is described as the house of bondage from which the LORD brought Israel out by strength of hand. The LORD killed all its firstborn, of man and of livestock, after Pharaoh stubbornly refused to release the people.

Abstract written by a model

Egypt is a land ruled by Pharaoh, remembered chiefly as Israel's house of bondage. Its irrigated-by-foot soil is contrasted with the rain-fed promised land of Canaan. Israel's forefather, a Syrian ready to perish, went down there, few in number, and grew into a great, populous nation. The Egyptians then mistreated and afflicted them with hard labor. The LORD brought the people out with a mighty hand, signs, and wonders against Pharaoh and his land, and the Red Sea overflowed its army, horses, and chariots as they pursued. Egypt also stands for feared boils and diseases. The account ends in warning: the LORD will bring the Israelites back in ships as slaves whom nobody will buy.

Egypt in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · 739 to 680 BC · the work’s page · read it where Egypt first appears

Abstract written by a model

Egypt is a land ruled by Pharaoh and described as under a cruel lord, whom the LORD gives it over to. Across the records it is chiefly an unreliable source of refuge. Judah goes down to it for help, trusting its horses, chariots and horsemen, but the LORD calls its help vain, names it Rahab who sits still, and warns of confusion and woe. Rabshakeh likens it to a bruised reed that pierces the hand leaning on it. It is also called a glory in which people hoped, and its captives are to be led away shamed by Assyria. Egypt faces judgment: idols tremble, its heart melts, waters dry up, and its counsel fails. Afterward it is healed, receives a savior, an altar and a pillar to the LORD, is called 'my people,' and is joined with Assyria and Israel. It also serves as the past exodus comparison for a new highway recovering the remnant.

Egypt in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · 629 to 579 BC · the work’s page · read it where Egypt first appears

Abstract written by a model

Egypt is a land ruled by Pharaoh (Hophra), containing Memphis and Tahpanhes, allied with Cush, Put and Ludim, and called an uncircumcised nation. Early on it is the land the LORD brought Israel out of, whose Memphis and Tahpanhes broke Israel's crown; Israel seeks help there and will be ashamed of it, as of Assyria. After Gedaliah's killing, Johanan and the remnant, fearing the Chaldeans, plan to flee there, believing it free of war, trumpet and hunger. The LORD, through Jeremiah, warns 'Don't go into Egypt!' since sword, famine and pestilence will come instead. The remnant goes anyway, in disobedience, and keeps burning incense to other gods. The LORD declares Nebuchadnezzar will strike Egypt, killing or capturing people, burning and carrying off the gods' houses and breaking Beth Shemesh's pillars, and that those dwelling there will be punished as Jerusalem was. Pharaoh Hophra will be given to his enemies. Egypt boasts of rising like the Nile and is likened to a beautiful heifer, yet is incurable, with medicines used in vain. Memphis becomes desolate and Egypt is delivered to Nebuchadnezzar, but afterwards it will be inhabited as in days of old.

Egypt in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · about 592 to 570 BC · the work’s page · read it where Egypt first appears

Abstract written by a model

Egypt is a land and nation, chiefly a place, whose story runs from Israel's past there to its own judgment. Early on, it is where the LORD made himself known to Israel and swore to bring them out to a good land; Israel did not forsake Egypt's idols, yet the LORD acted for his name's sake and led them out. It is also where two sisters first played the prostitute in youth, and Oholibah later lusts after the Egyptians until the LORD says she will stop remembering Egypt. Egypt is called a staff of reed to the house of Israel. The weight of the records falls on judgment: a sword cuts off man and animal, leaving desolation and waste from Seveneh to the border of Ethiopia. Its allies Ethiopia, Put, Lud, Cub and mixed peoples fall with it, and its cities, including Memphis, Pathros, Zoan, No, Sin and Tehaphnehes, are struck. Its pride is brought to nothing, its people scattered, and it is given to Nebuchadnezzar as wages for his army. It is later restored in Pathros as the lowest of kingdoms, and its waters run clear, so that they know the LORD is the LORD.

Chapter 32

Egypt is to be made desolate and waste, with its multitude, pride, and animals destroyed by the sword of the king of Babylon and the mighty ruthless of the nations. Its waters will later run clear like oil, and its multitude is to be cast down to the lower parts of the earth with those who go down into the pit. Through this, they will know that the LORD is the LORD.
