Micaiah the son of Imlah in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Micaiah the son of Imlah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Micaiah, son of Imlah, is a prophet of the LORD whom Ahab hates for prophesying evil. Asked by Jehoshaphat, he first echoes others, then truthfully foretells Israel's scattering and the lying spirit; he is struck, jailed, and stakes his prophecy on Ahab's fate.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 22

Micaiah is the one prophet of the LORD whom Jehoshaphat asks for; Ahab hates him because he prophesies evil. He first echoes the other prophets, then, pressed to tell the truth, foretells Israel scattered like shepherdless sheep and describes the heavenly council and the lying spirit. He is struck by Zedekiah, jailed on bread and water, and declares that if Ahab returns in peace the LORD has not spoken by him.

Micaiah in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Micaiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Micaiah, son of Imla and Uriel's child, is a prophet of the LORD whom Ahab hates for habitually prophesying evil. Urged to agree with other prophets, he swears to speak only God's word, mocks then truthfully warns of scattered Israel and a lying spirit, is struck by Zedekiah, imprisoned, and stakes his prophecy on Ahab's fate.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

Micaiah son of Imla was the one prophet Ahab hated for always speaking evil about him. Warned to match the other prophets, he swore to say only what God says; he first told Ahab to go up and prosper, then, when adjured to tell the truth, described Israel scattered like shepherdless sheep and a vision of the LORD sending a lying spirit into the prophets. He was struck by Zedekiah and ordered imprisoned, and he answered that if Ahab returned in peace the LORD had not spoken by him.
