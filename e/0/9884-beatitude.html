Beatitude

topic · 1 work · 8 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: beatitude, final liberation, highest good, liberation, perfection

Abstract written by a model

Beatitude is the highest good and final goal, also called liberation or perfection. It is reached through religious life, disciplined conduct, restraint, a pure soul, renunciation of fetters, destruction of Karman, and enduring hardships while wandering. A monk pursues it by overcoming pleasures, passions, greed, and attachment, following the Law and rules of conduct until crossing Samsâra. It is not obtained through possession, worldly undertakings, uncontrolled conduct, bathing, contact with water, or tending fire; claims that such practices liberate are refuted. Presented as the best thing, like the moon among the stars, Beatitude is brought about by a restrained sage who subdues the senses and is reached by those who avoid approval or disapproval of disputed gifts.

Beatitude is in one work on this site, Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I / First Book. / First Lecture, Called the Doctrine. / First Chapter. .. Fourth Chapter.

Perfection is not reached through possession, worldly undertakings, uncontrolled conduct, or attachment. A monk should remain restrained, free from greed and passions, and live according to the rules of conduct until perfection is reached.

Second Lecture, Called the Destruction of Karman. / First Chapter. .. Third Chapter.

Liberation is the highest good and the far-off goal seen by the wise. It is approached by following the Law, overcoming pleasures and passions, destroying Karman, and crossing Samsâra.

Third Lecture, Called the Knowledge of Troubles / First Chapter. .. Fourth Chapter.

A wise and thoroughly restrained monk should bear all hardships and wander until he reaches final liberation. Those who cross the flood of Samsâra attain this goal by renouncing fetters.

Fourth Lecture, Called Knowledge of Women. / First Chapter. .. Second Chapter.

Final liberation is the destination toward which a monk with a pure soul should wander. He is to continue until he reaches it.

Seventh Lecture, Called Description of the Wicked.

Perfection is wrongly claimed to arise from abstaining from salt, using cold water, bathing, or tending fire. Such assertions are refuted because beings living in water and mechanics who use fire would otherwise be liberated.

Eleventh Lecture, Called the Path.

Beatitude is presented as the best thing, like the moon among the stars. A sage who is restrained and subdues his senses brings about Beatitude, and those who give neither approval nor disapproval of disputed gifts will reach it.
