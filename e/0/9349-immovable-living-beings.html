immovable living beings

group · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: immovable beings, immovable creatures, immovable ones

Abstract written by a model

Immovable living beings include trees, creepers, grass, herbs, plants, and other bodies where living beings arise and grow. Their bodies and humours feed other living beings. Their character is shaped by Karman; killing or injury is sinful and covered by complete renunciation. After life ends, they take a new form, movable or immovable.

Third Lecture, Called Knowledge of Food.

Immovable beings include trees, creepers, grass, herbs, plants, and other bodies in which living beings arise and grow. Their bodies and humours serve as food for other living beings.

Seventh Lecture, Called Nâlandâ.

Immovable beings have their character through the taking effect of Karman relating to immovable beings. They may leave immovable bodies and be reborn as movable or immovable beings, within or beyond specified limits. Gautama teaches that injury to immovable beings is also covered by complete renunciation.
