Abstract written by a model

Holofernes is the chief captain of Nebuchadnezzar's army, second in rank to the king and leader of the army of Asshur. He is commanded to go against the western country, sparing those who yield and slaying those who resist. He musters the army, leaves Nineveh and campaigns destructively to the sea coast. Receiving submissions, he sets garrisons in the high cities and takes chosen men as allies. Though welcomed with garlands, dances and timbrels, he casts down borders and cuts down sacred groves, tasked with destroying the gods of the land. He camps between Geba and Scythopolis for a month to gather baggage. The people of Judea hear he has destroyed the temples of the nations and are terrified. Told that Israel has fortified the hill country, he grows angry and questions Moab's princes, Ammon's captains and the coastal governors. After hearing Achior, he rebukes him, scorns Israel's God, declares Nebuchadnezzar the only god, and sends Achior to Bethulia to be destroyed with the Israelites. He moves against Bethulia, posts garrisons over its springs, and on the advice of the rulers of Esau, Moab and the coast chooses siege over battle. Judith then comes to him with words of truth. He receives her before his tent with silver lamps going before him, promises her safety, praises her beauty and wisdom, and says her God will be his God and she will live in Nebuchadnezzar's palace. He orders his guards not to stop her nightly outings. On the fourth day he holds a feast for his servants only and sends Bagoas to bring her, having long watched to deceive her. Ravished by her, he drinks more wine than ever before. Lying drunk on his bed under the canopy, with his sword at the bedpost, he is beheaded by Judith with his own sword, and she says the Lord struck him by a woman's hand. Bagoas finds him dead and headless on the threshold, and the camp falls into turmoil. Achior is brought to see the man who despised Israel. Later the song, without naming him, says the mighty enemy was made weak by Judith's beauty and struck through the neck. His belongings are dedicated to the Lord, and his canopy is given as a gift.

Holofernes in Judith

Narrative, section by section written by a model

12 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Holofernes, chief captain and second to the king, is commanded to go against the west country, take the coasts, spare those who yield and slay those who resist. He musters the army, leads it out from Nineveh, and conducts a campaign of destruction through many lands up to the sea coast.

Chapter 3

Holofernes receives the messengers' words of submission and comes down toward the sea coast with his army, setting garrisons in the high cities and taking chosen men as allies. He is received with garlands, dances and timbrels, yet casts down borders and cuts down sacred groves, having been given the task of destroying all the gods of the land. He then advances toward Esdraelon near Dotaea, opposite the ridge of Judea, and camps between Geba and Scythopolis for a month to gather his army's baggage.

Chapter 4

Holofernes is identified as the chief captain of Nebuchadnezzar king of the Assyrians. The people of Judea hear that he has plundered and utterly destroyed the temples of the nations, and his approach terrifies them.

Chapter 5

Holofernes, chief captain of the army of Asshur, is told that Israel has fortified the hill country and prepared for war, and he becomes exceedingly angry. He questions the princes of Moab, the captains of Ammon, and the governors of the sea coast about the people, their cities, army, strength, and king. He hears Achior's advice, and his great men urge attacking, calling Israel prey to be devoured by his army.

Chapter 6

Holofernes, chief captain of Asshur's army, rebukes Achior before the assembled people, scorns Israel's God, and asserts that Nebuchadnezzar is the only god and that Israel will perish. He sentences Achior to be delivered to the Israelites so that he will be destroyed with them, and commands his servants to take him to Bethulia.

Chapter 7

Holofernes commands his army and allies to move against Bethulia and seize the passes of the hill country. He rides out with his cavalry to view the ascents and the springs, posts garrisons over the springs, and then accepts the advice of the rulers of Esau, Moab and the sea coast to besiege the city rather than fight, ordering them to do as they proposed.

Chapter 10

Holofernes is called the chief captain of the Assyrian army, and Judith says she is coming to him with words of truth. He is resting on a bed under a canopy woven with purple, gold, emeralds, and precious stones when told of her arrival. He comes out before his tent with silver lamps going before him and receives Judith.

Chapter 11

Holofernes tells Judith to take courage and promises that anyone who serves Nebuchadnezzar will not be harmed, blaming her people for the attack on them. He is pleased by her words, praises her beauty and wisdom, and promises that if she does as she says, her God will be his God and she will live in Nebuchadnezzar's palace.

Chapter 12

Holofernes offers Judith his own delicacies and wine, orders his guards not to stop her nightly outings, and on the fourth day holds a feast for his servants only. He sends Bagoas to bring her, is ravished by her and had been watching for a time to deceive her since first seeing her. At the feast he delights in her and drinks more wine than on any previous day of his life.

Chapter 13

Holofernes lies drunk on his bed in the tent after the long feast, with his servants gone. Judith cuts off his head with his own sword and tumbles his body from the bed. She calls him the chief captain of the army of Asshur and says the Lord struck him by the hand of a woman, and that he did not sin with her.

Chapter 14

Holofernes is found dead by Bagoas, cast upon the threshold of the bedchamber with his head taken from him. The Assyrian rulers are told he lies on the ground headless, and the camp is thrown into turmoil. Achior is brought to see the man who despised Israel and sent him to the Israelites as if to his death.

Chapter 16

Holofernes is not named in the song, but the text calls the enemy's mighty one one who fell not by young men, Titans, or giants but was made weak by Judith's beauty and struck through the neck with the sword. Afterward his belongings, given to Judith by the people, are dedicated to the Lord, and his canopy, taken from his bedchamber, is given as a gift.
