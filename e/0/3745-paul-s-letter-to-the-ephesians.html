This document is Paul's Letter to the Ephesians, six chapters written by Paul, an apostle and prisoner of Christ Jesus, to the saints at Ephesus. It moves from what God has done in Christ to how believers should live, ending with spiritual warfare and a personal close. Chapter 1 greets the readers and blesses God for choosing believers before the world's foundation, predestining them for adoption, redeeming them through Christ's blood, and sealing them with the Holy Spirit. Paul prays that they receive wisdom and revelation, and describes the power that raised Christ, seated him at God's right hand, and made him head over all things for the assembly, his body. Chapter 2 says God, rich in mercy, made the readers alive with Christ when they were dead in sins. They are saved by grace through faith, not works, and created for good works. Gentiles, once alienated from Israel, are brought near by Christ's blood. Christ is their peace, breaking down the dividing wall and making one new man of two. They become fellow citizens, God's household, and a holy temple built on Christ as chief cornerstone. Chapter 3 has Paul explain the mystery of Christ, revealed to him: Gentiles are fellow heirs, members of the body, and partakers of the promise. He is a servant of the Good News, making God's manifold wisdom known through the assembly. He prays that Christ dwell in their hearts, that they know his love and be filled with God's fullness, and closes with a doxology. Chapter 4 turns practical. Paul urges unity in one body, one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, and one God. Christ gave apostles, prophets, evangelists, shepherds and teachers to equip the saints and build up the body. Readers should put off the old man and put on the new, speak truth, avoid corrupt speech, not grieve the Holy Spirit, and forgive as God forgave them. Chapter 5 calls them to imitate God and walk in love, avoid immorality and covetousness, live as light, be filled with the Spirit, and follow household instructions for wives and husbands, linking marriage to Christ and the assembly. Chapter 6 continues with instructions for children, fathers, servants, and masters, then urges readers to put on the whole armor of God against the devil and spiritual forces, and to pray for all the saints. Paul asks for prayer to speak boldly as an ambassador in chains, announces that Tychicus is being sent to report and comfort them, and ends with blessings of peace, love, faith, and grace.

Paul’s Letter to the Ephesians / Chapter 1

Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus by God's will, greets the saints at Ephesus and the faithful in Christ Jesus with grace and peace. He blesses God the Father for choosing believers before the world's foundation, predestining them for adoption, and granting redemption and forgiveness through Christ's blood. God has made known the mystery of his will: to sum up all things in Christ, and believers have received an inheritance and have been sealed with the promised Holy Spirit as a pledge. Paul says he gives thanks and prays for the Ephesians because of their faith in the Lord Jesus and love for all the saints, asking that God give them a spirit of wisdom and revelation. He describes God's power as the power that raised Christ from the dead and seated him at God's right hand, far above every rule and name, with all things under his feet and him as head over all things for the assembly, which is his body.

Chapter 2

Chapter 2 tells the readers that God, rich in mercy and love, made them alive together with Christ when they were dead in transgressions and sins, raised them up and seated them with him in the heavenly places. It says they are saved by grace through faith, not by works, and are God's workmanship created in Christ Jesus for good works. It then reminds the Gentiles that they were once separate from Christ, alienated from Israel and without hope, but are now brought near by the blood of Christ. Christ is described as their peace, who broke down the middle wall, abolished the hostility, made one new man of two, and reconciled both to God through the cross. The Gentiles are now fellow citizens and members of God's household, built on the apostles and prophets with Christ Jesus as chief cornerstone, growing into a holy temple and habitation of God in the Spirit.

Chapter 3

Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus on behalf of the Gentiles, explains that by revelation God made known to him the mystery of Christ, which was hidden in past generations but is now revealed to the apostles and prophets. The mystery is that the Gentiles are fellow heirs, members of the body, and partakers of the promise in Christ Jesus through the Good News. Paul was made a servant of this Good News by God's grace, to preach the unsearchable riches of Christ and, through the assembly, make God's manifold wisdom known to the principalities and powers in the heavenly places. He asks the readers not to lose heart at his troubles, then bows his knees to the Father and prays that they be strengthened, that Christ dwell in their hearts, that they know Christ's love, and be filled with the fullness of God. The chapter ends with a doxology giving glory to God in the assembly and in Christ Jesus forever.

Chapter 4

In this chapter the prisoner in the Lord urges the readers to walk worthily of their calling with humility, patience, and love, keeping the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace, since there is one body, one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, and one God and Father of all. Christ, who descended and ascended above all the heavens, gave grace and gifts, including apostles, prophets, evangelists, and shepherds and teachers, to equip the saints and build up the body until all attain unity and the full stature of Christ. The readers are told to stop being children tossed by false doctrine, to speak truth in love, and to grow into Christ the head. They are also told not to walk as the Gentiles do, darkened and hardened in heart, but to put away the old man, be renewed in mind, and put on the new man created in righteousness and holiness. Practical commands follow: speak truth, be angry without sinning, stop stealing and labor, avoid corrupt speech, not grieve the Holy Spirit, put away bitterness and malice, and forgive as God in Christ forgave them.

Chapter 5

The chapter urges its readers to imitate God and walk in love as Christ loved and gave himself up for them. It warns against sexual immorality, uncleanness, covetousness, filthy talk and deceptive words, saying such people have no inheritance in the Kingdom of Christ and God and that God's wrath comes on the disobedient. It contrasts the readers' former darkness with their present life as light in the Lord, calling them to reprove the deeds of darkness, wake from sleep, live wisely, and be filled with the Spirit rather than drunk with wine. It then gives household instructions: wives are to be subject to their husbands as the assembly is to Christ, and husbands are to love their wives as Christ loved the assembly, sanctifying her and presenting her holy. The chapter closes by tying marriage to the mystery of Christ and the assembly and telling each husband to love his wife and each wife to respect her husband.

Chapter 6

Chapter 6 first gives household instructions: children are to obey and honor their parents, fathers are not to provoke their children, servants are to serve their masters sincerely as to Christ, and masters are to stop threatening because they share a Master in heaven who shows no partiality. It then urges readers to be strong in the Lord and put on the whole armor of God to stand against the devil's wiles and the spiritual forces of wickedness. The armor consists of the belt of truth, breastplate of righteousness, preparation of the Good News of peace on the feet, shield of faith, helmet of salvation, and sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God, along with prayer for all the saints. Paul asks for prayer that he may boldly make known the mystery of the Good News as an ambassador in chains. He says he is sending Tychicus to report on his affairs and comfort the readers, and closes with blessings of peace, love, faith, and grace.
