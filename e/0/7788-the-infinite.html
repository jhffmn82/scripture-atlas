the Infinite

topic · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: immortal, immortality, the infinite

Abstract written by a model

The Infinite is the ultimate state or origin to which a duly nourished soul may return in beatified form. It bounds life at each end and is associated with TAO. By discarding body and mind, Yen Hui becomes one with it, losing bias and becoming without beginning or end.

the Infinite is in one work on this site, Chuang Tzŭ. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

Immortality is presented as a possible state for the soul when it is duly nourished and not exhausted by the body’s mortal pursuits. The immortal soul may return beatified to the Great Unknown from which it came.

CHAPTER VI.

Immortality bounds life at each end, though this is not truly known until death. The soul finally reverts to an immortality associated with TAO.

Nan Po Tzŭ K'uei

The Infinite is what Yen Hui becomes one with after discarding body and mind. Confucius says that attaining this state removes bias and makes one without beginning or end.
