Abstract written by a model

Israel's demand for a king, made by the elders at Ramah citing Samuel's age and his sons' conduct, displeased Samuel and was called a rejection of the LORD as king. The LORD still told Samuel to grant it. Later, Samuel links it to Nahash's attack, calls it great wickedness, and the people confess it; the LORD will not forsake them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

The elders made the request at Ramah, citing Samuel's age and his sons' conduct. It displeased Samuel, and the LORD said it was a rejection of himself as king. Despite the warnings, the people persisted, and the LORD told Samuel to grant it.

Chapter 12

Samuel says Israel demanded a king when Nahash king of the Ammonites came against them, though the LORD was their king. Samuel calls it great wickedness in the LORD's sight, and the people afterward confess it as an evil added to all their sins, yet Samuel says the LORD will not forsake them.
