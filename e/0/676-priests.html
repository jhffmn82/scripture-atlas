Abstract written by a model

In Deuteronomy, the Levitical priests minister before the LORD, give verdicts with the judge, and hold the law from which the king copies his book. In The Book of Joshua, the Levitical priests bear the LORD's ark of the covenant, lead Israel across the Jordan, and stand on dry ground until all Israel has crossed. In The Second Book of Chronicles, the priests sanctify themselves, bring burnt offerings, sprinkle the blood received from the Levites, praise the LORD, and bless the people. In The Second Book of Chronicles, the Levitical priests bring the ark into the most holy place, where a cloud prevents them from standing to minister. In The Book of Ezekiel, the sons of Zadok minister to the LORD, offer sacrifices, teach the people, judge, and receive the LORD as their inheritance. In The Book of Ezekiel, the priests receive a most holy offering with the sanctuary in its middle, bordering the Levites' portion. In The Book of Hosea, the priests reject knowledge and God's law, feed on the people's sin, and are punished. In The Book of Malachi, the priests are accused of despising the LORD's name, corrupting the covenant of Levi, and causing many to stumble.

sons of Zadok in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where sons of Zadok first appears

Abstract written by a model

The sons of Zadok are the Levitical priests, holy offspring of Zadok who stand near the LORD and minister to the God of Israel. Their defining distinction is faithfulness: they kept the duty of the sanctuary when Israel went astray, so they alone may come near to offer the fat and blood, enter the sanctuary, and approach the LORD's table. The records unfold from their conduct to their role. First, they eat the most holy things in the holy rooms and must not enter the outer court until they lay aside their holy ministering garments and put on other garments before approaching the people. Next, they receive a young bull for a sin offering, cast salt on offerings, and from the eighth day make the people's burnt and peace offerings. They are then commended for faithfulness, bound by regulations on dress, hair, wine, marriage, teaching, judging, and the dead, and they teach the people holy from common. Their inheritance is the LORD himself, with a holy portion of land for their houses and the sanctuary; a priest also applies sin offering blood to the door posts, altar ledge corners, and inner court gate posts.

Chapter 42

Priests who are near to the LORD eat the most holy things in the holy rooms. When they enter, they may not go out of the holy place into the outer court until they lay down the garments in which they minister, because those garments are holy. They then put on other garments before approaching what is for the people.

Chapter 43

The Levitical priests of the offspring of Zadok are described as those near to God who minister to him. They receive a young bull for a sin offering, and they cast salt on the offerings. On the eighth day and onward, they are to make the people's burnt offerings and peace offerings on the altar.

Chapter 44

The Levitical priests, the sons of Zadok, are commended for performing the duty of the sanctuary when Israel went astray. They alone may come near to minister to the LORD, offer the fat and blood, enter the sanctuary, and approach his table. They are bound by the regulations on dress, hair, wine, marriage, teaching, judging, and the dead, and receive offerings as their portion.

Chapter 45

The priests, who come near to minister to the LORD, receive a holy portion of the land as a place for their houses and for the sanctuary. A priest also puts the blood of the sin offering on the house's door posts, the corners of the altar ledge, and the gate posts of the inner court.

priests (the LORD's priests addressed) in The Book of Malachi

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where priests (the LORD's priests addressed) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The priests are the LORD's addressed messengers, whose lips should keep knowledge. In chapter 1 they are accused of despising his name by offering polluted bread and blind, lame, sick, or violently taken animals, and he refuses their offerings. In chapter 2 he warns of a curse on them and their blessings, rebuke of their offspring, and dung on their faces. They turned from the path, caused many to stumble, corrupted the covenant of Levi, and showed partiality in the law, so he made them contemptible and wicked before the people.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Malachi / Chapter 1

The priests are accused of despising the LORD's name by offering polluted bread on his altar and calling his table contemptible. They are also said to offer blind, lame, and sick animals, to sniff at the service as a weariness, and to bring what was taken by violence. The LORD says he has no pleasure in them and will not accept an offering from their hand.

Chapter 2

The priests are told that if they do not listen or take the commandment to heart, the LORD will curse them and their blessings, rebuke their offspring, and spread dung from their feasts on their faces. They have turned from the path, caused many to stumble in the law, corrupted the covenant of Levi, and shown respect for persons in the law. As a result the LORD has made them contemptible and wicked before all the people.
