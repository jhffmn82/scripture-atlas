Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Ezekiel, Daniel is named alongside Noah and Job as a righteous man who would deliver only his own soul by righteousness. In Daniel (Greek), Daniel is a Jewish youth and faithful servant of God who interprets dreams, survives the lions' den, receives visions, and performs acts of judgment and deliverance.

Daniel in Daniel (Greek)

author unknown · about 164 BC · the work’s page · read it where Daniel first appears

Abstract written by a model

Daniel is a Jewish youth of the captivity of Judah, taken to Babylon and renamed Belteshazzar. He is a faithful worshipper of the living God of heaven, who gives him understanding in all visions and dreams. The records open with him refusing the king's delicacies and wine to avoid defiling himself, proposing a ten-day test, and being found ten times better than the magicians and enchanters with his companions Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. He serves until the first year of Cyrus. Under Nebuchadnezzar he reveals and interprets the king's secret dream, crediting God rather than his own wisdom, and is rewarded with rule over Babylon's province and chief governorship over the wise men, while securing posts for Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. He later interprets the tree dream and counsels the king to break off his sins by righteousness and mercy to the poor. Under Belshazzar, recommended by the queen as having the spirit of the holy gods, he declines rewards, rebukes the king's pride, reads MENE, TEKEL, and PERES, and is proclaimed third ruler. Under Darius he is one of three presidents, distinguished by an excellent spirit and faithfulness; he keeps praying three times a day toward Jerusalem despite the decree, is cast into the lions' den, and is found unharmed because he trusted his God. He prospers under Darius and Cyrus the Persian. The second half centres on his own dreams and visions: the four animals and the horn in Belshazzar's first year; the vision at Susa by the Ulai in the third year, in which Gabriel frightens and then raises him; his understanding of Jeremiah's seventy years and his prayer of confession for himself and his people; and the great vision by the Hiddekel of a man clothed in linen. Gabriel and the man in linen call him greatly beloved. He is commanded to seal the book until the time of the end, and is promised that he will rest and stand in his inheritance at the end of the days. Additional episodes show him as a young youth raised up by God's holy spirit to rescue Susanna by exposing the elders' false testimony, gaining great reputation. Living with the king and honored above his friends, he refuses to worship Bel, destroys Bel and the dragon, is thrown into the lions' den again, is fed through Habakkuk, and is found alive on the seventh day and drawn out by the king.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

The Book of Daniel with Greek Portions / Chapter 1

Daniel was among the Judahite youths taken to Babylon and was renamed Belteshazzar. He purposed not to defile himself with the king's delicacies and wine, asked the prince of the eunuchs for an exemption, and proposed the ten-day test to the steward. God gave him understanding in all visions and dreams, and he continued serving until the first year of King Cyrus.

Chapter 2

Daniel, also called Belteshazzar, is sought for execution with the wise men but answers Arioch with counsel and prudence, asks the king for time, and enlists Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah to seek God's mercy. The secret is revealed to him in a night vision; he praises God, tells Nebuchadnezzar the dream and its interpretation while crediting God rather than his own wisdom, and is rewarded with gifts, rule over Babylon's province, and chief governorship over the wise men. He then secures posts for Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego while he remains in the king's gate.

Chapter 4

Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, comes in last before the king after the magicians, enchanters, Chaldeans, and soothsayers fail, and is described as having the spirit of the holy gods. He is stricken mute for a while on hearing the dream, then interprets it for the king, applying the tree to him and announcing the decree of the Most High. He also counsels Nebuchadnezzar to break off his sins by righteousness and his iniquities by showing mercy to the poor.

Chapter 5

Daniel is recommended by the queen as a man with the spirit of the holy gods, whom the king named Belteshazzar. He declines Belshazzar's rewards, rebukes the king for his pride, and interprets the writing as MENE, TEKEL, and PERES. He is then clothed with purple, given a gold chain, and proclaimed third ruler in the kingdom.

Chapter 6

Daniel is one of three presidents and is distinguished above the others because an excellent spirit is in him, and no fault can be found in him because he is faithful. He keeps praying three times a day despite the decree, is cast into the den of lions, and is found unharmed the next morning because he trusted his God. He prospers in the reigns of Darius and Cyrus the Persian.

Chapter 7

Daniel has a dream and visions in the first year of Belshazzar, writes them down, and tells the sum of the matters. Grieved and troubled, he asks one of those standing by for the truth and receives the interpretation, especially about the fourth animal and the horn. He ends greatly troubled, with his face changed, keeping the matter in his heart.

Chapter 8

Daniel receives a vision in the third year of King Belshazzar while at the citadel of Susa by the river Ulai. Frightened by Gabriel, he falls on his face and into a deep sleep, and is touched and set upright. He is told to seal up the vision, then faints, is sick for some days, returns to the king's business, and wonders at the vision, which no one understands.

Chapter 9

In Darius's first year Daniel understands from the books the seventy years of Jeremiah's prophecy and turns to God with prayer, fasting, sackcloth and ashes. He confesses his own sin and his people's, and is visited by Gabriel, who calls him greatly beloved and gives him understanding of the vision.

Chapter 10

Daniel, also called Belteshazzar, mourns for three weeks and then sees by the river Hiddekel a great vision of a man clothed in linen, which he alone sees. He falls on his face powerless, is touched and strengthened, and is told he is greatly beloved and that his words were heard from the first day he humbled himself to understand.

Chapter 12

Daniel is told to shut up the words and seal the book until the time of the end. He looks and sees two others on the river banks, hears the man clothed in linen swear an oath but does not understand it, and asks about the outcome. He is told to go his way, and that he will rest and stand in his inheritance at the end of the days.

Chapter 13

Daniel is a young youth whose holy spirit God raises up as Susanna is led to execution. He declares himself clear of her blood, rebukes the people for condemning her without examination, and examines the elders separately about the tree under which they saw the couple. Their contradictory answers prove the false testimony, and from that day Daniel has a great reputation among the people.

Chapter 14

Daniel lives with the king and is honored above his friends, but worships only the living God and refuses to worship Bel. He exposes the priests' deceit with ashes, destroys Bel and its temple, kills the dragon with lumps of pitch, fat, and hair, and is handed over to the Babylonians and thrown into the lions' den. He is fed by Habakkuk's delivered dinner, is found alive on the seventh day, and is drawn out by the king.
