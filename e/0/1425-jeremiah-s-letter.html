Abstract written by a model

Jeremiah's letter, carried to Babylon by Elasah and Gemariah, sent by Zedekiah to Nebuchadnezzar, addresses the captive elders, priests, prophets and people. It urges them to settle, multiply, seek Babylon's peace, ignore false prophets, and expect return; Shemaiah later cites it to rebuke Jeremiah.

Chapter 29

The letter is carried to Babylon by Elasah son of Shaphan and Gemariah son of Hilkiah, whom Zedekiah king of Judah sent to Nebuchadnezzar. It is addressed to the elders, priests, prophets, and all the people taken captive. Its message is to settle, multiply, seek Babylon's peace, and not heed false prophets, with a promise of return. Shemaiah later cites its words, "The captivity is long. Build houses," as grounds for rebuking Jeremiah.
