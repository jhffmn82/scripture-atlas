Abstract written by a model

Gong Sun Chou is a questioner of Mencius. He asks whether Mencius could attain royal dignity through Qi and whether king Wen is the proper model; then probes the unperturbed mind, valour, will, passion-nature, words, sages, and Confucius. He also challenges Mencius’s conduct concerning illness and court attendance, and asks whether ancient officeholders served without salary.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou) / Chapter 1

Gong Sun Chou asks Mencius whether he could reproduce the achievements of Guan Zhong and Yan by obtaining the government of Qi. He is perplexed by Mencius’s claim that royal dignity could be attained easily and asks whether king Wen should instead be the model.

Chapter 2

Gong Sun Chou asks Mencius about the unperturbed mind, valour, the will, the passion-nature, words, and Mencius's standing in relation to sages. He also asks how Confucius differed from Bo Yi and Yi Yin.

Chapter 2

Gong Sun Chou questions Mencius about declining to attend court because of illness while visiting someone in condolence the next day.

Chapter 14

Gong Sun Chou asked Mencius whether the ancients held office without receiving salary.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 31

Gong Sun Chou asks Mencius whether worthy ministers may banish sovereigns who are not virtuous, as Yi Yin banished Tai Jia.

Chapter 32

Gong Sun Chou asks Mencius why superior men appear to eat without labouring.

Chapter 39

He said that one whole year's mourning was better than doing away with mourning altogether. He asked Mencius about the case of the king's son.

Chapter 41

Gong Sun Chou says that Mencius's principles are lofty and admirable but may be impossible to reach. He asks why Mencius does not adapt his teaching to make the principles seem attainable and encourage learners to exert themselves daily.
