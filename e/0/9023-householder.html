Abstract written by a model

A householder is a giver and authority over possessions and dwelling-places in rules concerning mendicants. The householder may promise or give clothes, but clothes altered through prohibited washing, dyeing, perfuming, cleaning, rubbing, or other acts must be refused. The householder may give a permitted bowl, arrange its preparation, or ask a mendicant to return later, but must not provide one filled with cooked or prepared food or drink; cold water and food-filled bowls are refused. The householder or steward may grant possession of a dwelling-place and lend small implements that must be returned indirectly. Finally, if the householder kills beings or commits sins in preparing or using a place, informed monks or nuns must not ease nature there or in a similar place.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Fifth Lecture, Called Begging of Clothes. / First Lesson. .. Second Lesson.

A householder may give clothes to a monk or nun, but must not make them acceptable by washing, dyeing, perfuming, cleaning, rubbing, or otherwise altering them for the mendicant's sake. If the householder promises clothes later or offers clothes made impure by prohibited acts, the mendicant must refuse them.

Sixth Lecture, Called Begging for a Bowl. / First Lesson. .. Second Lesson.

A householder may give a monk or nun a permitted bowl, ask them to return later, or have a bowl washed or otherwise prepared. The householder must not cause a bowl to be filled with cooked or prepared food or drink for the mendicant, and an offered bowl of cold water or food-filled bowl must be refused.

Seventh Lecture, Called Regulation of Possession. / First Lesson. .. Second Lesson.

A householder or steward grants permission for mendicants to take possession of a dwelling-place. A householder may lend a mendicant a needle, Pippalaka, ear-picker, or nail-parer, which must be returned without being placed directly into the householder's hand.

Tenth Lecture.

A householder may kill living beings and commit various sins when preparing or using a place for fellow-ascetics or Sramanas or Brâhmanas. A monk or nun who knows this should not ease nature in that place or another of the same sort.
