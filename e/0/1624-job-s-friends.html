Abstract written by a model

Job's friends are the opposing circle of companions who answer his suffering with accusation rather than comfort. Across the chapters, Job characterizes them harshly: deceitful like brooks that vanish in the dry season, forgers of lies, physicians of no value, and miserable comforters whose words are vain. He sarcastically calls them the people with whom wisdom will die, while insisting he is not inferior to them. The central tension is that they persecute and torment him with words, reproach him repeatedly, scoff at him, and plan to wrong him, which Job claims to know from their thoughts. The account unfolds in stages. Early on, Job says a faint man deserves kindness and that they, seeing terror, are afraid and nothing. He then mocks their wisdom and tells them to learn from animals, birds, earth, and fish. Next he calls their speech unrighteous and partial toward God, warns that God will reprove them, and labels their sayings proverbs of ashes and defenses of clay. He says that were their places reversed he would rather strengthen them. Later he pleads for pity, warns them to fear the sword and judgment, and declares their comfort nonsense and answers falsehood. Finally he refuses ever to justify them, notes they have all seen God's dealings themselves, asks why they are altogether vain, and promises to teach them about God's hand.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

Job says that a faint man should be shown kindness by his friend, and he accuses his brothers of dealing deceitfully like brooks that vanish. He says they are now nothing, seeing a terror and being afraid, and that their reproof reproves nothing. He charges that they would cast lots for the fatherless and make merchandise of a friend, and he asks them to look at him, return, and let there be no injustice.

Chapter 12

Job addresses his friends as 'the people' with whom wisdom will die, mocking their claim to wisdom. He insists he is not inferior to them and that everyone knows the things they have said. He tells them to learn from the animals, birds, earth, and fish.

Chapter 13

Job's friends are called forgers of lies and physicians of no value, and he wishes they would be completely silent so that they would be wise. He accuses them of speaking unrighteously and deceitfully for God and showing partiality, warns that God will reprove them and make them afraid, and calls their sayings proverbs of ashes and their defenses defenses of clay.

Chapter 16

Job's friends are called miserable comforters who have spoken many such things and vain words. Job says that if their places were reversed he could join words against them and shake his head at them, but he would rather strengthen and relieve them. He says they scoff at him.

Chapter 19

Job's friends are accused of crushing him with words, reproaching him ten times, and shamelessly attacking him. Job asks them to have pity, asks why they persecute him as God does without being satisfied with his flesh, and warns them to fear the sword, since wrath brings its punishments and there is a judgment.

Chapter 21

Job's friends are told to listen, be astonished, and lay their hands on their mouths, and Job expects them to mock him after he speaks. Job says he knows their thoughts and plans to wrong him, quotes their assertion about the house of the prince and the tent of the wicked, and tells them to ask wayfaring men. He declares their comfort nonsense and their answers falsehood.

Chapter 27

Job tells his friends he will never justify them. He says all of them have seen the matter themselves and asks why they have become altogether vain. He says he will teach them about the hand of God and will not conceal what is with the Almighty.
