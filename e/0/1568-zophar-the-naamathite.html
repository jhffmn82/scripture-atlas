Zophar the Naamathite

Also called: Zophar, Zophar, the Naamathite

Abstract written by a model

Zophar the Naamathite is one of Job's three friends who come from his own place to comfort him, weeping, tearing his robe and sitting silently seven days and nights. He then answers Job twice. First he rebukes Job's many words and mockery, doubts his claimed cleanness, calls God's mystery unfathomable, urges repentance and warns that the wicked have no escape. Then, stung by a reproof, he insists from ancient knowledge that the wicked man's triumph is brief and his ruin is his portion from God. At the end, the LORD's wrath falls on him with Eliphaz and Bildad for not speaking rightly, and he obeys the LORD's command.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Zophar the Naamathite is one of Job's three friends who hear of his troubles and come from his own place. He makes an appointment with the others to comfort Job, and joins in weeping, tearing his robe and sitting silently seven days and nights.

Chapter 11

Zophar answers by rebuking the addressee's many words and mockery, and quotes him as claiming a pure doctrine and cleanness before God. He wishes God would speak against the addressee, declares God's mystery unfathomable, and urges repentance with promises of restored security. He ends by warning that the wicked will have no escape.

Chapter 20

Zophar the Naamathite answers, speaking out of haste and saying he has heard a reproof that shames him. He argues from ancient knowledge that the wicked man's triumph is short, and closes by declaring that such ruin is the wicked man's portion from God.

Chapter 42

Zophar is included in the LORD's wrath against Eliphaz and his two friends for not speaking rightly of him. He goes with Eliphaz and Bildad and does what the LORD commanded.
