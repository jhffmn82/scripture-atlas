Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Matthew, the chief priests consistently oppose Jesus, conspiring against him, arranging his arrest and death, influencing the crowd, mocking him, sealing his tomb, and bribing the guards. In The Gospel According to John, the chief priests align with the Pharisees against Jesus, sending officers to arrest him, convening to resolve to kill him, ordering reports of his whereabouts, and plotting against Lazarus.

chief priests in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where chief priests first appears

Abstract written by a model

The chief priests, acting with the scribes and elders, are a group consistently opposed to Jesus. In chapter 21 they are indignant at his wonders, question his authority, fail to answer his question about John's baptism, and want to seize him but fear the crowds. In chapter 26 they gather in Caiaphas's court, plan to arrest him by deceit apart from the feast, pay Judas thirty pieces of silver, send an armed crowd, and seek false testimony. In chapter 27 they take counsel to put him to death, deliver him to Pilate, buy the potter's field with the returned silver, persuade the crowd to ask for Barabbas, mock him on the cross, and have the tomb sealed with a guard. In chapter 28 they bribe the soldiers with silver to claim the disciples stole the body.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

The chief priests, with the scribes, are indignant at the wonders and at the children's praise in the temple. With the elders they ask by what authority Jesus acts, but they cannot answer his question about John's baptism, saying 'We don't know.' Hearing his parables, they perceive he speaks about them and seek to seize him but fear the multitudes.

Chapter 26

The chief priests, with the scribes and elders, gather in Caiaphas's court and plan to seize Jesus by deceit but not during the feast. They pay Judas thirty pieces of silver and send a crowd with swords and clubs to arrest Jesus. They seek false testimony against him at the trial.

Chapter 27

The chief priests and elders take counsel to put Jesus to death, bind him, and deliver him to Pilate. They rebuff Judas, use the returned silver to buy the potter's field, persuade the crowd to ask for Barabbas, and mock Jesus on the cross. Afterward they ask Pilate to secure the tomb and seal the stone with a guard.

Chapter 28

The chief priests hear the guards' report of what happened. They assemble with the elders, take counsel, and give a large amount of silver to the soldiers, telling them to say the disciples stole the body at night, and promising to persuade the governor and keep the soldiers free of worry.

the chief priests in The Gospel According to John

author unknown · AD 90 to 100 · the work’s page · read it where the chief priests first appears

Abstract written by a model

The chief priests act as a group aligned with the Pharisees against Jesus. In Chapter 7 they send officers to arrest him and question them when they return empty-handed. In Chapter 11 they convene a council, fearing Roman reprisal, resolve to kill him, order his whereabouts reported, and plot against Lazarus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

The chief priests join the Pharisees in sending officers to arrest Jesus. They question the officers when they come back without him.

Chapter 11

The chief priests join the Pharisees in gathering a council, saying Jesus does many signs and fearing the Romans will take away their place and nation. They plan from that day to kill him and command that anyone who knows where he is must report it.
