Abstract written by a model

In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, faith is the means by which righteousness is accounted to believers rather than those who work; it justifies, gives access to grace, and brings peace with God through Jesus Christ. In The Letter to the Hebrews, faith is the basis for drawing near to God in fullness, leads to the saving of the soul, and distinguishes those who do not shrink back to destruction. In The Letter to the Hebrews, faith is assurance of things hoped for and proof of things not seen, pleasing to God and shown through examples from Abel and Noah to Abraham, Moses, and Rahab. In 1 John, faith is the victory that has overcome the world, shown by believing that Jesus is the Son of God. In Jude, faith is the one faith once for all delivered to the saints, for which recipients are urged to contend and upon which they are to build themselves up.

faith in Paul’s Letter to the Romans

author unknown · about AD 57 · the work’s page · read it where faith first appears

Abstract written by a model

Faith is the means by which righteousness is accounted to those who believe rather than work. In Chapter 4, Abraham's unweakened faith was counted as righteousness, and because the promise rests on faith, it accords with grace and is sure to all his offspring. In Chapter 5, faith justifies believers, gives access into grace, and brings peace with God through Jesus Christ.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Faith is what was accounted to Abraham for righteousness, and it is likewise accounted as righteousness to the one who does not work but believes in him who justifies the ungodly. Because the promise rests on faith, it is according to grace and sure to all Abraham's offspring. Abraham's faith did not weaken, and he grew strong through faith in the face of his own body's deadness and Sarah's womb.

Chapter 5

Faith is the means by which believers are justified and through which they have access into the grace in which they stand. It leads to peace with God through Jesus Christ.

faith in The Letter to the Hebrews

author unknown · about AD 64 to 69 · the work’s page · read it where faith first appears

Abstract written by a model

Faith is defined as assurance of things hoped for and proof of things not seen. It is required to be well pleasing to God. Chapter 11 shows it through a long series of examples, from Abel and Noah to Abraham, Moses, and Rahab, though none received the promise.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Faith is defined as assurance of things hoped for and proof of things not seen, and through it the elders obtained approval. Without it, it is impossible to be well pleasing to God. It is shown in a long series of examples, from Abel and Noah to Abraham, Moses, and Rahab, and in those who subdued kingdoms or endured torture, though none of them received the promise.
