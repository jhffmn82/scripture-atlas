Abstract written by a model

In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I, the body is described as loathsome, fragile, transient, and difficult to attain, yet worth preserving as an opportunity for liberation through disciplined care and exertion. In Chuang Tzŭ, the body requires nourishment and care, and when its frame is perfected and joined with pure vitality, it becomes fit for translation.

the body in Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the body first appears

Abstract written by a model

The body is loathsome within and without, painful, fragile, decaying, unstable, transient, and changeable. The informed person recognizes its foul interior humors and renounces attachment. Yet it is difficult to attain and offers an opportunity for liberation, making it worth preserving. Nourishment increases it and hardship makes it frail; an ascetic should care for and regulate it without attachment, endure pain, practice self-discipline, and dissolve it through exertion and reduced food.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Second Lecture, Called Conquest of the World. / First Lesson. .. Sixth Lesson.

The body decays, sustains pain, and contains foul interior humors. The informed person recognizes the body's loathsomeness and renounces attachment to it.

Fifth Lecture, Called Essence of the World. / First Lesson. .. Sixth Lesson.

The body is fragile, decaying, unstable, transient, uneternal, and subject to increase, decrease, and change. The body is worth the fight because the human body is difficult to attain and provides the opportunity for liberation.

Seventh Lecture, Called Liberation. / First Lesson. .. Eighth Lesson.

The body is described as increasing through nourishment and becoming frail in hardships. An ascetic should care for it without attachment, regulate its motions, endure its pains, and regard those pains as instrumental to the body’s dissolution.

body in Chuang Tzŭ

Chuang Tzŭ · the work’s page · read it where body first appears

Abstract written by a model

The physical body requires nourishment and care for life, yet care alone is insufficient; when its frame is perfect and joined with pure vitality, it becomes fit for translation.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIX.

The physical body requires proper nourishment and must not be abandoned, although its care alone cannot ensure the preservation of life. The text also says that a perfect physical frame, joined with pure vitality, makes one fit for translation.
