children of Ammon

group · 10 works · 29 quoted facts · 17 connections
Also called: Ammon, Ammonite, Ammonites, An Ammonite, the Ammonites, the children of Ammon, the cities of the children of Ammon, the king of the children of Ammon, the land of the children of Ammon

Abstract written by a model

In Deuteronomy, the Ammonites are barred from the LORD's assembly for withholding provisions from Israel and hiring Balaam to curse them. In Deuteronomy, the Ammonites possess land given to them by the LORD, and Israel is forbidden to trouble them or take it. In The Book of Judges, the children of Ammon oppress Israel, make war against it, and are defeated by Jephthah. In The First Book of Samuel, the Ammonites under Nahash are attacked by Saul and scattered. In The Second Book of Samuel, the children of Ammon hire foreign troops against David, flee before Abishai, and are conquered by David. In 1 Chronicles, the children of Ammon fight David's forces, lose their cities and crown, and suffer harsh treatment from David's army. In The Second Book of Chronicles, the children of Ammon join Moab against Judah after Israel had spared them, then destroy Mount Seir's inhabitants and turn against each other. In The Second Book of Chronicles, the king of the children of Ammon fights Jotham and the children of Ammon pay him tribute for three years.

and 4 more works

In The Book of Jeremiah, Ammon faces judgment, with Rabbah desolated, Malcam taken captive, and the nation driven out before its captivity is promised to be reversed. In The Book of Ezekiel, the children of Ammon are judged and destroyed among the nations. In The Book of Amos, Ammon is condemned for attacking pregnant women of Gilead and is threatened with fire and captivity. In The Book of Zephaniah, the children of Ammon are judged for reproaching the LORD's people and magnifying themselves against their border.

children of Ammon in The Book of Judges

author unknown · about 1000 BC · the work’s page · read it where children of Ammon first appears

Abstract written by a model

The children of Ammon are an enemy people ruled by a king. With the Philistines they oppressed Israel for eighteen years, crossing the Jordan to attack Judah, Benjamin and Ephraim, then encamping in Gilead. They make war on Israel and ignore Jephthah's message. The LORD delivers them into his hand, and he strikes them with great slaughter across twenty cities, subduing them. Jephthah later recalls this strife and victory.

Chapter 10

The Ammonites, with the Philistines, oppressed Israel for eighteen years after the LORD sold Israel into their hands. They crossed the Jordan to fight Judah, Benjamin and Ephraim, leaving Israel very distressed. They then gathered and encamped in Gilead.

Chapter 11

The children of Ammon make war against Israel and are the object of Jephthah's vow. After their king ignores Jephthah's message, the LORD delivers them into Jephthah's hand, and he strikes them from Aroer to Minnith, twenty cities, and to Abelcheramim with great slaughter, so they are subdued before Israel.

Chapter 12

The children of Ammon are recalled by Jephthah as those with whom he and his people were in great strife. He says he passed over against them and the LORD delivered them into his hand.

Seeing they had become odious to David, the Ammonites hired foreign forces and gathered from their cities for battle at the city gate. children of Ammon in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · about 592 to 570 BC · the work’s page · read it where children of Ammon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ammon is a nation, the children of Ammon, whose city Rabbah is a target of the LORD's polished sword. Its people see false visions and divine lies, and they rejoice with contempt, saying 'Aha!' against the sanctuary, Israel, and Judah. The LORD declares judgment and vengeance: he will judge Ammon in the land of its birth, pour out indignation, and deliver it to brutish men and to the children of the east as a possession. Rabbah becomes a stable for camels and Ammon a resting place for flocks. Ammon is plundered, cut off, destroyed, and forgotten among the nations.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

Ammon, whose city Rabbah is one target of the Babylonian sword, is the subject of a separate prophecy about its reproach. It is told that the sword is polished for slaughter while its people see false visions and divine lies. The LORD will judge it in the land of its birth, pour out his indignation, and deliver it to brutish men skillful to destroy. It will be fuel for the fire, its blood will be in the land, and it will be remembered no more.
