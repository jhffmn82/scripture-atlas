Abstract written by a model

The infected person, a leper, is examined by a priest, possibly isolated for seven days or more, and pronounced clean or unclean. If declared leprous, he wears torn clothes, lets his hair hang loose, covers his upper lip, cries 'Unclean! Unclean!', and dwells alone outside the camp while the plague lasts. Once healed, he is sprinkled seven times, washes, shaves, and bathes, then stays outside his tent seven days. On the eighth day he brings lambs to the Tent of Meeting, receives blood and oil, and is made clean by atonement.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

The infected person is brought to the priest, may be isolated for seven days or more, and is pronounced clean or unclean. One declared leprous must wear torn clothes, let the hair hang loose, cover the upper lip, cry 'Unclean! Unclean!', and dwell alone outside the camp. If cleared, the person washes their clothes and is clean.

Chapter 14

The leper, once healed, is brought to the priest and is sprinkled seven times and pronounced clean. He washes his clothes, shaves all his hair, and bathes, then dwells outside his tent seven days before shaving and washing again on the seventh day. On the eighth day he brings offerings, receives blood and oil on his ear, thumb, and toe, and is made clean by atonement, with a poorer alternative allowed if he cannot afford the full offering.
