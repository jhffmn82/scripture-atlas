Josiah

Also called: He, King Josiah, The king, his servants, the king, the king of Judah, the man who sent you to me

Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Josiah was a righteous king of Judah who renewed the covenant, destroyed idolatry, commanded the Passover, and was killed by Pharaoh Necoh at Megiddo. In 1 Chronicles, Josiah was the son of Amon who ended the royal succession and fathered four sons. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Josiah was a righteous king of Judah who reformed worship, renewed the covenant, kept a great Passover, and died after being wounded at Megiddo. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Josiah was a remembered king who reformed the people, removed abominations, and made godliness prevail.

Josiah in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Josiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Josiah was a righteous king of Judah who took the throne at age eight, son of Jedidah, and reigned thirty-one years in Jerusalem, following David's example. Hearing the book of the law, he tore his clothes and inquired of the LORD; through Huldah he learned that, because he was tender-hearted and humbled himself, he would be spared the coming judgment. He gathered the people, read the covenant, made a covenant to keep the LORD's commandments, destroyed idolatrous objects and high places, killed priests of the high places in Samaria, and commanded the Passover. No king before or after turned to the LORD as he did. Father of Jehoahaz and Jehoiakim, he was killed by Pharaoh Necoh at Megiddo and buried in his own tomb in Jerusalem.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 22

Josiah became king at age eight and reigned thirty-one years in Jerusalem, doing right and following David. After hearing the book of the law he tore his clothes and sent officials to inquire of the LORD. Through Huldah he learns that because his heart was tender and he humbled himself, he will die in peace and not see the coming evil.

Chapter 23

Josiah gathers the people, reads them the book of the covenant, and makes a covenant before the LORD to keep his commandments with all his heart and soul. He destroys idolatrous objects and high places, kills the priests of the high places in Samaria, and commands the Passover. He is described as unmatched among kings in turning to the LORD, but Pharaoh Necoh kills him at Megiddo, and he is buried in his own tomb in Jerusalem.

Josiah in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Josiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Josiah was a king of Judah who became king at age eight and reigned thirty-one years in Jerusalem. He did what was right in the LORD's eyes and walked in the ways of David. He began seeking God in his eighth year, purged idolatry in his twelfth, and ordered the temple repaired in his eighteenth. Hearing the law, he tore his clothes and led a covenant renewal. Huldah promised him a peaceful death. He kept a great Passover, supplying thousands of animals from his own substance. Later he ignored Neco's warning from God, disguised himself, and was wounded by archers at Megiddo. He died in Jerusalem, was buried in his fathers' tombs, and was mourned by all Judah, Jerusalem, and Jeremiah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 34

Josiah became king at age eight, reigned thirty-one years, did what was right in the LORD's eyes, and began seeking God in his eighth year. He purged idolatry, ordered the temple repaired, tore his clothes on hearing the law, and led the people in a covenant. He was told by Huldah that he would die in peace because of his humility.

Chapter 35

Josiah keeps a Passover in Jerusalem, sets the priests in their offices, instructs the Levites, and supplies thousands of lambs, young goats, and bulls from his own substance. Later he disregards Neco's warning, which came from the mouth of God, disguises himself, and fights at Megiddo, where archers wound him. He dies in Jerusalem, is buried in the tombs of his fathers, and is mourned by all Judah and Jerusalem and by Jeremiah.

Josiah in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Josiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Josiah is a king whose memory is likened to sweet incense, honey and banquet music. He reformed the people, removed abominations, set his heart right toward the Lord, and made godliness prevail in lawless days, ranking with David and Hezekiah as non-wicked kings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 49

Josiah's memory is compared to incense prepared by a perfumer, sweet as honey and like music at a banquet. He reformed the people, removed abominations, set his heart right toward the Lord, and made godliness prevail in lawless days. He is named with David and Hezekiah as the only kings who were not wicked.
