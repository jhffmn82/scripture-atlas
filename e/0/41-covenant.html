covenant (between the LORD and Israel) in Exodus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where covenant (between the LORD and Israel) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The covenant is the agreement the LORD makes with the people of Israel concerning all his words. It begins with his offer: obedience and covenant-keeping will make them his own possession, a kingdom of priests and a holy nation, and the people agree. It is then established when they promise obedience, the book is read, offerings are made, and Moses seals it with blood.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 19

The LORD conditions Israel's special standing on obeying his voice and keeping his covenant: they will be his own possession from among all peoples, a kingdom of priests and a holy nation. The people respond together that all the LORD has spoken they will do. This unit records the offer and acceptance but not its terms.

Chapter 24

The covenant is established as the people twice promise to obey the LORD's words, the book of the covenant is read, and the offerings are made. It is sealed when Moses sprinkles the people with blood, calling it the blood of the covenant the LORD has made with them concerning all these words.

covenant (LORD's covenant with Judah) in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where covenant (LORD's covenant with Judah) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The covenant is the LORD's charge to the fathers at the Exodus: obey his voice to be his people and he their God, receiving a land flowing with milk and honey. Cursing falls on non-hearers. Jeremiah proclaims it because Israel and Judah broke it, bringing its words upon them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

The covenant is the one the LORD commanded the fathers when he brought them out of Egypt: obey his voice so they would be his people and he their God, with the promised land flowing with milk and honey. Jeremiah is told to proclaim it, and its words are brought on the people because the house of Israel and the house of Judah have broken it.
