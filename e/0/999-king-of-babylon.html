In The Second Book of Nephi, the king of Babylon is the subject of a proverb, brought down to hell, cast out like an abominable branch, and denied burial.

king of Babylon in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where king of Babylon first appears

Abstract written by a model

The king of Babylon is mocked in a parable as an oppressor who struck peoples in wrath. Proud, he vowed to ascend to heaven and rival the Most High. He is instead brought down to Sheol and the pit, denied burial because he destroyed his land and killed his people, and his offspring are to be slaughtered.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

The king of Babylon is mocked in a parable as an oppressor who struck the peoples in wrath and who has now ceased. He is addressed as the shining one, son of the dawn, who said in his heart he would ascend to heaven and make himself like the Most High, but is brought down to Sheol and the pit. He is denied burial with other kings and cast out like an abominable branch because he destroyed his land and killed his people, and his offspring are to be slaughtered.
