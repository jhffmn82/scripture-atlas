Abstract written by a model

In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, Rishabha teaches his sons to acquire perfect knowledge of the Law, cease from sin, control themselves, and follow the path to the destruction of Karman. In Kalpa Sutra, Rishabha is described as a Kosalian Arhat and First Tîrthakara who renounces, attains Kevala, establishes the fourfold community, and reaches final liberation.

Rishabha in Kalpa Sutra

Bhadrabâhu · the work’s page · read it where Rishabha first appears

Abstract written by a model

Rishabha, a Kosalian Arhat of the Kâsyapa gotra, is called First King, First Mendicant, First Gina, and First Tîrthakara. Descending from Sarvârthasiddha, he was born to Nâbhi and Marudevî in Bharatavarsha and Ikshvâkubhûmi. He spent two millions of former years as a prince and six millions three hundred thousand as king, teaching sciences, arts, accomplishments, and occupations. He had a hundred sons, anointed them as kings, then renounced with four thousand royal persons and Kshatriyas. After one thousand years short of perfection, he attained Kevala and established a large fourfold community with Sramanas, nuns, lay votaries, and female lay votaries, alongside sages, Kevalins, Ganas, and Ganadharas. He died with ten thousand monks at mount Ashtâpada in Samparyaṅka position, attained final liberation, and was freed from all pains.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Life of Rishabha.

Rishabha, the Kosalian, was an Arhat of the Kâsyapa gotra and had five names: Rishabha, First King, First Mendicant, First Gina, and First Tîrthakara. He lived eight millions four hundred thousand years on the whole, established a large fourfold community, and was ultimately freed from all pains.
