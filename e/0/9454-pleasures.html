pleasures

Abstract written by a model

Pleasures are impermanent sensual enjoyments that resemble poison, thorns, and a venomous snake. Attachment causes anger, pain, repeated error, downfall, and continued worldly existence, while their happiness is slight and suffering prolonged. A person who does not renounce them misses the soul’s true end; one who renounces them may attain a godly state. Nami first enjoys and then abandons pleasures before enlightenment, whereas Brahmadatta continues longing for them despite knowing the Law. Ultimately, abandoning pleasures removes an obstacle to liberation.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Seventh Lecture.

A person who has not renounced pleasure misses the true end of the soul and goes astray repeatedly. A person who renounces pleasure does not miss the aim and may attain a godly state.

Ninth Lecture.

Nami enjoys excellent pleasures before becoming enlightened, then gives them up. He teaches that pleasures are thorns, poison, and like a venomous snake, and that desire for them leads to a bad end.

Thirteenth Lecture.

Sensual pleasures are impermanent, difficult to abandon, and produce pain. Brahmadatta continues to long for them despite knowing the Law, and his attachment contributes to his downfall.

Fourteenth Lecture.

Pleasures provide only brief happiness but cause prolonged suffering and obstruct liberation. The characters abandon them because they bind beings to worldly existence.
