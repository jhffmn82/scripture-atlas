alms

Abstract written by a model

Alms, the giving of bread to the hungry and garments to the naked, is presented in Tobit as a practice of great spiritual worth. It begins with Tobit's own generosity to kindred and nation, including a third tenth commanded by his father's mother Deborah. He then urges Tobias to give according to his abundance, or even from a little, without an envious eye. Alms is called a good gift before the Most High and a good treasure laid up against necessity. Raphael teaches it is better than laying up gold, delivers from death, and purges away all sin. Finally Tobit cites Manasses, who escaped the snare of death by giving alms, while Aman perished, and Tobit himself gave alms after his sight was restored.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Tobit / Chapter 1

Tobit did many alms deeds for his kindred and nation, giving his bread to the hungry and his garments to the naked. He also gave a third tenth to those to whom it was appropriate, as his father's mother Deborah had commanded him after he was left an orphan.

Chapter 4

Tobit urges Tobias to give alms according to his abundance, or from even a little, without an envious eye and without turning from any poor man. He says it lays up a good treasure against necessity, delivers from death and darkness, and is a good gift in the sight of the Most High. He also tells him to give bread to the hungry and garments to the naked.

Chapter 12

Raphael teaches that giving alms is better than laying up gold, and that it delivers from death and purges away all sin. Those who give alms and do righteousness will be filled with life, while sinners are enemies to their own life.

Chapter 14

Tobit tells his children to consider what alms does and how righteousness delivers. He cites Manasses, who gave alms and escaped the snare of death set for him, while Aman fell into the snare and perished. Tobit himself gave alms after his sight was restored.
