All flesh

group · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 0 connections
Also called: Pairs from all flesh with the breath of life in them, animals, every animal after its kind, every living thing, every man, grass, grass (metaphor for human frailty), living creatures (all flesh), man, the flower, the flower of the field, the people

Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, it refers to all living creatures on earth that enter the ship by pairs or die in the flood, with only those aboard Noah's ship remaining. In The Book of Isaiah, it symbolizes all flesh that withers and fades when the LORD's breath blows on it.

living creatures (all flesh) in Genesis

author unknown · about 449 BC · the work’s page · read it where living creatures (all flesh) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Clean and unclean animals, birds, livestock, and creeping things go into the ship by pairs, male and female, after their kinds, as God commanded; seven pairs of clean animals and of birds are to be taken to keep seed alive. All flesh on the earth and dry land that had the breath of life, including every man, dies in the flood. Only those in the ship with Noah are left.
