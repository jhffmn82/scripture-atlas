Abstract written by a model

In Leviticus, the tithe is holy and belongs to the LORD, covering land produce and every tenth animal. In Numbers, the tithe is given by the LORD to the Levites as their inheritance, with the Levites offering a tithe of it to Aaron. In Deuteronomy, the tithe is a yearly offering eaten before the LORD or stored for Levites, foreigners, orphans, and widows. In The Second Book of Chronicles, tithes and first fruits are brought in abundance and stored to support the priests and Levites. In The Book of Nehemiah, first fruits and tithes are brought to the house of God and distributed to the priests and Levites. In The Letter to the Hebrews, tithes are received by Melchizedek from Abraham, demonstrating Melchizedek's superiority. In Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong), the tithe is presented as a levy Dai Ying Zhi cannot immediately implement.

tithe in Leviticus

author unknown · 539 to 331 BC · the work’s page · read it where tithe first appears

Abstract written by a model

The tithe is holy and belongs to the LORD, covering the land's seed and fruit plus every tenth animal passing under the rod. Redeeming produce requires adding a fifth, while the animal tithe allows no examining or exchange and cannot be redeemed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 27

The tithe of the land's seed and tree fruit is the LORD's and holy, and redeeming any of it requires adding a fifth. The tenth of herds and flocks passing under the rod is holy, with no examining for good or bad and no exchange, and it cannot be redeemed.

tithe in Deuteronomy

author unknown · 624 BC · the work’s page · read it where tithe first appears

Abstract written by a model

The tithe is a yearly offering of one's seed increase, grain, wine, oil, and firstborn livestock, eaten before the LORD at his chosen place to teach fear of him. If the journey is too long, it may be converted to money and spent there. Every third year it is stored in town gates for Levites, foreigners, orphans, and widows.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

The Israelites must tithe all the yearly increase of their seed, along with grain, new wine, oil, and the firstborn of herd and flock, eating it before the LORD so they learn to fear him. If the way is too far, it may be turned into money and exchanged at the chosen place for cattle, sheep, wine, or strong drink. Every third year the tithe is stored within the gates for the Levite, foreigner, fatherless, and widow.

is_received_by Melchizedek [from Abraham] The Levites take tithes from their brothers according to the law, yet Abraham gave a tenth to Melchizedek.
