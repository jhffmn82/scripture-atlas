In The First Book of the Maccabees, Lysias governs royal affairs, commands campaigns against Israel, installs Antiochus as king, and persuades the king and leaders to make peace with the Jews and restore their laws. In The Second Book of the Maccabees, Lysias serves as the king’s guardian, kinsman, and chancellor, later becoming supreme governor, leading campaigns against the Jews, negotiating with them, and defending their treaty.

Lysias in The First Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 99 BC · the work’s page · read it where Lysias first appears

Abstract written by a model

Lysias is an honorable man of royal lineage, left by King Antiochus to govern royal affairs from the Euphrates to Egypt's borders and to guard the king's son Antiochus. Given half the forces and the elephants, he is charged to root out Israel and settle foreigners on its land. He sends Ptolemy, Nicanor, and Gorgias against Judah, but they are defeated. Discouraged, he gathers sixty thousand infantry and five thousand cavalry the next year, is defeated at Bethsura, and withdraws to Antioch to hire more soldiers. After the old king's death he installs the son as king. When Philip returns, Lysias persuades the king and leaders to make peace with the Jews and restore their laws, citing weakness and scant food.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

Lysias, described as an honorable man of royal lineage, is left in charge of the king's affairs from the Euphrates to the borders of Egypt and to raise the king's son Antiochus. He receives half the forces and the elephants, and is charged to root out Israel and the remnant of Jerusalem and settle foreigners on their land. He chooses Ptolemy, Nicanor, and Gorgias and sends them with a large army against Judah.

Chapter 4

Lysias is told of the defeat by escapees and is confounded and discouraged that the king's commands had not been carried out. The next year he gathers sixty thousand infantry and five thousand cavalry, is defeated at Bethsura, and withdraws to Antioch to gather hired soldiers for a greater return to Judea.

Chapter 6

Lysias, who had earlier been put to shame by the Jews, installs Antiochus's son as king upon learning of the old king's death. When Philip returns, he persuades the king and leaders to make peace with the Jews and restore their laws, citing weakness, scant food, and affairs of the kingdom.

Lysias in The Second Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 124 BC · the work’s page · read it where Lysias first appears

Abstract written by a model

Lysias is the king's guardian, kinsman and chancellor, later appointed supreme governor of Coelesyria and Phoenicia by Eupator. He first marched against the Jews with a huge army, intending to Hellenize Jerusalem and profit from the temple, and besieged Bethsuron. After a shameful flight, he recognized the Almighty fought for the Hebrews, negotiated terms, and wrote the Jews promising to advocate for them. Later he accompanied Antiochus Eupator, blamed Menelaus for the evils, and at Ptolemais defended the treaty, pacifying the angry people before departing to Antioch.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Lysias, the king's guardian, kinsman, and chancellor, marched against the Jews with a huge force intending to Hellenize Jerusalem and profit from the temple and high priesthood, and besieged Bethsuron. After a shameful flight from defeat, he recognized that the Almighty fought for the Hebrews and negotiated terms, promising to persuade the king to befriend them. He then wrote the Jews a letter confirming that he had presented their requests to the king and that what was possible was granted.

Chapter 13

Lysias, the king's guardian and chancellor, accompanied Antiochus with a great army and informed him that Menelaus was the cause of all the evils. At Ptolemais he defended the treaty, persuaded and pacified the angry people, gained their good will, and departed to Antioch.
