Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Lot travels with Abram, chooses the Plain of the Jordan, lives near Sodom, is captured and rescued, escapes Sodom, and fathers Moab and Ben Ammi. In Surah 7: The Heights (Al-A'raaf), Lot condemns his folk's abomination, and Allah saves him and his household except his wife before punishing his people. In Surah 11: Hud, Lot is distressed by the messengers' arrival, cannot protect them, and is told to leave by night while his wife remains for destruction. In Surah 15: The Rock (Al-Hijr), Lot receives the messengers and is told to travel with his household by night, while his wife remains behind and the rest of his family is delivered. In Surah 21: The Prophets (Al-Anbiyaa), Lot is rescued from a community practicing abominations, brought into Allah's mercy, and counted among the righteous. In Surah 26: The Poets (Ash-Shu'araa), Lot calls his folk to duty and obedience, condemns their conduct, and prays to be saved with his household. In Surah 29: The Spider (Al-Ankaboot), Lot believes Abraham, condemns his people's lewdness and corruption, and is delivered with his household except his wife. In Surah 54: The Moon (Al-Qamar), Lot warns his folk of the Lord's blow, and the Lord rescues his family in the last watch of the night.

Lot in Genesis

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Lot first appears

Abstract written by a model

Lot is a man, the grandson of Terah through Haran, who dies in Ur while Terah lives. Terah takes Lot toward Canaan, and they settle in Haran. Lot later travels with Abram, owning his own flocks, herds, and tents. When offered a choice of land, he picks the well-watered Plain of the Jordan, separates from Abram, and moves his tent as far as Sodom, where he lives as a foreigner. Four kings capture him with his goods, and Abram rescues him. In Sodom he sits in the gate, hosts two angels, and offers his virgin daughters to the crowd to protect them. Pulled inside by the angels, he lingers, is led out by the hand, and pleads to go to Zoar. He then lives in a cave in the mountain with his daughters, God having remembered Abraham and saved him. There his daughters get him drunk and lie with him, making him father of Moab and Ben Ammi.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Lot is the son of Haran, whose father dies in Ur of the Chaldees while Terah is still alive. His grandfather Terah takes him from Ur toward Canaan, and they settle in Haran.

Chapter 13

Lot travels with Abram out of Egypt and has his own flocks, herds, and tents. When Abram offers him the choice of land, he sees the well-watered Plain of the Jordan, chooses it, travels east, and separates from Abram. He lives in the cities of the plain and moves his tent as far as Sodom.

Chapter 14

Lot, Abram's brother's son, lived in Sodom and was taken captive with his goods by the four kings when they took Sodom's possessions. Abram rescued him and brought back both Lot and his goods.

Chapter 19

Lot sits in the gate of Sodom, bows to the two angels, and urges them into his house, where he feeds them. He offers his two virgin daughters to the crowd to protect his guests, and is pulled inside by the angels. He lingers when told to flee, is led out by the hand, pleads to go to the small city of Zoar instead of the mountains, and later moves to a cave in the mountain with his daughters. There his daughters get him drunk and lie with him, and he does not know when they lie down or get up.
