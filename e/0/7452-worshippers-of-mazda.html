Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, the Mazdayasnian follows Zarathustra’s order, opposes the Daêvas, practices ritual sacrifice, and confesses this identity. In Yasna, the Mazda-worshipper confesses devotion to the Lord’s lore and opposition to the Daêvas. In Yasna, Mazdayasnians offer sacrifice with their companions and families, seeking knowledge, victory over oppression, and the saints’ best life. In Visparad, Zarathustrian Mazdayasnians are summoned with priests, warriors, and husbandmen, celebrate sacrificial worship, and are instructed in lawful deeds, husbandry, and aid to the needy. In Afrinagan, Gahs and Miscellaneous Fragments, Mazdayasnians assemble for ritual service, oppose the Daêvas, and offer service and Myazda to avoid expulsion. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), the worshippers of Mazda worship Ahura Mazda, follow Mazda’s law, cultivate abundantly, and perform prescribed corpse-handling and purification duties. (Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII) In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII, the worshippers of Mazda follow rules for the dead, polluted ground, cleansing, healing, and offerings. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), the worshippers of Mazda perform and enforce cleansing duties, reject false teaching, and support and care for pregnant women.

and 2 more works

In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, worshippers of Mazda obey Ahura’s laws and the truest-giving Law, while Sraosha protects faithful people, houses, and communities receiving sacrifice. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, the worshippers of Mazda follow Zarathustra and Ahura Mazda’s laws, hate the Daêvas, worship through sacrifice and prayer, and seek holiness, protection, prosperity, and victory over the Drug.

Mazdayasnian in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Mazdayasnian first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mazdayasnian is a follower of Zarathustra's order and foe to the Daêvas, devoted to the lore of the Lord and ritual sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise. Mazdayasnians are called to liberality of Righteousness and sacred words; adults are warned against the magician's word. The speaker ultimately confesses this identity.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna III.

The Mazdayasnian confesses himself to be of Zarathustra's order and a foe to the Daêvas. He is devoted to the lore of the Lord and to the sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise of the ritual lords and regulators.

Yasna VIII.

Mazdayasnians are called to practice the liberality of Righteousness and to adhere to the sacred words. Their adults are warned that failure to do so brings them toward the magician's word.

Yasna XIV (Sp. XV).

The speaker confesses to being a Mazdayasnian.

Zarathustrian Mazdayasnians in Visparad

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Zarathustrian Mazdayasnians first appears

Abstract written by a model

Zarathustrian Mazdayasnians are a group whose chiefs are summoned with Fire-priests, warriors, and diligent husbandmen of the Mazdayasnian Faith. They celebrate sacrificial worship, homage, propitiation, and praise of the entire holy and clean creation at the sacrificial time for the Myazda-offering and at Ratufrîti. Steadfast in worship, they appear with wood-billets, perfumes, and supplicated blessings, while mortars are asked to become full of wisest teaching for them. They are then instructed to ready their feet, hands, and understandings for lawful deeds and avoidance of evil, further husbandry through good deeds, and render the needy rich.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Visparad III.

The chiefs of the Mazdayasnians are summoned, along with the Fire-priests, warriors, and diligent husbandmen of the Mazdayasnian Faith.

Visparad IV (Sp. V).

Zarathustrian Mazdayasnians celebrate at the sacrificial time for the Myazda-offering and at the time for the Ratufrîti.

Visparad XII (Sp. XIV).

The Mazdayasnians are steadfast in worship and appear with wood-billets, perfumes, and supplicated blessings. The mortars are asked to become full of wisest teaching for them.

Visparad XV (Sp. XVIII).

Zarathustrian Mazdayasnians are told to hold their feet, hands, and understandings in readiness for the well-doing of lawful deeds and the avoidance of unlawful and evil deeds. They are directed to further husbandry through good deeds and to render the needy rich.

The worshippers of Mazda worship Ahura Mazda and follow and fulfill Mazda’s law through love, beseeching, holy objects, priests, families, herds, and cultivation. The faithful worship with holy objects, establish houses with priests and thriving families and herds, cultivate the Earth, and may receive produce from those who till the land. The worshippers of Mazda must find a Dakhma, handle dead bodies according to prescribed conditions, perfume houses, dig graves, move corpses, and perform purification rites. worshippers of Mazda in Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where worshippers of Mazda first appears

Abstract written by a model

The worshippers of Mazda are a group governed by rules for the dead, polluted ground, cleansing, healing, and offerings. They construct houses for the dead, lay corpses on the Dakhma, observe waiting periods before returning to fire, and handle a still-born child and mother through required distances, food, confinement, and washing. Where dogs or men have died, they leave the ground fallow for a year, may sow and water only the rest, and are subject to Peshôtanus if they violate this. They inspect and cleanse affected ground, remove corpses from water, secure corpses, and provide places for bones. Healing with the knife requires proven treatment of worshippers of the Daêvas.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard V.

The worshippers of Mazda must build houses for the dead, place corpses on the Dakhma, and follow prescribed periods and purification rules. They must also handle a still-born child and the mother according to the stated distances, foods, confinement, and washing requirements.

Fargard VI.

Worshippers of Mazda must leave ground fallow for a year where dogs or men have died and must not sow or water it during that year. They must inspect the ground for bones, hair, flesh, dung, and blood, remove corpses from water, secure corpses against dispersal, and provide suitable places for bones.

Fargard VII.

They must follow the rules for cleansing, treatment of corpses, healing, and offerings. A healer may treat them with the knife only after proving his skill on worshippers of the Daêvas.

The worshippers of Mazda are faithful people governed by duties in Fargards IX, XV, and XVI. They reward lawful cleansing and punish ignorant cleansing; reject a foreign wrong creed knowingly taught as sin; and support every pregnant female. For a woman they clear and prepare a place, build shelter, provide food at a distance, wash her, kill specified Khrafstras, and do not suppress her issue.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard IX.

The worshippers of Mazda give cattle or another suitable reward to the person who cleanses them, if they can afford it. They must bind and punish one who performs cleansing without knowing the rites according to the law of Mazda.

Fargard XV.

The faithful are to look after every pregnant female, whether two-footed or four-footed, woman or bitch. They are also told how to support a pregnant bitch according to where she is found.

Fargard XVI.

The worshippers of Mazda must clear the way of wood, strew dry dust, erect a building for the woman, provide her food from three paces away, and wash her with gômêz and water. They must also kill specified Khrafstras and must not suppress her issue.
