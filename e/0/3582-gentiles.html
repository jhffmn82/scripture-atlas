Abstract written by a model

In The Acts of the Apostles, the Gentiles receive God’s word, the Holy Spirit, repentance, and baptism, and the council decides not to burden them with circumcision. In The Acts of the Apostles, the Gentiles at Antioch hear Paul and Barnabas, rejoice, glorify God’s word, believe, and spread it. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, the Gentiles are justified through faith, grafted into God’s olive tree, warned against boasting, and shown owing material service to the Jerusalem saints. In Paul’s Letter to the Galatians, the Gentiles receive Paul’s preaching without being compelled to live as Jews, while Peter is challenged for withdrawing from them. In Paul’s Letter to the Ephesians, the Gentiles are made fellow citizens with the saints, fellow heirs, and partakers of the promise through Christ, though they are warned against walking in futility and lust. In The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry, the Gentiles receive the gospel and help gather the remnants of the house of Israel to knowledge of the true Messiah. In The Second Book of Nephi, the Gentiles support the house of Israel, receive mercy and a book, and face blessings or judgment according to their treatment of Israel and their reconciliation to Christ. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, the Gentiles receive the gospel, Jesus’s teachings, and the Holy Ghost, with blessings conditional on faith, repentance, and righteousness.

Gentiles in The Acts of the Apostles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Gentiles first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Gentiles are non-Jewish people who come to receive God's word. In Caesarea, Cornelius's household hears Peter, receives the Holy Spirit, speaks in other languages, magnifies God, and is baptized in Jesus Christ's name. Jerusalem believers then conclude God has granted them repentance to life. Later, their turning to God brings the brothers great joy, and the council decides not to burden them with circumcision, asking only a few necessary things.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10

Cornelius and the household gathered with him in Caesarea are Gentiles, whom Peter, a Jew, would normally consider unlawful to visit or join himself to. They listen to Peter's message, and the Holy Spirit is poured out on them, with the gift evidenced by their speaking in other languages and magnifying God. Peter then commands that they be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ.

Chapter 11

Gentiles in The Book of Ether

Abstract written by a model

The Gentiles are called and commanded to repent of iniquity and sins, become clean, exercise faith in Jesus Christ, and come unto Him. Greater hidden knowledge is promised through faith and sanctification. They are warned not to let murderous or secret combinations gain power, but to awake when they appear. Though they may mock Moroni’s weak writing, they are urged toward meekness, humility, faith, hope, charity, grace, and a prepared place.

Chapter 4

The Gentiles are told to come unto Jesus Christ, who will show them greater things and knowledge hidden because of unbelief. They are promised these things when they exercise faith and become sanctified.

Chapter 8

The Gentiles are warned to repent and not allow murderous combinations to gain power over them. They are told to awake to their awful situation when such combinations appear among them.

Chapter 12

The Gentiles may mock the weakness of Moroni’s writing, but the Lord says His grace is sufficient for those who are meek and humble. They are urged to have faith, hope, and charity, and Moroni prays that they may receive grace and charity.
