sage

person · 1 work · 57 quoted facts · 13 connections
Also called: I, The sage, he helps, he learns, his, me, the sage, the sage desires

Abstract written by a model

The sage is a person defined by humility, restraint, and care for others. He seeks simple bodily satisfaction rather than the eyes’ endless longing, rejects self-display and striving, and achieves great things precisely by not making himself great. He sets aside excess, extravagance, indulgence, ambition, and purposive action. With no fixed mind of his own, he takes the people’s mind as his own, is good and sincere toward everyone, and treats the people as children. He is still, harmless, straightforward, bright without dazzling, and avoids both sharpness and license. He meets difficulty while it is easy and greatness while it is small, so he avoids difficulties and accomplishes the greatest things without doing what is great. He desires and learns what others neglect, helps the natural development of all things, knows the pain of disease without having it, and knows himself without parading or valuing himself openly. In engagements he does not demand speedy fulfilment. At the end, he does not accumulate: by expending for and giving to others, he possesses more, and in all his doing he does not strive.

12.

The sage seeks to satisfy the craving of the belly rather than the insatiable longing of the eyes. The sage puts aside the latter and prefers to seek the former.

22. 1. The partial becomes complete; the crooked, straight; the empty,

The sage embraces the one thing of humility and manifests it to all the world. By avoiding self-display, self-assertion, self-boasting, self-complacency, and striving, he shines, is distinguished, receives acknowledgment of his merit, acquires superiority, and cannot be opposed in striving by anyone in the world.

The sage is able to accomplish great achievements by not making himself great.

49. 1. The sage has no invariable mind of his own; he makes the mind

The sage has no invariable mind of his own and makes the mind of the people his mind. He is good and sincere toward all, appears indecisive and indifferent, and treats the people as his children.

57. 1. A state may be ruled by (measures of) correction; weapons of

The sage says that he will do nothing of purpose, keep still, take no trouble, and manifest no ambition. He teaches that these actions allow the people to transform themselves, become correct, become rich, and attain primitive simplicity.

58.

The sage is harmless and does not injure others with sharpness or angles. He is straightforward without allowing himself license, and bright without dazzling.

63. 1. (It is the way of the Tao) to act without (thinking of) acting;

The sage anticipates difficult things while they are easy and does things that would become great while they are small. By never doing what is great, the sage is able to accomplish the greatest things, and by seeing difficulty even in what seems easy, the sage avoids difficulties.

64. 1. That which is at rest is easily kept hold of; before a thing

The sage does not act with an ulterior purpose and therefore does no harm. The sage does not lay hold of things in that way and therefore does not lose his hold. He desires what other men do not desire, does not prize things difficult to get, learns what other men do not learn, and turns back to what the multitude of men have passed by.

The sage does not have the disease. The sage knows the pain inseparable from it.

The sage knows these things of himself but does not parade his knowledge. He loves without appearing to value himself, puts the latter alternative away, and chooses the former.
