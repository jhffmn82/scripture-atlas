Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, Aaron serves as Moses’ spokesman and is appointed, clothed, and consecrated as priest, though he makes the golden calf. In Leviticus, Aaron is consecrated as high priest, performs offerings and atonement, teaches the statutes, and ministers in the sanctuary. In Numbers, Aaron serves as Israel’s priest and chosen head of the priestly line until he dies on Mount Hor. In 1 Chronicles, Aaron and his sons are set apart to sanctify the most holy things, minister, burn incense, bless Israel, and make atonement. In Psalms, Aaron is named with Moses as a chosen priest who called on the LORD, kept his testimonies, and was forgiven. In The Wisdom of Solomon, Aaron serves as blameless high priest whose prayer and atoning incense halt the wilderness plague. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Aaron is exalted as Moses’ holy brother and everlasting priest who offers sacrifice, teaches the law, and blesses Israel. In Surah 7: The Heights (Al-A'raaf), Aaron is left in charge during Moses’ absence and protests the people’s worship of the calf.

and 4 more works

In Surah 20: Taa-Haa, Aaron shares Moses’ task, strengthens him, and warns the Children of Israel against worshipping the calf. In Surah 21: The Prophets (Al-Anbiyaa), Aaron is given the Criterion together with Moses as a light and Reminder for the righteous. In The Believers (Al-Muminoon), Aaron is sent with Moses, Allah’s tokens, and a clear warrant to Pharaoh and his chiefs. In Surah 26: The Poets (Ash-Shu'araa), Aaron is sent with Moses to Pharaoh’s folk as the Lord’s servant and messenger.

Aaron in Exodus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Aaron first appears

Abstract written by a model

Aaron is a Levite, son of Amram and Jochebed, husband of Elisheba, and father of Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar. He is Moses' brother, described by the LORD as a good speaker who will be Moses' mouth to the people. The records follow him from spokesman to priest. First, he meets Moses on God's mountain, speaks the LORD's words to the elders, and performs the signs. At eighty-three he goes with Moses to Pharaoh as prophet and speaker, and his rod becomes a serpent that swallows the magicians' rods. With the rod he brings plagues: blood in the waters, frogs, and lice. He accompanies Moses again for the locust warning. In the wilderness the murmuring people address him with Moses, and he lays up a pot of manna before the Testimony. He goes up the mountain with Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders and sees the God of Israel. Moses then leaves him with Hur to settle disputes. The central thread is his appointment to the priesthood. With his sons he keeps the lamp from evening to morning as a statute forever. He is clothed in holy garments and bears Israel's names on his shoulders and heart. He wears the gold plate that bears the iniquity of the holy things so the people are accepted. He is washed, anointed, and consecrated for seven days, burns incense morning and evening, and makes yearly atonement at the altar's horns. He and his sons must wash at the basin so they do not die. The major failure is the golden calf. He collects the people's golden earrings, fashions the calf, builds an altar before it, and proclaims a feast to the LORD. When Moses confronts him, he blames the people and says the calf simply came out of the fire. The text says he let the people lose control. At the close, the LORD commands that he be brought to the Tent of Meeting, washed, clothed, and anointed to minister as priest, and he washes his hands and feet at the basin with Moses and his sons.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

14 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Aaron, Moses' brother the Levite, is described by the LORD as a good speaker who is coming out to meet Moses and will be glad to see him. He is to be Moses' mouth to the people. He meets Moses on God's mountain and kisses him, speaks all the LORD's words to the elders, and performs the signs before the people.

Chapter 5

Aaron accompanies Moses to Pharaoh and speaks the LORD's demand. He stands with Moses on the road when the Israelite officers come out from Pharaoh and blame them both.

Chapter 6

Aaron is addressed with Moses in the LORD's command to bring Israel out of Egypt. The genealogy names him as a son of Amram and Jochebed, husband of Elisheba, and father of Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar. He and Moses are identified as those who spoke to Pharaoh.

Chapter 7

Aaron, eighty-three years old, serves as Moses' prophet and speaker to Pharaoh. He casts down his rod, which becomes a serpent and swallows the magicians' rods, and later stretches out the rod over the waters of Egypt so they turn to blood.

Chapter 8

Aaron stretches out his hand with his rod over the waters so frogs come up over Egypt. He also strikes the dust of the earth with his rod, turning it into lice throughout the land.

Chapter 10

Aaron accompanies Moses in going to Pharaoh and delivering the LORD's warning about the locusts. He is brought back with Moses to Pharaoh, and is called for again in haste by Pharaoh after the locusts come.

Chapter 16

Aaron is addressed together with Moses by the murmuring people, and is told by Moses to call the congregation to come close to the LORD. While he speaks, the LORD's glory appears in the cloud. He later takes a pot with an omer-full of manna and lays it up before the Testimony, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Chapter 24

Aaron is told to come up and worship from a distance with Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders, and he goes up and sees the God of Israel. Moses then leaves him and Hur with the elders so that anyone in a dispute can go to them.

Chapter 27

Aaron, with his sons, is assigned to keep the lamp in order in the Tent of Meeting, outside the veil before the covenant, from evening to morning before the LORD. This is a statute forever throughout their generations.

Chapter 28

Aaron is called, with his sons, to minister to the LORD in the priest's office and is to be clothed in holy garments made for glory and beauty. He bears the names of the children of Israel on his shoulders and on his heart as a memorial, carries the judgment of Israel before the LORD continually, and wears the gold plate on his forehead to bear the iniquity of the holy things so the people are accepted. The bells on his robe must sound when he enters and leaves the holy place so that he does not die.

Chapter 29

Aaron is to be washed, clothed in the tunic, robe of the ephod, ephod, breastplate, band, turban, and holy crown, and anointed with oil on his head. He and his sons lay hands on the offerings, receive blood on the right ear, thumb, and toe, and are sprinkled with blood and oil. He is to be consecrated for seven days and will eat the consecration meat and bread with his sons.

Chapter 30

Aaron is to burn incense on the altar every morning when he tends the lamps and at evening when he lights them. He is to make atonement on the altar's horns once a year with sin offering blood. He and his sons must wash their hands and feet at the basin so they do not die, and they are to be anointed and sanctified to serve as priests.

Chapter 32

Aaron collects the people's golden earrings, fashions the molded calf, builds an altar before it, and proclaims a feast to the LORD. When Moses confronts him, he blames the people's evil bent and says he threw the gold into the fire and the calf came out. The text says he had let the people lose control, causing derision among their enemies, and the calf is described at the end as the one Aaron made.

Chapter 40

The LORD commands that Aaron be brought to the door of the Tent of Meeting, washed, clothed in the holy garments, and anointed so he may minister in the priest's office. Aaron later washes his hands and feet at the basin with Moses and his sons.

Aaron in Leviticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Aaron first appears

Abstract written by a model

Aaron is a priest and the brother of Moses, whose role is set out across the records as the anointed head of a priestly line. He and his sons are consecrated together, anointed with oil, and given a lasting portion from the children of Israel, including the breast of the peace offerings and a share of the twelve cakes and the remainder of the meal offering, eaten in a holy place. He wears the tunic, sash, robe, ephod, the breastplate containing the Urim and Thummim, and a turban with a golden plate. His duties include offering sin offerings and making atonement for himself and the people, blessing the people, keeping the lamp stand in order from evening to morning, distinguishing holy from common and clean from unclean, and teaching Israel the statutes. He is forbidden wine and strong drink when entering the Tent of Meeting, and he is commanded to stay there seven days with his sons. The account unfolds in stages. First come the laws of the offerings and the portion given to Aaron and his sons. Next he is washed, clothed, anointed and sanctified, with blood placed on his right ear, thumb and great toe. Then he carries out the offerings Moses commanded, killing the calf for his own sin offering and then offering the people's offerings, and he blesses the people twice. His sons Nadab and Abihu die when they come near before the LORD. Aaron holds his peace, the LORD speaks to him directly with the prohibition and charge, and when Moses questions the burned sin offering Aaron answers and Moses is satisfied. Eleazar and Ithamar remain as surviving sons. Later he is addressed with Moses on clean and unclean animals and on discharges. He is told how to enter the Most Holy Place, bathed and in holy linen, with a bull and a ram, casting lots for two goats, bringing incense and blood within the veil, atoning for the sanctuary and altar, and confessing Israel's sins over the live goat. The closing units address him as ancestor of the priestly line, whose offspring with a defect may not approach to offer the bread of God, then on separating from holy things and on offerings without defect, and finally on the lamps and the holy bread.

Aaron in Numbers

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Aaron first appears

Abstract written by a model

Aaron is the priest, head of the house of Levi, and father of four sons: Nadab (the firstborn), Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar. Nadab and Abihu died for offering strange fire and left no children. Together with Moses he helped lead Israel out of Egypt, and the records follow him through the wilderness until his death. Early on he helps Moses count Israel's men and the Levites. The Levites are given to him and his sons to serve. He and his sons keep the priesthood and camp east of the tabernacle. They cover the sanctuary furniture, appoint the Kohathites to their burdens, and direct the Gershonites' service. At the LORD's command they bless the children of Israel with a three-part formula, and he lights the seven lamps and offers the Levites as a wave offering. His failings and his vindication follow. He joins Miriam in speaking against Moses and is rebuked, then confesses the sin and asks that it not be counted against them. When rebels complain against him, he takes his censer and incense, makes atonement, and stands between the dead and the living so that the plague stops. His rod alone sprouts, buds, blossoms and bears almonds, marking him as the LORD's chosen man. The priestly office is defined as a heavy duty with provision attached. He and his sons bear the iniquity of the sanctuary and guard the altar. They receive wave offerings, first fruits and the firstborn, which must be redeemed. He has no inheritance in the land, because the LORD is his portion and inheritance. His end comes at Meribah, where he joins Moses before the rock and is barred from the promised land because of the rebellion against the LORD's word. At the LORD's command he goes up Mount Hor, his garments are put on his son Eleazar, and he dies on the mountaintop on the first day of the fifth month in the fortieth year after the exodus, aged 123. All the house of Israel weeps for him thirty days.
