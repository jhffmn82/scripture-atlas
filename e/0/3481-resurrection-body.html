Abstract written by a model

The resurrection body is described in Chapter 15 as sown perishable, in dishonor and weakness, a natural body, then raised imperishable, in glory and power, a spiritual body. Illustrated by a dying seed and differing celestial and terrestrial glories, it requires perishable and mortal to put on imperishability and immortality; believers bear the heavenly image.

Chapter 15

The body is sown perishable, in dishonor, and in weakness as a natural body, and is raised imperishable, in glory, and in power as a spiritual body. The speaker compares it to a seed that dies and receives a body from God, and to the differing glories of celestial and terrestrial bodies. Flesh and blood cannot inherit God's Kingdom, so the perishable must put on imperishability and the mortal immortality.
