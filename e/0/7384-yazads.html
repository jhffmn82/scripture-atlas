three and thirty lords in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where three and thirty lords first appears

Abstract written by a model

The three and thirty lords are holy chieftains and lords of the ritual order, lords of Righteousness the Best. They are worthy of sacrifice and come nearest round about the Hâvanis at Hâvani. Ahura Mazda instituted their observances, and Zarathustra spoke them forth. Within the ritual order, they are announced and approached with praise, sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and worship; the speaker also seeks forgiveness for offenses against them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna / Yasna I.

The lords of the ritual order include the day-lords, time periods, festivals, seasons, and many named holy beings. They are announced, celebrated, and addressed in sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise, and the speaker asks forgiveness for any offense against them.

Yasna II.

The speaker approaches numerous lords of the ritual order with praise in the Zaothra and Baresman. They include the Bountiful Immortals, Asnya, Hâvani, Sâvanghi, Vîsya, Rapithwina, Frâdatfshu, Zantuma, Ushahina, and many others, including the three and thirty nearest round about the Hâvanis.

Yasna VI.

The lords of the ritual order are repeatedly worshipped throughout the sacrifice. They include the thirty and three who approach nearest around about at Hâvani, whose observances were inculcated by Ahura Mazda and uttered forth by Zarathustra, as well as many other named beings.

Yazads in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Yazads first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Yazads are holy, beneficent heavenly and earthly beings who dispose all things aright; the Yasna presents them as fit for sacrifice and homage, praising, invoking, and glorifying them, including Nairya-sangha.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna / Yasna I.

The Yazads are described as beneficent beings who dispose of things aright, both heavenly and earthly. They are announced and completed to in the Yasna as beings fit for sacrifice and homage.

Yasna XVII.

Nairya-sangha is identified as the Yazad of the royal lineage and is worshipped. All the holy Yazads are praised, invoked, and glorified.

the Yazads in Yasna

L.H. Mills · the work’s page · read it where the Yazads first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Yazads are holy beings, bestowers of better gifts who abide beside Kine for protection and support. Mighty and powerful, they are asked to grant greater, more beautiful, and precious gifts, including Immortality and Welfare. As lords of the ritual order, they are worshipped and sacrificed to, including Apãm-napât and Nairya-sangha.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna LXIII (Sp. LXII). / Yasna LXIV (Sp. LXIII). / Yasna LXV (Sp. LXIV).

The Yazads are addressed as holy beings who rule mightily and possess power. They are asked to grant what is greater, better, more beautiful, and more precious, including Immortality and Welfare.

Yasna LXXI (Sp. LXX).

The Yazads, lords of the ritual order, are worshipped and sacrificed to. The unit especially mentions the most strenuous Yazad and several named Yazads, including Apãm-napât and Nairya-sangha.
