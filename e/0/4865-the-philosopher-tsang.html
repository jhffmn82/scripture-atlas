the philosopher Tsang

person · 3 works · 12 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: The philosopher Tsang, Tsang

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book VIII (T'ai-po), the philosopher Tsang teaches caution, sincerity, proper conduct, and endurance; while ill, he says that a man's words are good when he is about to die, and praises a person who is able, humble, patient, and unwilling to enter into altercation. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XII (Yen Yuan), the philosopher Tsang says that the superior man meets with his friends on grounds of culture and that their friendship helps his virtue. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XIX: Tsze-Chang, the philosopher Tsang says that Tsze-chang has an imposing manner and is difficult to join in practicing virtue; he teaches that mourning for parents may reveal what people have not otherwise fully shown, praises Mang Chwang’s filial piety in preserving his father’s ministers and government, and tells Yang Fu to pity those found guilty rather than rejoice in his own ability.
