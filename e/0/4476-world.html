world

Also called: its own, the world, the world itself, them, they, this world

Abstract written by a model

The world is presented as the realm opposed to Jesus and his followers. It is ruled by a prince who has been judged, does not know the Father, cannot receive or know the Spirit of truth, and will see Jesus no more after a little while. It hated Jesus first, then hates the disciples because they are not of it, persecuting them out of ignorance of the one who sent Jesus. Having seen his works, it has no excuse for its sin. The account unfolds across four chapters: Chapter 14 states its inability to receive the Spirit and Jesus' coming departure from its sight; Chapter 15 describes its hatred as fulfilling scripture; Chapter 16 says the Counselor will convict it of sin, righteousness and judgment, that it will rejoice while the disciples weep, and that Jesus has overcome it though the disciples have trouble in it; Chapter 17 shows it as the realm the disciples came from and are sent into. Jesus does not pray for it, but asks that the disciples' unity lead it to believe.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

The world cannot receive the Spirit of truth because it does not see or know him. Jesus says the world will see him no more after a little while, and that he will reveal himself to his followers and not to the world. He also says the prince of the world is coming but has nothing in him, and that the world is meant to know that he loves the Father.

Chapter 15

The world hates Jesus and, because they are not of the world, hates the disciples too. Jesus says it would love its own, that it persecutes because it does not know the one who sent him, and that it has no excuse for its sin since it has seen his works and hated both him and the Father. This fulfills the written saying, 'They hated me without a cause.'

Chapter 16

The world is to be convicted by the Counselor about sin because they don't believe in Jesus, about righteousness because Jesus goes to the Father, and about judgment because the prince of this world has been judged. It will rejoice while the disciples weep and lament, and Jesus says he has overcome it, though in it the disciples have trouble.

Chapter 17

The world is the realm from which the disciples were given and into which they are sent. It hated the disciples because they are not of the world, and it has not known the Father. Jesus does not pray for it, but he asks that the disciples' unity lead the world to believe and know that the Father sent him and loved them.
