Tsze-chang

person · 5 works · 5 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Chang

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book V (Kung-Ye Ch'Ang), Tsze-chang asks the Master to assess Tsze-wan and Ch'an Wan for loyalty, purity, and perfect virtue. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XII (Yen Yuan), Tsze-chang asks the Master about intelligence, virtue, delusions, government, and the qualities required for a distinguished officer. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XV (Wei Ling Kung), Tsze-chang seeks guidance on conduct that wins appreciation everywhere and on what should be told to the Music-master. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XIX: Tsze-Chang, Tsze-chang discusses the duties of a scholar trained for public service and the qualities of the superior man. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XX (Yao Yueh), Tsze-chang asks Confucius how a person in authority should conduct government properly and about the five excellent things and four bad things.

Tsze-chang in The Analects of Confucius, Book XIX: Tsze-Chang

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Tsze-chang first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tsze-chang says that the scholar trained for public duty should sacrifice his life when danger threatens, think of righteousness when gain is offered, and be reverential in sacrifice and concerned with proper grief in mourning. He also asks about mutual intercourse and says that the superior man honors the talented and virtuous, bears with all, praises the good, and pities the incompetent.
