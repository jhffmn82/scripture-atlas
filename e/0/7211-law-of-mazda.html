Abstract written by a model

In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII, the law of Zarathustra is a fiend-destroying law greater, better, and fairer than other utterances, allowing remission through authority and repentance. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII, the law of Mazda is Mazda’s holy, fiend-destroying law, fulfilled through worship, holiness, cleansing, and good conduct. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), the law of Mazda is a fiend-destroying law that procures purity through good thoughts, words, and deeds and is not renounced by Zarathustra. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, the Law of the worshippers of Mazda is worshipped as the Lord’s will and identified with holiness, sacrifice, prayer, and fiend-destroying goodness. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, the Law of Mazda is a captive power recovered by destroying Vohu-Manô’s destroyer and is associated with Mithra’s strength. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, the law of Ahura is a righteous, unconquerable revelation that destroys the Daêvas, brings holiness, and is supported by Zarathustra and Vîstâspa. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, the good Law of the worshippers of Mazda is a holy, victorious, protective, and life-giving law associated with Zarathustra.

law of Mazda in Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where law of Mazda first appears

Abstract written by a model

The law of Mazda is a holy law belonging to Mazda, taught by Ahura Mazda to Yima before Zarathustra; Yima refuses to preach or bear it. It is fulfilled and strengthened through worship, sowing corn, holiness, and cleansing oneself with good thoughts, words, and deeds. For one who confesses sin and resolves not to repeat forbidden deeds, it removes sin and cleanses every evil thought, word, and deed. It is a fiend-destroying law, greater, better, and fairer than other utterances.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard II.

The law of Ahura is taught by Ahura Mazda to Yima before Zarathustra. Yima does not accept the role of preacher and bearer of the law.

Fargard III.

The law of Mazda is fulfilled and strengthened through worship, sowing corn, and acts of holiness. It takes away the confessed sins of a person who acknowledges them and resolves not to repeat forbidden deeds, cleansing evil thoughts, words, and deeds.

Fargard V.

The law of Mazda procures purity for the person who cleanses the self with good thoughts, words, and deeds. It is described as a fiend-destroying law greater, better, and fairer than other utterances.

Fargard VIII.

The law of Mazda removes the bonds of sin from one who confesses it and resolves not to repeat forbidden deeds. It is said to remove or cleanse sins including breach of trust, murdering one of the faithful, burying a corpse, deeds without atonement, and every evil thought, word, and deed.

the Law of the worshippers of Mazda in Yashts (Yasts) I-XII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where the Law of the worshippers of Mazda first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Law of the worshippers of Mazda is worshipped and is the will of the Lord. It is identified with holiness, governing sacrifice, prayer, and invocations, which must follow holiness’s perfection. Named with the waters made by Mazda and among recipients of sacrifice, it is fiend-destroying and the truest giver of good things, whose offspring belong to the good principle.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

II. Haptân Yast.

The Law of the worshippers of Mazda is worshipped. It is named alongside the waters coming in the shape of a horse, made by Mazda.

VIII. Tîr Yast.

The law of holiness is identified with the will of the Lord and governs the sacrifices, prayers, and invocations offered to Tistrya. Tistrya’s worship must be performed according to the perfection of holiness.

XI. Srôsh Yast Hâdhôkht.

The Law of the worshippers of Mazda is called the truest giver of all good things that are offspring of the good principle. It is also named among the friends and bodies receiving sacrifice, and is described as fiend-destroying.

Abstract written by a model

The Law of Mazda is a captive power in the hands of the ungodly, recoverable by one who destroys Vohu-Manô’s destroyer. It is closely associated with Mithra, opening his way and making his strength ten thousandfold, though scorned by an unholy priest.

The Law of Mazda is described as having been carried away as a prisoner into the hands of the ungodly. A person who destroys the destroyer of Vohu-Manô takes the Law of Mazda away from them.

XXIV.

The Law of Mazda is associated with Mithra and is named among the powers and beings that an unholy priest scorns. Mithra's strength is said to be ten thousandfold when connected with the Law of Mazda.

law of Ahura in Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where law of Ahura first appears

Abstract written by a model

The law of Ahura is a revelation and word that destroys the Daêvas. Praised as righteous, innocent, and unconquerable, it brings holiness and defeats enemies. Zarathustra first bears it among nations; Vîstâspa supports, professes, and follows it in thought, speech, and action.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

XXIII.

The law of Ahura is described as the word that destroys the Daêvas and as a revelation of the Law. Vîstâspa supports it, and Zarathustra is its first bearer among the nations.

XXVI.

The law is praised for its righteousness, innocence, and unconquerable power. It is associated with the extermination of enemies and with those who obtain holiness.

XIV.

The Law is followed in thought, speech, and action by king Vîstâspa, who professes it and uses it to destroy his foes and cause the Daêvas to retire.

good Law of the worshippers of Mazda in Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where good Law of the worshippers of Mazda first appears

Abstract written by a model

The good Law of the worshippers of Mazda is a holy, good, victorious law associated with Zarathustra. It spreads through all seven Karshvares after his sacrifices, and is worshipped through sacrifice, prayer, propitiation, and glorification. It supplies good stores and is linked to lawful thought, speech, and action; Zarathustra asks Hutaosa to spread and make it known. It embodies holiness, righteousness, innocence, unconquerable power, and the defeat of enemies. Finally, it is protective and life-giving: it should be supported, proclaimed, practiced, and offered even to the unfriendly, while followers care for the poor and avoid evil conduct.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

XXIII.

The good Law of the worshippers of Mazda is said to spread through all seven Karshvares of the earth after Zarathustra offers sacrifices with libations and bundles of baresma.

XVI. Dîn Yast.

The good Law of the worshippers of Mazda is worshipped and receives sacrifice, prayer, propitiation, and glorification. It is described as a supplier of good stores and is blessed together with Kista at the end of the unit.

XVII. Ashi Yast.

The law is associated with thinking, speaking, and acting according to the law, and with the good law that Hutaosa and certain nations are asked to follow. Zarathustra asks that Hutaosa spread the law and make it known.

XIX. Zamyâd Yast.

The Law of Mazda is associated with holiness, righteousness, innocence, unconquerable power, and the extermination of enemies. Zarathustra thinks, speaks, and acts according to it, and the Glory of Zarathustra enters its will.

XXIV. Vîstâsp Yast.

The Law is presented as holy, victorious, protective, and life-giving. It should be supported, proclaimed, practiced, embraced, and given even to those who are unfriendly, while its followers should care for the poor and avoid evil conduct.
