judgment on Judah in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where judgment on Judah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judgment on Judah is the LORD's announced punishment of Judah and Jerusalem for forsaking him and practicing idolatry, including burning incense to other gods and Baal. It begins with evil breaking out from the northern kingdoms, which besiege Jerusalem and Judah's cities. It then becomes unavoidable: the LORD will not listen to their cries, their gods cannot save them, and the planted olive tree is burned. It culminates in death by sword and famine, unburied bodies, the end of mourning and of mirth and gladness, and exile to an unknown land. Iniquity is repaid double, and nothing is hidden from the LORD. It is likened to a whirling storm bursting on the wicked.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

The LORD announces judgment in the form of evil coming from the north, with the northern kingdoms besieging Jerusalem and the cities of Judah. The stated cause is that the people have forsaken him, burned incense to other gods, and worshiped the works of their own hands. The LORD says he will utter his judgments against them for all their wickedness.

Chapter 11

The LORD declares he will bring evil on Judah and Jerusalem that they cannot escape, and he will not listen when they cry to him. Their gods will not save them, and the olive tree he planted and named beautiful is set on fire with its branches broken because of the evil of Israel and Judah in offering incense to Baal.

Chapter 16

The LORD pronounces death by sword and famine, bodies left unburied as food for birds and animals, the end of mourning customs and joy, and expulsion to an unknown land. Their iniquity will be repaid double, and the LORD says he sees all their ways and nothing is hidden from him.
