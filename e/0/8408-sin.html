sin

topic · 5 works · 13 quoted facts · 13 connections
Also called: causes of sin, evil deeds, five cardinal sins, iniquity, sinful, sinful actions, sinful acts, sinful causes, sinful conduct, sinful pleasure, sins, the sins, wickedness

Abstract written by a model

In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), sinful conduct includes greedy or improper taking and eating of food and disregarding prescribed practices. In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, sin arises from harm, violence, false speech, attachment, and passions, increasing Karman and bringing misery. In Sutrakritanga, Book II, sin arises through killing, injury, harmful conduct and speech, and related acts, which a monk avoids and renounces. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, sin causes descent through endless Samsâra, while abstaining from it is necessary for liberation. In Hymns of Guru Nanak, sin defiles man inwardly, brings punishment, and is washed away through humility, God's Name, and the Guru's instruction.

sin in Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where sin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sin is committed through harming living beings, destroying seeds, pleasure in fire, careless conduct, violence, false speech, attachment, and assenting to others’ injury. Its causes include deceit, greed, anger, and pride. It increases Karman and brings misery, wailing, suffering, trembling, and misery on decease; wisdom requires abstaining from its causes.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Seventh Lecture, Called Description of the Wicked.

Sin is committed through harming living beings, destroying seeds, and using fire for pleasure. Creatures that have committed sins wail, suffer, and tremble.

Ninth Lecture, Called the Law.

Sin is caused by deceit, greed, anger, pride, and harmful or improper actions. A wise man should combat these causes and abstain from them.

Tenth Lecture, Called Carefulness.

Sin is committed by injuring beings and by employing or assenting to others who injure them. It also arises through careless conduct, violent deeds, false speech, and other attachments.

sin in Sutrakritanga, Book II

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where sin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sin arises through killing or injuring living beings, slaughter, pleasure-seeking, passions, harmful conduct and speech, and food prepared through killing. It can be incurred even without sinful thoughts, speech, bodily functions, conscious consideration, or dreams. A monk avoids and renounces harm, love, and hatred, while those denying guilt for such acts are condemned as teaching and believing in error.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

I say: here in the East, West, North, and South there are some men, viz. Âryas,

Sins arise through killing living beings, acquiring objects of pleasure, sinful actions, passions, and other forms of misconduct. A monk abstains from these sins and commits himself to neither love nor hatred.

Fourth Lecture, Called Renunciation of Activity.

Sin is committed through failure to avoid and renounce harm toward the six classes of living beings. It may be committed even when the perpetrator lacks sinful thoughts, speech, bodily functions, an internal organ, conscious consideration of activity, or dreams.

Sixth Lecture, Called .

Sin is incurred through injuring living beings, slaughter, uncontrolled conduct, harmful speech, and partaking of food specially prepared through killing. Those who deny guilt for such acts are condemned as teaching and believing in error.

sin in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where sin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sin causes descent through endless Samsâra and arises from injury to living beings through ignorant ritual practice. Abstaining from it is necessary for liberation, while fools persist in it. Hatred and lust ensnare sinners, but Harikêsa avoids sin through guarded thought, speech, and body and rejects violent ritual acts.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Sixth Lecture.

Abstaining from sins is necessary for liberation; merely attending a teacher does not deliver a person from misery. Fools who continue through their sins believe themselves wise.

Eighth Lecture.

A monk who loves not even those who love him will be freed from sin and hatred. Ignorant sinners who yield to hatred and lust become ensnared and may go to hell.

Twelfth Lecture

Harikêsa protects his thoughts, speech, and body from sin. He teaches that violence in tending fires and using ritual materials causes living beings to be injured and leads the ignorant to commit sins repeatedly.

sin in Hymns of Guru Nanak

Guru Nanak · the work’s page · read it where sin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sin defiles man inwardly through sinful pleasure, though it seems sweet briefly before its fruit turns bitter. It arises when people neglect truth, virtue, self-restraint, and the Name while pretending goodness, and it brings chains and punishment at Death's gate. Outward washing cannot erase it; humility, God's Name, and the Guru's instruction wash it away, ending it when the true Guru unites man with God.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Majh ki War

Sin defiles a person inwardly even when the body and clothes are externally cleansed. The text says that sin is washed away through humility, God's Name, and the Guru's instruction.

Asa Chhant

Sin appears sweet for only a short time, after which its fruit becomes bitter. The fruit of sin is compared to a fleeting wave of the ocean or flash of lightning.

Ramkali .. Ramkali ki War I

Sin is committed by those who neglect truth, virtue, self-restraint, and the Name while pretending to be good. Sin brings punishment at death and cannot be erased by outward washing alone.
