sin

Also called: sinful, sinful pleasure, sins

Abstract written by a model

Sin defiles man inwardly through sinful pleasure, though it seems sweet briefly before its fruit turns bitter. It arises when people neglect truth, virtue, self-restraint, and the Name while pretending goodness, and it brings chains and punishment at Death's gate. Outward washing cannot erase it; humility, God's Name, and the Guru's instruction wash it away, ending it when the true Guru unites man with God.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Majh ki War

Sin defiles a person inwardly even when the body and clothes are externally cleansed. The text says that sin is washed away through humility, God's Name, and the Guru's instruction.

Asa Chhant

Sin appears sweet for only a short time, after which its fruit becomes bitter. The fruit of sin is compared to a fleeting wave of the ocean or flash of lightning.

Ramkali .. Ramkali ki War I

Sin is committed by those who neglect truth, virtue, self-restraint, and the Name while pretending to be good. Sin brings punishment at death and cannot be erased by outward washing alone.
