Nature

Abstract written by a model

In Chuang Tzŭ, Nature is identified with a man’s parents, gives human beings form, labor, old age, and death, produces the visible universe through the Positive and Negative principles, and brings safety and true greatness when followed. In Chuang Tzŭ, the natural is an abiding inner principle whose cultivation restores virtue and unconditioned fitness, exemplified by horses and oxen having four feet. In The Doctrine of the Mean, nature is what Heaven has conferred, and following it is the Path of duty that unites external and internal through its full development.

Nature in Chuang Tzŭ

Chuang Tzŭ · the work’s page · read it where Nature first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nature is identified with a man’s parents, commanding him and giving human beings form, labor, old age, and death. It is the substance of life and a powerful bandit from which there is no escape. Through interaction with the Positive and Negative principles, it produces the visible universe. It develops through its own events and laws, where God is manifested; accord with it brings safety and true greatness.

Nan Po Tzŭ K'uei

Nature is identified with a man's parents and is said to command him as parents command a son. It gives human beings their forms, labor, old age, and death.

CHAPTER XXIII.

Nature is described as the substance of life and as a powerful bandit from which there is no escape. Its interaction with the Positive and Negative principles produces the visible universe. Nature itself does not injure; man's own heart causes the injury.

Kuan Chung being at the point of death, Duke Huan went to see him.

Nature develops through its own events and laws, and the divine man does not force artificial measures into it. Accord with nature is presented as the way to safety and true greatness.

the natural in Chuang Tzŭ

Chuang Tzŭ · the work’s page · read it where the natural first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nature is an abiding inner principle enclosing the spiritual part. Cultivating it restores virtue and unconditioned fitness, while artificial will and fame should not erase what naturally abides, as horses and oxen having four feet exemplify.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XII.

Nature is the original condition that should prevail over artificial arrangements. By cultivating nature, one is carried back to virtue and ultimately becomes unconditioned, in accordance with the eternal fitness of things.

CHAPTER XVII.

The natural abides within and virtue abides in the natural. Horses and oxen having four feet exemplifies the natural, which should not be obliterated by the artificial, will, or fame.
