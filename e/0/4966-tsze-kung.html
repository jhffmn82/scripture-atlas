Tsze-kung

person · 9 works · 20 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Ts'ze, Tzŭ Kung, the disciple

Abstract written by a model

In Chuang Tzŭ, Tzŭ Kung, Confucius's chief disciple, visits Confucius, questions him about mourners, rules, and divine men, offers a gardener a well-sweep, and visits poor Yüan Hsien. In The Analects of Confucius, Book V (Kung-Ye Ch'Ang), Tsze-kung is described as a gemmed sacrificial utensil, asks the Master to assess him, declines comparison with Hui, expresses a wish for reciprocal restraint, and asks about the Master's teachings and Kung-wan's title. In The Analects of Confucius, Book VI (Yung Yey), Tsze-kung asks whether one who benefits the people and assists all can be called perfectly virtuous. In The Analects of Confucius, Book VI (Yung Yey), Ts'ze is described as intelligent and capable of serving easily as an officer of government. In The Analects of Confucius, Book IX (Tsze Han), Tsze-kung says Heaven endowed the Master abundantly and asks whether a beautiful gem should be kept in a case or sold for a good price. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XII (Yen Yuan), Tsze-kung asks about governmental requisites and friendship and judges Chi Tsze-ch'ang's words concerning speech, ornament, and substance. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XIII: Tsze-Lu, Tsze-kung asks about the qualities of officers, lower ranks, contemporary government, and judging people loved or hated by their neighbors. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XV (Wei Ling Kung), Tsze-kung discusses the Master's learning and memory and asks about practicing virtue and a lifelong rule of practice.

and 2 more works

In The Analects of Confucius, Book XVII (Yang Ho), Tsze-kung asks whether the superior man has hatreds and what disciples would record without Confucius's speech, and lists the people he hates. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XIX: Tsze-Chang, Tsze-kung discusses Chau's reputation, the superior man's faults, Chung-ni's learning, and Chung-ni's unmatched attainments.

Tzŭ Kung in Chuang Tzŭ

Abstract written by a model

Tzŭ Kung is a human being, Confucius’s disciple and chief disciple, portrayed as a visitor in a fine chariot. He questions Confucius about unconventional mourners, rules, and divine men; offers a gardener a well-sweep and is rebuked; then visits poor Yüan Hsien, whose reply leaves him abashed.

Tsze-kung in The Analects of Confucius, Book V (Kung-Ye Ch'Ang)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Tsze-kung first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tsze-kung is described as a utensil, specifically a gemmed sacrificial utensil. He asks the Master for an assessment of himself and what kind of utensil he is. He refuses comparison with Hui, saying Hui grasps a whole subject from one point while he reaches only a second. He states a wish for reciprocal restraint toward others, but the Master says he has not attained it. He says some of the Master's teachings can be heard while discourses on man's nature and Heaven cannot, and he asks why Kung-wan received the title Wan.
