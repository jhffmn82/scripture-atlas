prayer

Also called: In praying, Let my prayer, The insistent prayer of a righteous person, my petitions, my prayer, pray, prayed, prayer (David's prayer), prayer (the psalmist's prayer), praying, the prayer of faith, vain repetitions

Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the psalmist's prayer is asked to rise before the LORD like incense and evening sacrifice and is declared always set against evil deeds. In Psalms, David's prayer asks the LORD to hear his petitions, relieve him, answer quickly, deliver and guide him, revive him, and cut off his enemies. In The Gospel According to Matthew, prayer is taught to be done secretly without vain repetitions because the Father knows people's needs beforehand. In The Gospel According to Luke, prayer is taught through Jesus' model prayer and his urging of persistence in asking, seeking, and knocking. In The Gospel According to Luke, prayer is presented through teachings on persistent prayer, contrasting the Pharisee's self-righteous prayer with the tax collector's humble plea for mercy. In The Letter from James, prayer is prescribed for the suffering and sick, with the prayer of faith bringing healing and forgiveness and a righteous person's insistent prayer being powerfully effective.

Abstract written by a model

David's prayer opens with a plea that the LORD hear his petitions and relieve him, and it continues through requests for a quick answer, deliverance, guidance, and revival. Prayer is prescribed for the suffering, and for the sick together with the elders' anointing with oil in the Lord's name. The prayer of faith will heal the sick and bring forgiveness, and the insistent prayer of a righteous person is powerfully effective, as shown by Elijah, who prayed that it would not rain, and then prayed again so that the sky gave rain.
