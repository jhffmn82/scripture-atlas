Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Matthew, the coming of the Son of Man is described as visible like lightning, occurring after suffering, with him coming on the clouds with power and glory to gather the chosen. In The Good News According to Mark, the coming of the Son of Man follows cosmic disturbances, with him arriving in clouds with great power and glory to gather the chosen. In The Gospel According to Luke, the coming of the Son of Man is described as sudden and unmistakable, preceded by his suffering and rejection, and likened to the days of Noah and Lot. In 1 Thessalonians, the Lord's coming and the resurrection of the dead involve the Lord descending from heaven, the dead in Christ rising first, and living believers being caught up to meet him. In The Letter from James, the coming of the Lord is said to be at hand, and believers are urged to be patient because the judge stands at the door. In Revelation, Christ's coming is announced as soon and brings a reward for each person's work, blessing those who keep the prophecy's words.

Abstract written by a model

The coming of the Son of Man, described by Jesus in Chapter 24. It will be visible like lightning from east to west. After the suffering, his sign appears, tribes mourn, and he comes on the clouds with power and glory, sending angels to gather the chosen. Only the Father knows the day and hour; it arrives unexpectedly, as in Noah's days.

coming of the Son of Man in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where coming of the Son of Man first appears

Abstract written by a model

The coming of the Son of Man is described as sudden and unmistakable, like lightning flashing across the sky. It must first be preceded by his suffering and rejection by this generation. Likened to the days of Noah and Lot, ordinary life ends in sudden destruction, one taken, another left.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 17

Jesus says the Son of Man will be like lightning flashing across the sky in his day, but must first suffer many things and be rejected by this generation. He compares it to the days of Noah and Lot, when people went on with ordinary life until sudden destruction, and says that on that night one person will be taken and another left.
