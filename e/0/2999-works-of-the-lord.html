In Psalms, the works of the LORD are described as great, honorable, majestic, truthful, just, enduring, and worthy of remembrance. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the works of the Lord are described as ordered by his judgment, bounded and arranged for all time, glorious, obedient, enduring, and without imperfection.

praise of God's creation in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · about 179 BC · the work’s page · read it where praise of God's creation first appears

Abstract written by a model

A topic of praise for the Lord's creation: all his works are exceedingly good, visible to his eyes, and each serves its purpose and is approved in its time, so none should be questioned or called worse. It then turns to exhortation, urging people to look at the rainbow, praise its maker, and exalt the Lord with full strength, though no praise suffices and many greater works remain hidden.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 39

All the works of the Lord are exceedingly good, and every command will be done in its time. No one may ask "What is this?" or "Why is that?" because all things are created for their own uses and will be approved in their time. The speaker left in writing that the Lord will supply every need in its time and that none is to be called worse than another.

Chapter 43

The chapter urges people to look at the rainbow and praise its maker, and to glorify and exalt the Lord with full strength without growing weary. It says words cannot say enough, that he will surpass any praise, and that many greater works remain hidden because only a few have been seen.

works of the Lord in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · about 179 BC · the work’s page · read it where works of the Lord first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Lord's works are described as ordered by his judgment from the beginning, with boundaries set at their making and arrangement for all time and generations. They are full of his glory, obedient, never hungry, weary, or disobedient, and do not crowd one another. Most remain hidden like an unseen tempest, and even the saints cannot declare them all. They endure in many uses, exist in opposing pairs, and nothing is added or diminished; none is imperfect.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 16

The Lord's works are in his judgment from the beginning, and from their making he determined their boundaries. He arranged them for all time and for their generations. They are not hungry or weary, do not cease, do not push aside one another, and never disobey his word, though the majority of them are hidden like an unseen tempest.

Chapter 42

The speaker declares that the Lord's works are full of his glory, firmly settled so the universe may be established, and that even the saints cannot declare all of them. They are ordered in wisdom, nothing added or diminished, all live and endure in many uses and are obedient, exist in opposing pairs, and none is imperfect.
