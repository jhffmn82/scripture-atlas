Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang)

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This portion of Mencius records dialogues chiefly between Mencius and Wan Zhang, with King Xuan of Qi appearing near the end. It begins with questions about Shun: his filial conduct, marriage, accession, treatment of Xiang, and relation to Gu Sou. Mencius argues that Shun was accepted by Heaven and the people, and that succession rested on Heaven’s ordinance, explaining the different outcomes for Shun, Yu, Yi, and Qi. He then defends accounts of Yi Yin’s counsel to Tang and corrects stories about Confucius’s lodgings and Bai Li Xi’s career, insisting on Bai Li Xi’s wisdom and virtue. The discussion broadens into models of sage conduct, Bo Yi, Yi Yin, Hui of Liu Xia, and Confucius, and rules governing rank, territory, gifts, friendship, office, poverty, marriage, righteousness, and robbery. Finally, Mencius sets standards for scholars’ support and dealings with princes, respectful summons, learning through worthy friends and antiquity, and ministers’ duties to remonstrate, dethrone, or leave when rulers fail.

Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang) / Part I / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 7

Mencius answers Wan Zhang’s questions about Shun’s filial conduct, marriage, accession, treatment of Xiang, and relationship with Gu Sou. He explains that Heaven and the people accepted Shun through his conduct, and that succession depended on Heaven’s ordinance rather than personal appointment. Mencius contrasts the successions of Shun, Yu, Yi, and Qi, explaining why Qi succeeded Yu’s son while Yi did not receive the throne. He also explains that Yi Yin approached Tang through the principles of Yao and Shun, urging Tang to attack Xia and save its people.

Chapter 8 .. Chapter 9

Wan Zhang asks Mencius about stories concerning Confucius and Bai Li Xi. Mencius says Confucius lodged with Yan Chou You in Wei and with the city-master Chang after leaving Lu and Wei, not with the ulcer-doctor or the attendant Qi Huan. Mencius says Bai Li Xi was from Yu, did not remonstrate when Jin borrowed passage through Yu to attack Guo, later went to Qin, and became minister to the duke Mu of Qin. Mencius argues that Bai Li Xi was wise and possessed talents and virtue, and denies that he sold himself by feeding oxen.

Part II / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 5

Mencius discusses the conduct of Bo Yi, Yi Yin, Hui of Liu Xia, and Confucius, presenting them as contrasting sages whose examples influence others. He explains Zhou arrangements of ranks, territories, emoluments, friendship, presents, propriety, and the conditions under which Confucius accepted office. The unit also examines how rulers, common people, poverty, office, marriage, righteousness, and robbery relate to proper conduct.

Chapter 6 .. Chapter 9

Wan Zhang and Mencius discuss when scholars may accept support from princes, why scholars without office should not receive princely pay, and how rulers should properly honor virtue and talents. They also discuss why scholars should not seek interviews with princes and how summons must follow propriety. Mencius explains that scholars make friends with virtuous scholars and, through study, with the men of antiquity. King Xuan of Qi asks about high ministers, learning that relatives should remonstrate and may dethrone a prince, while ministers of a different surname should leave the State if remonstrance fails.
