Abstract written by a model

David's lamentation over Absalom's death, in the room over the city gate. Told by the Cushite, he weeps and cries that he wishes he had died instead. His loud grieving turns the day's victory into mourning for the people, until Joab's rebuke makes him rise and sit in the gate.

Chapter 18

After hearing from the Cushite that Absalom was dead, David was much moved and went up to the room over the gate and wept. He cried out, "My son Absalom! My son, my son Absalom! I wish I had died instead of you, Absalom, my son, my son!"

Chapter 19

David's loud weeping with covered face over Absalom turns the day's victory into mourning for the people. Joab's rebuke ends it, as David arises and sits in the gate.
