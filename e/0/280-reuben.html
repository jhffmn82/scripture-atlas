Abstract written by a model

In Numbers, the camp of Reuben is a tribal division encamped on the south side, led by Elizur, and marching second with Reuben, Simeon, and Gad. In Numbers, Reuben is a tribe led by Elizur, counted at 46,500 fighting men, and granted land east of the Jordan after promising to cross armed ahead of Israel. In Deuteronomy, the Reubenites possess land east of the Jordan and send their men of valor across armed before their brothers while their families and livestock remain in the cities. In The Book of Joshua, the Reubenites receive an inheritance east of the Jordan, cross ahead of their brothers, pledge obedience to Joshua, and build an altar as a witness that the LORD is God. In 1 Chronicles, the Reubenites descend from Reuben's sons, spread through Gilead, fight the Hagrites, and are later carried away by Assyria.

Reubenites in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · about 600 BC · the work’s page · read it where Reubenites first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Reubenites are a tribe of Israel who received their inheritance east of the Jordan from Moses, sharing the region with Gad and half of Manasseh. Their land, bordered by the Jordan's bank, ran from Aroer by the Arnon valley and included Heshbon, Dibon, Jahaz, Beth Peor, the plain cities and Sihon's kingdom. In Joshua 1 they are reminded to leave families and livestock behind while their fighting men cross ahead of their brothers, and they pledge obedience to Joshua as to Moses. In chapter 22 Joshua releases them to Gilead, where they build an altar by the Jordan, defending it as a witness that the LORD is God.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Joshua / Chapter 1

The Reubenites are addressed by Joshua and reminded of Moses' command that their wives, little ones, and livestock stay in the land east of the Jordan while their armed fighting men cross ahead of their brothers. They join in answering that they will do all Joshua commands and go wherever he sends them.

Chapter 13

The Reubenites received, with the Gadites, the inheritance Moses gave them east of the Jordan. Their border ran from Aroer by the Arnon valley, including Heshbon, Dibon, Jahaz, Beth Peor and the cities of the plain and Sihon's kingdom, with the Jordan's bank as their boundary.

Chapter 22

The Reubenites are released by Joshua and return to Gilead, where together with Gad and Manasseh they build a great altar by the Jordan. Accused of rebellion by Israel's delegation, they explain it is a witness and not for sacrifice, and they name it "A Witness Between Us that the LORD is God."

Reubenites in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · 449 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Reubenites first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Reubenites descend from Reuben's sons Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi, with Beerah as prince. Their livestock spread them from Aroer to Nebo, Baal Meon, and eastward in Gilead. They fought the Hagrites alongside allies and were later carried away by Assyria.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

The sons of Reuben are Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi, with a line through Joel to Beerah, the prince of the Reubenites. They lived from Aroer to Nebo and Baal Meon and eastward toward the Euphrates because their livestock multiplied in Gilead. They fought the Hagrites with the Gadites and half-tribe of Manasseh, and were later carried away by Assyria.
