Benjamin in Genesis

author unknown · about 449 BC · the work’s page · read it where Benjamin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Benjamin is the youngest son of Jacob, born to Rachel during her fatal labor on the way to Ephrath. Rachel named him Benoni, but his father named him Benjamin. He is Joseph's full brother, and Jacob, who loves him as the child of his old age, keeps him home after Joseph is presumed dead, calling him the only one left of his mother's sons. Joseph demands that Benjamin be brought to Egypt to prove the brothers' honesty. Jacob yields after Judah pledges to answer for him. Joseph blesses him, weeps, and gives him a portion five times larger than the others'. Joseph's silver cup is then found in Benjamin's sack, and Joseph declares he will become a slave, prompting Judah's speech.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 35

Benjamin is born to Rachel during her hard labor on the way to Ephrath. Rachel names him Benoni as she dies, but his father names him Benjamin. He is listed among the twelve sons of Jacob as a son of Rachel with Joseph.

Chapter 42

Benjamin, Joseph's brother, is kept home by Jacob for fear of harm. Joseph demands that he be brought to Egypt as proof of the brothers' honesty, and Jacob refuses to let him go, calling him the only one left of his mother's sons.

Chapter 43

Benjamin is sent down to Egypt with his brothers after Judah pledges to answer for him. Joseph sees him, calls him his brother, his mother's son, and blesses him, and is moved to tears. At the meal he is given a portion five times as large as any of his brothers'.

Chapter 44

Benjamin, the youngest brother, had Joseph's silver cup placed in his sack and it was found there. Joseph declared that he would be made a slave. Judah's speech describes him as the little child of his father's old age, the only one left of his mother, and the one his father loves.

Benjamin in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · 449 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Benjamin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Benjamin is a patriarch who fathered five sons in order: Bela firstborn, Ashbel, Aharah, Nohah, and Rapha. The chapter ends by tracing all listed descendants, including Ulam's archers, to him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

Benjamin became the father of Bela his firstborn, Ashbel, Aharah, Nohah, and Rapha. The chapter closes by saying that all the listed descendants, including Ulam's many archer sons and grandsons, were of the sons of Benjamin.
