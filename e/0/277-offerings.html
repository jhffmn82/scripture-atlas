Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Abraham is commanded to offer Isaac as a burnt offering but ultimately offers a ram instead. In Leviticus, a year-old lamb is brought as a burnt offering after purification, or birds are used if the woman cannot afford a lamb. In Leviticus, offerings are sacrifices presented so the giver is accepted, with rules for animals, vows, free will gifts, and thanksgiving sacrifices. In Leviticus, the burnt offering remains on the altar hearth overnight, is arranged on the altar each morning, and has its skin given to the priest. In Leviticus, the burnt offering makes atonement and is wholly burned on the altar after prescribed preparation. In Numbers, a burnt offering is an offering made by fire, with prescribed animals, meal offerings, and drink offerings for each holy day. In 1 Chronicles, David offers burnt offerings and peace offerings when the ark is placed in the tent, while priests offer burnt offerings continually at Gibeon. In The Second Book of Chronicles, the king and people offer sacrifices before the LORD, including vast numbers of cattle and sheep, and Solomon provides space for them.

and 5 more works

In The Second Book of Chronicles, Solomon offers burnt offerings according to Moses’ command on Sabbaths, new moons, and the three yearly feasts. In The Book of Ezra, burnt offerings are offered morning and evening along with daily, new moon, feast, and free will offerings. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, offerings and sacrifices are commanded and associated with obedience, kindness, alms-giving, first fruits, and tithes. In The Book of Ezekiel, burnt offerings are prescribed for Sabbaths, new moons, the prince’s free will offerings, and daily offerings of a year-old lamb. In The Letter to the Hebrews, sacrifices under the first covenant are contrasted with Christ’s single self-sacrifice, which puts away sin.
