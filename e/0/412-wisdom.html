Abstract written by a model

In Proverbs, Wisdom is a personified woman-like figure who calls people to heed her, promises security and blessing, rebukes those who reject her, and is portrayed as foundational to creation and wise living. In Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher, wisdom is examined as valuable and protective yet troubled by sorrow, vanity, human limitations, and the contempt faced by the poor man's wisdom. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the wise man seeks the wisdom of the ancients and, through the spirit of understanding, speaks wisdom that nations and the congregation praise. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, wisdom is presented as a gift from the Lord that the speaker seeks through prayer, finds in purity, and receives as instruction and rest.

Wisdom in Proverbs

author unknown · 969 to 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where Wisdom first appears

Abstract written by a model

Wisdom is a personified, woman-like figure in Proverbs who calls aloud at city gates, public squares and high places. She promises security to those who listen and rebukes the simple, mockers and fools who refuse her, saying she will not answer their calls. Her worth exceeds silver, gold and rubies; in her right hand is length of days and in her left riches and honor, her ways are pleasant and her paths peace, and she is a tree of life to those who hold her. The LORD used wisdom to found the earth, and the wise will inherit glory. She then builds a house with seven pillars, prepares meat, wine and a table, and sends out maidens to invite the simple to leave their ways and live. Later the records turn to how wisdom shows in people: on the lips of the discerning, in accepting commandments and correction, and in humility, while pride, despising a neighbor and folly mark its lack. The wise win souls, many counselors bring victory, and the foolish are punished by their own folly. The records close by declaring that getting wisdom is far better than gold and that understanding is a fountain of life.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Proverbs / Chapter 1

Wisdom is personified as a woman who calls aloud in the streets, public squares, noisy places and city gates. She rebukes the simple, mockers and fools for refusing her, says she will laugh at their disaster and not answer when they call, and promises that whoever listens to her will dwell securely without fear of harm.

Chapter 3

Wisdom is described as bringing happiness to whoever finds her, with profit and return better than silver and fine gold and a worth beyond rubies. In her right hand is length of days and in her left riches and honor, her ways are pleasant and her paths peace, and she is a tree of life to those who hold her. The LORD used wisdom to found the earth, and the wise will inherit glory.

Chapter 9

Wisdom builds her house with seven pillars, prepares meat and wine, sets her table, and sends out her maidens. She cries from the highest places of the city, inviting the simple to eat her bread and drink her wine, leave their simple ways, and live.

Chapter 10

Wisdom is found on the lips of those with discernment and is produced by the mouth of the righteous. It shows in accepting commandments, heeding correction and laying up knowledge, while those void of understanding are met with the rod.

Chapter 11

Wisdom comes with humility, whereas pride brings shame. One who despises his neighbor lacks it, and a person of understanding holds his peace. A nation without wise guidance falls, but many counselors bring victory. He who is wise wins souls, and the foolish serve the wise of heart.

Chapter 16

Getting wisdom is declared far better than gold, and getting understanding is better than silver. Understanding is called a fountain of life to its possessor, while fools are punished by their own folly.

wisdom in Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher

author unknown · about 299 to 199 BC · the work’s page · read it where wisdom first appears

Abstract written by a model

Wisdom is a topic in Ecclesiastes, presented with tension. The Preacher first recounts seeking it in Chapter 1, gaining great wisdom, yet finding that it is a chasing after wind and that more wisdom and knowledge bring more grief and sorrow. Chapter 7 praises it as good as an inheritance, a defense like money, a preserver of its owner's life, and a strength greater than ten rulers, though it felt far from him. Chapter 9 ranks it better than strength and weapons of war, but notes the poor man's wisdom is despised and one sinner can destroy much good.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Ecclesiastes / Chapter 1

The Preacher says he applied his heart to seek and search out by wisdom, and that he obtained great wisdom above all who were before him in Jerusalem, with great experience of wisdom and knowledge. He applied his heart to know wisdom, madness and folly, and perceived that this was also a chasing after wind. He concludes that in much wisdom is much grief, and that whoever increases knowledge increases sorrow.

Chapter 7

Wisdom is as good as an inheritance and is a defense like money, preserving the life of the one who has it. It is a strength greater than ten rulers, yet the Preacher says that when he sought to be wise, it was far from him.

Chapter 9

Wisdom is said to be better than strength and better than weapons of war, and the words of the wise heard in quiet are better than the cry of a ruler among fools. Yet the poor man's wisdom is despised and his words are not heard, and one sinner can destroy much good.

Abstract written by a model

Wisdom, spoken of as 'her', is a gift from the Lord. The speaker sought her in prayer from youth, found her in purity, and gained instruction and rest. She is near, obtainable without money, and worth more than silver and gold.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 51

Wisdom, referred to as 'her', is sought by the speaker openly in prayer when young and before the temple, from the first flower to the ripening grape. He found her in purity, joined his heart to her, and gained instruction, rest and a good possession through her. She is offered to others without money, is said to be near to find, and is worth more than a great sum of silver and much gold.
