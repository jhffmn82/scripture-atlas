marriage

topic · 3 works · 7 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: doesn’t give her in marriage, gives his own virgin in marriage, joined to his wife, married, marries, marries another, marry, marrying, one flesh, to marry, wed

Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Matthew, marriage is described as a man leaving his parents and joining his wife so the two become one flesh, joined by God and not to be separated; Jesus also discusses remaining unmarried and eunuchs for the Kingdom. In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, marriage is presented as a remedy for sexual immorality and lack of self-control, though it brings fleshly oppression and divided concern, and a wife is bound while her husband lives. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), marriage with idolaters and idolatresses is prohibited until they believe, while believing bondwomen and slaves are preferred and marital relations, nursing, engagement, and reconciliation are addressed.

marriage in The Gospel According to Matthew

Abstract written by a model

Marriage is described by Jesus as a man leaving his parents and joining his wife so the two become one flesh, joined by God and not to be separated. Disciples then call marrying inexpedient; Jesus says not all can accept this, mentioning eunuchs for the Kingdom.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 19

Jesus describes marriage as a man leaving his father and mother and being joined to his wife so that the two become one flesh, which God has joined and man must not separate. The disciples respond that if so, it is not expedient to marry. Jesus says not all can receive this saying, and he speaks of eunuchs, including those who make themselves so for the Kingdom of Heaven's sake.

Chapter 7

Marriage is presented as a remedy for sexual immorality and lack of self-control, since it is better to marry than to burn with passion. Marrying is not sin, but Paul says it brings oppression in the flesh and divided concern, so he regards remaining unmarried as better. A wife is bound as long as her husband lives.
