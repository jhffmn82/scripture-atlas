Abstract written by a model

In the Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I, penance suits the mendicant who gives up unnecessary clothing, endures painful conditions, reduces food, and continues effort despite bodily weakness. In the Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, austerities are earnest practices undertaken to remove pain and annihilate Karman, whose merit depends on motive rather than fame. In the Uttaradhyayana Sutra, austerities are practised by a monk seeking freedom from greed, producing merit and spiritual power, subduing the self, supporting meditation, and visibly empowering Harikêsa.

austerities in Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where austerities first appears

Abstract written by a model

Austerities are earnest practices of monks and Mahâvîra, undertaken to remove pain. Fasting makes the body lean and helps annihilate Karman, requiring control and right conduct. Hardship can dishearten weak men, who may return to house life. Their value depends on motive: penance for fame is not meritorious, while unseen penance is meritorious; fame should not be sought.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Second Lecture, Called the Destruction of Karman. / First Chapter. .. Third Chapter.

Austerities such as fasting make the body lean and help annihilate Karman. Monks are instructed to be earnest in austerities while maintaining control and right conduct.

Third Lecture, Called the Knowledge of Troubles / First Chapter. .. Fourth Chapter.

Austerities cause weak men to become disheartened when they are harassed by hardship or suffer the plucking out of their hair. Those unable to bear them may return to house life.

Eighth Lecture, Called on Exertion.

Penance is of no good when noble men perform it as monks for the sake of fame. Penance is meritorious when nobody else knows about it, and one should not spread one's own fame.

austerities in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where austerities first appears

Abstract written by a model

Austerities are practised by a monk seeking freedom from greed after recognizing material insufficiency. They produce merit and spiritual power, subdue the self, and help keep the monk safe with meditation. Continual penance involves vows and enduring harsh conditions, visibly empowering Harikêsa.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Uttaradhyayana Sutra / First Lecture.

Penance helps subdue the self and, together with meditation, keeps the monk in safety.

Twelfth Lecture

Harikêsa is emaciated by penance, and the gods declare that the value of penance has become visible through his power. He describes penance as his fire in the spiritual sacrifice.

Fifteenth Lecture.

The monk practises austerities continually, keeps the vows, and endures heat, cold, insects, lowly beds, lodgings, and frightening noises.
