The Book of Hosea

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

The Book of Hosea, in fourteen chapters, is a prophetic text in which the LORD accuses Israel of unfaithfulness, announces judgment, and ends by promising restoration. Its central figures are the LORD, Hosea son of Beeri, his wife Gomer, and the people addressed as Israel, Ephraim, Samaria and Judah. Chapter 1 opens with the LORD commanding Hosea to take a wife of prostitution because the land has forsaken him. Gomer bears three children named Jezreel, Lo-Ruhamah and Lo-Ammi, names that signal judgment on Israel. Mercy for Judah and a future united, countless people are promised. Chapter 2 develops the image of an unfaithful mother who credits her lovers and Baal with the LORD's gifts. He will strip her and end her feasts, then allure her into the wilderness, betroth her to himself forever, and show mercy to those once called 'not my people'. Chapter 3 has the speaker buy back an adulteress as the LORD loves Israel despite its other gods; Israel will live long without king or sacrifice, then return to seek the LORD and David their king. Chapters 4 through 8 present the charge. There is no truth, goodness or knowledge of God, and the land mourns. Priests, princes and kings are condemned for idolatry, prostitution, scheming and rebellion against the covenant. Ephraim turns to Assyria and Egypt instead of the LORD, and in chapter 6 the LORD says he desires mercy and knowledge of God more than sacrifice. Israel sows the wind and will reap the whirlwind, and Samaria's calf idol will be broken. Chapters 9 through 13 deepen the judgment. Israel will lose harvest, land and children and be scattered. Its altars and calves will be demolished, and its king will be destroyed. Chapter 11 recalls the LORD loving Israel as a child and teaching Ephraim to walk. His compassion is aroused, and he will not destroy Ephraim, for he is God and not man. Chapter 12 recalls Jacob and condemns Ephraim's dishonest wealth. Chapter 13 compares the LORD to a lion, leopard and bear and ends with Samaria falling by the sword. Chapter 14 closes with a call to return, supplies words of repentance and renounces Assyria, horses and idols. The LORD promises to heal and love them freely, to be like dew so Israel blossoms, and the book ends by asking who is wise enough to see that his ways are right.

The Book of Hosea / Chapter 1

The LORD's word comes to Hosea son of Beeri during the reigns of kings of Judah and Israel, and tells him to take a wife of prostitution because the land commits great adultery by forsaking the LORD. Hosea takes Gomer daughter of Diblaim, who bears a son, a daughter, and another son. The LORD names the children Jezreel, Lo-Ruhamah, and Lo-Ammi, each name signaling judgment on the house of Israel, including the end of its kingdom and the withdrawal of mercy and relationship. The LORD promises mercy and salvation for Judah without weapons, and foretells that the children of Israel will become innumerable, be called sons of the living God, and be united with Judah under one head.

Chapter 2

Chapter 2 presents the LORD speaking about a mother, who stands for the unfaithful people, and her children. The children are told to contend with their mother because she has played the prostitute, pursued her lovers, and credited them with the grain, wine, oil, wool and flax that the LORD actually gave, which were used for Baal. The LORD announces judgment: he will block her way, strip her, take back his gifts, end her feasts and Sabbaths, and lay waste her vines and fig trees. He then promises to allure her into the wilderness, speak tenderly, and turn the valley of Achor into a door of hope. He will make her call him 'my husband', remove the Baals' names from her mouth, establish peace, betroth her to himself forever, and show mercy to those who were 'not my people', who will answer 'You are My God!'

Chapter 3

The LORD commands the speaker to go again and love a woman who is loved by another and is an adulteress, as the LORD loves the children of Israel even though they turn to other gods and love cakes of raisins. The speaker buys her for fifteen pieces of silver and a homer and a half of barley. He tells her she must stay with him many days, not play the prostitute, and not be with any other man, and that he will be so toward her. The text then says the children of Israel will live many days without king, prince, sacrifice, sacred stone, ephod or idols. Afterward they will return, seek the LORD their God and David their king, and come with trembling to the LORD and his blessings in the last days.

Chapter 4

In Chapter 4 the LORD brings a charge against the children of Israel and the inhabitants of the land, declaring that there is no truth, goodness, or knowledge of God, only cursing, lying, murder, stealing, adultery, and bloodshed. Because of this the land mourns and its people, animals, birds, and fish waste away. The LORD rebukes the priests and prophet, saying his people are destroyed for lack of knowledge and that he will reject the priest who rejected knowledge and forgot God's law. The people consult wooden idols, sacrifice on hills under oaks, poplars, and terebinths, and indulge in prostitution and wine, which take away understanding. The LORD warns Judah not to follow Israel's offense, and says Ephraim is joined to idols and should be left alone, with the wind wrapping up the people and bringing disappointment over their sacrifices.

Chapter 5

Chapter 5 opens with the LORD addressing the priests, the house of Israel, and the house of the king, declaring judgment against them for being a snare at Mizpah and a net on Tabor. Ephraim and Israel are charged with prostitution and defilement, and their deeds and pride keep them from turning to God, so Israel, Ephraim, and Judah will stumble. Though they seek the LORD with flocks and herds, they will not find him because he has withdrawn. Alarms are sounded at Gibeah, Ramah, and Beth Aven, Ephraim is to become a desolation, and Judah's princes are accused of removing landmarks. Ephraim turns to Assyria and King Jareb for healing, but the LORD says they cannot cure him, and he will tear Ephraim and Judah like a lion and withdraw until they acknowledge their offense and earnestly seek him.

Chapter 6

Chapter 6 opens with a call by speakers to return to the LORD, who has torn and injured them but will heal and bind their wounds, revive them after two days, and appear like the rain. The LORD then answers, asking Ephraim and Judah what He shall do with them, since their love is like a morning cloud and disappearing dew. He says He has cut them down through the prophets and the words of His mouth, and that He desires mercy and knowledge of God more than sacrifice and burnt offerings. He charges that they, like Adam, broke the covenant and were unfaithful, citing Gilead's iniquity and bloodstains, priests who murder on the path toward Shechem, and the prostitution and defilement of Ephraim and Israel. The chapter ends with a harvest appointed for Judah when the LORD restores the fortunes of His people.

Chapter 7

Chapter 7 describes the LORD's desire to heal Israel being met by the exposed wickedness of Ephraim and Samaria, where falsehood, theft, and robbery prevail. The princes and king are portrayed as adulterers and schemers, burning like an oven with plots, drunkenness, and anger, and they devour their judges while their kings fall without anyone calling on the LORD. Ephraim mingles with the nations, loses strength without realizing it, and is likened to a pancake not turned over and to a dove easily deceived that calls to Egypt and goes to Assyria. The LORD declares he will spread his net over them, chastise them, and bring woe and destruction because they wandered from him, spoke lies against him, and plot evil despite his teaching and strengthening them. The chapter ends with their return not being to the Most High, their comparison to a faulty bow, and the prediction that their princes will fall by the sword and be derided in Egypt.

Chapter 8

In Chapter 8 the LORD calls for a trumpet to sound the alarm because something like an eagle is over his house, since Israel has broken his covenant and rebelled against his law. Israel claims to acknowledge God yet has rejected what is good, set up kings and princes without the LORD's approval, and made idols from silver and gold. The LORD's anger burns against Samaria's calf idol, which a workman made and which will be broken in pieces. Israel sows the wind and will reap the whirlwind, is swallowed up among the nations, and Ephraim has hired lovers in Assyria. Ephraim's multiplied altars became places for sinning, the LORD's written law was treated as strange, and the LORD will remember their iniquity, punish them, and send fire on the cities of Israel and Judah after they forgot their Maker and built palaces and fortified cities.

Chapter 9

Chapter 9 warns Israel not to rejoice like the nations, because it has been unfaithful to its God and loves the wages of a prostitute at every threshing floor. The LORD announces that Israel will lose its harvests and its land, with Ephraim returning to Egypt and eating unclean food in Assyria, while Egypt and Memphis gather and bury the fugitives. The people will treat the prophet as a fool, though the prophet watches over Ephraim with God, and the text recalls their corruption at Gibeah and Baal Peor. Ephraim's glory is said to fly away like a bird, with no birth, no pregnancy and no conception, and the prophet asks the LORD to give them a miscarrying womb and dry breasts. The LORD declares that he hated them in Gilgal, will drive them from his house, and will kill the beloved children of their wombs, so that they become wanderers among the nations.

Chapter 10

Chapter 10 describes Israel as a luxuriant vine whose prosperity led it to multiply altars and adorn sacred stones while its heart was divided. The LORD declares he will demolish these altars and stones, and that judgment will spring up like poisonous weeds because of false covenants. The people of Samaria will mourn the calves of Beth Aven, which will be carried to Assyria as a present to a great king, and Samaria and her king will float away like a twig. The LORD recalls Israel's sin since Gibeah, describes Ephraim as a trained heifer to be yoked, and urges the people to sow righteousness and seek him. Because they have plowed wickedness and trusted in their own way and mighty men, battle will destroy their fortresses as Shalman destroyed Beth Arbel, and Israel's king will be destroyed at daybreak.

Chapter 11

In Hosea 11, the LORD recalls loving Israel as a child and calling his son out of Egypt, yet the people turned to the Baals and engraved images. He taught Ephraim to walk and drew them with ties of love, but they did not know he healed them. Because they refused to repent, they will not return to Egypt, the Assyrian will be their king, and the sword will fall on their cities. Even so, the LORD's compassion is aroused and he declares he will not destroy Ephraim, for he is God and not man. The chapter ends with a promise to settle the returning people in their houses, while noting that Ephraim and Israel deceive him and Judah strays.

Chapter 12

Chapter 12 accuses Ephraim of feeding on wind, multiplying lies and desolation, making a covenant with Assyria, and sending oil to Egypt. The LORD has a controversy with Judah and will repay Jacob according to his deeds, recalling how Jacob took his brother by the heel in the womb, struggled with the angel, and wept at Bethel. The LORD calls the people to turn to their God, keep kindness and justice, and wait continually. Ephraim boasts of wealth gained by defrauding with dishonest scales and claims no sin can be found in him, but the LORD, who brought Israel out of Egypt, says he will make them dwell in tents again. The LORD recalls his prophets and visions, condemns the worthless sacrifices at Gilgal, and declares that Ephraim's bitter provocation will leave his blood on him.

Chapter 13

Chapter 13 describes the LORD's indictment of Ephraim and Israel for idolatry. Ephraim, once exalted, died through guilt over Baal, and the people made silver molten images and kissed calves. The LORD says he alone is God and savior, that he knew them in the wilderness, and that they forgot him once they were filled. He therefore threatens to attack them like a lion, leopard, and bereaved bear, and he recalls giving and removing a king in anger. The chapter ends with Ephraim's stored-up guilt, a drying east wind, and Samaria falling by the sword with horrific violence against its infants and pregnant women.

Chapter 14

Chapter 14 is a closing call for Israel to return to the LORD their God after falling because of their sin. It supplies words of repentance: asking forgiveness, offering vowed bulls, renouncing reliance on Assyria, horses, and the work of their hands as gods. The LORD then promises to heal their waywardness, love them freely, and turn away his anger. He likens himself to dew that makes Israel blossom, spread roots and branches, and shelter people who flourish like grain and vine. The LORD asks Ephraim what it has to do with idols, and the chapter ends by asking who is wise enough to understand that the LORD's ways are right, with the righteous walking in them and the rebellious stumbling.
