Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Job, God's justice is impartial, gives each person the result of their work, does not pervert judgment, and hears the cries of the afflicted. In Proverbs, justice is something kings may pervert for the afflicted if they forget the law, and Lemuel is told to judge righteously and serve the poor and needy. In The Book of Isaiah, justice is absent from the people, prompting the LORD to act with his own arm and righteousness. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, justice is the just, demanding force of the whole law, requiring restoration and punishment while mercy satisfies its demands only through faith unto repentance.

justice in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where justice first appears

Abstract written by a model

Justice is the just, demanding force of the whole law: it judges hearts, words, works, and thoughts; requires restoration to proper order; and requires punishment, including the life of a murderer. It exposes those without faith to the law’s demands and consigns unrepentant mankind forever to be cut off from God’s presence. Mercy may satisfy its demands only through faith unto repentance; the plan of redemption cannot destroy or rob justice.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 12

God's judgments and works are just, and justice condemns those whose hearts, words, works, and thoughts are not spotless. The plan of redemption cannot destroy the works of justice.

Chapter 34

Justice requires the life of the person who has murdered and exposes those without faith unto repentance to the whole law of its demands. Mercy can satisfy these demands for those who exercise faith unto repentance.

Chapter 41

It requires that all things be restored to their proper order and that men be judged according to their works.

Chapter 42

Justice consigns unrepentant mankind to be cut off from God’s presence and requires the punishment affixed to the law. Justice exercises all its demands, while it cannot be robbed by mercy.
