Job's suffering

Abstract written by a model

Job's suffering is an evil that came upon him, marked by very great grief and bitterness, which he claims God caused without cause. It begins when Satan, with the LORD's permission but limited to sparing his life, strikes him with painful sores, worsened by his wife's urging to renounce God. Job then describes being broken by a storm, multiplied wounds, swiftly fleeing joyless days, and suffering that falls on blameless and wicked alike. It culminates in loss of glory and crown, uprooted hope, estrangement from family and friends, an emaciated body, and cries for justice unanswered.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Job's suffering begins when Satan, with the LORD's permission, strikes him with painful sores from the sole of his foot to his head. It is limited by the LORD's command to spare his life. It is made worse by his wife's urging to renounce God, and his grief is called very great by the friends who sit with him.

Chapter 9

Job describes his suffering as being broken with a storm, having wounds multiplied without cause, being unable to catch his breath, and being filled with bitterness. He says his days flee swiftly like runners, swift ships and a swooping eagle, and see no good. He fears all his sorrows, says he despises his life, and sees no point in laboring in vain. He holds that the suffering falls alike on the blameless and the wicked, and wishes God's rod and terror taken from him.

Chapter 19

Job's suffering includes the loss of his glory and crown, the uprooting of his hope, and estrangement from brothers, relatives, friends, servants, wife, and children. His breath is offensive to his wife, his bones cling to his skin and flesh, and he has escaped only by the skin of his teeth. He says he cries out of wrong but receives no justice.
