Abstract written by a model

Sarah, first called Sarai, is the beautiful wife and half-sister of Abram/Abraham (daughter of his father, not his mother). The records open with her barrenness and childlessness as she travels with Terah, Abram and Lot from Ur toward Canaan and settles in Haran. In Egypt, Abram has her say she is his sister; Pharaoh takes her, plagues follow, and she is returned. Still childless, she gives her servant Hagar to Abram, then deals harshly with Hagar after being despised, and Hagar flees. God renames her Sarah, promises to bless her and give Abraham a son by her, and says she will be a mother of nations with kings descending from her. At ninety, old and past childbearing, she overhears the promise at the tent door, laughs, then denies it out of fear. Abimelech takes her after Abraham again calls her his sister, but does not approach her and restores her, declaring her vindicated. She conceives and bears Isaac in Abraham's old age as God had spoken, says God has made her laugh, and later, seeing Hagar's son mocking, asks Abraham to cast out Hagar and her son so he will not share Isaac's inheritance. Sarah lives 127 years and dies at Kiriath Arba (Hebron) in Canaan. Abraham mourns her and buries her in the cave of Machpelah, Ephron's field before Mamre.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Sarai is the wife of Abram and is barren, having no child. She goes with Terah, Abram, and Lot from Ur of the Chaldees toward Canaan and lives in Haran.

Chapter 12

Sarai, Abram's wife, goes with him from Haran to Canaan and then to Egypt. Abram asks her to say she is his sister because she is beautiful; she is taken into Pharaoh's house, and the LORD sends plagues because of her. Pharaoh then returns her to Abram.

Chapter 16

Sarai, Abram's wife, is childless and says the LORD has restrained her from bearing, so she gives her Egyptian servant Hagar to Abram as a wife. When Hagar despises her, Sarai blames Abram, asks the LORD to judge between them, and then deals harshly with Hagar, who flees from her.

Chapter 17

Sarai, Abraham's wife, is renamed Sarah by God. God promises to bless her and to give Abraham a son by her, and says she will be a mother of nations from whom kings of peoples will come. Abraham notes that she is ninety years old, and God says she will bear Isaac at the set time next year.

Chapter 18

Sarah is told to make cakes from three seahs of fine meal and overhears from the tent door that she will have a son. Being old and past childbearing, she laughs within herself, then denies laughing out of fear and is told, "No, but you did laugh."

Chapter 20

Sarah is taken by Abimelech after Abraham calls her his sister, but Abimelech does not come near her. She is restored to Abraham, and Abimelech tells her that the thousand pieces of silver given to her brother is a covering of the eyes and that she is vindicated before all. It is learned that she is Abraham's half-sister, the daughter of his father but not his mother, and that she said of him, 'He is my brother.'

Chapter 21

Sarah conceives and bears Abraham a son in his old age, as God had spoken, and says God has made her laugh. After seeing Hagar's son mocking, she tells Abraham to cast out the servant and her son so that he will not be heir with Isaac.

Chapter 23

Sarah lived one hundred twenty-seven years and died in Kiriath Arba (Hebron) in the land of Canaan. Abraham mourned and wept for her, and afterward buried her in the cave of the field of Machpelah before Mamre.
