Abstract written by a model

In Sutrakritanga, Book II, food comprised substances on which living beings feed and was to be consumed carefully only when lawfully given, pure, free from living matter, properly measured, and sufficient to sustain control and life. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), food used seasonably by the people came from brood-hen and brood-sow families and 100-mu farms, securing eight-mouth families against want.

food in Sutrakritanga, Book II

Abstract written by a model

Food includes substances on which living beings feed. It should be approached carefully because of living beings involved, and a monk should consume only food that is lawfully given, pure, free from living matter, properly measured, and sufficient to sustain control and life.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

I say: here in the East, West, North, and South there are some men, viz. Âryas,

A monk should not take food, drink, dainties, or spices obtained through buying, stealing, force, or sinful conduct toward living beings. He may consume food that is pure, free from living matter, lawfully given, properly measured, and sufficient to support control.

Third Lecture, Called Knowledge of Food.

Living beings feed on substances such as earth, sap, humours, milk, boiled rice, gruel, plants, and movable or immovable beings. The lecture teaches that one should be careful and circumspect with regard to food.
