Abstract written by a model

Achior is an Ammonite, leader of all the children of Ammon, and also addressed alongside the children of Moab. In Chapter 5 he tells Holofernes the truth, giving a history of Israel and advising attack only if Israel has sinned against its God. His words provoke anger and calls for him to be cut to pieces. In Chapter 6 Holofernes calls him a hireling of Ammon and sends him to Bethulia. He is bound and left at the foot of the hill, freed by Israelites, brought before Ozias and the rulers, praised, and taken into Ozias's house. In Chapter 14 he sees Holofernes' head, faints, revives, and blesses Judith. Having seen what God had done, he believes, is circumcised, and joins the house of Israel to this day.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Achior, leader of all the children of Ammon, promises to tell Holofernes the truth and gives a full history of Israel from the Chaldeans through Egypt, the Red Sea, and the conquest of the hill country. He advises that Holofernes attack only if Israel has sinned against its God, and otherwise pass by. His words provoke complaint, and Holofernes' great men and those of Moab and the sea coast say he should be cut to pieces.

Chapter 6

Achior is denounced by Holofernes as a hireling of Ammon for his prophecy that Israel's God will defend Israel, and is sent to Bethulia to share the fate of the Israelites. He is bound and left at the foot of the hill, freed by the Israelites, and brought before Ozias and the city's rulers, where he recounts Holofernes' words. The people comfort and praise him, and Ozias takes him into his house.

Chapter 14

Summoned from the house of Ozias, Achior sees Holofernes' head, falls on his face, and his spirit fails. After reviving he bows at Judith's feet, blesses her, and asks to hear what she did. Having seen what the God of Israel had done, he believes in God exceedingly, is circumcised, and is joined to the house of Israel to this day.
