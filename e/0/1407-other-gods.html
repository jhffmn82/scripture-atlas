Abstract written by a model

The nations' so-called gods are no gods, made by men, unprofitable lies and vanity. In chapter 2, the LORD notes that no nation changes its gods, yet his people traded their glory for them, calling wood father and stone mother, and he challenges these gods to save in trouble. In chapter 16, serving them causes judgment and exile, after which the nations confess their vanity.

Chapter 2

The LORD calls them no gods, noting that no nation changes its gods, yet his people exchanged their glory for what doesn't profit. Israel tells wood 'You are my father' and a stone 'You have given birth to me', and Judah has as many gods as towns. The LORD challenges them to arise and save in the time of trouble.

Chapter 16

The fathers and their descendants walked after, served and worshiped other gods, which is the stated cause of the judgment. The people will serve them in exile, and the nations will eventually confess that such gods are vain and unprofitable, asking whether a man should make gods that are no gods.
