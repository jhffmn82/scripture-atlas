oaths in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where oaths first appears

Abstract written by a model

A warning against habitual oath-taking: constantly uttering the Name leaves one unclean of sin, like a continually scourged servant with bruises. Many oaths fill a man with iniquity, and false swearing leaves him unjustified and his house full of calamities.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 23

The text warns against accustoming the mouth to oaths or to continually uttering the Name, comparing the swearer to a continually scourged servant who never lacks bruises. A man of many oaths is filled with iniquity, the scourge does not leave his house, and if he swears falsely he is not justified and his house is filled with calamities.

oaths in Surah 5: The Table (Al-Maaida)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where oaths first appears

Abstract written by a model

require expiation [when sworn in earnest] Unintentional oaths do not incur accountability, but earnest oaths require expiation when broken. Expiation consists of feeding ten needy persons, clothing them, freeing a slave, or fasting three days for one unable to do the others.
