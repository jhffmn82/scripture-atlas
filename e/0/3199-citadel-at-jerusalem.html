Abstract written by a model

The citadel in Jerusalem is a hostile fortress housing transgressors of the law and Gentiles, serving as a lurking place against the sanctuary. Its troops continually harassed Israel. Judas besieged it in the 150th year, and some besieged slipped out to complain to the king. Jonathan later besieged it with war engines and asked Demetrius to remove its troops, but received only a conditional promise. Finally Simon blockaded it until its occupants starved and surrendered, cleansed it, and entered in the 171st year with celebration, establishing a yearly festival.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

Those in the citadel keep hemming Israel in around the sanctuary and strengthening the Gentiles. Judas besieges it in the 150th year, and some of the besieged slip out to complain to the king.

Chapter 11

Jonathan gathered the Judeans and built many engines of war to besiege the citadel. Lawless men reported this to Demetrius, but Jonathan continued the siege. Later he asked Demetrius to remove the citadel's troops, since they fought against Israel continually, and Demetrius only promised to do so if he found an opportunity.

Chapter 13

The citadel in Jerusalem is blockaded so that its occupants starve, and Simon cleanses it of its pollutions after they surrender. He enters it on the twenty-third day of the second month in the 171st year with praise, palm branches, harps, cymbals, and songs, and orders the day kept yearly with gladness.
