Abstract written by a model

The twenty-four elders are a group seated on twenty-four thrones around God's throne, wearing white garments and gold crowns. Their defining role is worship: they repeatedly fall down before God and throw their crowns before the throne. In Chapter 4 they declare God worthy of glory, honor, and power as creator. In Chapter 5 one elder comforts the narrator about the Lion of Judah, and after the Lamb takes the book they sing a new song, holding harps and golden bowls of incense, the prayers of the saints. In Chapter 11 they worship again, thanking God for reigning and speaking of judgment and reward.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

The twenty-four elders sit on twenty-four thrones around God's throne, dressed in white garments with gold crowns on their heads. When the living creatures give glory to him who sits on the throne, the elders fall down, worship him, and throw their crowns before the throne. They declare that God is worthy to receive glory, honor, and power because he created all things.

Chapter 5

One elder tells the narrator not to weep because the Lion of Judah, the Root of David, has overcome. After the Lamb takes the book, the elders fall down before him, each holding a harp and golden bowls of incense, which are the prayers of the saints, and they sing a new song. At the end they fall down and worship.

Chapter 11

The twenty-four elders, seated on their thrones before God's throne, fall on their faces and worship God. They give thanks that he has taken his great power and reigned, and speak of the nations' anger, his wrath, the judgment of the dead, and the reward for his servants.
