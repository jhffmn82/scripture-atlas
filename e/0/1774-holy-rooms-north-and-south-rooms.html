Abstract written by a model

The holy rooms are north and south chambers set apart as holy, where priests eat and store the most holy offerings (meal, sin and trespass offerings) near the LORD. Chapter 42 describes the north building: a hundred cubits long, fifty wide, with a north door and three stories of galleries, the upper rooms shorter. Chapter 46 shows priests boiling trespass and sin offerings and baking meal offerings at the back, so the people in the outer court are not sanctified.

Chapter 42

The north building is one hundred cubits long and fifty wide, with a north door, three stories of galleries, and upper rooms shorter than the lower and middle ones because the galleries take space from them. Rooms on the south and east have a similar arrangement of doors and dimensions. The guide declares the north and south rooms holy, as places where priests eat and lay the most holy things.

Chapter 46

The guide brings the narrator through the entry at the side of the gate to these rooms, which face north and have a place at the back toward the west. There the priests boil the trespass and sin offerings and bake the meal offering.
