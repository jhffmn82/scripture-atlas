Rehoboam

person · 4 works · 41 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: King Rehoboam, Rehoboam king of Judah, Rehoboam the son of Solomon, he, his son, king, the foolishness of the people, the king

Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, Rehoboam became king of Judah, lost the northern tribes through his harsh answer, avoided war with Israel after Shemaiah's warning, replaced gold shields with bronze, and was succeeded by Abijam. In 1 Chronicles, Rehoboam is named as Solomon's son and as the father of Abijah in the list of royal descendants. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Rehoboam ruled Judah, fortified its cities, humbled himself after abandoning the LORD's law, fought Jeroboam, and was succeeded by Abijah. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Rehoboam is described as the foolishness of the people and one who lacked understanding, whose counsel made the people revolt.

Rehoboam in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Rehoboam first appears

Abstract written by a model

Rehoboam, son of Solomon and Naamah the Ammonitess, became king of Judah at forty-one and reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem. At Shechem he rejected the old men's counsel and answered Israel roughly, losing the northern tribes; he fled to Jerusalem after Adoram was stoned. He heeded the LORD through Shemaiah and did not fight Israel. Shishak took the treasures, so he replaced gold shields with bronze. Continually at war with Jeroboam, he was buried in David's city, succeeded by his son Abijam.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 12

Rehoboam went to Shechem to be made king, took counsel from the old men and then the young men, and rejected the old men's advice. He answered Israel roughly, threatening more yoke and scorpions, lost the northern tribes, and fled to Jerusalem after Adoram was stoned. He assembled 180,000 warriors to fight Israel but obeyed the LORD's word through Shemaiah and went home.

Chapter 14

Rehoboam, son of Solomon and of Naamah the Ammonitess, begins to reign in Judah at forty-one and reigns seventeen years in Jerusalem. When Shishak takes the treasures, he replaces the gold shields with bronze ones guarded by the captains of the guard. He is at war with Jeroboam continually, and when he dies he is buried in David's city and succeeded by his son Abijam.

Rehoboam in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Rehoboam first appears

Abstract written by a model

Rehoboam, son of Solomon and Naamah, was a king of Judah who lived in Jerusalem. At Shechem he rejected the old men's counsel and answered the people roughly, so he reigned only over the Israelites in Judah's cities; Judah and Benjamin were his. After Hadoram was stoned he fled to Jerusalem. Told by the LORD through Shemaiah not to fight Israel, he turned back from assembling 180,000 warriors and fortified cities for defense. He had eighteen wives, sixty concubines, twenty-eight sons and sixty daughters, loving Maacah most and appointing her son Abijah chief prince. Once strong, he abandoned the LORD's law with all Israel, then humbled himself after Shemaiah's message. Shishak took the gold shields, and he made bronze ones. He fought continual wars with Jeroboam. He began reigning at forty-one, ruled seventeen years, did evil by not seeking the LORD, was buried in David's city, and was succeeded by Abijah. His acts are recorded in the histories of Shemaiah and Iddo.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10

Rehoboam goes to Shechem to be made king, asks the people for three days, and consults first the old men and then the young men. He abandons the old men's counsel and answers the people roughly, promising to add to the yoke and chastise with scorpions. He reigns only over the Israelites in Judah's cities, and after Hadoram is stoned he hurries to his chariot to flee to Jerusalem.

Chapter 11

Rehoboam assembles 180,000 warriors to fight Israel but turns back when the LORD forbids it through Shemaiah. He fortifies cities in Judah, is strengthened for three years by those who come to him, and fathers twenty-eight sons and sixty daughters by eighteen wives and sixty concubines. He loves Maacah above all and appoints her son Abijah chief prince, intending him to be king.

Chapter 12

Rehoboam, once strong, abandoned the LORD's law along with all Israel, and then humbled himself when Shemaiah delivered the LORD's message. After Shishak took the gold shields, he made bronze ones and had the guards carry them whenever he entered the LORD's house. He began to reign at forty-one, reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, did evil by not setting his heart to seek the LORD, and was buried in David's city, succeeded by his son Abijah.
