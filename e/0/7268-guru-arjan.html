Abstract written by a model

In So Dar and So Purukh (the Rahiras), Guru Arjan teaches trust in God's provision, presents human life as a chance to meet God, urges fellowship with saints and repetition of God's name, and humbly seeks preservation despite base actions. In Sohila (Kirtan Sohila), Guru Arjan urges people to serve the saints, earn God's name, meet the Guru, and arrange their affairs, teaching that by the Guru's favour God will dwell in the heart.

Guru Arjan in So Dar and So Purukh (the Rahiras)

The Sikh Religion · the work’s page · read it where Guru Arjan first appears

Abstract written by a model

Guru Arjan is associated with Rag Gujari and teaches trust in God’s provision. He presents human life as a chance to meet God, urges fellowship with saints and repetition of God’s name, and humbly seeks preservation despite base actions.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Sodar

Guru Arjan teaches that God provides food for every creature and that people need not fear for their daily sustenance. He says that the society of the saints saves people and, through the Guru’s favour, raises and renews them.

So Purukh

Guru Arjan teaches that obtaining a human body is an opportunity to meet God. He urges humanity to join the saints, repeat God's name, and prepare to cross the terrible ocean. He confesses that his actions have been base and asks God to preserve his honour.
