Heracleitus

person · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections

Abstract written by a model

Heracleitus teaches that all things are one and that opposites coexist as the world’s structure, while sense knowledge and common views are unreliable. Presented as a Greek parallel to Chuang Tzŭ, he is not a mystic, though he founded a line of mystical thinkers; later he is again mentioned for this parallel.

Heracleitus is in one work on this site, Chuang Tzŭ. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

_Note on the Philosophy of Chaps._ i-vii.

Heracleitus is presented as the closest Greek parallel to Chuang Tzŭ, teaching that all things are one, that contraries coexist, and that sense knowledge and common views are unreliable. Unlike Chuang Tzŭ, he is said not to have fully developed mystical flight from the world or absorption in God.

The triumph of Confucianism in the centuries which intervened between

Heracleitus is mentioned as a possible Western parallel to Chuang Tzŭ, because the writer sees a remarkable parallel between their teachings.
