Emperor Nin-Toku

person · 2 works · 20 quoted facts · 11 connections
Also called: Emperor-Sage, Heavenly Sovereign, His Augustness, His Augustness Oho-sazaki, Oho-sazaki, the Heir Apparent, this Heavenly Sovereign Oho-sakaki

Abstract written by a model

In Kojiki, His Augustness Oho-sazaki is Emperor Ō-Jin’s son and heir apparent, administers the realm, cedes the Empire to Uji-no-waki-iratsuko, and rules it after his death. In Kojiki (Records of Ancient Matters), Volume III (Emperor Nin-toku to Empress Sui-ko), Emperor Nin-Toku is the Heavenly Sovereign who rules the Empire, undertakes public works and reforms, is praised as Emperor-Sage, leads military action, and dies aged eighty-three.

Emperor Nin-Toku in Kojiki (Records of Ancient Matters), Volume III (Emperor Nin-toku to Empress Sui-ko)

Basil Hall Chamberlain · the work’s page · read it where Emperor Nin-Toku first appears

Abstract written by a model

Emperor Nin-Toku is a Heavenly Sovereign who lived in the palace of Takatsu at Naniha and ruled the Empire. He married Empress Her Augustness Iha-no-hime, Princess Kami-naga, and younger half-sisters Yata-no-waki-iratsume and Uji-no-waki-iratsume; his marriages produced six august children, while those to his half-sisters produced none. He established tribes as proxies for the Empress, heirs, children, and Yata-no-waki-iratsume. His reign included embankments, granaries, pools, channels leading waters to the sea, and a port, using Hada people for labour. He remitted taxes and forced labour for three years, restoring them after prosperity returned, and was praised as Emperor-Sage. Later relationships and conflict led him to follow the Empress into Yamashiro and order an army against King Hayabusa-wake and Queen Medori. He died aged eighty-three and was buried on the Ear-Moor of Mozu.

His Augustness Oho-sazaki dwelt in the palace of Takatsu at Naniha and ruled the Empire. He married Empress Her Augustness Iha-no-hime, Princess Kami-naga, and his younger half-sisters Yata-no-waki-iratsume and Uji-no-waki-iratsume. His marriages produced six august children, while the two marriages to his younger half-sisters produced no children.

Sect. CXX: Emperor Nin-Toku (Part II.—Various Deeds). .. Sect. CXXX.—Emperor Nin-Toku (Part XI.—His Age and Place of Burial).

He established tribes as proxies, ordered public works, remitted taxes and forced labour for three years, and later restored them when the people prospered. He pursued relationships with Princess Kuro, Yata-no-waki-iratsume, and Queen Medori, followed the Empress into Yamashiro, and ordered the army against King Hayabusa-wake and Queen Medori. He lived eighty-three years and was buried on the Ear-Moor of Mozu.
