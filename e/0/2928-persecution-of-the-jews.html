Abstract written by a model

A persecution ordered by the king, who sends an old man of Athens to force Jews to abandon their laws; a decree extends it to neighboring Greek cities at Ptolemy's suggestion. It brings forced sacrifices, processions, and killings of circumcisers and Sabbath-keepers, interpreted as chastening, not destruction.

Chapter 6

The persecution begins when the king sends an old man of Athens to enforce Greek practices, and a decree extends it to neighboring Greek cities at Ptolemy's suggestion. It includes forced sacrificial eating, forced processions, and killing of those who refuse, such as women who circumcised their children and Sabbath-keepers burned in caves after betrayal to Philip. The narrator interprets it as chastening of the race, not destruction.
