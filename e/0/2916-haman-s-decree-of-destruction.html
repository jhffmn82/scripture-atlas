Haman's decree of destruction

Abstract written by a model

Haman's decree is a royal edict, issued in King Ahasuerus's name and sealed with his ring, ordering the destruction of all Jews, including wives and children, and the plunder of their goods. Haman has the recorders write it on the thirteenth day of the first month, addressed to rulers across 127 provinces from India to Ethiopia in their languages. It sets the fourteenth of Adar as the day, and copies are published in every province. Publication in Susa causes Jewish mourning; Mordecai sends a copy to Esther, who joins him in prayer.

Chapter 3

Haman has the king's recorders write the decree on the thirteenth day of the first month, sealed with the king's ring and addressed to captains, governors and rulers from India to Ethiopia in their languages. It orders the Jews, with their wives and children, to be destroyed and their goods plundered, on the fourteenth day of Adar (the first day is also named in one verse). Copies are published in every province, and all nations are told to be ready for that day.

Chapter 4

The decree has been published in Susa and in the provinces, and its publication causes mourning among the Jews everywhere. Mordecai sends a copy to Esther through Hathach as proof, along with word of Haman's promise of ten thousand talents to the treasury. Esther's and Mordecai's prayers describe it as an attempt by enemies to destroy God's inheritance and stop the mouth of those who praise him.
