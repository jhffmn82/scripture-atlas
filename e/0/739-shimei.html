Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Samuel, Shimei curses David and throws stones, then later confesses his sin to David, who swears he will not die. In The First Book of Kings, Shimei is confined to Jerusalem by Solomon after previously cursing David, breaks his oath by going to Gath, and is killed by Benaiah.

Shimei in The Second Book of Samuel

author unknown · about 950 BC · the work’s page · read it where Shimei first appears

Abstract written by a model

Shimei, a Benjamite of Saul's house, son of Gera, living at Bahurim. He curses David and throws stones, blaming him for Saul's house and Absalom's rise; David forbids Abishai to kill him. Later he hurries to meet David at the Jordan with a thousand Benjamites, confesses sin, and David swears he will not die.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 16

Shimei son of Gera, of Saul's house, comes out at Bahurim cursing David, throwing stones and dust, and calling him a man of blood who is paying for Saul's house and for losing the kingdom to Absalom. David forbids Abishai to kill him, and Shimei keeps cursing along the hillside.

Chapter 19

Shimei, son of Gera the Benjamite of Bahurim, hurries to meet David at the Jordan with a thousand men of Benjamin and confesses that he sinned. Abishai wants him put to death for cursing the LORD's anointed, but David swears that he will not die.

Shimei in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Shimei first appears

Abstract written by a model

Shimei, son of Gera from Bahurim, a Benjamite who cursed David. Sworn safe from the sword, he is confined to Jerusalem by Solomon; after three years he crosses to Gath for runaway slaves, breaking the oath, and Benaiah kills him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

David recalls that Shimei cursed him but was sworn not to be killed by the sword, and tells Solomon to bring his gray head down to Sheol with blood. Solomon confines him to Jerusalem on pain of death; after three years he crosses to Gath to retrieve two runaway slaves, and Benaiah kills him on Solomon's command.
