Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the land of the LORD receives the LORD's favor, is where glory dwells and truth springs from the earth, and yields its increase when the LORD gives what is good. In The Book of Jeremiah, the plentiful land given to Israel is the LORD's land and inheritance, polluted by abominations and destined for desolation and expulsion. In Baruch, the land promised to the fathers is given to Israel, and the LORD promises to restore the people to it, where they will rule, increase, and never again be removed. In The Book of Ezekiel, the land of Israel is given to the fathers of Israel and Jacob as an everlasting dwelling, judged for violence and abominations, restored and renewed, and apportioned among the tribes and resident aliens. In The Book of Hosea, Israel's land mourns and wastes away because of the people's sins, and its animals, birds, and fish die. In The Book of Joel, the land of Joel's people is laid waste and mourns as crops fail, pastures burn, brooks dry up, and livestock and flocks become desolate. In The Book of Zechariah, the land is purged of idol names, prophets, and the spirit of impurity, with two parts cut off and the remaining third left in it.

land of Israel in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · about 592 to 570 BC · the work’s page · read it where land of Israel first appears

Abstract written by a model

The land of Israel is a place given by the LORD to the fathers of Israel and to Jacob, meant as an everlasting dwelling for the people and their descendants. The records trace it from judgment to restoration to ordered division. At the start it is told that the end has come on its four corners; it is full of bloody crimes and violence, and its houses and holy places will be profaned by the worst of the nations. Its inhabitants claim it as their possession, but the LORD promises it to the gathered exiles, who will remove its detestable things. Because of the abominations and violence of its people, the LORD vows to make it a desolation and an astonishment, its pride of power ended and its mountains empty of travelers, so that people will know he is the LORD. Those in the waste places still claim it as their inheritance. Having been defiled and made desolate, with the nations claiming it, it is then promised renewal: tilled, free from famine, cities inhabited and ruins rebuilt, so that it is called like the garden of Eden. The LORD will bring his people up from their graves, gather them from the nations, and make them one nation on the mountains of Israel. Finally the land is apportioned by lot among the twelve tribes, with Joseph holding two portions, and also to aliens living among them. An offering of 25,000 by 10,000 reeds, holy in all its border, holds the sanctuary, the priests' and Levites' portions, and lies beside the city possession. A southern border runs from Tamar to Meribath Kadesh, the brook, and the great sea.
