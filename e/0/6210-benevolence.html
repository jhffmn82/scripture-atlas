benevolence in The Tao Teh King, or The Tao and Its Characteristics

Lao-Tse · the work’s page · read it where benevolence first appears

Abstract written by a model

Benevolence is a quality and, when attributes are lost, the highest virtue. It does not guide heaven, earth, or sages by impulse. It comes into vogue when the Great Tao ceases to be observed and appears when Tao’s attributes are lost, before righteousness. In its highest form it is always carried out, though its possessors need no conscious doing.

benevolence in Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou)

Mencius · the work’s page · read it where benevolence first appears

Abstract written by a model

Benevolence is the tranquil habitation of man and a practice of loving and nourishing others through excellence. It matters most in government: when a benevolent person occupies high station, it benefits those below, overspreads the kingdom, gains the people, removes opponents, and supports the flourishing and preservation of States. Without it, rulers cannot preserve what they hold and face sorrow, disgrace, death, and ruin. A prince’s benevolence makes all his acts benevolent, while violating it leads toward perishing and makes productive discussion impossible. Benevolence also directs personal conduct: one should examine oneself when love is not returned or when treated perversely, rather than blame others. Preserved in the heart by the superior man, it bears its richest fruit in serving one’s parents.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

10 sections; each heading opens the text

Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou) / Part I / Chapter 1

Benevolence should be practiced through government rather than held only as a reputation or intention. When a benevolent person occupies a high station, benevolence can benefit those below and overspread the kingdom.

Chapter 3

Benevolence determines the gaining and losing of the throne and the flourishing, preservation, decay, and perishing of States. A lack of benevolence prevents rulers and other people from preserving what they hold.

Chapter 4

A man should examine his own benevolence when he loves others and no responsive attachment is shown to him.

Chapter 7

Benevolence gives a prince no opponent in the kingdom. Seeking that result without being benevolent is compared to handling a heated substance without first dipping it in water.

Chapter 8

Benevolence is violated by princes who take pleasure in things by which they perish. The text presents such violation as making productive discussion with them impossible.

Chapter 9

Benevolence is presented as necessary for rulers who want to gain the people and the kingdom. Princes who do not set their wills on benevolence face sorrow, disgrace, death, and ruin.

Chapter 10

Benevolence is described as the tranquil habitation of man. Those who throw themselves away say they cannot dwell in it.

Chapter 20

Benevolence in the prince causes all his acts to be benevolent.

Chapter 27

Benevolence bears its richest fruit in the service of one's parents.

Part II / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 33

Benevolence involves loving others and nourishing people through excellence. Mencius says a person should examine himself for a lack of benevolence and become especially benevolent when treated perversely.
