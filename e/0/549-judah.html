Abstract written by a model

In Numbers, the camp of Judah leads Israel's divisions on the east side and sets out first in the march order. In The Book of Joshua, the tribe of Judah receives a southern land allotment, though it fails to drive the Jebusites from Jerusalem and cedes part to Simeon. In The Book of Judges, Judah is a tribal group chosen to lead the fight against the Canaanites and later against Benjamin. In The Book of Judges: are_ruled_by Philistines Three thousand men of Judah go down to the cave in Etam's rock to bind Samson and deliver him to the Philistines, reminding him that the Philistines rule over them. In The Second Book of Samuel: Judah is a group of men loyal to David. They anoint him king at Hebron over the house of Judah while Ishbosheth reigns over Israel, and later unite as one to bring him back over the Jordan, answering Israel's complaints fiercely. In The First Book of Kings, Judah is the southern kingdom loyal to David's house but guilty of evil and attacked by Egypt. In The Second Book of Kings, Judah is the southern kingdom that declines into idolatry, judgment, Babylonian domination, and exile. In 1 Chronicles, Judah is a tribal group whose armed men joined David at Hebron and whose people were later carried captive to Babylon.

Judah in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Judah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judah is a kingdom and people, centered on Jerusalem, whose fortunes rise and fall with the faithfulness of its kings. It holds a covenant with the LORD, has priests (sons of Aaron) and Levites, and maintains Temple worship with daily offerings, incense, show bread and the lamp stand. The records follow it through successive reigns. Under Rehoboam it is fortified and strengthened by Levites, priests and faithful Israelites. Under Abijah it defeats Israel's larger army by crying to the LORD. Under Asa it seeks the LORD, builds fortified cities, enjoys quiet, defeats the Ethiopians, swears an oath with all its heart, and has rest for many years. Under Jehoshaphat it brings tribute, is garrisoned, taught from the law, and given judges; high places are removed, though hearts are not fully set on God. It is delivered without fighting when it gathers to seek the LORD, and it returns to Jerusalem rejoicing. Decline follows. Jehoram leads it astray with high places; Edom and Libnah revolt, and Philistines and Arabians plunder the king's house. After Jehoiada's death its princes turn to Asherah poles and idols, and Syrians destroy its princes. Under Amaziah it supplies 300,000 men but is defeated by Israel at Beth Shemesh and raided by dismissed Israelite soldiers. Under Ahaz it suffers heavy losses, including 120,000 men in a day, captivity, and raids by Edomites and Philistines, while Ahaz builds high places for other gods in every city. Renewal comes under Hezekiah: God gives one heart to obey the king and princes, the assembly rejoices, idols are broken down, and tithes and dedicated things are brought. Sennacherib's invasion is survived through the LORD, and wrath over Hezekiah's pride is averted when he humbles himself. Manasseh seduces Judah into greater evil, but after his repentance it serves the LORD, though it still sacrifices at high places. After Amon's death the people of the land make Josiah king. Josiah purges high places, Asherah poles and images, the people hear the book of the covenant, curses are declared against the place, and throughout his days Judah does not depart from following the LORD.

Abstract written by a model

Judah is the realm and people, whose capital is Jerusalem and whose kings include Josiah, Jehoiakim, Zedekiah, Jeconiah and Jehoiachin, addressed throughout Jeremiah's ministry. It is first presented as the treacherous sister of Israel: it saw Israel's divorce yet feared nothing, played the prostitute, and returned to the LORD only in pretense. The records stress its deep and persistent sin. The people are rebellious, stiff-necked and unwilling to take correction, with faces harder than rock. They swear by no-gods, trust lying words and the temple, love false prophecy, and commit theft, murder and adultery. They burn incense to Baal, bake cakes for the queen of the sky, and burn their sons and daughters at Topheth. They set up gods as numerous as their cities and altars as numerous as their streets, conspire, and break the covenant. Their sin is engraved on their hearts and altars, and they did worse than their fathers. The LORD repeatedly calls them to turn, including through Baruch's scroll, and they do not listen. Judgment follows: watchers and northern kingdoms attack, the drought brings mourning and languishing gates, and the LORD's anger kindles a fire. Nebuchadnezzar carries off Jeconiah, princes and craftsmen. Zedekiah is condemned and told to submit to Babylon, and its cities become a desolation without inhabitants. Judah is carried away captive, and the treasures of its kings go to Babylon. A remnant and the poorest are left under a governor, and some scattered Jews return. Johanan and the people refuse to stay and flee to Egypt, where they are threatened, with only a few to return. Throughout, promises of restoration run alongside judgment. The LORD says he will not make a full end, and that in those days Judah will walk with Israel and come out of the north to the inherited land. He will reverse its captivity, so that it is a habitation of righteousness, will make a new covenant with Judah and Israel, and will save Judah in the days of the Branch. Its children will seek him weeping, and he will pardon the remnant. The account closes with Zedekiah's fall, Judah's captivity, and Jehoiachin's release from prison in Babylon.

Chapter 1

Judah is the realm whose kings Josiah, Jehoiakim, and Zedekiah reigned during Jeremiah's ministry. Its cities are to be attacked by the northern kingdoms. Its kings, princes, priests, and people are the ones against whom Jeremiah is made a fortified city, iron pillar, and bronze walls.

Chapter 3

Judah is called Israel's treacherous sister, who saw Israel's divorce yet had no fear and also played the prostitute. She has not returned to the LORD with her whole heart, only in pretense, and is judged less righteous than Israel. The LORD promises that in those days the house of Judah will walk with the house of Israel and come together out of the north to the inherited land.

Chapter 4

Judah is addressed with a call to break up fallow ground, circumcise their hearts, and sound the alarm, and it is warned that watchers from a far country will raise their voice against its cities. Its land is to be made desolate, with cities laid waste and forsaken, though the LORD says he will not make a full end.

Chapter 5

The people are shown as stubborn: stricken but not grieved, refusing correction, with faces harder than rock, and having broken the yoke and burst the bonds. They have forsaken the LORD, sworn by no-gods, denied that evil will come, and have a revolting and rebellious heart that does not fear him. They are told they will lose their harvest and serve strangers in a foreign land, and that their sins have withheld good from them.

Chapter 7

The people of Judah are accused of trusting lying words, stealing, murdering, committing adultery, swearing falsely, burning incense to Baal, and following other gods. Families make cakes for the queen of the sky, and the children of Judah set abominations in the temple and burn their sons and daughters at Topheth. They have not listened to the prophets, have stiffened their necks, and have done worse than their fathers. The LORD announces that he will cast them out as he did Ephraim's offspring and that their cities will lose all mirth and gladness.

Chapter 9

The LORD says he will make the cities of Judah a desolation without inhabitant. Judah is also listed among the nations to be punished as uncircumcised in flesh.

Chapter 11

The men of Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem are addressed with the covenant words and are accused of a conspiracy, of returning to the iniquities of their forefathers, and of serving other gods. They have set up as many gods as they have cities and altars to Baal as they have streets, and the LORD will bring inescapable evil on them, ignoring their cries and leaving their gods unable to save them.

Chapter 13

Judah's pride is to be ruined as the belt was, and the whole house of Judah had been made to cling to the LORD but would not hear. Its cities of the South are shut up with no one to open them, and all of Judah is wholly carried away captive.

Chapter 14

Judah mourns and its gates languish as the drought strikes. The people sit in black on the ground and the land is cracked without rain. Judah is named in the people's prayer asking whether the LORD has utterly rejected it.

Chapter 17

Judah's sin is described as written with an iron pen and diamond point on their hearts and altars, and even their children remember their altars and Asherah poles. The LORD says its wealth and high places will be plundered and that it will serve enemies in an unknown land. Its kings and people are addressed at the gates and told that Sabbath obedience would keep the city forever, while disobedience would bring fire.

Chapter 20

The LORD says he will give all Judah into the hand of the king of Babylon, who will carry its people captive to Babylon and kill them with the sword. The riches, gains, precious things, and treasures of the kings of Judah will also be given to their enemies and carried off to Babylon.

Chapter 24

Judah's king Jeconiah, its princes, and its craftsmen and smiths have been carried captive to Babylon by Nebuchadnezzar. The LORD distinguishes between Judah's captives, who are regarded as good, and Zedekiah its king with his princes, who are regarded as bad.

Chapter 25

Judah is told it has not listened to the LORD or his prophets, and that it will become a desolation along with the surrounding nations. Its cities, kings and princes are among those made to drink the cup, becoming a desolation, an astonishment, a hissing and a curse.

Chapter 27

Judah's king Zedekiah and its people are told to submit to Babylon to avoid sword, famine, and pestilence. The text recalls that Nebuchadnezzar earlier carried away Jeconiah son of Jehoiakim, king of Judah, and Judah's and Jerusalem's nobles to Babylon.

Chapter 31

Judah and its cities are promised that, when the LORD reverses their captivity, people will bless it as a habitation of righteousness and mountain of holiness. Farmers and shepherds will dwell there together. The LORD will sow the house of Judah along with the house of Israel and make a new covenant with both.

Chapter 33

Judah's cities are called waste, but the LORD promises to restore its fortunes, bring back joy and thanksgiving, and let shepherds and flocks fill its cities again. In the days of the Branch, Judah will be saved.

Chapter 34

Judah's cities are under attack, with only Lachish and Azekah remaining as fortified cities. The LORD declares he will make the cities of Judah a desolation without inhabitant, and its princes and people are condemned for breaking the covenant.

Chapter 35

Judah is the land of King Jehoiakim, where the Rechabites live as nomads and which Nebuchadnezzar enters. The LORD declares he will bring on Judah and all Jerusalem's inhabitants the evil he has pronounced, because they did not listen or answer.

Chapter 36

Judah's people are the intended hearers of the scroll, in hope that each will turn from his evil way and be forgiven. In the ninth month of the fifth year, people from Jerusalem and the cities of Judah gather for a fast, where Baruch reads the words. The LORD says he will bring on Judah's men the evil pronounced because they did not listen.

Chapter 40

Judah's land is under a Babylonian-appointed governor, with only a remnant and the poorest left. Jews scattered in other lands return to Judah and gather abundant wine and summer fruits.

Chapter 43

The land of Judah is where the LORD's voice told the people to dwell, but Johanan, the captains, and the people refuse to obey and do not stay. The remnant had returned to it from all the nations where they had been driven, and it is now left for Egypt.

Chapter 44

Judah's cities are described as wasted and desolate because of the people's wickedness and incense to other gods. The remnant that fled to Egypt is threatened with being cut off, and only a few who escape will return to the land of Judah.

Chapter 50

Judah's children come together with the children of Israel, weeping, to seek the LORD and inquire toward Zion. Judah's sins will be sought and not found, for the LORD will pardon the remnant he leaves. Judah is also described as oppressed with Israel by captors who refuse to release them.

Chapter 52

Judah's king Zedekiah and its princes were killed or captured, and the chapter concludes that Judah was carried away captive out of its land. Its king Jehoiachin is later released from prison in Babylon.

Judah in The Book of Hosea

author unknown · 759 to 719 BC · the work’s page · read it where Judah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judah is the people or house of Judah, addressed in the Book of Hosea during the reigns of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah. It begins with a promise of mercy: the LORD will save it not by weapons but by himself, and its children will later be gathered with Israel's under one head. Chapter 5 turns to warning: Judah stumbles with Israel and Ephraim, its princes are like those who remove a landmark, and the LORD's wrath and a lion's tearing await it. Chapter 6 laments its fleeting love, like morning cloud and dew, yet promises an appointed harvest when the LORD restores his people's fortunes.

The Book of Hosea / Chapter 1

Judah's kings Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah mark the time of Hosea's word. The LORD says he will have mercy on the house of Judah and save it by the LORD their God, not by bow, sword, battle, horses, or horsemen. Its children will later be gathered with the children of Israel under one head.

Chapter 5

Judah will stumble with Israel and Ephraim, and its princes are likened to those who remove a landmark. The LORD will pour out his wrath on them like water and be like rottenness to the house of Judah. Judah saw its wound, and the LORD will be like a young lion to it, tearing in pieces and carrying off with no one to deliver.

Chapter 6

Judah is addressed alongside Ephraim, with the LORD asking what He shall do to it because its love is fleeting like a morning cloud and dew. It is also told that a harvest is appointed for it when the LORD restores the fortunes of His people.
