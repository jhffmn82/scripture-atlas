Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, mercy is what the speakers await from the LORD, asking twice for it while grounding their request in endured contempt. In Psalms, forgiveness and cleansing is what David seeks from God through blotting out transgressions, washing, a clean heart, a renewed spirit, and restored joy. In Psalms, mercy meets together with truth as part of a vision of reconciliation. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, mercy is contrasted between human mercy toward a neighbor and the Lord’s mercy toward all flesh through reproving, chastening, teaching, and return. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), Allah turns to believers in mercy and is described as Merciful. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), forgiveness concerns Allah’s pardon for wrongdoing, its conditions, and its limits regarding delayed repentance, disbelief, and ascribing partners to Him. In Surah 6: The Cattle (Al-An'aam), Allah prescribes mercy for Himself, and those who repent and do right after ignorant wrongdoing find Him Forgiving and Merciful. In Surah 7: The Heights (Al-A'raaf), Allah’s mercy saves Noah and those with him, Hud and those with him, and Lot’s household, and is near to those who do good.

In Surah 9: The Repentance (At-Tawba), Allah’s mercy reaches the Prophet, the Muhajirin, the Ansar, the three left behind, and those who acknowledge their faults while mixing righteous and bad action. In Surah 10: Jonas (Yunus), mercy comes from Allah after adversity, brings rejoicing to believers, removes affliction, forgives, and is shown to the folk of Jonah when they believe. In Surah 15: The Rock (Al-Hijr), Allah is described as the Forgiving and the Merciful, and Abraham says only the astray despair of the mercy of their Lord. In Surah 17: The Night Journey (Al-Israa), mercy is hoped for by those seeking nearness to Allah, shown in His provision and revelation, and requested for parents. In Surah 18: The Cave (Al-Kahf), mercy is sought by the young men of the Cave, given to the knowledgeable servant, shown in protecting the orphan boys’ treasure, and reflected in patience toward wrongdoers. In Surah 19: Mary (Maryam), Allah’s mercy is shown to prophets and servants, promises the Garden, and describes Jesus as a mercy for mankind. In Surah 21: The Prophets (Al-Anbiyaa), mercy rescues prophets and believers from affliction, brings the righteous into Allah’s care, and makes Muhammad a mercy for the peoples. In Surah 30: The Romans (Ar-Room), Allah’s mercy appears in rain and winds, causing people to rejoice even though some then attribute partners to their Lord. In Surah 39: The Groups (Az-Zumar), Allah’s mercy is offered to those who have been prodigal to their own hurt, who are told not to despair and contrasted with disbelievers. In Surah 42: Consultation (Ash-Shura), Allah’s mercy reaches whom He wills through repentance, pardon, increased reward, and saving rain. In Surah 45: Crouching (Al-Jaathiya), mercy belongs to Allah and believers who do good works are brought into it as part of evident triumph.

forgiveness in Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where forgiveness first appears

Abstract written by a model

Forgiveness concerns Allah’s pardon for wrongdoing and the conditions under which it is granted or withheld. It is incumbent toward people who do evil in ignorance, repent quickly, and improve, and it may be sought by those who do evil or wrong their own souls. Allah is forgiving and merciful, may pardon feeble men, women, and children unable to devise a plan or find a way, and promises pardon alongside the reward of those who strive. Yet forgiveness is not for evil deeds when repentance is delayed until death attends, nor for those who die while disbelieving. Allah pardons all save ascribing partners unto Him, except whom He wills. The account thus moves from repentance and improvement, through Allah’s mercy and pardon, to firm limits on delayed repentance, disbelief, and ascribing partners.
