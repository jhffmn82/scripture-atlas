Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, the Philistines of Gerar quarrel with Isaac's herdsmen over wells and water. In The Book of Judges, the Philistines rule Israel for forty years and oppose Samson, who kills many of them and destroys their temple. In The First Book of Samuel, the Philistines repeatedly wage war against Israel, capture the ark, suffer defeats, and ultimately defeat Saul and occupy Israelite cities. In The Second Book of Samuel, the Philistines repeatedly fight David and Israel, but David defeats and subdues them and their giants. In 1 Chronicles, the Philistines defeat Saul, occupy Israelite cities, and are later subdued by David, who defeats their armies and giants. In The Second Book of Chronicles, the Philistines first bring tribute to Jehoshaphat and later raid Judah with the Arabians during Jehoram's reign. In The Book of Jeremiah, the Philistines are identified as the remnant of Caphtor and are threatened with destruction by the LORD. In The Book of Ezekiel, the Philistines are condemned for perpetual hostility and threatened with destruction and divine vengeance.

and 2 more works

In The Book of Zephaniah, the Philistines and their coastal cities are warned that Gaza, Ashkelon, Ashdod, and Ekron will be destroyed. In The Book of Zechariah, the Philistine cities face judgment, their pride is cut off, and a remnant is left for God.

Philistines in The Book of Judges

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Philistines first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Philistines are an uncircumcised, Dagon-worshipping people who rule over Israel and Judah for forty years, delivered to them by the LORD. They are the main adversary of Samson, who is announced as the one to begin saving Israel from them. Samson's marriage among them provokes conflict: he strikes thirty men in Ashkelon, burns their grain and olive groves, and kills a thousand at Lehi. They retaliate by burning his wife and her father, then seize him through Delilah, blind him, and bind him in Gaza. Crediting Dagon, they summon him to entertain them, and he collapses the house, killing more of them in death than in life.

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

The Philistines held the children of Israel under their power for forty years by the LORD's act of delivering Israel to them. The angel announced that Samson would begin to save Israel from their hand.

Chapter 14

The Philistines are described as uncircumcised and as ruling over Israel at that time. Samson's parents object to his taking a wife from among them, but the LORD is said to be seeking an occasion against them. Samson later strikes thirty men in Ashkelon.

Chapter 15

The Philistines lose their grain and olive groves to Samson's fire and respond by burning Samson's wife and her father. They then encamp in Judah and spread out in Lehi to bind Samson, and shout as they meet him, but Samson breaks his bonds and strikes a thousand of them. They are described as rulers over Judah.

Chapter 16

The Philistines are the people whom Delilah falsely announces are upon Samson, and who later seize him, put out his eyes, take him to Gaza and bind him in bronze fetters. They credit Dagon with delivering Samson, their enemy, into their hands, and call him to entertain them. Samson avenges himself on them as the house collapses, killing more of them in his death than in his life.

Philistines in The First Book of Samuel

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Philistines first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Philistines are a group of five-lord city-states (Ashdod, Gaza, Ashkelon, Gath, Ekron) who worship Dagon and other gods, and who appear throughout these records as the persistent enemy of Israel and of King Saul. Their story is one of recurring war, with reversals in both directions, ending in a major victory over Saul. They first defeat Israel in two battles, killing four thousand and then thirty thousand, and capture the ark. Their gathering at Aphek precedes this. The ark, however, brings trouble: placed in Dagon's house at Ashdod and moved to Gath and Ekron, it leads their lords to consult priests and diviners. They return it with a trespass offering of five golden tumors and five golden mice, one per lord, and test whether the LORD's hand struck them by watching the cows go to Beth Shemesh. When they attack Israel at Mizpah, the LORD thunders and confuses them; they are routed, subdued, stop entering Israel's border, and the cities they took are restored. Later they regroup, encamp at Michmash with vast forces, send out raiders, and bar blacksmiths so the Hebrews cannot make swords or spears. Jonathan's attack throws their camp into panic, and they strike each other and flee. The war lasts all of Saul's days. At Socoh and Azekah they face Israel across a valley until their champion Goliath is killed; they flee, pursued to Gai and the gates of Ekron, and their camp is plundered. Saul hopes they will kill David, but David kills two hundred of them for the dowry. David also defeats them at Keilah, and a Philistine raid draws Saul away from pursuing David. The final movement begins with their armies gathering at Shunem and Aphek, with Achish drafting David before his dismissal. They terrify Saul, then fight Israel at Mount Gilboa, rout its men, and kill Saul's three sons. They cut off Saul's head, spread the news through their land, place his armor in the house of the Ashtaroth, fasten his body to the wall of Beth Shan, and occupy the cities Israelites abandoned.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

12 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

The Philistines defeated Israel at the first battle, killing about four thousand men. They feared the ark and the 'mighty gods' who struck the Egyptians, urged each other to fight like men, then defeated Israel again, killed thirty thousand footmen, and captured the ark.

Chapter 5

The Philistines take the ark to Ashdod and place it in Dagon's house. Their lords are gathered twice to decide what to do with it, and they send it first to Gath and then to Ekron. In the end they say to send it away to its own place so it will not kill them.

Chapter 6

The Philistines consult priests and diviners about returning the ark and agree to send a trespass offering of five golden tumors and five golden mice, one for each lord. They test whether the LORD's hand struck them by seeing if the cows go to Beth Shemesh. Their five lords follow the cart to the border and return to Ekron the same day.

Chapter 7

The lords of the Philistines go up against Israel when they hear Israel has gathered at Mizpah. The LORD thunders and confuses them, and they are struck down and pursued to Beth Kar. They are subdued, stop coming within Israel's border, and the cities they took from Israel are restored, from Ekron to Gath.

Chapter 13

The Philistines responded to the strike on their Geba garrison by assembling thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen, and people numerous as the sand, and they encamped at Michmash. They sent raiders out in three companies, and their garrison went to the pass of Michmash. They also prevented Israel from having blacksmiths so the Hebrews could not make swords or spears.

Chapter 14

The Philistines taunt Jonathan and his armor bearer, inviting them up, and about twenty of them are killed. Their camp, garrison and raiders tremble, they strike each other with swords in confusion, and they flee. Israel pursues them from Michmash to Aijalon, and they finally go to their own place; the war against them lasts all the days of Saul.

Chapter 17

The Philistines gather their armies at Socoh and encamp between Socoh and Azekah, standing on one mountain facing Israel across the valley. When they see their champion Goliath dead, they flee, and Israel and Judah pursue them to Gai and the gates of Ekron, with the wounded falling on the way to Shaaraim, Gath and Ekron. Israel then plunders their camp.

Chapter 18

The Philistines are the enemy whose hand Saul hopes will fall on David, both in battle and in the dowry scheme. David kills two hundred of them to meet the dowry, and he continues to act more wisely than Saul's servants whenever their princes go out to fight.

Chapter 23

The Philistines fight against Keilah and rob its threshing floors, and David and his men defeat them with a great slaughter and take their livestock. Later a Philistine raid on the land draws Saul away from pursuing David.

Chapter 28

The Philistines gather their armies to fight Israel and encamp at Shunem, with Achish drafting David and his men to go with him. Their army terrifies Saul, and Samuel prophesies that the LORD will deliver Israel and Saul into their hand.

Chapter 29

The Philistines gather all their armies at Aphek, with their lords passing in review by hundreds and thousands. After David's dismissal, they go up to Jezreel, where the Israelites are encamped by the spring.

Chapter 31

The Philistines fought Israel, routed its men on Mount Gilboa, and killed Saul's three sons. They stripped the slain, cut off Saul's head, sent word throughout their land, placed his armor in the house of the Ashtaroth, and fastened his body to the wall of Beth Shan. They also moved into the cities Israelites abandoned.

Philistines in The Second Book of Samuel

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Philistines first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Philistines are a group recurrently at war with Israel and opposed to David. They had hanged the bodies of Saul and Jonathan at Beth Shan. After David's anointing they go up to seek him, spreading themselves in the valley of Rephaim, where he strikes them at Baal Perazim and later from Geba to Gezer. David subdues them, takes the bridle of the mother city, and dedicates their silver and gold to the LORD. Their giants, descended from the giant of Gath, are killed, and warriors like Eleazar and Shammah defeat them, even as their garrison holds Bethlehem.

Philistines in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Philistines first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Philistines are a recurring enemy of Israel across these records. They first defeat Saul at Mount Gilboa, killing his sons Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchishua, and take his head and armor, placing the armor in the house of their gods and the head in the house of Dagon. They then occupy cities the Israelites abandon. Later they battle at Pasdammim, camp in the valley of Rephaim, and hold a garrison in Bethlehem. When David is anointed king over all Israel, they pursue him but are beaten at Baal Perazim, leaving their gods to be burned, and are struck from Gibeon to Gezer. David subdues them and takes Gath, and some of the silver and gold he dedicates to the LORD comes from them. Last, wars at Gezer and Gath see their giants killed.

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10

The Philistines fight Israel, pursue Saul and his sons, and kill Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchishua. They strip the slain, take Saul's head and armor, send word to their idols and people, place his armor in the house of their gods and fasten his head in the house of Dagon. They also come to live in the cities the Israelites abandon.

Chapter 11

The Philistines gather to battle at Pasdammim, where the people flee until Eleazar and others defend the barley plot and kill them. They are encamped in the valley of Rephaim and hold a garrison in Bethlehem, which the three break through to fetch water for David.

Chapter 14

The Philistines go up to seek David when they hear he is anointed king over all Israel, and they raid the valley of Rephaim. David defeats them at Baal Perazim, and they leave their gods there, which are burned with fire. They raid the valley again and their army is struck from Gibeon to Gezer.

Chapter 18

The Philistines are defeated and subdued by David, who takes Gath and its towns from their hand. Some of the silver and gold David later dedicates to the LORD came from them.

Chapter 20

The Philistines fought Israel in wars at Gezer and Gath and in another unnamed engagement. In these wars their giants, including Sippai, Lahmi, and the man of great stature, were killed by Israelite warriors.
