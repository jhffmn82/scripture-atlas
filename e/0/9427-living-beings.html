living beings

Abstract written by a model

Living beings are one of six classes and include beings with life, divided into movable and immovable forms existing above, below, and on earth. They feel pain when ill-treated, injured by slaughter, or involved in sin, and are universally averse to pain. Therefore they should not be beaten, treated violently, abused, tormented, injured, or slain. They arise or come forth in earth and occur in earth, water, plants, animals, and other bodies. They feed on various nourishing substances, including bodies of earth, water, fire, wind, plants, and movable and immovable beings. Throughout existence they undergo changes through Karman, are impelled by Karman, and belong to the Circle of Births, where they may take different forms. The unit moves from their division and capacity for pain to food, harm, renunciation of wicked deeds, and abstention from injury against all living beings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

I say: here in the East, West, North, and South there are some men, viz. Âryas,

Living beings are divided into beings with life and things without life, with living beings further divided into movable and immovable ones. They experience pain and agony when ill-treated, and therefore should not be beaten, treated with violence, abused, tormented, or deprived of life.

Now the explanation of the second subject, viz. merit, is as follows:

Living beings are universally averse to pain, as shown by the philosophers' refusal to hold burning coals. Those who harm living beings are said to experience suffering, while those who refrain from violence avoid such consequences.

Third Lecture, Called Knowledge of Food.

Living beings arise in earth, water, plants, animals, and other bodies, and they feed on various substances. They consume earth-bodies, water-bodies, fire-bodies, wind-bodies, plant-bodies, and the bodies of movable and immovable beings, and they experience and undergo changes through Karman.

Fourth Lecture, Called Renunciation of Activity.

Living beings are described as capable of being harmed through slaughter, cruelty, and other sins. As a person feels pain, so living beings do, and therefore they should not be injured or killed.

Sixth Lecture, Called .

Living beings are described as movable and immovable and existing above, below, and on earth. A person who understands their nature and fears injuring them abstains from wicked deeds.

Seventh Lecture, Called Nâlandâ.

Living beings are divided into movable and immovable beings and remain within the Circle of Births. The unit teaches that renunciation should extend to injury against all living beings, since beings may later take different forms.
