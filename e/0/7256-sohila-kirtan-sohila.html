Sohila (Kirtan Sohila)

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This collection of hymns centers on praise of the one Creator, remembrance of God, and refuge in the Guru and saints. Guru Nanak begins with Sohila: in a house filled with God’s praise, the soul-bride awaits her Lord as the day of the Caller approaches. He then affirms that, despite many philosophical schools, teachers, doctrines, seasons, and forms, the Guru of gurus and God are one. A cosmic worship follows, with sun, moon, stars, wind, forests, and fragrance offered to the divine light present in all things; the speaker longs for God’s lotus feet and the Name. Guru Ram Das presents saints as those absorbed in God’s love and Name, whose company destroys lust and wrath and frees people from transmigration, while pride brings pain and death. Guru Arjan closes by urging service to the saints, meeting the Guru before life passes, and receiving God’s word. Those awakened by God understand the Ineffable, attain peace, and escape return to the world.

Guru Nanak, Rag Gauri Dipaki

Guru Nanak's Rag Gauri Dipaki describes singing the Sohila in a house where God's praise is sung and He is meditated on. It praises the Fearless Lord and the Creator, whose gifts and care cannot be fully appraised. The speaker, described as a bride, awaits meeting her Lord while relations prepare for her marriage. The passage urges remembrance of the Caller because the day is approaching.

Guru Nanak, Rag Asa

Guru Nanak teaches that there are six schools of philosophy, six teachers, and six doctrines, but the Guru of gurus is one despite having various forms. He urges preservation of the system in which the Creator is praised. He explains that, like one sun across many seasons and divisions of time, there is one God although His forms are many.

Guru Nanak, Rag Dhanasari

Guru Nanak presents a vision of worship addressed to God, in which the sun, moon, stars, wind, forests, and sandal perfume form a cosmic offering. God is described as having countless eyes, forms, feet, and organs of smell, yet no single one of each. The light in everything is identified as God's light, made manifest through the Guru's teaching. Guru Nanak longs for God's lotus feet and asks for divine favour so he may dwell in God's name.

Guru Ram Das, Rag Gauri Purbi

Guru Ram Das teaches that lust and wrath are destroyed through meeting the saints, who are found by predestination and absorbed in God’s love. He praises saluting and prostrating before saints, while warning that pride causes pain and death. The saints of God are absorbed in God’s Name and have overcome the fear and pain of transmigration. The speaker asks God for preservation and declares that the Name is Nanak’s support and brings happiness.

Guru Arjan, Rag Gauri Purbi

Guru Arjan teaches that people should serve the saints and earn the profit of God's name. He says human life grows shorter, urges people to meet the Guru and arrange their affairs, and contrasts this world’s wickedness and superstition with salvation through knowing God. Those awakened by God and given the essence of His word can understand the Ineffable, find peace in God's palace, and avoid returning to this world. Nanak prays to become the dust of the saints' feet and receive happiness.
