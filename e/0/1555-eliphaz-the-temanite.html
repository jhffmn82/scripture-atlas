Eliphaz the Temanite

person · 1 work · 7 quoted facts · 4 connections

Abstract written by a model

Eliphaz the Temanite is a friend of Job, one of three who come from Job's own place to sympathize and comfort him. He joins in weeping, tearing his robe, and sitting silently seven days and nights. He then speaks to Job in dialogue, first noting Job's past strengthening of others, arguing the innocent never perish while the wicked reap what they sow, and recounting a night vision in which a voice spoke of mortal injustice before God. Later he rebukes Job as a speaker of vain knowledge, appeals to the aged and tradition, and describes the wicked's ruin. In his last speech he accuses Job of great wickedness and urges return to the Almighty with promised restoration. Finally the LORD tells him his wrath is kindled against him and his two friends for not speaking rightly as Job did, and Eliphaz, with Bildad and Zophar, does as commanded.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Eliphaz the Temanite is one of Job's three friends who hear of his evil and come from his own place. He makes an appointment with the others to sympathize with and comfort Job, and joins in weeping, tearing his robe and sitting silently seven days and nights.

Chapter 4

Eliphaz the Temanite answers Job, asking whether Job will be grieved if he speaks, and observes that Job has strengthened others but now faints under trouble. He argues that the innocent never perish and the wicked reap what they sow, then relates a secret night vision in which he heard a voice about the injustice of mortals before God.

Chapter 15

Eliphaz the Temanite answers by rebuking the addressee as a speaker of vain knowledge who turns his spirit against God. He appeals to the authority of the aged and of wise men's tradition, and declares what he has seen about the wicked: that they suffer terror, loss, and destruction.

Chapter 22

Eliphaz the Temanite answers with a speech that accuses the man he addresses of great wickedness and endless iniquities. He lists specific wrongs, describes God's height and the fate of wicked men, and ends by urging return to the Almighty with promises of restoration.

Chapter 42

The LORD addresses Eliphaz, telling him that his wrath is kindled against him and his two friends because they did not speak rightly of him as Job did. Eliphaz goes with Bildad and Zophar and does what the LORD commanded.
