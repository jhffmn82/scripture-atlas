Jerusalem is the city the LORD chose out of all the tribes of Israel to put his name there forever, and it houses the LORD's house and the king's house. Abstract written by a model

Jerusalem, paired with Judah and Mount Zion, is the city at the center of these records: a place that is both a literal location and a people (its sons, remnant, and inhabitants). It is the site of the LORD's work, the source of his word in the latter days, and the object of judgment, threat, comfort, and restoration. Most important is its movement from ruin to renewal. Jerusalem in The Book of Jeremiah

Abstract written by a model

Jerusalem, also called Zion and the daughter of Zion, is the city called by the LORD's name and the central object of his judgment across these records. It is portrayed as filled with oppression, wickedness, and abominations such as adulteries, idolatrous incense, and false swearing. It is unclean, has been a provocation to the LORD since it was built, and is judged in part because of what Manasseh did there. A search of its streets for one just person finds none, though the LORD would pardon it if one were found. The records begin with warnings and appeals. Jerusalem is told to wash its heart of wickedness, and northern peoples are to set their thrones at its gates. It is besieged, its cry goes up in the drought, and no one will pity or mourn it. Its widows outnumber the sand of the seas. The Sabbath command at its gate sets the choice: if the Sabbath is kept, the city will remain forever; if it is violated, an unquenchable fire will devour its palaces. The judgment then sharpens. The LORD declares he will make it heaps, a dwelling place of jackals, an astonishment and hissing, and like Topheth. It will be given into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar and the Chaldeans, who will burn it. It is the first to drink the cup of wrath, and elders recall Micah's saying that it would become heaps. Jeremiah warns Zedekiah to serve Babylon or see the city become a desolation. Siege ramps are built, the Chaldeans briefly withdraw from fear of Pharaoh's army and are told they will return, and famine leaves no bread in the city. The fall comes in Zedekiah's eleventh year. The city is breached after a siege that began in his ninth year. The Chaldeans burn the king's house, the people's houses, and the LORD's house, and break down the walls all around. Jerusalem is left a desolation, later cited as a warning to those in Egypt. Alongside the judgment run promises of restoration. In those days all nations will gather to it in the LORD's name, it will be called the LORD's Throne and The LORD our righteousness, and it will dwell safely. It will be healed and be a name of joy, praise, and glory before all nations. Left vessels will be restored, and fields near it will be bought again.

Jerusalem in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Jerusalem first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jerusalem is a city central to Jesus's story. It first appears as the place of his presentation in the temple, where Simeon and Anna meet him, his parents bring him yearly for Passover, and at twelve he is found among the teachers. As Jesus travels toward it, he laments that it kills prophets and stones those sent to it, that it refused to be gathered by him, and that its house is left desolate. Approaching it from the Mount of Olives, he weeps, saying it did not know the time of its visitation, and predicts enemies surrounding it and leaving no stone on another. He foretells armies, desolation, death by the sword, captivity among nations, and trampling by Gentiles until their times are fulfilled. Finally, after his resurrection, Jerusalem is where the disciples gather, where Jesus appears, where repentance and forgiveness are to be preached to all nations beginning there, and where they wait to be clothed with power and return joyfully to the temple.

Jerusalem in The Acts of the Apostles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Jerusalem first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jerusalem is a city that serves as the central place of the apostles' witness and authority throughout Acts. It is near Olivet, a Sabbath day's journey away, and its residents call Judas's field Akeldama, the field of blood. At the start, Jesus commands the apostles to wait there for the Father's promise, and they gather in the upper room. Persecution then arises there, scattering others while the apostles remain; they send Peter and John to Samaria, and Philip is sent along the road down to Gaza. Saul, who had made havoc of believers there, is feared after his conversion until Barnabas brings him to the apostles, and the Hellenists then seek to kill him. Jerusalem hosts the council where apostles and elders debate the circumcision question and send out a decision and letter. Later it becomes the dangerous destination for Paul: he is warned not to go, is received by James and the elders, and is accused in the temple, seized by a stirred-up crowd, and held in the barracks. Agabus's prophecy begins to be fulfilled. Finally, Jewish leaders there plot against Paul through Festus, and Paul refuses to be judged there.

Chapter 25

Jerusalem is where Festus goes after arriving in the province and stays more than ten days. The high priest and principal Jews there ask him to summon Paul, plotting to kill him on the way. Festus later asks Paul whether he will go there to be judged, and Paul refuses.
