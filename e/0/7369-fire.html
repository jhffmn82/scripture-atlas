Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, Fire is Ahura Mazda’s sacred son and holy lord of ritual order, repeatedly worshipped with offerings, wood, perfume, and holy waters. In Yasna, Fire of Ahura Mazda is the lofty lord of holy creation who receives Yasna, sacrifice, homage, propitiation, praise, and perfume. In Yasna, Fire is Ahura Mazda’s sacred son and a force of Righteous Order that receives offerings, blesses worshippers, and grants prosperity and renewal. In Visparad, Fire is worshipped as Ahura Mazda’s son and holy lord of ritual order, with fire’s seed attributed to Yazads and Rashnus. In Visparad, Fire of Ahura Mazda is announced and presented with perfume and sacred offerings for sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise. In Afrinagan, Gahs and Miscellaneous Fragments, Fire is Ahura Mazda’s son and holy lord of ritual order who receives sacrifice, praise, and brings blessing. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII, Fire is a sacred witness that destroys unseen fiends and must be removed and ritually managed after death. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fire is Ahura Mazda’s son, protected from uncleanness, fed clean wood, and blessing those who properly tend it.

and 2 more works

In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, Âtar is Ahura Mazda’s valiant son who fights for Glory, protects the Good Principle, and drives away wicked foes. In Nyayish, Âtar, the son of Ahura Mazda, receives offerings and blesses proper worshippers with glory, healing, herds, sons, mastery, and joy.

Fire in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Fire first appears

Abstract written by a model

Fire is Ahura Mazda’s son, Mazda-made, sacred, and a holy, lofty lord of the ritual order. It is worshipped as the Yazad of the spoken name and receives sacrifice, homage, propitiation, praise, wood-billets, and perfume. From Yasna III through Yasna XXV, it is repeatedly addressed and worshipped together with all the fires, sacred wood, perfume, and holy waters. Yasna XVII names several fires, Berezi-savangha, Vohu-fryâna, Urvâzista, Vâzista, Spenista, Nairya-sangha, and the fire that is house-lord of all houses. The records consistently present Fire as central to ritual offerings and praise.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna III.

Fire is called the son of Ahura Mazda and the Yazad of the spoken name. Sacred wood and wood-billets are approached with perfume and praise or a Yast for Fire's propitiation.

Yasna IV.

Fire, called Ahura Mazda's son, receives offerings for sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise, together with all the fires.

Yasna VI.

Fire is worshipped as Ahura Mazda's son and as a holy lord of the ritual order. It is worshipped together with all the fires, wood-billets, perfume, and the good, Mazda-made, holy waters.

Yasna VII.

The Fire is addressed as Ahura Mazda's son. Wood-billets with perfume are offered for its propitiation, and offerings are made to it together with all the fires.

Yasna XVII.

The Fire is worshipped as Ahura Mazda’s son and as the holy lord of the ritual order. Several fires are named and worshipped, including Berezi-savangha, Vohu-fryâna, Urvâzista, Vâzista, Spenista, Nairya-sangha, and the fire that is house-lord of all houses.

Yasna XXII.

Fire is addressed as Ahura Mazda's son. Its wood and perfume are approached with praise, and its propitiation is sought together with that of all the fires.

Yasna XXV.

Fire is addressed as Ahura Mazda's son and the holy lord of the ritual order, and is worshipped. All the Fires are also worshipped, along with wood-billets bearing the Fire's perfume.

Fire of Ahura Mazda is a fire and son of Ahura Mazda, the lofty lord of the entire holy creation and lord of the friendly and most helpful person. Fire in Yasna

L.H. Mills · the work’s page · read it where Fire first appears

Abstract written by a model

Fire is Ahura Mazda’s sacred, bounteous son and a powerful force of Righteous Order. Called the Fire of the Lord and bearing the most potent name for grace, it is an offering of praise and holiness, meet for sacrifice and worthy of homage. Received joyfully in a house, it helps settlements yet harms the hater as with weapons. It gives a good bestowal to the two striving throngs, accompanying Piety and Righteous moral Order, and grants Weal and Deathlessness. Fed as prescribed with wood, perfume, sacred butter, and placed andirons, it remains aflame in the house and furthers progress and heroic renovation until its completion. It gives glory, nourishment, booty, offspring, instructors, reward, and renown. Addressing those for whom it cooks night and morning meals, it keenly examines those who approach. It blesses the worshipper who brings properly prepared wood, Baresman, or Hadhânaêpata plant, extending blessing to kine and people, while receiving praises and seeking propitiation of the good waters as lofty lord of ritual.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna XXXIV.

The Fire of Ahura Mazda is strong through Righteousness, swift, and powerful. It brings joy and help to the house that receives it, while harming the hater as if struck by weapons.

Yasna XXXVI.

The Fire of Ahura Mazda is called the most bounteous one of His Spirit and possesses the most potent name for grace. It is asked to give zeal, approach, aid, and cover with pollutions whoever brings pollutions to it.

Yasna XLIII.

Fire’s flame is described as strong through the Holy Order and as receiving an offering of praise and holiness from Zarathustra. Zarathustra desires to meditate on this offering and plan for its holy power among Ahura Mazda’s people.

Yasna XLVII.

Ahura Mazda’s Fire gives the gifts to the two striving throngs in a good bestowal. Its action accompanies the prosperous increase of Piety and Righteous moral Order.

Yasna LVIII (Sp. LVII).

The Fire of Ahura Mazda is praised and asked to come for help in the great effort and for the joy-producing grace of the great interest of the cause. It is asked to grant both Weal and Deathlessness.

Yasna LXII (Sp. LXI).

The Fire is offered sacrifice, homage, praise, benedictions, wood, perfume, sacred butter, and properly placed andirons. It is asked to remain aflame, shining, and growing in the house, and to grant glory, nourishment, booty, understanding, power, instructors, good reward, good renown, and preparation of the soul. The Fire examines those who approach it, blesses the person who brings properly prepared wood or sacred plants, and gives blessings concerning cattle, people, an active mind and soul, and a blest life.

Fire is a sacred witness and son of Ahura Mazda. It destroys thousands of unseen Daêvas, fiends, Yâtus, and Pairikas wherever sweet-smelling woods’ perfume travels. Fire in Vendidad (Vendîdâd)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Fire first appears

Abstract written by a model

Fire is the son of Ahura Mazda, approached with worship and protected from uncleanness. It is kept thirty paces from the cleansing ground and fifteen paces from a woman who has an issue of blood; the unclean avoid it during nine nights of seclusion. Cleansing words drive Pairika and the demon of uncleanness away. Fire is fed dry, clean wood carried by well-washed hands, while an offender brings vast loads of hard, soft, or sweet-scented wood as atonement. During the night, Fire calls the master of the house, the husbandman, and then Sraosha, blessing whoever brings clean wood.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard IX.

Fire is identified as the son of Ahura Mazda and must be kept thirty paces from the cleansing ground. The unclean one must avoid Fire during the nine nights of seclusion and may approach it afterward.

Fargard XI.

The fire is cleansed by saying aloud, "Thy fire, first of all, do we approach with worship, O Ahura Mazda!" Pairika and the demon of uncleanness that come upon the fire are driven away.

Fargard XIV.

The offender must bring ten thousand loads of hard, well-dried wood and ten thousand loads of soft or sweet-scented wood to the fire of Ahura Mazda as atonement.

Fargard XVI.

The fire must be kept fifteen paces from the woman. Her building must be erected higher than the house so that her look does not fall upon the fire.

Fargard XVIII.

Fire, the son of Ahura Mazda, calls the master of the house and the husbandman for clean wood during the first and second parts of the night. In the third part he calls Sraosha, and he blesses the person who brings him dry, clean wood with well-washed hands.
