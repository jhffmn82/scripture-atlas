Abstract written by a model

In The Good News According to Mark, resurrection is described as involving neither marriage nor being given in marriage, with those raised being like angels in heaven. In The Gospel According to Luke, resurrection is described as involving neither marriage nor being given in marriage for those worthy to attain it, who cannot die any more. In The Gospel According to John, resurrection and judgment is the destiny of those who have done evil, while those who have done good attain the resurrection of life. In The Gospel According to John, resurrection is affirmed by Martha as occurring at the last day, and Jesus demonstrates it by raising Lazarus. In The Acts of the Apostles, resurrection of the dead is presented as a doctrine taught by Moses and the prophets and central to Paul's trials. In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, resurrection of the dead is a doctrine some among the Corinthians deny and Paul disputes. In Paul’s Letter to the Philippians, resurrection is the event whose power Paul wants to know and whose attainment he hopes to reach.

resurrection of the dead in The Acts of the Apostles

author unknown · about AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where resurrection of the dead first appears

Abstract written by a model

Resurrection of the dead is presented as a doctrine taught by Moses and the prophets, with Christ to be first to rise. Paul's trial centers on it: in Chapter 23 it divides Pharisees, who affirm resurrection, angel and spirit, from Sadducees, who deny them. In Chapter 24 he hopes in God for the resurrection of the just and unjust. In Chapter 26 he calls it credible and tied to the fathers' promise, Christ proclaiming light.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 23

Paul says he is being judged concerning the hope and resurrection of the dead, which sets the Pharisees against the Sadducees. The text explains that the Sadducees deny resurrection, angel, and spirit while the Pharisees confess all of them.

Chapter 24

Paul states that he hopes toward God for a resurrection of the dead, both of the just and unjust, which his accusers also look for. He says this is the one thing for which he is being judged before the council.

Chapter 26

Paul asks why it is judged incredible if God raises the dead, and says his accusation concerns the hope of the promise to the fathers. He states that the Christ must suffer and, by the resurrection of the dead, be first to proclaim light to the people and the Gentiles.

resurrection of the dead in Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians

author unknown · AD 53 to 54 · the work’s page · read it where resurrection of the dead first appears

Abstract written by a model

Resurrection of the dead is a doctrine some among the Corinthians deny and Paul disputes. Its reality is bound to Christ's: without it, preaching, faith and the dead in Christ are vain. It came by man, as death did; Christ rises first, then his people at his coming, defended against objections.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 15

Its reality is tied to Christ's: if the dead are not raised, Christ was not raised, preaching and faith are vain, and those asleep in Christ have perished. It came by man, as death did, and Christ's resurrection as first fruits is followed by those who are Christ's at his coming. The speaker defends it against objections about how and with what body, and mentions people being baptized for the dead as a point in its favor.
