Also called: A mendicant, sick monk

Abstract written by a model

Mendicant is a person governed by prohibitions on approving or forbidding others’ bodily care, reciprocal acts, and cures directed at him. The account first covers washing, grooming, decorating, postures, places, charms, and living plant materials; it then narrows to reciprocal actions producing karman, including mutual wiping or rubbing of feet.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Thirteenth Lecture.

The mendicant should neither be pleased with nor prohibit another person's acts of wiping, washing, treating, grooming, decorating, or otherwise caring for his body. This applies whether the other person acts while seated in the Aṅka or Paryaṅka posture, in a garden or park, or reciprocally. The mendicant should also neither be pleased with nor prohibit attempts to cure him by pure or impure charms or by using living bulbs, roots, rind, or sprouts.

Fourteenth Lecture.

A mendicant should neither be pleased with nor prohibit reciprocal actions that relate to himself and produce karman. This includes wiping or rubbing each other’s feet.
