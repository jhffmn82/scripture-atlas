Ashi Vanguhi

Abstract written by a model

In Visparad, Ashi is worshipped as good and blessed, embodying the blest order of rites and recompense. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, Ashi Vanguhi is a powerful, healing, welfare-giving maid who grants glory, prosperity, wisdom, intelligence, victory, flocks, and Aryan Glory.

Ashi Vanguhi in Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Ashi Vanguhi first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ashi Vanguhi is a shining, high, tall-formed, fair, powerful, healing, welfare-giving maid and great Ashi, worthy of sacrifice. Daughter of Ahura Mazda and sister to the Amesha-Spentas, Sraosha, Rashnu, Mithra, and the Law of the worshippers of Mazda, she has a loud-sounding chariot and far-piercing rays. She gives glory, prosperity, heavenly wisdom, and enlivening intelligence to Saoshyants and those she follows or who invoke her. Made by Ahura Mazda, she enters the household and royal palace of a gift-giving faithful man, bringing welfare, flocks, victory, intelligence, and Aryan Glory. She rejects libations from specified people and laments sexual or reproductive wrongdoing.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XVII. Ashi Yast.

Ashi Vanguhi is described as shining, powerful, healing, wise, and worthy of sacrifice. She grants boons, gives glory and prosperity, aids those who invoke her, and is identified as the daughter of Ahura Mazda and the mother’s child of Ârmaiti Spenta. She rejects libations from certain men, a courtezan, young boys, and girls who have known no man, and laments three forms of sexual or reproductive wrongdoing.

XVIII. Âstâd Yast.

Ashi Vanguhi is made by Ahura Mazda and enters the family and fine royal palace of the man who gladdens the faithful with his gifts. She brings fulness of welfare, flocks, victory, intelligence, and the Glory of the Aryas.
