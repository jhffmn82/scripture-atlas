Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, the Fravashis of the saints receive offerings, praise, and worship, including the sacred Fravashi of Zarathustra Spitâma. In Yasna, the Fravashis of the saints are ancient spiritual beings who protect creation and are worshipped with homage. In Yasna, the Fravashis of the saints bless worshippers, heal, hinder hostility, and promote prosperity and glory. In Visparad, the Fravashis of the saints are worshipped collectively and individually, encompassing the dead, living, unborn, and future prophets. In Afrinagan, Gahs and Miscellaneous Fragments, the Fravashis of the saints receive sacrifice and homage and are invoked for prosperity and good order. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, the Fravashis of the faithful protect the world, aid the faithful, and fight evil.

Fravashis of the saints in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Fravashis of the saints first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Fravashis of the saints are good, heroic, bountiful, holy, redoubted, mighty, and overwhelming. They belong to the saints and provide mighty, overwhelming succour for them. Across the Yasna, they are announced and celebrated, approached with Yasts, praise, and worship, and repeatedly receive offerings for sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise. They are associated with ancient lore, next of kin, pious women, girls, maidens diligent among the cattle, and saints both dead and living. Worship includes them with the stars, moon, sun, Ahura, and Mithra, and specifically includes the sacred Fravashi of Zarathustra Spitâma. They are also named with Ahura Mazda as those who name the month.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna / Yasna I.

The Fravashis of the saints are announced and celebrated in the Yasna. They are described as redoubted and overwhelming evil, and the speaker includes the Fravashi of his own soul among those addressed.

Yasna III.

The Fravashis of the saints are approached with Yasts. They are described as mighty and overwhelming, and are associated with the saints, those who held to the ancient lore, and those of the next of kin.

Yasna IV.

The Fravashis of the saints receive offerings and are described as mighty and overwhelming for the succour of the saints.

Yasna VI.

The Fravashis of the saints are described as good, heroic, and bountiful. They are worshipped in the sacrifice, including together with the stars, moon, sun, and Ahura and Mithra, and the sacred Fravashi of Zarathustra Spitâma is also worshipped.

Yasna VII.

The Fravashis of the saints receive offerings, including offerings made to the ancient lore and next of kin. They are also named with Ahura Mazda as those who name the month.

Yasna XXIII.

They are approached with praise and worship. Their Fravashis include those of ancient lore, next-of-kin, pious women, girls, and maidens diligent among the cattle.

Yasna XXIV.

The Fravashis of the saints receive offerings for sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise. The presentation includes the Fravashis of saints who are dead and living.

Fravashis of the saints (ancient lore) in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Fravashis of the saints (ancient lore) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Fravashis of the saints are good, heroic, and bountiful spiritual beings of ancient lore. Existing from of old, they encompass those of houses, villages, communities, provinces, the house, Vîs, Zantuma, Dahvyuma, Zarathustrôtema, Ahura Mazda, and holy persons. They hold heaven, water, land, and cattle apart, and protect children in the womb from miscarriage. They are associated with saints and their next-of-kin, and are praised, invoked, glorified, and worshipped with good Yasnas and homage. The records proceed from their identity and scope, through their protective ordering of the world, to honor for holy teachers, disciples, children, men, and women from Gaya Maretan to Saoshyant.

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna XVII.

The good, heroic, and bountiful Fravashis of the saints are praised, invoked, and glorified. Those of the house, the Vîs, the Zantuma, the Dahvyuma, and the Zarathustrôtema are included.

Yasna XXIII.

They include the Fravashis of houses, villages, communities, provinces, Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Gaya Maretan, Zarathustra Spitâma, and other holy persons. They hold heaven, water, land, cattle, and children in the wombs safely enclosed apart.

Yasna XXVI.

The Fravashis are praised, invoked, and worshipped as good, heroic, and bountiful. The unit honors the Fravashis of Ahura Mazda, the saints, holy teachers and disciples, holy children, and all holy men and women from Gaya Maretan to Saoshyant.

Fravashis of the saints in Yasna

L.H. Mills · the work’s page · read it where Fravashis of the saints first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Fravashis of the saints are good, heroic, bountiful spirits belonging to saints, holy men, and holy women. Desired from first to last, they are worshipped and sacrificed to across the bodily and mental worlds. Worshippers ask them to come to the house and accompany the sacrificer hand in hand. Their blessed gifts heal, further better men, hinder the hostile, and promote abundant riches and glory. Their reach is figured as extending earth, rivers, and the high-reaching sun. They include Fravashis of saints who live or have lived, are born or yet to be born, and those connected with houses, villages, districts, provinces, and the Zarathustrôtemas. They are invited to draw near and are said to have borne waters upstream.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna XXXVII.

The Fravashis of the Just are worshipped. The worship extends to the Fravashis of saints, holy men, and holy women.

Yasna LX (Sp. LIX).

The good, heroic, and bountiful Fravashis of the saints are asked to come to the house and go hand in hand with the worshippers. Their healing gifts are associated with the furtherance of better men, hindrance of the hostile, and growth of riches and glory.

Yasna LXIII (Sp. LXII). / Yasna LXIV (Sp. LXIII). / Yasna LXV (Sp. LXIV).

The saints’ Fravashis, including those of the living, the dead, the born, and the yet unborn, are invited to draw near. They are described as having borne the waters upstream from the nearest waters below.

Yasna LXXI (Sp. LXX).

The good, heroic, and bounteous Fravashis of the saints are worshipped and sacrificed to, including those of the bodily and mental worlds, houses, villages, districts, provinces, and the Zarathustrôtemas.

Fravashis of the faithful in Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Fravashis of the faithful first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Fravashis of the faithful are awful, overpowering, holy, beneficent, undecaying beings belonging to the Amesha-Spentas and greater than words can express. They help Ahura Mazda with vigour, strength, glory, help, and joy, preserve the material world, and maintain the sky, waters, earth, cattle, plants, and unborn children through brightness and glory. They give victory, health, glory, prosperity, and protection to faithful invokers, the sick, worthy sovereigns, houses, and those in distress. During Hamaspathmaêdha they come and go through boroughs for ten nights, seeking sacrifice, prayer, food, and clothing. They seek and bring water for kindred, boroughs, towns, and countries; fight for their own lands; and appear as watchful warriors or well-winged birds rushing to help. They serve as weapons and shields, protecting against the Drug, fiends, evil-doers, Angra Mainyu, and weapons. Associated with perception, intellect, and conscience, and belonging to the Saoshyants, they stood firm at the creation of the world and, with Vohû Manô and Âtar, destroyed Angra Mainyu. They are protected by Ahura Mazda and Sraosha and are asked to carry hymns and worship to Ahura Mazda and the Amesha-Spentas.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

XIII. Farvardîn Yast.

The Fravashis of the faithful sustain the sky, waters, earth, cattle, plants, and unborn children, and help preserve the material world from the Drug. They give victory, health, glory, prosperity, and protection to those who invoke and honor them, and during the Hamaspathmaêdha they visit settlements seeking sacrifice, prayer, food, and clothing.

XXII.

The Fravashis of the faithful are worshipped as strong, beneficent, victorious, healing, and effective. They seek water for their kindred, boroughs, towns, and countries, fight in local battles, protect worthy rulers, and ask for praise, sacrifice, meditation, blessings, food, and clothes. They are said to have stood firm when the Good Spirit and the Evil One created the world and to have destroyed Angra Mainyu’s malice.

XXIII.

The Fravashis are worshipped as good, strong, beneficent, undecaying, and holy. They include those of the Amesha-Spentas, divine beings, elements, plants, animals, human figures, holy creations, and many named holy people.

XXVI.

The Fravashis of the faithful are worshipped as awful, beneficent, overpowering, and victorious. They are asked to come quickly, help, protect those in distress, bless houses, and carry hymns and worship to Ahura Mazda and the Amesha-Spentas.
