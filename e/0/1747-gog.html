Gog

Abstract written by a model

Gog is a prince of Rosh, Meshech, and Tubal, from the land of Magog, who leads an army and allied peoples. Prophets of Israel had long foretold that God would bring him against Israel. The LORD declares himself Gog's adversary, turns him around, hooks his jaws, and draws him from the far north to plunder Israel's unwalled villages. There, judgment falls on him and his hordes through sword, pestilence, blood, rain, hailstones, fire, and sulfur. His bow and arrows are struck from his hands, and he and his multitude fall in the open field. They are buried in Israel, in the valley of Hamon Gog.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 38

Gog, prince of Rosh, Meshech, and Tubal of the land of Magog, is declared an adversary of the LORD, who will turn him around, put hooks in his jaws, and bring him out. He will devise an evil plan to take plunder from Israel's unwalled villages, and the LORD says he is the one spoken of long ago by the prophets. He and his hordes will face judgment by sword, pestilence, blood, rain, hailstones, fire, and sulfur.

Chapter 39

Gog, prince of Rosh, Meshech, and Tubal, is declared an enemy of the Lord GOD, who will turn him around and bring him from the uttermost parts of the north onto the mountains of Israel. His bow and arrows are struck from his hands, and he and all his hordes and allied peoples fall in the open field. He is given a burial place in Israel, the valley of Hamon Gog, where he and his multitude are buried.
