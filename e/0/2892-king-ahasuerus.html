Abstract written by a model

King Ahasuerus is a great king who reigns from the throne in Susa over 127 provinces stretching from India to Ethiopia. He holds a ring used to seal decrees, a golden sceptre whose extension spares the life of anyone entering uncalled, a robe of fine linen, a horse, servants, noble friends and two chamberlains. He levies a tax on land and sea, and his strength, valour, wealth and glory are recorded in the book of the Persians and Medes. The story shows a powerful but easily swayed ruler whose decrees, once sealed, cannot be countermanded. It opens with Mordecai informing him of a plot, and the chamberlains Gabatha and Tharrha, who are executed, and the matter recorded. In his third year he holds a long feast displaying his wealth, then grows angry when Vashti refuses his summons and follows Memucan's advice in issuing a decree to all provinces. His anger subsides, and he gathers virgins, loves Esther above all, crowns her queen, holds a seven-day banquet and grants a remission of taxes. He then honors Haman above his friends, orders all to bow to him, accepts his accusation against the Jews, and gives him the ring to seal decrees of destruction, with the silver as well. Esther, who has not been called for thirty days, risks appearing uncalled. The king looks at her with anger, but God changes his spirit to gentleness; he embraces her, lays the sceptre on her neck, and offers up to half his kingdom. Unable to sleep, he has the records read, learns Mordecai was never rewarded, and has Haman honor Mordecai. At Esther's feast he learns of the threat to her and her people, reacts with anger at finding Haman on her couch, and orders him hanged on his own gallows. His wrath subsides. He gives Esther Haman's possessions and Mordecai the ring, extends the sceptre, and authorizes writings in his name permitting the Jews to defend themselves. He hears the Susa death toll, permits a second day of killing, and gives Haman's sons' bodies to be hanged. The account ends with his tax levy, the record of his glory, Mordecai as his viceroy, and Esther as the wife he made queen.

Esther (Greek) / Chapter 1

Ahasuerus, great king over one hundred twenty-seven provinces, was saved from a plot by the chamberlains Gabatha and Tharrha after Mordecai informed him, and he had them executed and recorded the matter. In his third year he held a long feast and banquet displaying his wealth, then grew grieved and angry when Vashti refused his summons. He sought legal judgment from his advisers and followed Memucan's advice by issuing a decree sent to all provinces.

Chapter 2

King Ahasuerus's anger subsided and he no longer mentioned Vashti. He accepted his servants' plan to gather virgins, loved Esther above all, put the queen's crown on her, held a seven-day banquet for his friends and great men, and granted a remission of taxes. He examined and hanged the two chamberlains who sought to kill him and ordered Mordecai's goodwill noted in the royal library.

Chapter 3

King Ahasuerus highly honors Haman, sets his seat above all his friends, and orders that all bow to him. He accepts Haman's accusation against the Jews, gives Haman his ring to seal the decrees, tells him to keep the silver and treat the nation as he will, and then sits drinking with Haman while Susa is confused. The letter sent in his name presents the destruction as serving peace and order in the kingdom.

Chapter 4

The king is the one before whom Esther must appear uncalled at risk of death unless he stretches out his golden sceptre, and he has not called her for thirty days. Haman promised him ten thousand talents for the destruction of the Jews. Esther prays that the Lord put harmonious speech into her mouth before the king, called the lion, and turn his heart to hate the one who fights against the Jews.

Chapter 5

The king sits on his throne in glorious apparel, terrifying, and looks at Esther with intense anger. God changes his spirit to gentleness, so he springs from the throne, embraces and comforts her, and lays the golden sceptre on her neck. He offers her up to half his kingdom, and agrees to come with Haman to her feast and to return the next day.

Chapter 6

Ahasuerus, deprived of sleep by the Lord, has the records read to him and learns that Mordecai exposed the plot of the two chamberlains without being rewarded. He asks Haman what should be done for the man he wishes to honor, praises Haman's answer, and orders him to do it for Mordecai the Jew without neglecting a word of it.

Chapter 7

The king offers Esther up to half his kingdom and asks who dared to threaten her and her people. He reacts angrily when he finds Haman on the couch by the queen, and after hearing about the gallows he orders Haman hanged on it. His wrath then subsides.

Chapter 8

King Ahasuerus gives Esther Haman's possessions, gives Mordecai the ring taken from Haman, and extends the golden sceptre to Esther. He tells Esther and Mordecai to write in his name and seal with his ring, since such writings cannot be countermanded, and his name is on the letter sent to the provinces permitting the Jews to defend themselves.

Chapter 9

Ahasuerus, the king, saw his letters arrive and his order take force on the thirteenth of Adar. He heard the Susa death toll and asked Esther what more she wished. He permitted the second day of killing and gave the bodies of Haman's sons to the Jews to hang. His kingdom is the one to which Mordecai sent the Purim letter.

Chapter 10

King Ahasuerus levies a tax on his kingdom by land and sea. His strength, valour, wealth and glory are said to be written in the book of the Persians and Medes. Mordecai serves as his viceroy, and Esther is the woman he married and made queen.
