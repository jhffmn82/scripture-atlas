Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, good Mazdayasnian Religion is followed through thoughts, words, and deeds, borne by Mazda to H(a)oma, and maintained through remembrance, practice, and the Mãthra. In Yasna, Mazdayasnian Faith is announced, worshipped, praised, invoked, and offered through its law, blessings, sciences, forces, and ritual practice. In Yasna, Mazdayasnian Faith is established and maintained by Zarathustra, proclaimed with the pure Daêna, joined to wisdom, law, Asha, and true life, and opposed to the Daêvas. In Yasna, Good Faith is worshipped as the good law of thrift. In Visparad, Mazdayasnian Faith follows Zarathustra, joins religious communities, advances settlements through Justice, and is practiced through offerings, praises, Yasnas, doctrines, recitations, and movements.

good Mazdayasnian Religion in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where good Mazdayasnian Religion first appears

Abstract written by a model

The good Mazdayasnian Religion is the faith of the good Religion, of Ahura and according to Zarathustra, with a long descent. Leaders of houses, villages, Zantus, and provinces are urged to follow it through thoughts, words, and deeds. Ancient and spirit-made, it is a star-bespangled girdle borne by Mazda to H(a)oma, who wears it on mountain summits to spread the Mãthra’s precepts and headings and help its teacher. It is worshipped through remembrance, practice, and the Mãthra, maintained in the saint’s understanding.

Mazdayasnian Faith in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Mazdayasnian Faith first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Mazdayasnian Faith is a good religion with a long descent, announced and completed in the Yasna. It is worshipped and named with Mãthra Spenta and the Zarathustrian law, then identified with the good Religion. As Mazdayasnian law, it is addressed alongside the Bountiful Immortals, just men and women, and Zaothras, and connected to teaching liberality of Righteousness. Praised as holy, imposing, best, and most beautiful, it includes kindred marriage, has no faltering utterance, and wields the felling halbert. Blessed and good, it is invoked with its sciences, forces, fire-priests, charioteers, warriors, and tillers. Its blessings accompany sacrifice; its fulfilment, Gâtha recital, and propitiation belong to ritual. Finally, recollection and practice are offerings, and its law is worshipped as most true, Mazda-made, holy religious Knowledge.

Mazdayasnian Faith in Yasna

L.H. Mills · the work’s page · read it where Mazdayasnian Faith first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Mazdayasnian Faith is a benignant, good, holy religion and religious choice. Its practice, teachings, and diffusion are prayerful purposes. Zarathustra seeks to establish and maintain it with Ahura Mazda's grace and the Good Mind, declaring its truths and advancing it through supporters. Proclaimed with the pure Daêna, it is guarded against Wrath of Rapine and joined by believing natures. Associated with wisdom, law, true life, Asha, and a great cause, it is received with desire and worshipped whole, opposed to the Daêvas.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna XXXV.

The Mazdayasnian Faith is described as benignant. Its practice, teachings, and diffusion are among the purposes of the prayer.

Yasna XLVI.

Zarathustra seeks to establish and maintain the Faith with Ahura Mazda's grace and the Good Mind. Its truths are to be declared, and its supporters are called to advance it.

Yasna XLIX.

Faith is to be proclaimed together with the pure Daêna as the Faith of Ahura Mazda. The faithful guard it against Wrath of Rapine, and only believing natures are to join in the best recompense.

Yasna LI.

The good Religion is associated with wisdom, correct laws, the true life, Asha, and the great cause. The people are urged to receive it with desire for the gaining of Asha.

Yasna LXXI (Sp. LXX).

The entire Mazdayasnian Faith is sacrificed to and worshipped, including its system set up against the Daêvas.
