Abstract written by a model

In Numbers, Zelophehad's daughters inherited his property after his death in the wilderness, and their marriages preserved it within his tribal family. In The Book of Joshua, Zelophehad was identified as a descendant of Manasseh who had five daughters and no sons.

Zelophehad in Numbers

author unknown · 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Zelophehad first appears

Abstract written by a model

Zelophehad, son of Hepher of Manasseh's families, died in the wilderness in his own sin, not as part of Korah's company, leaving no sons. His daughters, including Mahlah, were granted his inheritance by the LORD's command, and their marriages were arranged to keep it within their father's tribal family.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 27

Zelophehad, son of Hepher, of the families of Manasseh, died in the wilderness in his own sin and had no sons. He was not part of Korah's company that gathered against the LORD, and his daughters were granted his inheritance.

Chapter 36

Zelophehad is referred to as the brother of the Gilead family heads, whose inheritance was commanded by the LORD to be given to his daughters. His daughters' marriages keep his inheritance in the tribe of the family of their father.

Zelophehad in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · about 600 BC · the work’s page · read it where Zelophehad first appears

Abstract written by a model

Zelophehad, son of Hepher in a lineage through Gilead, Machir and Manasseh, had no sons, only five daughters: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah and Tirzah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 17

Zelophehad is identified as the son of Hepher, son of Gilead, son of Machir, son of Manasseh. He had no sons, only daughters, who named Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah and Tirzah.
