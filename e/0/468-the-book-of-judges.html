The Book of Judges

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This document is the book of Judges, a narrative of Israel's cycle of failure after Joshua's death. It opens with incomplete conquest: Judah and Simeon succeed, Caleb's daughter Achsah is given to Othniel, but most tribes fail to drive out the Canaanites. An angel at Bochim rebukes Israel, and the text sets out the pattern: a new generation forgets the LORD and serves Baal, the LORD hands Israel to oppressors, he raises a judge to save them, and Israel relapses when the judge dies. Remaining nations are left to test Israel. Judges follow in turn: Othniel defeats Cushan Rishathaim; Ehud kills Eglon of Moab; Shamgar strikes Philistines; Deborah and Barak defeat Sisera, with Jael killing him by a tent peg, celebrated in a victory song that names tribes that fought and those that held back. Gideon, called while Midian devastates Israel, destroys his father's Baal altar, tests God with a fleece, and with three hundred men, trumpets, pitchers and torches routs Midian, then kills the kings Zebah and Zalmunna. He refuses rule but makes an ephod that becomes a snare. His son Abimelech murders his seventy brothers, becomes king at Shechem, destroys the city, and dies when a woman drops a millstone on him, fulfilling Jotham's curse. After Tola and Jair, Israel is oppressed by Philistines and Ammonites; Jephthah, driven out by his kin and recalled as chief, vows to sacrifice whatever first meets him and his only daughter does. He fights Ephraim, whose men are identified by 'Shibboleth.' Ibzan, Elon and Abdon follow. Samson, a Nazirite born to Manoah's wife, marries a Philistine, uses a riddle, burns Philistine fields with foxes, kills a thousand with a jawbone, and is betrayed by Delilah, who learns his strength lies in his hair. Blinded and enslaved, he pulls down Dagon's temple pillars, killing more in death than in life. The final chapters, repeatedly noting there was no king and everyone did what was right in his own eyes, show moral collapse: Micah's idols and hired Levite priest are taken by Danites who conquer Laish; a Levite's concubine is raped and killed at Gibeah of Benjamin. Israel's tribes war on Benjamin, nearly destroying it, then take wives for the survivors by slaughtering Jabesh Gilead and seizing the daughters of Shiloh.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

21 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

After Joshua's death, the Israelites ask the LORD who should first fight the Canaanites, and he names Judah. Judah allies with his brother Simeon, defeats the Canaanites and Perizzites at Bezek, captures and mutilates Adoni-Bezek, and takes Jerusalem, Hebron, Debir, and other places. Caleb gives his daughter Achsah to Othniel for capturing Kiriath Sepher, and she also obtains springs of water. The house of Joseph takes Bethel with the help of a man who shows them the entrance, while Benjamin, Manasseh, Ephraim, Zebulun, Asher, Naphtali, and Dan fail to drive out the inhabitants of their lands. Many Canaanites remain, some put to forced labor, and the Amorites push Dan into the hill country.

Chapter 2

The LORD's angel comes from Gilgal to Bochim and rebukes Israel for disobeying the LORD by making covenants with the land's inhabitants and not breaking down their altars. He announces that the LORD will no longer drive those peoples out, and the people weep and sacrifice at Bochim. Joshua sends the people to their inheritances, and Israel serves the LORD through his lifetime and that of the elders who outlived him; Joshua dies at 110 and is buried in Timnath Heres. A new generation that does not know the LORD then serves Baal and the Ashtaroth, provoking the LORD's anger, so he gives Israel over to raiders and enemies. He raises up judges to save them, but Israel repeatedly relapses after each judge dies, so the LORD declares he will leave the remaining nations to test whether Israel will keep his way.

Chapter 3

Chapter 3 explains that the LORD left certain nations in the land, including the five lords of the Philistines, the Canaanites, the Sidonians and the Hivites, to test Israel and to teach its generations war. Israel lived among these peoples, intermarried with them, and served the Baals and the Asheroth, so the LORD sold them to Cushan Rishathaim king of Mesopotamia for eight years until Othniel the son of Kenaz saved them, after which the land had rest forty years. After Othniel died, Israel again did evil and the LORD strengthened Eglon king of Moab, who oppressed them eighteen years. The Benjamite Ehud, who was left-handed, killed the very fat Eglon with a two-edged sword in his upper room, escaped, and led Israel to strike about ten thousand Moabites at the Jordan fords, giving the land rest for eighty years. Shamgar the son of Anath is then briefly noted for striking six hundred Philistines with an ox goad and saving Israel.

Chapter 4

After Ehud's death, the children of Israel again did evil, and the LORD sold them into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, whose army captain Sisera commanded nine hundred iron chariots and oppressed Israel for twenty years. Deborah, a prophetess and judge, summoned Barak and relayed the LORD's command to lead ten thousand men from Naphtali and Zebulun to Mount Tabor; Barak agreed only if she went with him, and she told him the honor would go to a woman. Sisera gathered his chariots and army at the river Kishon, where the LORD confused them before Barak and all of Sisera's army fell by the sword. Sisera fled on foot to the tent of Jael, wife of Heber the Kenite, who covered him, gave him milk, and killed him in his sleep by driving a tent peg through his temples. Barak arrived to find Sisera dead, and the children of Israel increasingly prevailed until they destroyed Jabin.

Chapter 5

Deborah and Barak the son of Abinoam sing a victory song praising the LORD, the God of Israel, for delivering Israel. The song recalls the LORD's coming from Seir and Edom, Israel's earlier unrest in the days of Shamgar and Jael, and Deborah's rise as a mother in Israel. It lists the tribes that joined the fight (Ephraim, Benjamin, Machir, Zebulun, Issachar, Naphtali) and those that held back (Reuben, Gilead, Dan, Asher), and curses Meroz for not helping. The kings of Canaan fought at Taanach, the stars and the river Kishon fought against Sisera, and Jael, wife of Heber the Kenite, struck Sisera dead with a tent peg and hammer. Sisera's mother waits in vain at the window, and the land has rest forty years.

Chapter 6

Because the children of Israel did evil, the LORD delivered them into Midian's hand for seven years, and Midian, Amalek and the children of the east devastated their crops and livestock until Israel cried out. The LORD sent a prophet to recall his deliverance from Egypt and Israel's failure to listen, then his angel appeared to Gideon, son of Joash, at Ophrah, called him a mighty man of valor, and commissioned him to save Israel. After Gideon's offering was consumed by fire from a rock, he built an altar, and that night he obeyed the LORD by tearing down his father's altar of Baal and cutting down the Asherah, offering Joash's second bull. When the townsmen demanded Gideon's death, Joash defended him and Gideon was named Jerub-Baal. As the enemy camped in Jezreel, the LORD's Spirit came on Gideon, who gathered Israelite tribes and then tested God with a fleece, first wet with dew on dry ground and then dry on dewy ground.

Chapter 7

In Judges 7, Gideon (also called Jerubbaal) and the Israelite army camp at the spring of Harod opposite the Midianite camp in the valley. The LORD says the army is too large, so Gideon sends home twenty-two thousand fearful men, and a test at the water reduces the remaining ten thousand to three hundred who lapped. Encouraged by a Midianite's dream of a barley cake overturning a tent and its interpretation as Gideon's sword, Gideon divides the three hundred into three companies armed with trumpets, empty pitchers, and torches. At night they blow trumpets, break the pitchers, and shout, and the LORD sets the Midianites' swords against one another so the army flees. Men from Naphtali, Asher, Manasseh, and Ephraim pursue, and the Ephraimites capture and kill the princes Oreb and Zeeb and bring their heads to Gideon beyond the Jordan.

Chapter 8

Gideon calms the men of Ephraim, who rebuke him for not calling them, by praising their capture of the Midianite princes Oreb and Zeeb. He then pursues the Midianite kings Zebah and Zalmunna with his three hundred men, but the men of Succoth and Penuel refuse them bread. Gideon defeats the remaining Midianite army, captures the two kings, punishes Succoth with thorns and briers and destroys the tower of Penuel while killing its men, and kills Zebah and Zalmunna because they had killed his brothers at Tabor. The men of Israel offer to make Gideon ruler, which he refuses, but he collects gold earrings and makes an ephod that becomes a snare as Israel plays the prostitute with it. After forty years of rest Gideon dies, and Israel turns to the Baals and fails to show kindness to Gideon's house.

Chapter 9

Abimelech, son of Jerubbaal, persuades the men of Shechem to back him, hires reckless men with silver from the house of Baal Berith, kills his seventy brothers at Ophrah on one stone, and is made king; only Jotham survives by hiding. Jotham tells the parable of the trees from Mount Gerizim and curses Abimelech and Shechem with mutual fire, then flees to Beer. After three years God sends an evil spirit between them, and Shechem turns on Abimelech, backing Gaal the son of Ebed, who is defeated through the warning of Zebul and driven out. Abimelech destroys Shechem, sows it with salt, and burns about a thousand people in the stronghold of the house of Elberith. At Thebez a woman drops a millstone on his head, he has his armor bearer kill him, and the text says God repaid the wickedness of Abimelech and Shechem as Jotham cursed.

Chapter 10

Chapter 10 records two judges: Tola of Issachar, who judged Israel twenty-three years and was buried in Shamir, and Jair the Gileadite, who judged twenty-two years, had thirty sons and thirty cities, and was buried in Kamon. Israel then again did evil, serving the Baals, Ashtaroth and the gods of many neighboring peoples, and abandoned the LORD. In anger the LORD sold them into the hands of the Philistines and the Ammonites, who oppressed them for eighteen years, including beyond the Jordan in Gilead. Israel cried out and confessed sin; the LORD reminded them of his past rescues and told them to cry to their chosen gods, but they put away the foreign gods and served him, and his soul was grieved by their misery. The Ammonites encamped in Gilead, Israel assembled at Mizpah, and the princes of Gilead offered headship over Gilead to whoever would begin the fight.

Chapter 11

Jephthah the Gileadite, a mighty man of valor and the son of a prostitute, is driven out by Gilead's other sons and flees to the land of Tob. When the children of Ammon make war on Israel, the elders of Gilead bring him back and make him head and chief over Gilead. Jephthah sends messengers to the king of the children of Ammon, rebutting the claim to the land by recounting Israel's history with Edom, Moab, and Sihon the Amorite, but the king does not listen. After the LORD's Spirit comes on him, Jephthah vows that whatever first comes out of his house to meet him will be the LORD's burnt offering, and the LORD delivers Ammon into his hand. His only child, his daughter, comes out to meet him; she tells him to do as he vowed, mourns her virginity on the mountains for two months, and then he does with her according to his vow, after which Israel's daughters yearly celebrate her four days a year.

Chapter 12

The men of Ephraim confront Jephthah for not summoning them to fight the children of Ammon and threaten to burn his house. Jephthah answers that they failed to save him when he called, so he risked his life and the LORD delivered the Ammonites to him. He gathers the men of Gilead and fights Ephraim, seizing the fords of the Jordan and killing anyone who cannot pronounce 'Shibboleth' correctly, and forty-two thousand of Ephraim fall. Jephthah judges Israel six years and is buried in the cities of Gilead. Ibzan of Bethlehem, Elon the Zebulunite, and Abdon son of Hillel the Pirathonite then judge Israel for seven, ten, and eight years respectively, each dying and being buried in his own place.

Chapter 13

After the children of Israel again did evil, the LORD delivered them into the hand of the Philistines for forty years. The LORD's angel appeared to the barren wife of Manoah, a Danite of Zorah, and told her she would bear a son who would be a Nazirite to God and begin to save Israel from the Philistines. Manoah prayed for the man of God to return, and the angel came again, repeated his instructions, declined Manoah's food, and told him to offer a burnt offering to the LORD. When the angel ascended in the flame of the altar, Manoah realized who he was and feared they would die, but his wife reasoned that the LORD's acceptance of the offering showed otherwise. She bore a son, Samson, whom the LORD blessed and whose Spirit began to move him in Mahaneh Dan.

Chapter 14

Samson sees a Philistine woman in Timnah and asks his parents to get her for him as his wife, despite their objection; the narrator says the matter was of the LORD, who sought an occasion against the Philistines. On the way to Timnah, Samson tears apart a young lion with the LORD's Spirit on him, and later finds bees and honey in its carcass, eating the honey and sharing it with his parents without telling them its source. At his wedding feast he poses a riddle about the lion and honey to thirty companions, wagering thirty linen garments and thirty changes of clothing. The companions threaten Samson's wife, she weeps until he tells her the answer, and she reveals it to them, so they answer him on the seventh day. Samson, with the LORD's Spirit on him, goes to Ashkelon, kills thirty men and pays the garments with their plunder, then goes angrily to his father's house, and his wife is given to his companion.

Chapter 15

In Judges 15, Samson comes to visit his wife at harvest time but her father refuses him entry, having given her to Samson's companion. Samson retaliates by tying torches to the tails of three hundred foxes and loosing them to burn the Philistines' grain and olive groves; the Philistines respond by burning his wife and her father, and Samson strikes them with great slaughter and takes refuge in the cave in Etam's rock. The Philistines then camp at Lehi in Judah, and three thousand men of Judah bind Samson with two new ropes to hand him over. At Lehi the LORD's Spirit comes on Samson, he breaks the ropes, and with a fresh donkey's jawbone he strikes a thousand men. Thirsty, he calls on the LORD, God splits the hollow place in Lehi so water comes out, and the narrator notes that Samson judged Israel twenty years in the days of the Philistines.

Chapter 16

Samson visits a prostitute in Gaza, and the Gazites lie in wait to kill him, but he rises at midnight and carries off the city gate doors, posts and all, to a mountain before Hebron. He then loves Delilah of the valley of Sorek, and the lords of the Philistines offer her eleven hundred pieces of silver each to learn the source of his strength. After three false answers that she tests on him, Delilah presses him daily until he reveals he is a Nazirite whose strength depends on never being shaved. She has his seven locks shaved while he sleeps on her knees, and the Philistines seize him, put out his eyes, and set him to grind at the mill in the prison in Gaza. At a sacrifice to Dagon, with his hair growing back, Samson is brought out to entertain them, prays to the LORD for strength, pulls down the two middle pillars, and kills more people in his death than in his life; his family buries him between Zorah and Eshtaol, and the text says he judged Israel twenty years.

Chapter 17

Chapter 17 introduces Micah, a man of the hill country of Ephraim, who confesses to his mother that he took her eleven hundred pieces of silver, which she had cursed. He returns it, and she dedicates it to the LORD for a carved image and a molten image, for which she gives two hundred pieces to a silversmith. Micah keeps a house of gods, makes an ephod and teraphim, and consecrates one of his sons as priest. The text notes that there was no king in Israel and everyone did what was right in his own eyes. A young Levite from Bethlehem Judah, seeking a place to live, arrives at Micah's house, and Micah hires him as priest for ten pieces of silver per year, clothing, and food, then declares that the LORD will do him good because he has a Levite as priest.

Chapter 18

The tribe of Dan, lacking an inheritance, sends five men to spy out the land; at Micah's house in the hill country of Ephraim they recognize the young Levite and ask him to seek counsel from God, and he tells them their way is before the LORD. The five find Laish, a quiet, secure people far from the Sidonians, and urge their brothers to attack. Six hundred armed Danites travel to Micah's house, where the five take the engraved image, ephod, teraphim, and molten image, and the priest agrees to go with them. Micah pursues but retreats when threatened, and the Danites strike Laish with the sword, burn it, and rebuild it as Dan. They set up Micah's engraved image, with Jonathan son of Gershom, son of Moses, and his sons as priests.

Chapter 19

A Levite from the far side of the hill country of Ephraim goes to Bethlehem Judah to bring back his concubine, who had left him, and her father keeps delaying their departure for several days. The Levite finally leaves late in the day with his servant, the concubine, and two donkeys, refuses to lodge in the Jebusite city, and stops at Gibeah of Benjamin, where an old man from Ephraim takes them in. Wicked men of the city surround the house and demand the Levite be brought out for sex; the old man offers his virgin daughter and the concubine instead, and the Levite pushes his concubine out to them. They rape and abuse her all night, and she collapses at the door, and in the morning the Levite finds her, takes her home on the donkey, cuts her into twelve pieces, and sends them throughout Israel. All who see it say nothing like it has happened since Israel left Egypt and call for consideration and counsel.

Chapter 20

All the children of Israel assemble at Mizpah, where the Levite recounts how the men of Gibeah attacked him and raped his concubine, who died, and how he cut her in pieces and sent her throughout Israel. Israel vows to punish Gibeah and demands that Benjamin hand over the wicked men, but Benjamin refuses and musters twenty-six thousand swordsmen plus seven hundred chosen men of Gibeah. Benjamin defeats Israel on two days, killing twenty-two thousand and then eighteen thousand, even though the LORD had directed Israel to go up, with Judah first. After weeping, fasting and offering sacrifices at Bethel, Israel is told by the LORD that he will deliver Benjamin into their hand, and on the third day Israel uses ambushers and a feigned retreat. The ambushers burn Gibeah, the smoke signal turns the battle, and Benjamin loses twenty-five thousand men, with only six hundred escaping to the rock of Rimmon while Israel destroys the cities of Benjamin.

Chapter 21

The men of Israel, who had sworn at Mizpah not to give their daughters to Benjamin, gather at Bethel and weep that one tribe is missing from Israel. Finding that no one from Jabesh Gilead came to the assembly, they send twelve thousand valiant men to strike its inhabitants, sparing four hundred young virgins who are brought to the camp at Shiloh. These women are given to the returned Benjamites, but there are not enough. The elders then tell the Benjamites to hide in the vineyards at the yearly feast of the LORD in Shiloh and seize wives from the dancing daughters of Shiloh, and the Benjamites do so and return to rebuild their cities. The book closes with the people going home and the remark that there was no king in Israel and everyone did what was right in his own eyes.
