In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, Aquila is Paul's fellow worker in Christ Jesus who risked his neck for Paul's life, earned thanks from Gentile assemblies, and hosted an assembly in his house.

Aquila in The Acts of the Apostles

author unknown · about AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where Aquila first appears

Abstract written by a model

Aquila is a Jewish tentmaker from Pontus, husband of Priscilla, expelled from Rome under Claudius. He hosts and works with Paul, sails with him to Ephesus, and there instructs Apollos with Priscilla.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

Aquila is a Jew from Pontus who recently came from Italy with his wife Priscilla because Claudius ordered the Jews out of Rome. He shares Paul's tent-making trade and hosts him, then sails with Paul and is left at Ephesus. There he and Priscilla take Apollos aside and explain the way of God to him more accurately.
