Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the throne and kingdom of the LORD in the heavens rules over all as glorious, majestic, everlasting, and enduring through all generations. In Daniel (Greek), the everlasting kingdom endures forever, is established by the God of heaven, destroys other kingdoms, and is given to the one like a son of man and the saints. In The Gospel According to Matthew, God's Kingdom is prayed for, belongs to the Father with power and glory, is to be sought first with his righteousness, and is given to a fruit-producing nation instead of the leaders. In The Gospel According to Matthew, the Kingdom of Heaven is the central subject of Jesus's preaching, described as the Father's place and God's Kingdom, with conditions for entry and membership illustrated through parables. In The Good News According to Mark, God's Kingdom is proclaimed by Jesus as at hand, its mystery is given to the disciples, and it is likened to growing seed and a mustard seed while requiring childlike reception and being difficult for the rich to enter. In The Gospel According to Luke, God's Kingdom is the central subject of Jesus' teaching, both present and still to be entered, and is described through preaching, prayer, growth, judgment, childlike reception, and the difficulty of entry for the rich. In The Gospel According to John, God's Kingdom cannot be seen unless one is born anew or entered unless one is born of water and Spirit, as stated to Nicodemus. In The Acts of the Apostles, God's Kingdom is preached and testified about by Paul in Rome for two years with all boldness and without hindrance.

and 2 more works

In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, God’s Kingdom is not inherited by the unrighteous, including the sexually immoral, idolaters, adulterers, thieves, drunkards, slanderers, and extortionists. In Revelation, the kingdom of the world becomes the Kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ after the seventh angel sounds, and he will reign forever and ever.

Kingdom of Heaven in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where Kingdom of Heaven first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Kingdom of Heaven is the central subject of Jesus's preaching in these records, proclaimed as Good News in the synagogues of Galilee with a call to repent because it is at hand. It is also described as the place of the Father and called God's Kingdom. It belongs to the poor in spirit, to those persecuted for righteousness' sake, and to little children and ones like them. Entry demands righteousness exceeding that of the scribes and Pharisees and requires turning and humbling oneself like a child. A rich man enters with difficulty, and some make themselves eunuchs for its sake. Within it, those who keep and teach even the least commandments are called great, those who break them least, and the least member is greater than John. Since John's days it suffers violence, and the violent take it by force. Its places beside Jesus are for those whom his Father has prepared. Most of the records are parables. It is likened to a field with good seed and darnel, a mustard seed that becomes a tree, yeast in meal, hidden treasure, a valuable pearl, and a dragnet. These images show small beginnings, worth giving up everything for, and a final separation of righteous and wicked. It is also likened to a king settling accounts, a master hiring laborers (the last first, the first last), a king's wedding feast, and ten virgins awaiting the bridegroom, some ready and admitted, others not. Several parables end with many called but few chosen. Overall it moves from announcement, to its conditions and members, to its contested status, to parables of growth, value, reversal, and judgment.

Chapter 22

Chapter 25

The Kingdom of Heaven is likened to ten virgins who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom. The parable shows that some are ready and are admitted, while others are not.

God's Kingdom in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where God's Kingdom first appears

Abstract written by a model

God's Kingdom is the central subject of Jesus' teaching in these records: a reality that is both present and still to be entered. It begins in Chapter 9 as the message the twelve are sent to preach, demanding undivided commitment. In Chapter 10 the seventy announce that it has come near, and rejecting the messengers brings a harsher judgment than Sodom's. In Chapter 11 it is prayed for, and Jesus' casting out of demons shows it has come, in contrast to Satan's divided kingdom. In Chapter 12 disciples are told to seek it, and the Father gladly gives it to the little flock, so they need not fear. Chapter 13 describes it through the mustard seed and yeast, growing from small beginnings, and says people from every direction will sit down in it with Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and the prophets, while some are excluded and the last become first. In Chapter 17 Jesus tells the Pharisees it does not come with observation and is within them. Chapter 18 says it belongs to little children, that one must receive it like a child, that entry is very hard for the rich, and that those who leave family for its sake gain much now and eternal life.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

God's Kingdom is what the twelve are sent to preach and what Jesus speaks of to the multitudes. Jesus says some standing there will not taste death until they see it, and that one who looks back after putting his hand to the plow is not fit for it.

Chapter 10

The seventy are told to announce that God's Kingdom has come near to the cities they enter, whether those cities receive them or reject them. Rejecting the messengers brings a warning of a worse judgment than that of Sodom.

Chapter 11

The prayer asks that God's Kingdom come. Jesus says that if he casts out demons by God's finger, then God's Kingdom has come to his hearers, and he contrasts it with Satan's kingdom, which cannot stand if divided.

Chapter 12

Jesus tells his disciples to seek God's Kingdom, promising that all these necessities will be added to them. He says it is the Father's good pleasure to give the Kingdom to the little flock, who should therefore not be afraid.

Chapter 13

Jesus compares it to a mustard seed that grows into a large tree in which birds live, and to yeast a woman hid in three measures of flour until it was all leavened. He says people will come from east, west, north and south to sit down in it, and that Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and the prophets are in it. Some who are excluded will weep, and the last will be first and the first last.

Chapter 17

Asked by the Pharisees when it would come, Jesus answered that God's Kingdom does not come with observation and that people will not say 'Look, here!' or 'Look, there!' He said that it is within them.

Chapter 18

Jesus says it belongs to little children and that whoever does not receive it like a little child will not enter it. He says it is very hard for the rich to enter, easier for a camel to pass through a needle's eye, and that people who leave family for its sake will receive many times more in this time and eternal life to come.
