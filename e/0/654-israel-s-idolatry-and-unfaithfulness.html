Israel's unfaithfulness and idolatry in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · 629 to 579 BC · the work’s page · read it where Israel's unfaithfulness and idolatry first appears

Abstract written by a model

Israel's unfaithfulness is an evil, bitter forsaking of the LORD. He charges it with trading him for broken cisterns, prostitute-like worship on every high hill and green tree, and rejecting service. Israel, once a noble vine, became degenerate; its iniquity can't be washed away, despite claimed innocence and forgetfulness.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

The LORD describes it as forsaking him for broken cisterns, bowing on every high hill and under every green tree like a prostitute, and breaking the yoke saying 'I will not serve'. He compares Israel to a noble vine turned degenerate, to a swift dromedary and a wild donkey in heat, and says its iniquity cannot be washed away. The sin includes innocent blood in its skirts, claims of innocence, and forgetting the LORD for days without number.

Chapter 3

The people's unfaithfulness is depicted as prostitution and adultery with many lovers, on high mountains and under green trees, and with stones and wood, which polluted the land. Its consequences include withheld showers and no latter rain, and Israel's refusal to be ashamed. The people finally confess that shame has devoured their fathers' labor, flocks, herds, sons, and daughters, and that they have not obeyed the LORD's voice.

abominations and defilement of the sanctuary in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · about 592 to 570 BC · the work’s page · read it where abominations and defilement of the sanctuary first appears

Abstract written by a model

Abominations and defilement of the sanctuary denote Israel's idolatry and its pollution of God's holy name, which is evil and drives God far from his sanctuary. In Chapter 6 idolatry is a lewd, departing heart and worship on high hills, causing coming judgment and self-loathing among survivors. In Chapter 8 Ezekiel sees escalating abominations: the image of jealousy, hidden idols, weeping for Tammuz, and sun worship, alongside violence filling the land. In Chapter 43 prostitution, kings' dead bodies, and thresholds set beside God's create a wall and bring consuming anger; Israel must remove them so God may dwell among them forever.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

Israel's idolatry is described as evil abominations, including a lewd heart that departed from the Lord, eyes that play the prostitute after idols, and offering pleasant aroma on high hills and under green trees and oaks. It is the cause of the coming judgment and the reason the survivors will loathe themselves.

Chapter 8

The Lord shows Ezekiel a progression of abominations: the image of jealousy, idols and creeping things in a hidden chamber, women weeping for Tammuz, and sun worship at the temple door. Each is followed by a promise of greater ones, and the Lord says Judah has also filled the land with violence.

Chapter 43

Prostitution and the dead bodies of kings in their high places are named as the defilement of God's holy name, along with setting their threshold and door post beside his. God says this left a wall between him and the people, and that he consumed them in anger for these abominations. Israel is told to put them away far from him so that he may dwell among them forever.
