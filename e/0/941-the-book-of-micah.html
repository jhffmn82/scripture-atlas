Abstract written by a model

This document is the book of Micah, a prophetic work delivered by Micah of Morasheth during the reigns of Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah, concerning Samaria and Jerusalem. The LORD is the central figure, acting as witness, judge, shepherd, and redeemer, while Micah serves as lamenting messenger. The people, rulers, priests, and prophets of Jacob and Israel are the main objects of the message. It opens with the LORD coming down in judgment. Samaria will become rubble, the wound reaches Judah and Jerusalem's gate, and Micah mourns while naming towns that will suffer captivity. Chapter 2 pronounces woe on those who plot evil and seize fields and houses; they reject the prophets and will be driven out, yet the LORD promises to gather the remnant of Jacob like sheep. Chapter 3 condemns rulers who hate good and devour the people, and prophets who prophesy for money, while Micah claims to be filled with the LORD's Spirit. It ends with the declaration that Zion will be plowed like a field and Jerusalem become rubble. The tone then turns to hope. Chapter 4 pictures a coming time when nations stream to the LORD's mountain, weapons become farming tools, and each sits secure under vine and fig tree. The LORD will gather the lame into a remnant and reign on Zion forever, though Zion must first go to Babylon before being redeemed, and gathered nations will be threshed. Chapter 5 promises a ruler from Bethlehem Ephrathah with ancient origins, who will shepherd in the LORD's strength, be peace, and deliver the people from Assyria. The remnant is pictured as dew and as a lion, and the LORD will remove horses, chariots, witchcraft, and idols and take vengeance on disobedient nations. Chapter 6 is a courtroom scene before the mountains. The LORD recalls the exodus, Moses, Aaron, Miriam, and Balak and Balaam, and states what is good: act justly, love mercy, and walk humbly with God. He then charges the city with dishonest scales and violence, and promises desolation for following Omri and Ahab's ways. Chapter 7 begins with lament that the godly have vanished and families betray one another. The speaker resolves to wait on the LORD, confessing sin and trusting in vindication, with rebuilt walls and people returning from Assyria and Egypt. It closes with a prayer for shepherding and praise of the LORD as the God who pardons iniquity, delights in loving kindness, and keeps his oath to Jacob and Abraham.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

The LORD's word comes to Micah of Morasheth in the days of Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah, concerning Samaria and Jerusalem. The LORD is called as witness against the peoples and comes down from his holy temple, treading the high places while mountains melt and valleys split. He declares that the disobedience of Jacob and the sins of Israel are embodied in Samaria and Jerusalem, and that Samaria will become a rubble heap with its idols and temple gifts destroyed. Micah laments, wailing and going stripped and naked, because the incurable wound has reached Judah and the gate of Jerusalem. He then names towns such as Gath, Beth Ophrah, Lachish, Moresheth Gath, and Mareshah that will suffer shame, evil, and captivity, and calls on the people to shave their heads for the children taken away.

Chapter 2

Chapter 2 opens with a woe against those who plot evil at night and carry it out by day, coveting and seizing fields and houses and oppressing people and their heritage. The LORD announces a planned disaster against them, from which they cannot remove their necks, and says they will be mocked with a lament over their divided land and will have no one to assign land by lot in the LORD's assembly. The people tell the prophets not to prophesy, and the text answers by charging that the LORD's people have risen up as an enemy, stripping travelers, driving women from their houses, and taking blessing from children. They are told to depart because their uncleanness destroys, and a lying prophet of wine and strong drink would suit them. The chapter ends with the LORD promising to assemble Jacob and gather the remnant of Israel like sheep, with their king and the LORD at their head breaking through the gate.

Chapter 3

The speaker addresses the heads of Jacob and rulers of the house of Israel, accusing them of hating good, loving evil, and brutally devouring the LORD's people, and says the LORD will not answer them when they cry out. The LORD then condemns the prophets who lead his people astray, proclaiming peace for those who feed them and war against those who do not, and declares that night and darkness will fall on them and that they will have no answer from God. The speaker says he is full of power by the LORD's Spirit to declare Jacob's disobedience and Israel's sin. He accuses the rulers of abhorring justice and building Zion and Jerusalem with blood, while leaders, priests, and prophets act for bribes, price, and money yet claim the LORD is among them. The chapter ends with the LORD's pronouncement that Zion will be plowed like a field, Jerusalem will become heaps of rubble, and the temple mountain will be like the high places of a forest.

Chapter 4

Chapter 4 describes a future time when the mountain of the LORD's temple is exalted and many nations stream to it to learn the LORD's ways, as the law goes out of Zion and the LORD's word from Jerusalem. The LORD judges between peoples, weapons become farming tools, and each person sits secure under vine and fig tree, while his people vow to walk in the LORD's name forever. The LORD promises to gather the lame and driven away of Zion into a remnant and a strong nation, and to reign over them on Mount Zion forever. The daughter of Zion is told the former dominion will return, yet she must first endure labor pains, leave the city, and go to Babylon, where the LORD will redeem her from her enemies. Many nations gather against Zion to gloat, not knowing the LORD has gathered them like sheaves to the threshing floor, and Zion is commanded to arise and thresh them with iron horn and bronze hoofs.

Chapter 5

Chapter 5 describes a besieged Israel whose judge is struck on the cheek, then promises a ruler from Bethlehem Ephrathah whose origins are ancient and who will shepherd in the strength of the LORD and be great to the ends of the earth. He will be peace and deliver the people from Assyria, whose land will be ruled with the sword by seven shepherds and eight leaders of men. The remnant of Jacob is pictured both as dew and showers among many peoples and as a lion among the animals of the forest that tramples and tears with no one to deliver. The LORD declares that he will cut off Israel's horses, chariots, cities and strongholds, and destroy witchcraft, soothsayers, engraved images, pillars and Asherah poles. He will execute vengeance in anger and wrath on the nations that did not listen.

Chapter 6

The LORD summons Israel to plead a case before the mountains and hills, which are called to hear his indictment of his people. He asks what he has done to burden them and recalls bringing them out of Egypt and sending Moses, Aaron, and Miriam, as well as his dealings with Balak king of Moab and Balaam son of Beor. A speaker asks how to approach the exalted God, wondering about burnt offerings, calves, thousands of rams, rivers of oil, or even a firstborn, and is told that the LORD has shown what is good: to act justly, love mercy, and walk humbly with God. The LORD's voice then calls to the city, charging it with treasures of wickedness, a short ephah, dishonest scales, deceitful weights, and violent, lying rich men. He announces that he has struck and desolated them, that they will eat without being satisfied, sow without reaping, and be made a ruin and a hissing for following the statutes of Omri and the works of Ahab's house.

Chapter 7

The speaker laments that the godly have vanished from the land and that corruption, bribery, violence, and betrayal reach even into families and close friendships. Despite this, the speaker resolves to look to the LORD, confessing sin and bearing the LORD's indignation until the LORD pleads his case, brings him into the light, and shames the enemy who asked 'Where is the LORD your God?' The text then promises a day of rebuilt walls, extended boundaries, and people coming from Assyria, Egypt, and beyond, though the land will also be desolate because of its inhabitants' deeds. It closes with a prayer that the LORD shepherd his people as in the days of coming out of Egypt, with the nations ashamed and trembling, and praises the LORD as a God who pardons iniquity, delights in loving kindness, and keeps his oath to Jacob and Abraham.
