Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Assyria conquers and deports Israel, threatens Jerusalem, loses its army to the LORD’s angel, and sees its king assassinated. In Judith, the Assyrians are the enemy force led by Holofernes that threatens Bethulia and escorts Judith to his tent. In The Book of Isaiah, Assyria is the LORD’s instrument against nations before becoming the object of his judgment and later a place of restoration. In The Book of Jeremiah, Assyria is the place Israel turns to for the River’s waters instead of trusting the LORD. In The Book of Ezekiel, the Assyrians are the neighboring lovers and forces with whose idols Oholah and Oholibah defile themselves. In The Book of Hosea, Assyria is the foreign power Ephraim turns to, becoming Israel’s king before the people return from it. In The Book of Micah, Assyria invades the land, but seven shepherds and eight leaders will rule it with the sword. In The Book of Zephaniah, Assyria is the northern land against which the LORD stretches out his hand to destroy it.

and 4 more works

In The Book of Zechariah, Assyria is the land from which the LORD gathers his people and whose pride he brings down. In The Second Book of Nephi, Assyria brings trouble and conquest, burdens Zion, and yields a remnant recovered by a highway. In The Second Book of Nephi, Assyrian is the rod of the Lord’s anger that spoils nations, boasts of conquest, and is brought underfoot. In The Second Book of Nephi, the rod of God’s anger represents the Assyrian and the LORD’s indignation in his hand.

Assyria in The Second Book of Kings

Abstract written by a model

Assyria's king besieges and captures Samaria, deports Israel to Halah, the Habor, and the cities of the Medes, and replaces them with peoples from Babylon, Cuthah, Avva, Hamath, and Sepharvaim. When the settlers suffer lions, he orders one of the exiled priests sent back to teach them the law of the god of the land. Assyria's king and servants are said to have blasphemed the LORD, and its kings are described as having laid waste nations and burned their gods. Its king hears of Tirhakah king of Ethiopia coming out to fight and sends a threatening letter to Hezekiah. Its camp is struck by the LORD's angel with 185,000 dead, and its king returns home and is later assassinated, with Esar Haddon his son reigning in his place.

Assyrians in Judith

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Assyrians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Assyrians are the enemy force threatening Bethulia, led by the chief captain Holofernes. Ozias has promised to surrender the town to them after five days, and Judith warns that defeat would mean captivity, plunder of the sanctuary and dishonor. They are portrayed as mighty, multiplied in power, proud of horse, rider and footmen, and trusting in shield, spear, bow and sling, yet ignorant that the Lord breaks battles. They intend to defile the tabernacle and destroy the altar's horn. Later, an Assyrian watch meets Judith, marvels at her beauty, and escorts her to Holofernes' tent amid an excited camp.

Assyria in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Assyria first appears

Abstract written by a model

Assyria is a kingdom, represented chiefly by its proud king, that the records present as the LORD's instrument and later the object of his judgment. It is first foretold as the power the LORD will bring against Ahaz, his people and his father's house, likened to a hired razor and to the mighty flood of the River. It carries off the riches of Damascus and the plunder of Samaria, takes Ashdod through Sargon's commander Tartan, and leads away captives of Egypt and Ethiopia. Called the rod of the Lord's anger, it is sent against a profane nation but does not intend to serve God's purpose. It aims to destroy many nations, boasts of its conquests, compares Samaria to Damascus, advances through towns to Nob and shakes its hand at Jerusalem. It oppresses God's people without cause and lays a yoke and burden on them. The turn comes as the LORD promises to punish its king, burn its forest down to a countable remnant, and break the yoke from Zion's people. He swears to break the Assyrian in his land and tread him on his mountains. A deep burning place, long prepared for the king, is kindled by the LORD's breath. The Assyrian is struck with the rod, falls by a sword not of man, flees, sees his young men put to forced labor and his princes fear the banner. Yet Assyria also appears in restoration: a highway for the remnant, a highway between Egypt and Assyria where they worship together, and the LORD's name for it, 'the work of my hands.'

Chapter 7

The king of Assyria is foretold as the one the LORD will bring on Ahaz, his people, and his father's house, in days unlike any since Ephraim departed from Judah. The LORD will whistle for the bee in Assyria's land, and the king of Assyria is likened to a hired razor that shaves the head, the hair of the feet, and the beard.

Chapter 8

The king of Assyria is foretold to carry away the riches of Damascus and the plunder of Samaria before the child can speak. He is identified with the mighty flood waters of the River, coming with all his glory as the LORD's instrument against this people.

Chapter 10

Assyria is called the rod of the Lord's anger and is sent to take plunder and trample a profane nation, though it does not intend to serve God's purpose and aims only to destroy many nations. It boasts of conquests and moves through Aiath, Migron, Michmash, Geba, and other towns to halt at Nob and shake its hand at Jerusalem. The Lord promises to punish its king, burn its forest down to a countable remnant, direct anger at its destruction, and break its burden and yoke from Zion's people.

Chapter 11

Assyria is named as one of the places from which the Lord will recover the remnant of his people. A highway will be provided for the remnant that is left from Assyria.

Chapter 14

The LORD swears to break the Assyrian in his land and tread him underfoot on his mountains. Then the Assyrian's yoke and burden will leave the shoulders of the people.

Chapter 19

Assyria appears in the promise that a highway will run from Egypt to Assyria, with the Assyrian entering Egypt and the Egyptian entering Assyria. The Egyptians will worship with the Assyrians. The LORD calls Assyria 'the work of my hands.'

Chapter 20

Assyria's king Sargon sent Tartan, who fought against Ashdod and took it. The LORD says the king of Assyria will lead away the captives of Egypt and the exiles of Ethiopia, young and old, naked and barefoot, to Egypt's shame.

Chapter 30

The Assyrian will be dismayed at the LORD's voice and struck with his rod, with each stroke accompanied by tambourines and harps. A deep, large burning place, long prepared for the king, is kindled by the LORD's breath like a stream of sulfur.

Chapter 31

The Assyrian will fall by a sword not of man or mankind, which will devour him. He will flee, his young men will become subject to forced labor, and his rock will pass away in terror while his princes fear the banner.

Assyrian in The Second Book of Nephi

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Assyrian first appears

Abstract written by a model

Assyrian is the rod of the Lord's anger, sent with indignation to spoil, prey upon, and tread people down. He intends to destroy many nations, boasts that his princes are kings, and compares conquered places. In the Lord's land he is threatened with leanness and burning, then brought underfoot on the mountains; his yoke and burden depart.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 20

The Assyrian is the rod of the Lord's anger and is sent to take spoil, prey, and tread people down. He intends to destroy and cut off many nations, boasts of his conquests, and is later threatened with leanness, burning, and the removal of his burden and yoke.

Chapter 24

The Lord will bring the Assyrian into his land and tread him underfoot upon the mountains. Then the yoke and burden of the Assyrian will depart from the people.
