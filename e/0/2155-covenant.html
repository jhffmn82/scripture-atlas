Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Chronicles, the covenant with David is the LORD's promise fulfilled in Solomon's throne and house, with the further promise of an enduring Davidic throne conditional on obedience. In Psalms, the LORD's covenant with David is a sworn promise to establish David's offspring and throne forever, later reaffirmed conditionally for subsequent generations.

covenant (the LORD's covenant with David) in Psalms

author unknown · undated · the work’s page · read it where covenant (the LORD's covenant with David) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The LORD's covenant with David is a sworn promise, firm forever, to establish David's offspring and throne; the LORD will not break or alter it. Chapter 89 states it, then the psalmist laments an apparent renunciation. Chapter 132 reaffirms the oath, extending it to later generations on condition that David's children keep the covenant and testimony.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 89

The covenant is stated as the LORD's sworn promise to establish David's offspring and throne forever, which he says he will not break nor alter what his lips have uttered. The psalmist later complains that the LORD has renounced the covenant of his servant, in apparent contradiction of this promise.

Chapter 132

The LORD has sworn in truth to David and will not turn from it, promising to set David's offspring on his throne. The promise extends to later generations on the condition that David's children keep the covenant and the testimony the LORD will teach them.
