Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, Mithra is the lofty, imperishable, holy Yazad who rules the provinces and is associated with Raman Hvâstra. In Yasna, Mithra, province-lord of the provinces, is the Yazad of the spoken name with wide pastures, a thousand ears, and myriad eyes who receives offerings and praise. In Visparad, Mithra is the holy lord of ritual order with wide pastures who is praised alongside sacred entities. In Afrinagan, Gahs and Miscellaneous Fragments, Mithra of the wide pastures is the Yazad of the spoken name, thousand ears, and myriad eyes who receives sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, Mithra is the powerful, all-knowing lord of wide pastures and countries who protects truth-speakers, grants boons, destroys evil, and travels in a heavenly chariot. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, Mithra is the god and lord of wide pastures and all countries who, with Rashnu and Verethraghna, seizes kingly Glory after it leaves Yima. In Nyayish, Mithra is the watchful, truth-speaking lord of wide pastures and all countries who is invoked with Ahura Mazda and receives sacrifices and libations.

Mithra in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Mithra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mithra is a Yazad of the spoken name, repeatedly portrayed as lofty, everlasting or imperishable, holy, wide-pastured, thousand-eared, and myriad-eyed. He governs or is lord of all the provinces and is associated with Raman Hvâstra. Across Yasna I, Mithra is announced and celebrated as province-ruler. Yasna II approaches him with praise in the Zaothra and Baresman. Yasna III names him alongside Ahura as one of the lofty and imperishable two and as province-lord. Yasna VI worships him both in his own attributes and with Ahura as one of the lofty, imperishable, holy two. Yasna VII reiterates these qualities, his connection with Ahura, and his lordship of the provinces.

Yasna / Yasna I.

Mithra is described as lofty, everlasting, holy, wide-pastured, thousand-eared, and myriad-eyed. He is announced and celebrated in the Yasna as the Yazad of the spoken name and as the province-ruler.

Yasna II.

Mithra is approached with praise in the Zaothra and Baresman. He is described as having wide pastures, a thousand ears, and myriad eyes, as the Yazad of the spoken name, and as the governor of all the provinces.

Yasna III.

Mithra is approached together with Ahura as one of the lofty and imperishable two. He is also named as Mithra of the wide pastures, with a thousand ears and myriad eyes, and as province-lord of the provinces.

Yasna VI.

Mithra is worshipped as being of the wide pastures, with a thousand ears and myriad eyes, and as the Yazad of the spoken name. He is also worshipped with Ahura as one of the lofty, imperishable, and holy two, and is called the province-lord of all the provinces.

Yasna VII.

Mithra is described as having wide pastures, a thousand ears, and myriad eyes, and as the Yazad of the spoken name. He is also named with Ahura as lofty and imperishable and as the lord of the provinces.

Mithra in Yashts (Yasts) I-XII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Mithra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mithra is a holy, heavenly, powerful, all-knowing god and the lord of wide pastures, also lord of all countries. Sleepless and ever awake, he has a thousand ears, many senses, ten thousand eyes, and ten thousand powerful, undeceivable spies. Ahura Mazda made his dwelling on Hara Berezaiti, from which Mithra surveys the material world; after sunset he surveys everything between earth and heavens. He is keeper and maintainer of all creatures and maintains Mazda’s creation without sleeping. Mithra protects those who do not lie, but withholds strength and glory from those who lie unto him. Kings, poor men deprived of their rights, and cows driven astray invoke him. At his will he gives boons and pasture-fields, and as a boon grants riches, strength, and victory. He destroys malice among Daêvas, men, Yâtus, Pairikas, and oppressors; yet in his might he can impart brightness and glory as well as sickness, death, poverty, and sterility to those who anger or offend him. He is accompanied or assisted by Sraosha, Nairyô-sangha, Rashnu, Rashnu-Razista, Kista, Âtar, and the Fravashis of the faithful. He has friendship with Verethraghna. Protected and associated with the Law of Mazda, Mithra travels between Karshvares in high-wheeled, heavenly, golden, ever-swift chariots drawn by four white, shining, beneficent, knowledgeable steeds. Armed with a hundred-knotted, hundred-edged club and strong long arms, he is feared by Angra Mainyu, Aêshma, Bûshyãsta, Daêvas, and Varenya fiends. Worshipped with sacrifice, proper words, and consecrated libations, he increases the creation of Spenta Mainyu and shines like the moon when making his body shine.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

VI. Khôrshêd Yast.

Mithra is called the lord of wide pastures and is described as having a thousand ears and ten thousand eyes. His club is offered sacrifice, and the friendship between the moon and the sun is called the best of all friendships.

XII.

Mithra is the sleepless and ever-awake lord of wide pastures, powerful, all-knowing, undeceivable, and endowed with ten thousand spies. Ahura Mazda built him a dwelling on Hara Berezaiti, and Mithra watches over the material world, protects those who lie not, helps those who invoke him, grants many boons, and destroys enemies and malice.

XXIV.

Mithra watches over the moving world, sees and hears all things, and travels across the earth and heavens. He drives a golden chariot with divine companions and many weapons, smites liars and enemies, drives away fiends, and grants brightness, glory, health, riches, sovereignty, and offspring to those who satisfy him. He may also bring sickness, death, poverty, sterility, and the loss of offspring to those who anger or offend him.

Mithra in Nyayish

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Mithra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mithra is a lord of wide pastures and all countries, a truth-speaking chief in assemblies, powerful, well-shapen, strong, watchful, sleepless, and ever awake, with a thousand ears, ten thousand eyes, and full knowledge. Ahura Mazda made him most glorious among heavenly gods. He is hailed and invoked with Ahura Mazda for help and ease, while sacrifices and libations are offered to him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Nyayish / I. Khôrshêd Nyâyis.

Mithra is hailed as the lord of wide pastures and is described as truth-speaking, a chief in assemblies, well-shaped, strong, sleepless, and ever awake. He is invoked together with Ahura Mazda for help, and sacrifices are offered to him.

II. Mihir Nyâyis.

Mithra is described as the lord of wide pastures and all countries, truth-speaking, powerful, watchful, sleepless, and ever awake. Sacrifices and libations are offered to him, and he is invoked with Ahura as a great and imperishable holy god for help and ease.
