Abstract written by a model

In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, grace is the standing and realm believers access by faith, abounding through Jesus Christ and reigning through righteousness to eternal life while freeing believers from sin’s dominion. In Paul’s Letter to the Ephesians, grace is God’s gift by which believers are saved through faith, and it is given to Paul to preach Christ’s unsearchable riches to the Gentiles. In Titus, the grace of God appears bringing salvation, instructing believers to live godly lives while awaiting the blessed hope.

grace in Paul’s Letter to the Romans

author unknown · about AD 57 · the work’s page · read it where grace first appears

Abstract written by a model

Grace is the standing and realm in which believers live, accessed by faith. In Chapter 5 it abounds through Jesus Christ far more than sin and trespass, reigning through righteousness to eternal life. Chapter 6 says believers live under grace, not law, rejecting sinning to magnify grace; sin will not dominate them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Grace is the standing in which believers have access by faith, and it abounds to the many through the one man, Jesus Christ, much more than the trespass did. Where sin abounded, grace abounded more exceedingly, so that grace might reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

Chapter 6

Grace is the realm under which believers live rather than under law. The text rejects the idea of continuing in sin so that grace may abound, and it rejects sinning because of being under grace. Because of grace, sin will not have dominion over them.

grace in Paul’s Letter to the Ephesians

author unknown · about AD 62 · the work’s page · read it where grace first appears

Abstract written by a model

Grace is God's gift by which believers are saved through faith, not works, and is linked to the exceeding riches of his kindness in Christ Jesus. In chapter 2 it saves believers; in chapter 3 it is given to Paul, the least of saints, as an administration to the Gentiles to preach Christ's unsearchable riches.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Grace is the means by which believers have been saved, stated twice in the chapter. It is tied to God's kindness toward us in Christ Jesus, whose exceeding riches he will show in the ages to come.

Chapter 3

The grace of God was given to Paul toward the Gentiles as an administration, and by it he was made a servant of the Good News according to the working of God's power. It was given to the very least of all saints so that he might preach the unsearchable riches of Christ.
