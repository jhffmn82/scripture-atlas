assembly of God at Corinth in Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians

author unknown · AD 53 to 54 · the work’s page · read it where assembly of God at Corinth first appears

Abstract written by a model

The assembly of God at Corinth is a community of believers addressed in Paul's First Letter to the Corinthians. They are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called saints, called by God into fellowship with Jesus Christ, and enriched in all speech and knowledge, lacking no gift. Most of the record concerns their problems and the correction they receive. Chloe's household reported divisions and contentions, with members claiming allegiance to Paul, Apollos, Cephas, or Christ. Paul reminds them that not many were wise, mighty, or noble by the flesh, and he rests their faith on God's power rather than human wisdom. The record unfolds as a series of rebukes and instructions. In the early chapters they are called fleshly babies in Christ, yet also God's fellow workers, God's building and temple, indwelt by the Spirit, and Christ's. Some are puffed up and boasting, and Paul, who calls them his beloved children, asks whether he should come with a rod or in gentleness. They are rebuked for arrogance over sin among them and told to purge the wicked man, and for suing one another. They are told to remain in the condition in which they were called, since they were bought with a price and should not become bondservants of men, and that the time is short, so they should live free from worldly cares. Formerly heathen led away to mute idols, they are commanded to flee idolatry and avoid fellowship with demons, and are given guidance on eating food. Their gatherings are criticized because of divisions, and many are weak, sickly, or have died. They are one body because they share one loaf, the body of Christ with members individually. They are zealous for spiritual gifts, are urged to seek the best ones, and are given instructions for orderly worship and for building up the assembly. The record ends on the Good News they received: they stand in it and are saved if they hold firmly to it. Some deny the resurrection, which would leave them in their sins if Christ had not been raised. Paul closes by calling them beloved brothers to be steadfast, immovable, and abounding in the Lord's work.

Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians / Chapter 1

The assembly is addressed as those sanctified in Christ Jesus and called saints, enriched in all speech and knowledge and lacking in no gift. It is reported to have contentions, with members saying they follow Paul, Apollos, Cephas, or Christ, and it is reminded that not many of its members were wise, mighty, or noble according to the flesh.

Chapter 2

The brothers are those to whom the speaker came, determined to know only Jesus Christ crucified. The speaker wanted their faith to rest on the power of God rather than the wisdom of men. They are included among those who received the Spirit from God and can know what God has freely given.

Chapter 3

The Corinthians are rebuked as fleshly babies in Christ, not yet ready for solid food, because of their jealousy, strife, and factions. They are told they are God's fellow workers, farming, building, and temple, in whom God's Spirit lives. They are urged not to boast in men, since all things are theirs, and they are Christ's.

Chapter 4

The Corinthian believers are told not to judge before the time and are rebuked for being puffed up, boasting of what they received, and acting as if already rich and reigning. They are contrasted with the suffering apostles, addressed as Paul's beloved children, urged to imitate him, and asked whether they want him to come with a rod or in gentleness.

Chapter 5

The Corinthian believers are rebuked for being arrogant and boasting rather than mourning over the sin among them. They are told to gather, deliver the man to Satan, purge the old yeast, keep the feast with sincerity and truth, not associate with immoral brothers, and put away the wicked man from among themselves.

Chapter 6

Brothers are the fellow believers who are suing one another and, according to the writer, wronging and defrauding each other. The text suggests a wise man among them should be able to decide between them.

Chapter 7

The Corinthian believers are instructed to remain in the condition in which they were called, and not to become bondservants of men since they were bought with a price. They are told the time is short, and are urged to live free from cares and to attend to the Lord without distraction.

Chapter 10

The brothers are told not to be ignorant of the fathers' history and to take it as an example. They are warned to be careful lest they fall, to flee idolatry, to avoid fellowship with demons, and to seek their neighbor's good. They are also instructed on how to eat food in the butcher shop and at an unbeliever's meal.

Chapter 11

The Corinthian believers are praised for remembering Paul and holding the traditions, but not praised for how they gather, since divisions exist among them and their meetings are for the worse. They are told to examine themselves, to wait for one another when they eat, and to eat at home if hungry, with the warning that many among them are weak, sickly, or have died.

Chapter 12

The brothers are told the writer does not want them ignorant about spiritual things, and are reminded that as heathen they were led away to mute idols. They are called the body of Christ and members individually. They are urged to earnestly desire the best gifts and promised a most excellent way.

Chapter 14

The brothers are told to seek to abound in building up the assembly, to be mature in thought and babies in malice, and to desire earnestly to prophesy without forbidding other languages. They are given instructions for orderly gatherings, and are asked whether the word of God went out from them or came to them alone.

Chapter 15

The addressed brothers received the Good News, stand in it, and are saved if they hold firmly to it. Some among them say there is no resurrection of the dead, and the speaker rebukes this, warns them not to be deceived, and says some have no knowledge of God, to their shame. He closes by calling them beloved brothers to be steadfast, immovable, and abounding in the Lord's work.

assembly of God in Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians

author unknown · AD 53 to 54 · the work’s page · read it where assembly of God first appears

Abstract written by a model

The assembly of God is the Corinthian gathering, which belongs to God. In Chapter 11 it is marked by divisions and factions, and Paul faults the members for despising it, shaming the poor, and failing to wait for one another. In Chapter 14 it is the place where gifts build up the body: orderly prophecy convicts visitors, who then worship God.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

The Corinthian gathering is marked by divisions and factions and is said to be for the worse rather than the better. Paul faults them for despising God's assembly and shaming those who have little, and instructs them to wait for one another so that their coming together is not for judgment.

Chapter 14

The assembly is what gifts are meant to build up, and each person who comes together brings a psalm, teaching, revelation, language, or interpretation. Unlearned or unbelieving visitors would call it crazy if all spoke in languages, but would be reproved, have their heart's secrets revealed, and worship God if all prophesied.

Corinthian believers (the readers) in Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians

author unknown · AD 53 to 54 · the work’s page · read it where Corinthian believers (the readers) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Corinthian believers are the recipients of Paul's letter, a community addressed with rebuke, reminder, and instruction. Early on they are shamed for lawsuits before unbelievers and for wronging and defrauding brothers, yet reminded they were washed, sanctified and justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, are members of Christ, and were bought with a price, so they should flee sexual immorality and glorify God in their bodies. Next they are called the seal of Paul's apostleship, with an argument about material support and an exhortation to run the race to win. Finally they receive practical directions: weekly saving for the collection, receiving Timothy, honoring laborers like the house of Stephanas, standing firm in love, and exchanging greetings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

The readers are rebuked and shamed for their lawsuits before unbelievers and for wronging and defrauding their brothers. They are warned not to be deceived about the unrighteous, reminded that some of them were once such people but were washed, sanctified and justified, and urged to flee sexual immorality and glorify God in their bodies.

Chapter 9

The Corinthians are called the seal of Paul's apostleship in the Lord and his work in the Lord. Paul argues that having sowed spiritual things among them, it is no great matter to reap material support from them, and he points out that others partake of this right over them. He urges them to run the race so as to win.

Chapter 16

The Corinthians are told to save weekly for the collection, approve men to carry it to Jerusalem, receive Timothy without fear, and send him on in peace. They are urged to be in subjection to the house of Stephanas and others who labor, to acknowledge such people, and to stand firm, be courageous, and do everything in love. They receive greetings from the assemblies of Asia, Aquila and Priscilla with the assembly in their house, and all the brothers, and are told to greet one another with a holy kiss.

Corinthians in 2 Corinthians

Paul · AD 55 to 56 · the work’s page · read it where Corinthians first appears

Abstract written by a model

Corinthians is a community of believers addressed by Paul and his co-workers, portrayed as both recipients of instruction and a living testimony. Early on, they are described as a letter of Christ, written in the ministers' hearts, known and read by all, inscribed by the Spirit of the living God on hearts of flesh rather than with ink on stone, and serving as the ministers' commendation. They are told to forgive and comfort an offender already sufficiently punished, with their obedience proving their commitment. The ministers serve for their sake, with life working in the believers and thanksgiving abounding through grace. Finally, they are urged not to receive God's grace in vain, told they are restricted by their own affections rather than the apostles, asked to open their hearts, warned against unequal yoking with unbelievers, and identified as a temple of the living God, called to separate.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

The recipients are told they were caused sorrow in part by the offender, and that the punishment inflicted by the many on him is sufficient. They are urged to forgive and comfort him and to confirm their love toward him, and their obedience in all things was the proof Paul sought by writing.

Chapter 3

The Corinthian believers are described as the writers' letter, written in their hearts, known and read by all, and revealed as a letter of Christ. It was written with the Spirit of the living God on hearts of flesh, not with ink on stone tablets. They serve as the writers' commendation.

Chapter 4

The believers are those for whose sake the ministers serve and for whose sake all things happen. Life works in them while death works in the ministers. The ministers expect God to present them together with the believers, and the grace multiplied through many leads to abounding thanksgiving.

Chapter 6

The Corinthians are urged not to receive God's grace in vain and are told that they are restricted not by the apostles but by their own affections. They are asked to open their hearts in return, are warned not to be unequally yoked with unbelievers, and are identified as a temple of the living God. They are called to come out from among unbelievers and be separate, with the promise that God will receive them.

assembly of God at Corinth in 2 Corinthians

Paul · AD 55 to 56 · the work’s page · read it where assembly of God at Corinth first appears

Abstract written by a model

The assembly of God at Corinth is a community of believers located in Corinth, addressed by Paul and Timothy together with all the saints in Achaia. The records portray a congregation that stands firm in faith, shares in Paul's afflictions, sufferings and comfort, and helps him through supplication. Paul treats them as his boast, as he is theirs. Early on, they are shown as strong: they abound in faith, utterance, knowledge, earnestness and love, and they were first to start the grace of giving, the collection for the saints, a year earlier. They are urged to complete it according to what they have and to show proof of their love to the sent brothers before the assemblies. Their relationship with Paul is marked by repentance and reconciliation. Grieved by his letter, they showed earnest care, zeal and vindication, refreshed Titus's spirit, and received him with fear and trembling, leaving Paul confident about them. Concerns also emerge. Paul hopes their faith grows and their obedience is made full, so he need not show boldness when present. He reminds them they were promised to Christ as a pure virgin, fears their minds being deceived, and rebukes them for tolerating preachers of another Jesus, spirit or good news. He notes that the signs of an apostle were worked among them, that he seeks them and not their possessions, and that he fears strife, jealousy and factions. The account ends with warnings that Paul will not spare those who sinned, a call to test themselves whether they are in the faith, and exhortations to rejoice, be perfected, be of the same mind and live in peace, with greetings from all the saints.

Paul’s Second Letter to the Corinthians / Chapter 1

The assembly at Corinth, along with all the saints in Achaia, is addressed by Paul and Timothy. They are told that Paul's afflictions and comforts serve their comfort and salvation, that they share in the sufferings and the comfort, and that their prayers help Paul. They are said to stand firm in faith, and Paul claims they are his boasting as he is theirs.

Chapter 7

The recipients are called beloved and urged to cleanse themselves and open their hearts to the author. They were grieved by his letter to repentance, showing earnest care, defense, indignation, fear, longing, zeal, and vindication, and proving themselves pure in the matter. They refreshed Titus's spirit and received him with fear and trembling, and the author is confident concerning them in everything.

Chapter 8

The addressees are said to abound in faith, utterance, knowledge, earnestness and love, and are urged to abound in this grace as well. They were first to start a year ago, and are told to complete the doing as they had been ready to be willing, according to what they have. They are asked to show proof of their love to the sent brothers before the assemblies.

Chapter 10

The Corinthians are urged by Paul to look beyond how things appear and are told he hopes not to need to show boldness when he is present. Their obedience is to be made full, and Paul hopes that as their faith grows he will be enlarged among them in his sphere so as to preach beyond them. They are within the boundaries God appointed to Paul.

Chapter 11

The Corinthians are said to have been promised in marriage to one husband, Christ, to be presented as a pure virgin. Paul fears their minds will be deceived and rebukes them for putting up with anyone who preaches another Jesus, a different spirit or a different good news. He also says they bear with those who bring them into bondage, devour them, take them captive, exalt themselves or strike them on the face.

Chapter 12

The Corinthians are said to have compelled Paul to boast, and he reminds them that the signs of an apostle were worked among them. He tells them he seeks them and not their possessions, that all things are for their edifying, and that he fears finding them in strife, jealousy, factions and other disorders.

Chapter 13

The Corinthians are warned, especially those who have sinned, that Paul will not spare them when he comes. They are seeking proof that Christ speaks in Paul, and are told to test themselves to see whether they are in the faith. They are exhorted to rejoice, be perfected, be comforted, be of the same mind, live in peace, and greet one another with a holy kiss, and they receive greetings from all the saints.
