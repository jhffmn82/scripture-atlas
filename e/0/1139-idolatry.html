Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Solomon, idolatry is depicted as praying to dead, powerless objects for aid and success. In The Wisdom of Solomon, idolatry is presented as the worship of images hateful to God and as the beginning of fornication and every evil. In The Book of Isaiah, idolatry is condemned as an abomination and an unprofitable worship of human-made images. In Baruch, idolatry is characterized as false and futile because idols are lifeless human creations unable to act, judge, help, or defend. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, idolatry is described as exchanging God’s glory and truth for images and worship of the creature. In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, idolatry is cited as a sin to flee, with sacrifices to idols described as sacrifices to demons.

idolatry in The Wisdom of Solomon

author unknown · about 99 BC · the work’s page · read it where idolatry first appears

Abstract written by a model

Idolatry is presented as the worship of images, hateful to God along with the ungodly. It began with a grieving father honoring his dead child's image, then hardened into custom and law by princes' commands, and spread through flattery of distant kings and an artisan's beautified likeness. Calamity and tyranny led people to name stones and stocks divine. It is called the beginning of fornication and of every evil.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

Idolatry is traced to a grieving father's image of his dead child, then to a custom turned into law by the commands of princes, and to images of distant kings made to flatter them. An ambitious artisan's beautified likeness drew the crowd to devotion, and people in calamity or under tyranny gave stones and stocks the Name that shouldn't be shared. It is called the beginning of fornication, the corruption of life, and the beginning, cause and end of every evil.

idolatry in The Book of Isaiah

Abstract written by a model

Idolatry is an abomination and unprofitable; in Chapter 44 a man burns part of a tree for warmth and cooking, makes the rest a god, and begs deliverance, contrasted with the LORD, sole God and Rock.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 44

Idolatry is portrayed as futile and absurd: a man burns part of a tree for warmth and cooking and makes the rest into a god, bowing to it and asking it to deliver him. The text calls this an abomination of a deceived heart and contrasts it with the LORD, who alone is God and Rock.
