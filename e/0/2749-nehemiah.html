Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Nehemiah, Nehemiah serves as a Jewish cupbearer who becomes governor of Judah, leads Jerusalem’s wall reconstruction, reforms the community, and seeks God’s remembrance. In The Second Book of the Maccabees, Nehemiah builds the temple and altar, founds a library, and oversees the recovery and naming of the sacred fire. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Nehemiah rebuilds Jerusalem’s fallen walls, restores its gates and bars, and repairs the people’s houses.

Nehemiah in The Book of Nehemiah

author unknown · about 399 to 299 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nehemiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nehemiah, son of Hacaliah and brother of Hanani, is a Jewish cupbearer to King Artaxerxes in Susa who becomes governor of Judah. The records open with him hearing of Jerusalem's affliction and its broken wall; he weeps, fasts, prays, and confesses Israel's sin. Having obtained the king's permission, letters and timber, he travels to Judah, inspects the walls secretly by night, and rallies the people to rebuild, answering mockers by trusting the God of heaven. He faces opposition from Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite, who were grieved that someone sought Israel's welfare. He sets watches, arms the builders by family groups, keeps a trumpeter beside him, and with his brothers, servants and guards does not undress. He confronts the nobles and rulers over usury and has them swear to restore what was taken. Throughout his twelve years as governor, from the twentieth to the thirty-second year of Artaxerxes, he refuses the governor's bread out of fear of God, lends to the people, buys no land, and hosts 150 men at his table. He refuses four invitations to meet at Ono, denies Sanballat's accusation, rejects Shemaiah's advice to hide in the temple, and the wall is finished in fifty-two days. He then appoints Hanani and Hananiah over Jerusalem, sets rules for the gates, and finds the genealogy of the first returnees. Later he is named governor alongside Ezra and the Levites in comforting the people, is first among those sealing the covenant, and leads the thanksgiving companies on the wall. After returning from leave from the king, he finds Tobiah housed in the temple courts and throws out his belongings, has the rooms cleansed, restores the Levites' portions, appoints treasurers, shuts the gates against Sabbath trade, and confronts, curses and strikes men who married foreign women. He repeatedly asks God to remember him for good, a note running from the prayerful opening to the close.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

10 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Nehemiah / Chapter 1

Nehemiah, son of Hacaliah, is in Susa the palace when he asks Hanani and the men from Judah about the Jews and Jerusalem. On hearing of their affliction and the broken wall, he weeps, mourns, fasts, and prays. He confesses sin on behalf of Israel and his own father's house, asks to be granted mercy in the sight of 'this man', and is identified as cupbearer to the king.

Chapter 2

As the king's wine-bearer, the narrator (the text gives no name in this chapter) is asked about his sad face, tells of Jerusalem's ruin, prays, and obtains permission, letters and timber to go to Judah and build. In Jerusalem he secretly inspects the walls by night, then rallies the people to rebuild and answers the mockers by trusting the God of heaven.

Chapter 4

The narrator prays against the mockers, sets a watch day and night, and posts guards by family groups with swords, spears, and bows. He exhorts the nobles, rulers, and people not to fear, keeps the trumpeter by his side, orders the people to rally to the trumpet, and has everyone lodge in Jerusalem. He and his brothers, servants, and guards do not take off their clothes.

Chapter 5

Nehemiah, angered by the people's cry, confronted the nobles and rulers over usury, held a great assembly, and had them promise and swear to restore what they had taken. He did not take the governor's bread during his twelve years as governor from the twentieth to the thirty-second year of Artaxerxes, because of the fear of God. He worked on the wall, bought no land, hosted one hundred fifty men at his table, and asked God to remember him for the good he had done.

Chapter 6

Nehemiah refuses four invitations to meet at Ono, saying he is doing a great work and cannot come down. He denies the accusation in Sanballat's open letter and rejects Shemaiah's advice to hide in the temple, discerning that God had not sent him. He prays that God remember Tobiah, Sanballat, Noadiah and the other prophets, and the wall is completed under his leadership in fifty-two days.

Chapter 7

As narrator, he appoints his brother Hanani and Hananiah to govern Jerusalem and instructs them on opening, closing, and guarding the gates. Moved by God, he gathers the nobles, rulers, and people to be listed by genealogy and finds the book of the genealogy of the first returnees.

Chapter 8

Nehemiah, identified as the governor, joined Ezra and the Levites in telling the people not to mourn or weep because the day was holy to the LORD. His role here is as one of the leaders comforting the people after the reading.

Chapter 10

Nehemiah, identified as the governor and son of Hacaliah, is the first named among those who sealed the covenant. He appears at the head of the list before the priests, Levites, and chiefs.

Chapter 12

Nehemiah is named as governor in whose days the Levites and priests served, alongside Ezra the priest and scribe. Speaking in the first person, he brought the princes of Judah up on the wall, appointed the two great thanksgiving companies, and followed the second company with half of the people. He stood in God's house with half of the rulers after the processions.

Chapter 13

Nehemiah returned to Jerusalem after leave from the king and was grieved to find Tobiah housed in the temple courts, so he threw out Tobiah's belongings and had the rooms cleansed. He restored the Levites' portions, appointed treasurers, shut the gates against Sabbath trade, threatened the merchants, and confronted, cursed, struck and made oaths of men who married foreign women. He closes several times asking God to remember him for good.

Nehemiah in The Second Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 124 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nehemiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nehemiah is a figure who built the temple and altar in Jerusalem and founded a library of books on kings and prophets. Charged by Persia's king, he sent priests' descendants to find hidden fire; a great blaze followed, and he and companions named the liquid Nephthar, 'Cleansing'.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

The Second Book of the Maccabees / Chapter 1

Having received a charge from the king of Persia, he sent descendants of the priests to seek the hidden fire, and when they found thick liquid he had it drawn out and sprinkled on the wood and sacrifices. A great blaze was kindled, he led or joined in the prayer, and he had the rest of the liquid poured on large stones. He and his companions called the liquid Nephthar, meaning Cleansing.
