Also called: Ahithophel the Gilonite

Abstract written by a model

Ahithophel, from Giloh, is a counselor whose advice was esteemed as if inquired of God's inner sanctuary, serving both David and Absalom. He joins Absalom's conspiracy, and David prays that his counsel become foolishness. He advises Absalom to go in to David's concubines, then to let him pursue David with twelve thousand men that night and strike only the king. When his counsel is not followed, he goes home, sets his house in order, hangs himself, and is buried in his father's tomb.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 15

Ahithophel, David's counselor from Giloh, was sent for by Absalom while Absalom was offering sacrifices, and he joined the conspiracy. David was told of this and prayed that the LORD turn his counsel into foolishness.

Chapter 16

Ahithophel is with Absalom in Jerusalem and advises him to go in to David's concubines so all Israel will hear he is abhorred by his father and his supporters will be strengthened. The text says his counsel was regarded as if inquired at the inner sanctuary of God, both with David and with Absalom.

Chapter 17

Ahithophel advised Absalom to let him choose twelve thousand men and pursue David that night, striking only the king while he was weary. When his counsel was not followed, he saddled his donkey, went home, set his house in order, hanged himself, and was buried in his father's tomb.
