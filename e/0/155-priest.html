priest

person · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: The anointed priest, The priest, The priest who offers it for sin, priest (Aaron's line), the priests

Abstract written by a model

In Leviticus, the priest of Aaron's line makes atonement for offenders with the trespass offering, tends the altar, eats the sin offering in a holy place, and wholly burns his own meal offering. In Numbers, the priest receives wave offering portions, offers offerings to atone for a defiled Nazirite, presents the offerings at the vow's completion, and waves the boiled shoulder, cake, and wafer.

priest (Aaron's line) in Leviticus

author unknown · 539 to 331 BC · the work’s page · read it where priest (Aaron's line) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The priest, of Aaron's line, makes atonement for offenders with the trespass offering, resulting in forgiveness. He tends the altar by wearing linen, burning wood each morning, and removing ashes outside the camp in other garments. He eats the sin offering in a holy place, shared by male priests, but his own meal offering is wholly burned.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

The priest makes atonement for the guilty person with the trespass offering and tends the altar by putting on linen garments, removing the ashes, burning wood each morning, and carrying the ashes outside the camp in other garments. He eats the sin offering he offers in a holy place, and every male among the priests may eat it. A priest's own meal offering must be wholly burned and not eaten.
