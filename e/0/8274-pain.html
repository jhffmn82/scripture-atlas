Abstract written by a model

In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I, pain is described as suffering and hardship arising from harmful actions and worldly existence, including the physical pains endured by Mahâvîra. In The Tao Teh King, or The Tao and Its Characteristics, pain at having disease is described as inseparable from the disease and as preserving a person from it.

pain in Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where pain first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pain is unpleasant, disagreeable, and greatly feared by living beings. It includes suffering, terror, unhappiness, and painful feelings, and harmful actions toward living bodies contribute to it. It arises through worldly pleasures, loss, bodily decay, sickness, birth and death, and a chain from wrath and delusion to hell; actions are its source. The afflicted fool remains caught in its whirl, while the wise avoid its causes and preach renunciation. Pain also appears as physical hardship from cold, fire, hunger, wounds, and attacks. Mahâvîra endured such pains without treatment, resentment, or attachment.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I / First Book. / First Lecture, Called Knowledge of the Weapon. / First Lesson. .. Seventh Lesson.

Beings experience pain, suffering, great terror, unhappiness, and painful feelings. Harmful actions toward living bodies contribute to this condition.

Second Lecture, Called Conquest of the World. / First Lesson. .. Sixth Lesson.

Pain arises from worldly pleasures, loss, bodily decay, sickness, and the cycle of birth and death. The afflicted fool remains turned around in the whirl of pains.

Third Lecture, Called Hot and Cold. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson.

Pain is connected with animal existence and with the chain from wrath through delusion, conception, birth, death, and hell. The wise person should avoid pain by avoiding these causes.

Fourth Lecture, Called Righteousness. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson.

Pain is unpleasant, disagreeable, and greatly feared by all sorts of living beings. Those who understand pain preach renunciation and recognize that pain results from actions.

Eighth Lecture, (Called) the Pillow of Righteousness. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson.

Physical suffering came from grass, cold, fire, flies, gnats, hunger, thirst, attacks, blows, wounds, torn hair, cut flesh, dust, and disturbed postures. Mahâvîra bore these pains without medical treatment, resentment, or attachment.
