Abstract written by a model

In The Letter to the Hebrews, angels are presented as spiritual beings lesser than the Son who serve as spirits sent to those who will inherit salvation. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), the angels question Allah’s placing of a viceroy on earth, acknowledge their limited knowledge, and prostrate before Adam except Iblis. In Surah 3: The Family of Imraan (Aal-i-Imraan), the angels witness Allah’s oneness, support believers in battle, give Zachariah glad tidings of John, and tell Mary that Allah chose and purified her. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), angels take in death those who wrong themselves and question them about their circumstances and failure to migrate. In Surah 13: The Thunder (Ar-Ra'd), angels guard people before and behind them by Allah’s command, hymn His praise with awe, and enter the heavenly Home through every gate. In Surah 15: The Rock (Al-Hijr), angels prostrate before the mortal Allah creates, except Iblis, and bring Abraham good tidings and punishment for a guilty people while rescuing Lot’s family. In Surah 16: The Bee (An-Nahl), the angels descend with the Spirit by Allah’s command, cause people to die, prostrate to Allah, fear their Lord, and do what they are commanded. In Surah 18: The Cave (Al-Kahf), the angels are commanded to prostrate before Adam, and they do so except Iblis.

In Surah 25: The Criterion (Al-Furqaan), the angels will descend grandly when heaven is rent asunder and bring no good tidings to the guilty. In Surah 34: Sheba (Saba), the angels deny that those who worshipped them were worshipping them and declare Allah alone their Guardian. In Surah 41: Explained in detail (Fussilat), the angels descend upon the upright who say that Allah is their Lord, reassure them, give them glad tidings of Paradise, and protect Allah’s friends. In Surah 43: Ornaments of gold (Az-Zukhruf), the angels are described as slaves of the Beneficent, while people falsely call them females and will be questioned about that testimony. In Surah 53: The Star (An-Najm), the angels’ intercession has no effect without Allah’s permission, while disbelievers name them with female names. In Surah 74: The Cloaked One (Al-Muddaththir), angels are appointed as wardens of the Fire, numbering nineteen. In Surah 79: Those who drag forth (An-Naazi'aat), angels are described as hastening and governing the event.

angels in The Letter to the Hebrews

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where angels first appears

Abstract written by a model

Angels are spiritual beings presented in Hebrews as lesser than the Son. God makes them winds and flames of fire, and they serve as spirits sent to those who will inherit salvation. They are commanded to worship the firstborn, were never called Son, and spoke a word whose transgressions were justly penalized. The world to come was not subjected to them, humans and Jesus were made a little lower than them, and Jesus helps Abraham's offspring, not angels.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

The Letter to the Hebrews / Chapter 1

The angels are shown to be lesser than the Son, who has become as much better than they as his inherited name is more excellent than theirs. God never said to any of them "You are my Son" or "Sit at my right hand," and they are commanded to worship the firstborn. They are described as winds and a flame of fire, and as serving spirits sent out to serve those who will inherit salvation.

Chapter 2

Angels spoke a word that proved steadfast, with every transgression receiving a just penalty. The world to come was not subjected to them, and man and Jesus are described as made a little lower than them. Jesus does not give help to angels but to the offspring of Abraham.

They are also described as protecting friends in the life of the world and in the Hereafter, while those with Allah glorify Him continually.
