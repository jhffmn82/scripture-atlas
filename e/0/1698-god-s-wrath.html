Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the LORD's wrath is directed at humanity, consuming and troubling people, though one psalm says it has been fully taken away and asks whether it will last forever. In Psalms, God's wrath and terrors weigh heavily on Heman, with fierce wrath, the LORD's waves, and terrors afflicting, cutting off, and engulfing him. In The Book of Ezekiel, God's wrath is poured out on Israel as fire that melts them like metal in a furnace, consuming them with indignation and bringing their own way on their heads. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, God's wrath is revealed from heaven against ungodliness and unrighteousness, unfolding as God giving people up to uncleanness, vile passions, and a reprobate mind, deeming such practices worthy of death. In Revelation, God's wrath is finished in the seven last plagues and contained in seven golden bowls given to the seven angels.

wrath (the LORD's anger) in Psalms

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where wrath (the LORD's anger) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The LORD's wrath is directed at humanity, under which all their days pass. In one psalm it is said to be fully taken away, yet the psalmist asks whether it will last forever. In the later psalm it consumes and troubles people, and the speaker pleads that the Lord relent.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 85

The LORD's wrath is said to have been completely taken away, and he has turned from the fierceness of his anger. The psalmist nonetheless asks that his indignation cease and questions whether he will be angry forever or draw out his anger to all generations.

Chapter 90

God's anger is portrayed as consuming and troubling the people, and all their days are said to pass away under his wrath. The speaker asks who knows its power according to the fear due to God, and then pleads that the Lord relent.
