Solomon's wealth in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Solomon's wealth first appears

Abstract written by a model

Solomon's wealth is a topic of extraordinary riches. It begins with 666 talents of gold yearly, excluding trade and tribute, then lists gold shields, an ivory throne overlaid with gold, and gold vessels. Silver became as common as stones in Jerusalem, the Tarshish fleet brought luxuries, and he surpassed all earth's kings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10

Solomon's wealth included 666 talents of gold in one year, besides revenue from traders, merchants, kings, and governors, and gifts from the queen of Sheba. He had golden bucklers and shields, an ivory throne overlaid with fine gold, and gold vessels, with silver considered of little value; his Tarshish fleet brought gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks. He received yearly tribute, made silver as common as stones in Jerusalem, and exceeded all earth's kings in riches.

Solomon’s riches in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Solomon’s riches first appears

Abstract written by a model

Solomon's riches exceed all earth's kings. He receives 666 talents of gold yearly plus merchants', kings' and governors' tribute, makes gold shields, an ivory-and-gold throne and gold vessels, making silver valueless. Tarshish ships bring luxuries every three years; he keeps four thousand stalls and twelve thousand horsemen.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

Solomon received 666 talents of gold in one year, besides what traders, merchants, Arabian kings, and governors brought, and he made gold shields, a great ivory and gold throne, and gold drinking vessels, so that silver was not considered valuable. Ships of Tarshish brought gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks every three years, kings brought yearly tribute, and he held four thousand stalls and twelve thousand horsemen.
