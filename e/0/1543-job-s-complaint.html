Job's complaint

topic · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Job's cause, my cause, my complaint, my declaration, my reasoning, my speech, the pleadings of my lips

Abstract written by a model

Job's complaint is his case against God, which he declares set in order. In Chapter 13 he asserts his righteousness, wants to plead before God, asks to learn his iniquities, and vows to maintain his ways though expecting death. In Chapter 23 he calls it rebellious even today under God's heavy hand, and longs to argue before God, learn the answer, and be freed from his judge.

Job's complaint is in one work on this site, The Book of Job. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

Job says he has set his cause in order and knows he is righteous, and he asks who will contend with him, since he would then be silent and give up the spirit. He wants to plead it before God and to be told what his iniquities and sins are, and he intends to maintain his ways before God even though he expects to be killed.

Chapter 23

Job calls his complaint rebellious even today, because God's hand is heavy in spite of his groaning. He wants to set the complaint in order before God and fill his mouth with arguments, so that he might know God's answer and be delivered forever from his judge.
