Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, men multiply, take wives, become continually evil in their hearts, and are limited to one hundred twenty years because they are flesh. In Psalms, humanity is frail and short-lived, troubled by God's anger, and asks to be taught to number its days. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, mankind is short-lived, faces an evil end, and receives the Lord's patience, mercy, and forgiveness. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, humanity is condemned to death through Adam's trespass, yet many are justified to life and made righteous through Jesus Christ. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, mankind is fallen, subject to justice, judged according to its works and desires, and rewarded or condemned according to its choices.

mankind in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where mankind first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mankind is a fallen group: through the fall it is carnal, sensual, devilish, cut off from the presence of the Lord, and subject to justice. Without the atonement it is hardened, lost, and must perish. This life is a probationary preparation to meet God, perform labors, repent, and choose good or evil. After departing the mortal body, its spirits are taken home to God and retain soul between death and resurrection. At an appointed, unknown time, mankind rises from death and is judged according to works, the desires of the heart, law, and justice. What it sends out is restored: good and desires of happiness bring restoration unto good and happiness; evil brings restoration unto evil. Mercy, justice, and righteous judgment are likewise received according to conduct. Those who repent and desire righteousness to the end are rewarded unto righteousness, redeemed of the Lord, and taken from endless night of darkness. Mankind is ultimately its own judge through its choices.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 34

All mankind are hardened, fallen, and lost, and must perish except through the atonement. Mankind is given this life to prepare to meet God and perform its labors.

Chapter 40

Men do not know the appointed time of resurrection, and time is measured unto them. Their spirits are taken home to God after they depart from the mortal body.

Chapter 41

They are judged according to their works and the desires of their hearts, and they are restored to good or evil according to what they have done and desired.

Chapter 42

Mankind became fallen, carnal, sensual, and devilish by nature, and was placed in a probationary state to prepare. Mankind can be reclaimed from spiritual death through repentance and the plan of redemption, but is judged according to works, law, and justice.
