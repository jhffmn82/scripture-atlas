charity

topic · 6 works · 13 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Charity, a gift, alms, almsgiving and charity, an alms, charitable giving, contribution, giving in alms, merciful deeds, spending, that which he expendeth, the alms, the poor-due

Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, almsgiving and charity are commanded duties, including helping the poor, mourning with mourners, visiting the sick, and giving gifts graciously. In The Gospel According to Matthew, charitable giving or merciful deeds should be done secretly rather than before people, and the Father who sees in secret will reward it openly. In Surah 9: The Repentance (At-Tawba), the poor-due is paid by believers, purifies wealth, helps givers grow, and is distributed among specified recipients. In Surah 30: The Romans (Ar-Room), charity given while seeking Allah's Countenance is increased manifold, and giving to relatives, the needy, and the wayfarer is described as best. In Surah 58: The Pleading Woman (Al-Mujaadila), alms are to be offered before holding conference with Allah's messenger, while those unable to provide them are excused. In The Table-Talk of the Prophet Mohammad (Part 1), charity includes almsgiving, justice between people, helping riders and their baggage, kind speech, assisting questioners, walking toward prayer, and removing harmful objects, with secret giving highly praised.

almsgiving and charity in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · about 179 BC · the work’s page · read it where almsgiving and charity first appears

Abstract written by a model

Almsgiving and charity is a commanded duty: alms must not be neglected, and stretching a hand to the poor completes one's blessing. The teaching widens to not avoiding weepers, mourning with mourners, visiting the sick to gain love, and giving gifts with grace, even toward the dead.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

The reader is told not to neglect giving alms and to stretch out a hand to the poor so that his blessing may be complete. Charity extends to not avoiding those who weep, mourning with mourners, and visiting the sick, by which love is gained. A gift is said to have grace in the sight of every living person, and grace is not to be withheld from the dead.

the poor-due in Surah 9: The Repentance (At-Tawba)

author unknown · undated · the work’s page · read it where the poor-due first appears

Abstract written by a model

The poor-due is paid by believers and required of those who repent. Taken from wealth, it purifies and helps givers grow; the Prophet’s prayer reassures them. Allah accepts alms, distributed to the poor, needy, collectors, reconciled hearts, captives, debtors, Allah’s cause, and wayfarers.
