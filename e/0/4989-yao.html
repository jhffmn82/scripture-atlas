Yao

person · 6 works · 10 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: Di Yao, the high meritorious sovereign

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book VIII (T'ai-po), Yao is praised as a majestic sovereign whose virtue corresponded to the grandeur of Heaven. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XX (Yao Yueh), Yao tells Shun that the Heaven-determined order of succession rests in him and charges him to hold fast the due Mean. In Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong), Yao confronts disorder from floods, vegetation, and beasts, then raises Shun to office. In Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou), Yao rules as a model sovereign whose conduct should be imitated from beginning to end. In Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang), Yao associates Shun in government and presents him to Heaven and the people. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), Yao naturally had benevolence and righteousness, loved the virtuous, and focused his limited wisdom on what mattered.
