righteousness

topic · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: iniquity, righteousness and wickedness, wickedness

Abstract written by a model

In Proverbs, righteousness is set against wickedness: it delivers from death, leads to life, and brings blessing, while wickedness profits nothing, leads to sin, and ends in perishing. In Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou), righteousness is the principle of shame and dislike, requiring benevolence, wisdom, and inward complacency; accumulated through constant practice, it nourishes the vast, flowing passion-nature rather than being external or merely nourishment.

Chapter 2

Righteousness nourishes the vast, flowing passion-nature when it is accumulated through constant practice. Mencius criticizes Gao for treating righteousness as something external and says that the mind must feel complacency in righteous conduct.
