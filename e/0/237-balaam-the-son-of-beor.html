Balaam the son of Beor

person · 1 work · 13 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Balaam, Balaam’s, the man whose eyes are open

Abstract written by a model

Balaam, son of Beor, is a seer from Pethor by the River in Aram, who hears the words of God and describes himself as one whose eyes are open. Balak says whomever Balaam blesses is blessed and whomever he curses is cursed, and summons him to curse Israel. Balaam consults God, who first forbids and then permits him to go if he speaks only God's word. He tells the envoys he cannot go beyond the LORD's word even for a house full of silver and gold. On the road he strikes his donkey three times, is rebuked by the LORD's angel, confesses 'I have sinned,' and is told to speak only what he is given. At Balak's high places he builds altars and makes offerings at three sites, but refuses to curse Israel, saying God has not cursed them and he cannot reverse the blessing. He wishes to die the death of the righteous. Seeing the LORD is pleased to bless Israel, he abandons divination, the Spirit of God comes on him, and he speaks parables blessing Israel and foretelling its future, with short oracles on Amalek and the Kenite. He then returns to his place.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 22

Balaam is summoned by Balak to curse Israel and twice consults God, who first forbids him and then allows him to go with the princes of Moab if he speaks only God's word. He tells Balak's envoys he cannot go beyond the LORD's word even for a house full of silver and gold. On the road he strikes his donkey three times, is rebuked by the LORD's angel, confesses 'I have sinned,' and is told to speak only what he is given. He meets Balak, says he can speak only the word God puts in his mouth, and is brought to the high places of Baal, where he sees part of the people.

Chapter 23

Balaam directs the building of altars and offerings at three sites, meets God, and speaks only the word the LORD puts in his mouth. He refuses to curse Israel, pronouncing instead that God has not cursed or defied them and that he cannot reverse the blessing. He wishes to die the death of the righteous and tells Balak that he must do all the LORD speaks.

Chapter 24

Balaam, seeing that the LORD is pleased to bless Israel, abandons divination and turns toward the wilderness, where the Spirit of God comes on him. He speaks two parables blessing Israel and foretelling its future, plus short oracles on Amalek, the Kenite, and others. He tells Balak that he cannot go beyond the LORD's word even for a house full of silver and gold, then returns to his place.
