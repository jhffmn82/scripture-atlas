action

Also called: inaction, movement, perils of action

Abstract written by a model

Action is ignored by the divine man, who influences creation while inert. It becomes dangerous when imposed against natural order, causing Hun Tun’s death. Arising from movement after inner repose, it is required of the ruled for the empire, but rulers relying on it lack sufficient energy.

CHAPTER I.

The divine man ignores action and remains inert while nevertheless influencing all creation and causing crops to thrive.

CHAPTER VII.

It is portrayed as dangerous when imposed on what should follow natural order; Shu and Hu's action causes Hun Tun's death.

CHAPTER XIII.

Action arises from movement and follows established inner repose. The ruled must practise action to subserve the interests of the empire, while rulers who rely on action find their energy inadequate.
