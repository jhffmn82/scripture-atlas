Jezebel in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Jezebel first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jezebel, daughter of Ethbaal and wife of Ahab king of Samaria, is a domineering figure who incites Ahab to evil and cuts off the LORD's prophets. She threatens Elijah through a messenger after hearing of the prophets' killing, then arranges Naboth's false trial and stoning to seize his vineyard. The LORD foretells her fate: dogs will eat her by the rampart of Jezreel.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 19

Jezebel hears from Ahab all that Elijah did, including the killing of the prophets with the sword. She sends a messenger to Elijah swearing by the gods that she will make his life like one of theirs by about the same time the next day.

Chapter 21

Jezebel rebukes Ahab's sulking and writes letters in his name, sealed with his seal, ordering a false trial and stoning of Naboth. She then tells Ahab to take the vineyard, and the LORD declares that dogs will eat her by the rampart of Jezreel.

Jezebel in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where Jezebel first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jezebel, a king's daughter and mother of Joram, is blamed for shedding the LORD's servants' blood. Prophecy foretells her death; she defiantly taunts Jehu, is thrown down by eunuchs and trampled, leaving only skull, feet, and palms.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

Jezebel is named as the one at whose hand the blood of the LORD's servants was shed, and the prophecy says dogs will eat her at Jezreel with no one to bury her. She paints her eyes, adorns her head, and taunts Jehu from the window, calling him Zimri. Her eunuchs throw her down, Jehu tramples her, and only her skull, feet, and palms are found afterward.
