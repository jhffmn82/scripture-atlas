Silas

Abstract written by a model

Silas is a prophet and a chief man among the brothers, also a Roman citizen. Chosen with Judas Barsabbas to carry the letter to Antioch and confirm it by word, he encourages the brothers, then Paul takes him as companion. In Philippi he is beaten, imprisoned in stocks, and prays and sings with Paul at midnight. He then joins Paul in Thessalonica, is sent to Beroea, stays there with Timothy, and is summoned to Athens.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 15

Silas, with Judas called Barsabbas, is chosen as a chief man among the brothers to carry the letter to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas and to confirm it by word of mouth. He, as a prophet, encourages and strengthens the brothers, and later Paul chooses him as his companion when he leaves Antioch.

Chapter 16

Silas is seized with Paul, dragged before the rulers, beaten with rods, and put in the inner prison with his feet in stocks. At midnight he prays and sings hymns with Paul, and he hears the word spoken to the jailer's household.

Chapter 17

Silas joined Paul in Thessalonica, where some were persuaded and joined him and Paul. He was sent with Paul by night to Beroea and stayed there with Timothy when Paul was sent on toward the sea. Paul sent a commandment for Silas and Timothy to come to him in Athens very quickly.
