Joshua

person · 6 works · 79 quoted facts · 23 connections
Also called: Hoshea the son of Nun, I, Joshua the son of Nun, Joshua, the son of Nun, Joshua’s, a man, a man in whom is the Spirit, his servant, his servant Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man

Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, Joshua serves as Moses' young companion, leads Israel against Amalek, and defeats them by the sword. In Numbers, Joshua, originally called Hoshea, serves as a faithful spy, is appointed Moses' successor, and is commissioned to help divide Canaan. In Deuteronomy, Joshua is commissioned and charged to lead Israel into the promised land as Moses' successor. In The Book of Joshua, Joshua succeeds Moses as Israel's leader, leads the conquest and division of the land, renews Israel's covenant, and dies at 110. In The Book of Judges, Joshua dismisses Israel to its inheritances, and Israel serves the LORD throughout his life before he dies and is buried at Timnath Heres. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Joshua son of Nun succeeds Moses, leads Israel to its inheritance, defeats enemies, and enters the land with Caleb.

Joshua in Numbers

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Joshua first appears

Abstract written by a model

Joshua, son of Nun of the tribe of Ephraim, was originally named Hoshea until Moses called him Joshua. He is first one of the twelve spies sent into Canaan. With Caleb he tears his clothes, urges the congregation not to rebel or fear, and is threatened with stoning. He and Caleb are the only adults of that generation who will enter the Promised Land, and he survives when the other spies die. The LORD names him a man in whom is the Spirit and appoints him to succeed Moses, with the laying on of hands before Eleazar and the congregation. He follows the LORD completely, consults Eleazar by the Urim, and is appointed with Eleazar to divide the land of Canaan.

Joshua in Deuteronomy

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Joshua first appears

Abstract written by a model

Joshua, son of Nun, is Moses' designated successor, commissioned to lead Israel across the Jordan into the promised land and cause the people to inherit it. Early on he is described as one who stands before Moses and will enter the land, and Moses is told to encourage him. Moses then charges him not to fear, recalling what the LORD did to the two kings. At the Tent of Meeting the LORD commissions him, and Moses charges him before all Israel to be strong and courageous. Finally, he is full of the spirit of wisdom through Moses' laying on of hands, and Israel obeys him.

Joshua in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Joshua first appears

Abstract written by a model

Joshua, son of Nun, is the servant of Moses and later servant of the LORD, who succeeds Moses as leader of Israel. Commissioned by the LORD in the opening chapter to lead the people across the Jordan, he is told to be strong, courageous and faithful to the law, and the eastern tribes pledge to obey him as they did Moses. The LORD magnifies him in the sight of all Israel so that they fear him all his days as they feared Moses, and his fame spreads through the land because the LORD is with him. His story unfolds in three phases. First, conquest: he sends spies to Jericho, moves the people to the Jordan, sets up twelve memorial stones, circumcises Israel, and meets the armed man near Jericho whom he worships and obeys. He carries out the LORD's plan against Jericho, saves Rahab's household, and curses anyone who rebuilds the city. After the defeat at Ai he laments, sanctifies the people, and Israel executes judgment on Achan. He then takes Ai by ambush, builds an altar on Mount Ebal, and reads the law to the assembly. He makes a covenant with the deceiving Gibeonites, then makes them wood cutters and water drawers. He defeats the five kings, as the sun and moon stand still, destroys the southern cities, and takes Hazor and the northern land, leaving nothing undone of what the LORD commanded Moses. He camps at Gilgal. Second, division of the land: old and advanced in years, he is commanded to divide the remaining land among the nine tribes and half-tribe of Manasseh. With Eleazar and the tribal heads he distributes inheritances by lot, giving Hebron to Caleb, addressing the children of Joseph, sending surveyors, casting lots at Shiloh, and relaying the command about cities of refuge. He himself receives Timnathserah, which he builds and lives in. He blesses and sends home the eastern tribes. Third, farewell: he gathers Israel's leaders, urges courage, faithfulness and love for the LORD, and says he is going the way of all the earth. At Shechem he recounts the LORD's acts, declares that he and his house will serve the LORD, makes a covenant, writes the words in the book of the law, and sets up a witness stone. He dies at 110 and is buried in Timnathserah in Ephraim's hill country.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

21 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Joshua / Chapter 1

Joshua, son of Nun and Moses' servant, is commissioned by the LORD to lead the people across the Jordan and is told to be strong, courageous, and faithful to the law. He commands the officers to have the people prepare to cross within three days and reminds the eastern tribes of their duty to help their brothers. The Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh pledge to obey him as they did Moses.

Chapter 2

Joshua the son of Nun secretly sends two men from Shittim to view the land, including Jericho. At the end of the unit the spies return to him and tell him all that happened, saying that the LORD has delivered the land into their hands.

Chapter 3

Joshua rises early, moves the people from Shittim to the Jordan, and tells them to sanctify themselves because the LORD will do wonders. He commands the priests to take up the ark and cross over before the people, and announces that the Jordan will be cut off and the nations driven out. The LORD tells him that he will be magnified in the sight of all Israel as Moses was.

Chapter 4

Joshua relays the LORD's instructions to the twelve chosen men, sets up twelve stones in the middle of the Jordan, and later sets up the twelve stones at Gilgal. The LORD magnifies him in the sight of all Israel, so that they fear him as they feared Moses all his life. He tells the people how to explain the stones to their children.

Chapter 5

Joshua makes flint knives at the LORD's command and circumcises the sons of Israel at the hill of the foreskins. Near Jericho he meets a man with a drawn sword, asks whether he is for Israel or its enemies, then falls on his face and worships, asks what his lord says to his servant, and obeys the order to take off his sandals.

Chapter 6

Joshua receives the LORD's battle plan for Jericho, directs the priests and people to carry it out, and commands silence until the day of the shout. He gives the shout command on the seventh day, instructs that the city be devoted and its metals go to the treasury, sends the two spies to bring Rahab out, and saves her household alive. He pronounces a curse on whoever rebuilds Jericho, and the LORD is with him so that his fame spreads through the land.

Chapter 7

Joshua sent men to spy out Ai, then tore his clothes and lay on his face before the LORD's ark until evening after the defeat, lamenting to the LORD. Following the LORD's instructions, he sanctified the people, conducted the selection that identified Achan, urged him to confess, and led Israel in carrying out the judgment on him.

Chapter 8

Joshua receives the LORD's instruction to take all the warriors up to Ai, plans the ambush, and leads the feigned retreat that draws the men of Ai out of the city. At the LORD's command he stretches out his javelin toward Ai and does not draw back his hand until all its inhabitants are destroyed. He then burns Ai, hangs its king, builds an altar on Mount Ebal, writes the law on stones, and reads all its words to the assembly.

Chapter 9

Joshua receives the disguised Gibeonites at Gilgal, questions them, and makes peace and a covenant with them to let them live. After learning of the deception he confronts them, pronounces them cursed, and appoints them wood cutters and drawers of water for the congregation and the LORD's altar, sparing them from being killed.

Chapter 10

Joshua marched all night from Gilgal to relieve Gibeon, was assured by the LORD of victory, and asked the sun and moon to stand still during the battle. He had the five kings killed and hanged, then took Makkedah, Libnah, Lachish, Eglon, Hebron, and Debir, utterly destroying them, and returned to Gilgal.

Chapter 11

Joshua attacks the coalition suddenly at Merom, hamstrings the horses and burns the chariots as the LORD told him, and takes Hazor. He wages war a long time, captures the whole land, destroys the Anakim, leaves nothing undone of what the LORD commanded Moses, and gives the land to Israel by tribal divisions.

Chapter 12

Joshua and the children of Israel struck the kings west of the Jordan, from Baal Gad in the valley of Lebanon to Mount Halak. Joshua gave that land to the tribes of Israel as a possession according to their divisions.

Chapter 13

Joshua is described as old and well advanced in years. The LORD tells him that very much land remains to be possessed and commands him to divide it as an inheritance among the nine tribes and the half-tribe of Manasseh.

Chapter 14

Joshua, along with Eleazar the priest and the heads of the fathers' houses, distributed the inheritances by lot. At Gilgal he heard Caleb's request, blessed him, and gave him Hebron as an inheritance.

Chapter 17

Joshua gave Zelophehad's daughters an inheritance according to the LORD's commandment. When the children of Joseph complained of having one lot, he told them to clear the forest of the Perizzites and Rephaim, and then assured them the hill country would be theirs if they drove out the strong, iron-chariot Canaanites.

Chapter 18

Joshua rebuked the children of Israel for neglecting to possess the land and instructed them to appoint three men per tribe to survey it in seven portions. He sent the surveyors out, received their written description at Shiloh, and cast lots for the tribes before the LORD, dividing the land according to their divisions.

Chapter 19

After the land was distributed, the children of Israel gave Joshua the son of Nun an inheritance among them. According to the LORD's commandment he received the city he asked for, Timnathserah in Ephraim's hill country, where he built the city and lived. He is also named among those who distributed the inheritances by lot at Shiloh.

Chapter 20

Joshua receives the LORD's word and is told to speak to the children of Israel about assigning the cities of refuge. He acts as the intermediary of this command.

Chapter 22

Joshua calls the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh, praises them for keeping all that Moses and he commanded, and charges them to love the LORD and keep his law. He blesses them, tells them to return with great wealth and share the plunder with their brothers, and sends them to their tents. He is also noted as having given the other half of Manasseh an inheritance west of the Jordan.

Chapter 23

Joshua, old and well advanced in years, called together all Israel's elders, heads, judges, and officers to address them. He urged courage, faithfulness to the law, and love for the LORD, and warned of the consequences of turning away. He stated that he was going the way of all the earth.

Chapter 24

Joshua assembles Israel at Shechem, delivers the LORD's recital of his acts, and challenges the people to choose whom to serve, declaring that he and his house will serve the LORD. He warns the people, makes a covenant, writes the words in the book of the law, and sets up a stone as witness before sending them to their inheritances. He then dies at 110 years old and is buried in Timnathserah in the hill country of Ephraim.
