Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, the Chaldeans surround Jerusalem, capture Zedekiah, break down the city walls, carry away the temple bronze, and cause fear that drives people to Egypt. In The Book of Jeremiah, the Chaldeans are Babylon's besieging army that captures Jerusalem, burns it, carries its treasures to Babylon, and is later promised judgment. In The Book of Ezekiel, the Chaldeans are portrayed as Babylonians who defile Oholibah and are brought with the Assyrians to judge her. In Daniel (Greek), the Babylonians worship the dragon, accuse the king after Daniel destroys it, and demand Daniel's execution. In Habakkuk, the Chaldeans are raised up as a bitter, hasty, feared nation that conquers mercilessly, trusts in its strength and net, and is appointed for judgment. In The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry, the Chaldeans are commanded to be fled from, and the Lord's arm shall come upon them.

Chaldeans in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Chaldeans first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Chaldeans are the people of Babylon, fighting for Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, and appearing throughout the records chiefly as the besieging army against Jerusalem. Early on they surround the city, build siege ramps, and are told by the LORD to be the ones to whom defenders may surrender and live. They withdraw briefly when Pharaoh's army approaches, but the LORD says they will return, take the city, and burn it. Jeremiah is accused of defecting to them, and he warns Zedekiah that the city will burn if he does not surrender. The siege ends with the Chaldean army pursuing and overtaking Zedekiah in the plains of Jericho, bringing him to Nebuchadnezzar at Riblah, burning the houses, breaking down Jerusalem's walls, and carrying the temple's bronze to Babylon. Afterward, Ishmael kills Chaldean men at Mizpah along with Gedaliah, the governor the king of Babylon appointed, and Johanan's people fear the Chaldeans, which motivates their plan to go to Egypt. The records close with prophecies reversing their fortune: a sword will fall on Chaldea and Babylon, the land will be plundered, and the Chaldeans will be repaid for the evil they did in Zion.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

The Chaldeans besiege the city from outside the walls. The LORD says he will turn the defenders' weapons back and gather them into the middle of the city. Anyone who goes out and passes over to the Chaldeans will live and escape with his life.

Chapter 32

The Chaldeans, under Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, are besieging Jerusalem and have built siege ramps against it. The LORD says the city is given into their hand and that they will come, set it on fire, and burn it with the houses where incense was offered to Baal.

Chapter 37

The Chaldeans, who were besieging Jerusalem, withdraw when they hear news of Pharaoh's army. The LORD says they will return, fight against the city, take it, and burn it with fire. Irijah accuses Jeremiah of defecting to them.

Chapter 38

The Chaldeans are the besieging army to whom Jeremiah says people should go out in order to live. Jeremiah says the city will be given into their hand and burned if Zedekiah does not surrender, and that the king's wives and children will be brought out to them.

Chapter 39

The Chaldean army pursued the fleeing Zedekiah and overtook him in the plains of Jericho, then brought him to Nebuchadnezzar at Riblah. The Chaldeans also burned the king's house and the people's houses and broke down the walls of Jerusalem.

Chapter 41

Chaldean men of war found at Mizpah were killed by Ishmael along with Gedaliah's other companions. Afterward the people with Johanan feared the Chaldeans because Ishmael had killed the governor the king of Babylon had appointed, which motivated their plan to go to Egypt.

Chapter 50

The Chaldeans' land is to become a prey, and the LORD has a work to do there. A sword is to fall on the Chaldeans, the inhabitants of Babylon, her princes, wise men, boasters, mighty men, horses, chariots and treasures. They are told that Chaldea will be plundered and that the LORD will punish its king and land.

Chapter 51

The Chaldeans are the people of Babylon, whose slain will fall in their land and streets. They are to be repaid for all the evil they did in Zion, and their inhabitants are to be made drunk and sleep a perpetual sleep.

Chapter 52

The Chaldeans surrounded the city, pursued King Zedekiah and overtook him in the plains of Jericho, scattering his army. Those with the captain of the guard broke down Jerusalem's walls and broke up the temple's bronze pillars, bases and sea, carrying the bronze to Babylon.

Chaldeans in Habakkuk

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Chaldeans first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Chaldeans are a bitter, hasty, feared nation that the LORD raises up in Habakkuk 1. They march across the earth seizing dwellings not theirs, with swift horses and horsemen, scoffing at kings and strongholds, gathering prisoners like sand, and killing nations without mercy. They are declared guilty because their strength is their god and they trust in their net, yet they are appointed for judgment and punishment.

The Book of Habakkuk / Chapter 1

The LORD says he is raising up the Chaldeans, a bitter and hasty nation that marches across the earth to possess dwellings not theirs. They are feared and dreaded, with swift horses and horsemen who fly like an eagle to devour, gather prisoners like sand, and scoff at kings and strongholds. They are called guilty because their strength is their god, and Habakkuk says they are appointed for judgment and to punish.
