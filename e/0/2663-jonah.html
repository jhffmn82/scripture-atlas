Abstract written by a model

This is the Book of Jonah, a four-chapter narrative about the prophet Jonah, son of Amittai, and his troubled obedience to the LORD. In chapter 1, the LORD commands Jonah to preach against Nineveh's wickedness, but he boards a ship at Joppa bound for Tarshish to flee. A great storm strikes; lots identify Jonah as the cause, and he tells the mariners to throw him into the sea. They do, the sea calms, they fear the LORD and offer sacrifice, and the LORD prepares a huge fish that swallows Jonah for three days and nights. In chapter 2, Jonah prays from inside the fish, recounting his affliction, remembering the LORD when his soul fainted, vowing thanksgiving, and declaring that salvation belongs to the LORD; the fish then vomits him onto dry land. In chapter 3, the LORD commands him a second time, and Jonah goes to Nineveh and proclaims its overthrow in forty days. The people believe God, fast and wear sackcloth, and the king sits in ashes and decrees that all, including animals, turn from evil and violence. God sees their works and relents. In chapter 4, Jonah is angry, explaining that he fled because he knew God is gracious and merciful, and asks to die. He waits east of the city; God provides a vine for shade, then a worm withers it, and Jonah again wishes to die. God contrasts Jonah's concern for a vine he did not labor for with His own concern for Nineveh's more than 120,000 people and many animals.

The Book of Jonah / Chapter 1

The LORD commands Jonah, son of Amittai, to go to Nineveh and preach against its wickedness, but Jonah instead boards a ship at Joppa bound for Tarshish to flee the LORD's presence. The LORD sends a great wind and storm, and the frightened mariners cry to their gods and throw cargo overboard while Jonah sleeps below. When lots identify Jonah as the cause, he tells them he fears the LORD, the God who made the sea and dry land, and asks to be thrown into the sea. After rowing hard in vain, the mariners pray to the LORD and throw Jonah overboard, and the sea stops raging. The mariners fear the LORD, offer a sacrifice and make vows, and the LORD prepares a huge fish that swallows Jonah for three days and three nights.

Chapter 2

Chapter 2 presents Jonah's prayer to the LORD, his God, from inside the fish's belly. Jonah recounts his affliction, how the LORD threw him into the depths of the seas where waves, weeds, and the deep surrounded him, and how he felt banished yet resolved to look again toward the LORD's holy temple. He says the LORD brought his life up from the pit, and that when his soul fainted he remembered the LORD. He contrasts those who regard vain idols with himself, vowing to sacrifice with thanksgiving and declaring that salvation belongs to the LORD. At the end, the LORD speaks to the fish, and it vomits Jonah onto dry land.

Chapter 3

The LORD's word comes to Jonah a second time, commanding him to go to Nineveh and preach the message given to him. Jonah obeys, enters the exceedingly great city, and cries out that Nineveh will be overthrown in forty days. The people of Nineveh believe God and proclaim a fast and put on sackcloth, from greatest to least. The king of Nineveh removes his royal robe, sits in ashes, and decrees that people and animals fast, wear sackcloth, cry to God, and turn from evil and violence, hoping God might relent. God sees their works, relents of the disaster he had threatened, and does not carry it out.

Chapter 4

Jonah is displeased and angry, and prays to the LORD that he fled to Tarshish because he knew God is gracious, merciful, slow to anger, and relents of doing harm; he asks to die. The LORD asks whether it is right for him to be angry. Jonah leaves the city, builds a booth on its east side, and waits to see what will happen to Nineveh. God prepares a vine to shade him, which makes Jonah glad, then prepares a worm that withers it and a sultry east wind, so Jonah grows faint and again asks to die. God asks if he is right to be angry about the vine, and Jonah says he is, even to death. God contrasts Jonah's concern for the vine, which he did not labor for, with His own concern for Nineveh, its more than 120,000 people who cannot discern right from left, and its many animals.
