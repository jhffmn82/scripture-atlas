Abstract written by a model

In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), water is governed by rules concerning living beings, acceptance, crossing, and natural drying. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII, water serves as a divine witness and purifier whose contact with corpses makes it unclean until prescribed restoration. In Mencius, Book VI (Gaozi), water subdues fire as benevolence and flows into four seas under Yu's regulation.

water in Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra)

Hermann Jacobi · the work’s page · read it where water first appears

Abstract written by a model

Water, hot or cold, may contain living beings and is governed by rules of acceptance and crossing. Water from bare ground, associated with cobwebs, recently used for watering flour, sesamum, rice, or washing, or fetched in a wet, moist, or dirty vessel mixed with cold water is unacceptable. Changed, strained water used long ago may be accepted. A monk or nun crosses circumspectly and dries naturally after swimming or wading.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Seventh Lesson.

Water recently used for watering flour, sesamum, rice, or washing, when it has not acquired a new taste or been strained, should not be accepted. Water used long ago that has changed its taste or nature and has been strained may be accepted, while water from bare ground or fetched in a wet, moist, or dirty vessel mixed with cold water is unacceptable.

Third Lecture, Called Walking. / First Lesson. .. Third Lesson.

A monk or a nun should cross water circumspectly, without unnecessary touching or diving. After swimming or wading, they should remain on the bank with a wet or moist body until the water has dried, and should not wipe or warm the body before then.

water in Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where water first appears

Abstract written by a model

Water is a divine witness: brimstoned, golden, and truth-knowing. It kills no man, though Astô-vîdhôtu, Vaya, the flood, birds, and chance are connected with death. It flows to corpses, Dakhmas, unclean remains, and bones, then returns through the sea Pûitika cleansed. Corpse-contact makes water unclean and unfit to drink until removal and required drawing off, melting, flowing, or passing intervals restore it. It purifies clothing, wood, corn, eating vessels, a cow, and man, with gômêz and earth where prescribed. It is kept from graves, sprinkled from skull to feet, and requires atonement when found by a man who touched a corpse.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard V.

Water does not kill a man; Astô-vîdhôtu, Vaya, the flood, birds, and chance are described in connection with his death. Water flows to corpses, Dakhmas, unclean remains, and bones, then returns through the sea Pûitika after being cleansed.

Fargard VI.

Water containing a corpse is unclean and unfit to drink until the corpse is removed. Depending on the water source, part of the water must be drawn off or the water must melt, flow, or pass through specified intervals before it becomes clean.

Fargard VII.

It is used in cleansing clothing, wood, corn, eating vessels, and a cow. Water brought into contact with a corpse is defiled, and a woman with a still-born child may drink it only to save her life with permission from a holy man.

Fargard VIII.

Water is kept thirty paces from a grave prepared in a house. It is used in purification after gômêz, and it is sprinkled over the body in a prescribed sequence from the skull to the feet.

VIII.

Water is used with gômêz to cleanse a man who has touched a corpse when others will not cleanse him. If he finds water on his way, the water requires atonement, for which he must receive the stated penalty.
