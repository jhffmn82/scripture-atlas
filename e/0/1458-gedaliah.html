In The Book of Jeremiah, Gedaliah is appointed governor over Judah by the king of Babylon, settles at Mizpah, urges service to the Chaldeans, rejects Johanan's warning, and is killed by Ishmael.

Gedaliah the son of Ahikam in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Gedaliah the son of Ahikam first appears

Abstract written by a model

Gedaliah, son of Ahikam and grandson of Shaphan, is a governor appointed by the king of Babylon over the cities of Judah and the land. He settles at Mizpah, responsible for the poorest people left behind, and urges them to serve the Chaldeans for their well-being. He rejects Johanan's warning that Ishmael plans to kill him and forbids action. Hosting Ishmael and ten men at a meal in Mizpah, he is struck down and killed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 40

Gedaliah, made governor over the cities of Judah by the king of Babylon, settles at Mizpah and swears to the captains and their men that serving the Chaldeans will bring well-being. He disbelieves Johanan's warning that Ishmael means to kill him and forbids Johanan to act, saying he speaks falsely of Ishmael.

Chapter 41

Gedaliah, whom the king of Babylon had made governor over the land, hosted Ishmael and his ten men at a meal in Mizpah. Ishmael struck him down with the sword and killed him there.
