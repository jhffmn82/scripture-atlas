Baasha

Also called: Baasha king of Israel, Baasha the son of Ahijah

Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, Baasha was a king of Israel who conspired against Nadab, killed him, destroyed Jeroboam's house, fought Asa, built Ramah, and was condemned by the LORD. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Baasha was a king of Israel who built Ramah to blockade Asa's Judah, then abandoned the project when Ben Hadad's armies struck Israelite cities.

Baasha in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Baasha first appears

Abstract written by a model

Baasha, of the house of Issachar, was a king of Israel. He conspired against Nadab, killed him at Gibbethon in Asa's third year, and reigned twenty-four years from Tirzah. He destroyed Jeroboam's whole house, fulfilling the LORD's word, yet walked in Jeroboam's evil way. He fought Asa, built Ramah, then withdrew to Tirzah when Ben Hadad attacked. The LORD condemned him through Jehu. He was buried in Tirzah, succeeded by his son Elah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 15

Baasha son of Ahijah, of the house of Issachar, conspired against Nadab, struck him at Gibbethon in Asa's third year, and became king over all Israel, reigning twenty-four years in Tirzah. He destroyed all of Jeroboam's house, leaving no one who breathed. He did evil, walking in Jeroboam's way. He fought Asa all their days, built Ramah against Judah, and stopped and went to live in Tirzah when Ben Hadad attacked.

Chapter 16

Baasha is condemned by the LORD through Jehu for walking in Jeroboam's way, making Israel sin, and striking down the house of Jeroboam. He sleeps with his fathers, is buried in Tirzah, and is succeeded by his son Elah.
