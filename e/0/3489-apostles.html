Abstract written by a model

In The Acts of the Apostles, the apostles testify to Jesus’ resurrection, perform signs and wonders, teach despite persecution, distribute believers’ proceeds according to need, and continue in prayer and the ministry of the word. In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, the apostles are portrayed as faithful stewards who are made a spectacle, suffer deprivation and persecution, and bless those who curse them.

apostles in The Acts of the Apostles

author unknown · about AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where apostles first appears

Abstract written by a model

The apostles are the group Jesus chose, told to wait in Jerusalem and be his witnesses in Jerusalem, Judea, Samaria and the farthest parts of the earth. After seeing him taken up, they return to Jerusalem, stay in the upper room, and pray with one accord alongside the women and Jesus' family. Their number is restored to twelve when Matthias is counted with the eleven after Judas fell away. They then testify to the resurrection with great power, perform signs and wonders, gather in Solomon's porch, and receive proceeds that believers lay at their feet for distribution according to need. Jailed by the high priest and freed by an angel, they keep teaching in the temple and at home despite being beaten and commanded to stop, leaving rejoicing. Finally, to avoid forsaking the word of God to serve tables, they have disciples select seven men to oversee daily service, pray and lay hands on them, and themselves continue in prayer and the ministry of the word.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

The Acts of the Apostles / Chapter 1

The apostles, whom Jesus had chosen, were shown him alive over forty days and were told to wait in Jerusalem and to be his witnesses to the uttermost parts of the earth. After watching him taken up, they returned to Jerusalem, stayed in the upper room, and continued in prayer. The eleven are named, and Matthias was counted with them after the lot fell on him.

Chapter 4

The apostles testify to the resurrection of the Lord Jesus with great power, and great grace is on them all. Believers who sell lands or houses lay the proceeds at the apostles' feet, and distribution is made to each according to need.

Chapter 5

The apostles received the gift at their feet, did many signs and wonders among the people, and gathered with one accord in Solomon's porch. They were jailed by the high priest, freed by an angel, and told to teach in the temple, which they did at daybreak. After being beaten and commanded not to speak in Jesus' name, they left rejoicing and continued teaching and preaching Jesus every day.

Chapter 6

The twelve summon the multitude of disciples and say it is not appropriate for them to forsake the word of God to serve tables. They direct the disciples to select seven men to appoint over the business, while they continue in prayer and the ministry of the word. They pray and lay hands on the seven chosen.

the apostles in Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians

author unknown · AD 53 to 54 · the work’s page · read it where the apostles first appears

Abstract written by a model

The apostles are stewards required to be faithful, yet displayed last of all like men sentenced to death and made a spectacle to world, angels and men. They are fools for Christ, weak, dishonored, deprived and beaten, yet bless those who curse them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

The apostles are presented as stewards who must be faithful, displayed last of all like men sentenced to death, and made a spectacle to the world, to angels and to men. They are described as fools, weak, and dishonored, hungry, thirsty, naked, beaten, homeless, and toiling with their own hands, blessing those who curse them and enduring persecution.
