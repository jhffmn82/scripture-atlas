Abstract written by a model

Judith is a beautiful, wealthy, devout Hebrew widow from Bethulia, daughter of Merari and widow of Manasses. She has lived three years and four months in widowhood, in a tent on her roof, wearing sackcloth and fasting except on Sabbaths, new moons and feasts. No evil is spoken of her because she fears God exceedingly. She is attended by a maid who oversees all she has. The story opens with her rebuking the elders for testing God with their oath, and she tells them the Lord will deliver Israel by her hand. She prays with ashes on her head, asking God to make her speech and deceit a wound to those who plot against his covenant and house. She then discards her widow's clothing, dresses beautifully, and goes with her maid to the Assyrian camp, saying she is a Hebrew fleeing her people. Brought to Holofernes, she flatters him, promises to lead him to Jerusalem, and is praised by him and his servants as unmatched in beauty and wisdom. For three days she stays in the camp, eating only her own provisions, and goes out each night to wash at the fountain and pray. At the feast she is left alone with the drunken Holofernes, prays for strength, and cuts off his head with his sword. She carries it in her food bag back to Bethulia, where Ozias blesses her above all women. She has the head hung on the wall, outlines the plan that makes the Assyrians flee, and tells the people all she did. Achior bows at her feet and blesses her. Joakim the high priest and the elders bless her as the exaltation of Jerusalem and great glory of Israel, and she receives Holofernes' tent and furnishings. She leads the women in a dance and begins a song of thanksgiving, crediting the Lord with using her beauty against the enemy. She dedicates Holofernes' belongings and canopy to the Lord and returns to Bethulia, honored in all the land. She never knew a man after Manasses died. She frees her maid, distributes her goods to kin, dies at 105, and is buried in Manasses' cave.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

Judith is introduced as the daughter of Merari and widow of Manasses, living three years and four months in widowhood in a roof tent with sackcloth and fasting, beautiful, wealthy, and with no evil said of her because she feared God. She summons the elders, rebukes them for testing God with the oath, and tells them she will go out with her maid and that the Lord will deliver Israel by her hand within the appointed days, without revealing her act.

Chapter 9

Judith falls on her face, puts ashes on her head, uncovers her sackcloth, and cries loudly to the Lord at the hour of the evening incense. She identifies herself as a widow and asks God to give into her hand the might she has conceived. She asks that her speech and deceit become a wound to those who plan harm against God's covenant and house.

Chapter 10

Judith rises from prayer, discards her widow's clothing, and dresses beautifully, then leaves Bethulia with her maid after the elders bless her. Stopped by an Assyrian watch, she says she is a Hebrew fleeing her people and that she will show Holofernes how to win the hill country without losing a man. She is escorted to his tent, and bows before him until his servants raise her up.

Chapter 11

Judith speaks to Holofernes, flattering him and claiming that she will not lie to him. She says she fled her people because of their coming sin, offers to pray in the valley each night to learn when they sin, and promises to lead him to Jerusalem and set his throne there. She is praised by Holofernes and his servants as unmatched in beauty and wisdom.

Chapter 12

Judith refuses Holofernes' food and wine, saying her own provisions will be used until the Lord works by her hand. She stays three days in the camp, going out each night to wash at the fountain and pray, then accepts the invitation to the feast through Bagoas, dresses in her finery, and sits beside Holofernes. She says her life is magnified in her that day and eats and drinks only what her servant prepared.

Chapter 13

Judith, left alone in the tent with the drunk Holofernes, prays for strength, takes his sword, and cuts off his head with two blows. She leaves with her maid, carrying the head in the food bag, passes through the camp and reaches the gates of Bethulia, where she announces that God has destroyed the enemies by her hand and shows the head. Ozias blesses her above all women, and the people say "Amen! Amen!" to his praise.

Chapter 14

Judith instructs her kindred to hang Holofernes' head on the wall and lays out a plan to make the Assyrians flee, which the Israelites will then pursue. She has Achior summoned and tells the assembled people everything she has done from the day she went out until she spoke to them, and the people shout and rejoice. Achior bows at her feet and blesses her, and Bagoas later finds her gone from her lodging and the Assyrians blame her for the shame on Nebuchadnezzar's house.

Chapter 15

Judith is visited by Joakim the high priest and the elders from Jerusalem, who bless her as the exaltation of Jerusalem, the glory of Israel, and the rejoicing of her race, saying she did these things by her hand. She is given Holofernes' tent, silver cups, beds, bowls, and furniture, which she loads on her mule and wagons. She distributes branches to the women and leads all the women in the dance, wearing an olive garland, with the men following.

Chapter 16

Judith begins and leads a song of thanksgiving in which she credits the Lord with using her beauty to weaken the enemy's mighty one. She dedicates Holofernes' belongings and the canopy to the Lord, returns to Bethulia, and is honored, though no man knew her after her husband Manasses died. She frees her maid, distributes her goods to kin, dies at 105, and is buried in Manasses' cave.
