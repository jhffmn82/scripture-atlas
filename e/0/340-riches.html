Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, wealth cannot buy life or redemption, is left to others at death, and cannot be carried away. In Proverbs, wealth and riches ransom a man's life yet deceive, dishonest gain dwindles while steady gathering grows, riches profit nothing in wrath, and a sinner's wealth is stored for the righteous. In Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher, riches and wealth are vanity: love of silver never satisfies, goods multiply consumers, hoarded wealth perishes, and the dead carry nothing away. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, riches and poverty come from the Lord, wealth may be gained through diligence and self-denial but left to others at death, and the Lord can swiftly make the poor rich. In The Letter from James, the riches of oppressors are corrupted, their garments are moth-eaten, and their gold and silver corrode to testify against them and consume their flesh like fire.

Love of silver and abundance is never satisfied and is called vanity. As goods increase, those who eat them increase, and the owner gains only the sight of them. Wealth kept to its owner's harm perishes by misfortune, and nothing can be carried away at death.
