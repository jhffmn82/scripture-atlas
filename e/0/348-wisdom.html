wisdom

topic · 4 works · 12 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: counsel, people who take advice, prudence, strength and wisdom, the place of understanding, the wisdom of old men, the wise, understanding, wisdom and might, wisdom and understanding, wise, wise men

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Job, wisdom and understanding are tied to aged men but belong truly to God, who removes understanding from elders and chiefs while Job claims equal understanding to his friends. In The Book of Job, wisdom is described as hidden from all living creatures, beyond the value of precious gems, known only to God, and finally declared to be the fear of the Lord. In Psalms, wisdom is said to begin with the fear of the LORD, and those who do his work are said to have good understanding. In Proverbs, wisdom is described as the wise's life-giving teaching, gained by walking with the wise and taking advice. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, wisdom is called beautiful in elderly people, and the one who finds it is called great, though no one is greater than the one who fears the Lord.

wisdom and understanding in The Book of Job

Abstract written by a model

Topic of wisdom and understanding, tied to aged men but truly belonging to God. In Job 12, Job says God removes understanding from elders and chiefs, leaving them wandering, while Job claims equal understanding to his friends.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 12

Job says that wisdom is with the aged and understanding with length of days, but that wisdom and might truly are with God, who also has counsel and understanding. God takes understanding away from elders, trusted speakers, and the chiefs of the people of the earth, leaving them wandering without a way. Job claims to have understanding as well as his friends.

wisdom in The Book of Job

author unknown · about 574 BC · the work’s page · read it where wisdom first appears

Abstract written by a model

Wisdom is a topic in Chapter 28, described as something that cannot be found in the land of the living, the deep, or the sea. It is hidden from all living creatures, including birds of prey and man. Its price exceeds gold, silver, rubies and other gems, and only God knows its place. It is finally declared to be the fear of the Lord.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 28

Wisdom is asked about repeatedly as something that cannot be found in the land of the living, in the deep, or in the sea, and it is hidden from all living creatures. Its price is beyond gold, silver, onyx, sapphire, coral, crystal, rubies, and topaz, and only God knows its place. It is finally declared to be the fear of the Lord.
