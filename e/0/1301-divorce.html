divorce

topic · 3 works · 6 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: a writing of divorce, certificate of divorce, divorced, divorces, divorces her husband, divorces his wife, put away his wife

Abstract written by a model

In Deuteronomy, divorce requires writing a certificate, placing it in the wife's hand, and sending her from the house; it frees her to remarry, but she may not return to her first husband after a second marriage ends. In The Gospel According to Matthew, divorce is permitted only for sexual immorality; divorcing a wife otherwise makes her an adulteress, and marrying a divorced woman constitutes adultery. In The Gospel According to Matthew, divorce is a contested practice that Moses allowed due to hardened hearts, though it was not intended from the beginning, and Jesus permits it only for sexual immorality. In The Good News According to Mark, divorce is allowed by Moses because of hardness of heart, but Jesus teaches that marriage makes two people one flesh and that divorcing and remarrying constitutes adultery.

divorce in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where divorce first appears

Abstract written by a model

Divorce is a contested practice: Moses allowed it due to hardened hearts, though not intended from the beginning. Jesus permits it only for sexual immorality; otherwise divorcing and remarrying, or marrying a divorced woman, is adultery.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 19

The Pharisees ask whether divorce is lawful for any reason. Jesus says Moses allowed it because of hardness of hearts, but it was not so from the beginning. He states that whoever divorces his wife except for sexual immorality and marries another commits adultery, and so does the man who marries a divorced woman.
