Ezekiel in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · about 592 to 570 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ezekiel first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ezekiel is a priest, son of Buzi, living among the captives by the river Chebar in the land of the Chaldeans (at Tel Aviv), in the fifth year of King Jehoiachin's captivity. He is called by the LORD through visions of God, and is addressed throughout as 'son of man'. He is sent as prophet and appointed watchman to the rebellious house of Israel, to speak the LORD's words whether they listen or not. The records follow his career from call to ongoing commission. In the opening chapters he sees the glory of the LORD, is raised by the Spirit, eats a scroll, and has his forehead made hard like a diamond so he can face a hostile people. He sits overwhelmed for seven days among the captives, and is warned he will be made mute until the LORD opens his mouth. He then enacts signs: portraying Jerusalem's siege on a tile, lying 390 days on his left side and 40 on his right to bear the iniquity of Israel and Judah, eating rationed bread and water, and, after protesting he has never been unclean, being allowed cow's dung for baking. He shaves his hair and beard with a sword and divides it to symbolize Jerusalem's fate, and is told to prophesy against the mountains of Israel. Later, seated with the elders of Judah, he is lifted by the Spirit in visions to Jerusalem, shown abominations, and sees the twenty-five men at the east gate; when Pelatiah dies he cries out for the remnant, then is carried back to the captives and tells them all. He is called a sign to the house of Israel, acting out exile by bringing out baggage and digging through a wall. He prophesies against false prophets, including prophesying women, receives elders who come to him, judges the elders and the bloody city, and sighs and wails over the coming sword of the king of Babylon. People mock him as a speaker of parables. A pivotal moment comes when his wife dies in the evening and, at the LORD's command, he does not mourn, again becoming a sign; his muteness will end when the escapee comes. Finally he laments over Tyre and prophesies against Pharaoh and Egypt, with a promise that his mouth will be opened among the house of Israel.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

17 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

Ezekiel is identified as the priest, son of Buzi, among the captives by the river Chebar in the land of the Chaldeans, in the fifth year of King Jehoiachin's captivity. The LORD's word comes to him and the LORD's hand is on him, and he sees visions of God. After seeing the likeness of the LORD's glory he falls on his face and hears a voice that speaks.

Chapter 2

The son of man is told to stand, and the Spirit enters him and sets him on his feet so he hears the speaker. He is sent to the children of Israel to speak the words of the Lord GOD whether they listen or not, and is commanded not to fear them, not to be rebellious, and to open his mouth and eat what is given. He sees a hand holding a scroll, which is spread before him.

Chapter 3

Ezekiel is told to eat the scroll, which he does, and is commanded to speak the LORD's words to the house of Israel. His forehead is made hard like a diamond, the Spirit carries him in bitterness to the captives at Tel Aviv by the river Chebar, and he sits overwhelmed for seven days. He is then made a watchman, told to shut himself in his house, and warned that he will be bound and made mute until the LORD opens his mouth.

Chapter 4

Addressed as 'son of man', he is commanded to portray Jerusalem on a tile, enact a siege, lie 390 days on his left side and 40 days on his right to bear the iniquity of Israel and Judah, and be bound with ropes. He is to eat rationed mixed-grain bread and drink measured water. When told to bake it over human dung, he protests that he has never eaten anything unclean, and the LORD allows cow's dung instead.

Chapter 5

The son of man is commanded to take a sharp sword like a barber's razor, shave his head and beard, weigh the hair on balances, and divide it into three parts. He is to burn one third, strike another around with the sword, scatter the last to the wind, and bind a few hairs in his robe, burning some of those too. His actions symbolically enact the fate of Jerusalem.

Chapter 6

The son of man is told to set his face toward the mountains of Israel and prophesy to them the word of the Lord GOD. He is also told to strike with his hand, stamp with his foot, and say "Alas!" because of the abominations of the house of Israel.

Chapter 8

Ezekiel sits in his house with the elders of Judah when the Lord GOD's hand falls on him, and he is lifted by a lock of his head in visions of God to Jerusalem. He is told to look, dig through a wall, and enter, and is repeatedly asked 'do you see?' as he witnesses successive abominations.

Chapter 11

Ezekiel is lifted by the Spirit to the east gate of the LORD's house, where he sees the twenty-five men and is told to prophesy against them. When Pelatiah dies, he falls on his face and cries out asking whether the LORD will make a full end of the remnant of Israel. The Spirit then carries him in the vision to the captives in Chaldea, and he tells them all that the LORD showed him.

Chapter 12

Ezekiel, addressed as son of man, is told he dwells among a rebellious house that sees and hears but does not perceive. He obeys the commands to bring out his baggage by day, dig through the wall in the evening, and carry it on his shoulder in the dark. He is also told to eat and drink with trembling, and to deliver the LORD's messages about the exile, the desolation, and the proverb. He is called a sign to the house of Israel.

Chapter 13

Ezekiel, addressed as son of man, receives the LORD's word and is told to prophesy against the prophets of Israel who speak out of their own heart. He is also told to set his face against the women who prophesy from their own heart and to deliver the LORD's message of judgment against them.

Chapter 14

Addressed as 'son of man', he is visited by elders of Israel and receives two words from the LORD, one about idolatrous inquirers and one about the land's judgment. He is told that the remnant carried out will come to him, and that he will see their ways and be comforted.

Chapter 20

The son of man is commanded to speak to the elders, judge them by making known their fathers' abominations, and prophesy to the house of Israel. He is also told to set his face toward the south and prophesy against its forest. At the end he cries "Ah Lord GOD!" because people say he is a speaker of parables.

Chapter 21

Ezekiel, addressed as son of man, receives several word-of-the-LORD commands in this unit. He is to set his face toward Jerusalem and prophesy against Israel, sigh bitterly before the people, cry and wail, and beat his thigh and strike his hands together. He is also to mark out two ways for the sword of the king of Babylon and to prophesy against the children of Ammon.

Chapter 22

Addressed as son of man, Ezekiel receives the LORD's word three times. He is asked to judge the bloody city and make known her abominations, to hear that Israel has become dross, and to tell the land that it is not cleansed nor rained on in the day of indignation.

Chapter 24

Ezekiel is told to write down the day and to speak the cauldron parable to the rebellious house. He is told that the desire of his eyes will be taken and that he must not mourn aloud, weep, or eat mourner's bread, and he obeys after his wife dies in the evening. He explains the meaning to the people and is told he is a sign to them, and that his mouth will be opened to the survivor so he is no longer mute.

Chapter 27

Ezekiel, addressed as 'son of man,' is commanded by the LORD to take up a lamentation over Tyre and to deliver the Lord GOD's message to the city.

Chapter 29

Ezekiel, addressed as son of man, is told to set his face against Pharaoh and prophesy against him and all Egypt. He receives two words from the LORD, and is promised that his mouth will be opened among the house of Israel.

Ezekiel (son of man) in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · about 592 to 570 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ezekiel (son of man) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ezekiel, repeatedly addressed by the LORD as "son of man," is a prophet and watchman set over the house of Israel. The records show him receiving the LORD's word and being commissioned to prophesy, with an earlier vision at the river Chebar. His role is to speak warnings, or else bear responsibility for the blood of the wicked. After a fugitive brings news of Jerusalem's defeat, his mouth is opened and he is no longer mute. The people flock to hear him as to a pleasant song but do not obey, and when the prophecy comes to pass they will know a prophet has been among them. He is then carried by the Spirit into the valley of bones, answers 'Lord GOD, you know,' and prophesies to the bones and the wind as commanded. He is told to write on two sticks and join them into one, explaining the meaning to the people. He is also commanded to set his face toward Gog and prophesy against him, twice, including that the LORD will bring Gog against Israel. In the twenty-fifth year of the captivity, he is brought in visions of God to a very high mountain in the land of Israel. He is told to see, hear, and set his heart on all that is shown, and to declare it to Israel. A guide leads him through the outer court, north rooms, and measurements of the area. He is brought to the east gate, sees the glory approach and fill the house, and falls on his face. The Spirit lifts him into the inner court, where he is commanded to show the house, its form and laws, to Israel, writing them in the people's sight, and to mark all ordinances for the rebellious house. The sequence ends with him led through the river at ankle, knee, and waist depth until it cannot be crossed, then seeing the many trees and hearing the healing effect explained.

The people flock to hear him as to a pleasant song but do not obey, and when the prophecy comes to pass they will know a prophet has been among them.

Chapter 37

Addressed as son of man, he is carried by the LORD's Spirit into the valley of bones, answers 'Lord GOD, you know' when asked if the bones can live, and prophesies to the bones and to the wind as commanded. He is then told to write on two sticks and join them into one in his hand, and to explain this to the people who ask what it means.

Chapter 38

Ezekiel, addressed as son of man, receives the LORD's word and is told to set his face toward Gog and prophesy against him. He is commanded to deliver the message twice, including telling Gog that the LORD will bring him against Israel.

Chapter 40

In the twenty-fifth year of the captivity, the LORD's hand brings him in visions of God to a very high mountain in the land of Israel. He is told to see with his eyes, hear with his ears, and set his heart on all that is shown, and to declare it all to the house of Israel.

Chapter 42

He is brought by the guide into the outer court and the north rooms, where he observes the galleries, doors, and dimensions. He is told by the guide what the rooms are for and what the priests must do there. He then accompanies the guide as the whole area is measured.

Chapter 43

The narrator is brought to the east gate, sees the glory approach, and falls on his face. The Spirit lifts him into the inner court, where he hears a voice from the house while a man stands beside him. He is commanded to show the house to Israel, make known its form and laws, and write them in the people's sight.

Chapter 44

The speaker is brought by the east gate and then the north gate, where he sees the LORD's glory fill the house and falls on his face. He is addressed as 'Son of man' and told to mark well, see, and hear all the ordinances and laws, and to tell the rebellious house of Israel the LORD's words.

Chapter 47

Addressed as "Son of man," Ezekiel is brought back to the temple door and led through the waters at ankle, knee, and waist depth until the river is too deep to cross. He is then returned to the bank, where he sees the many trees, and hears the man explain the river's healing effect.
