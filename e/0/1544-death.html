death

Also called: Hades, If a man dies, will he live again?, The last enemy, a shameful death, comes to his end, death and the hope of afterlife, destruction, dies, my release, our end

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Job, death is portrayed as final, though the speaker asks whether a man who dies will live again and says he would wait for his release if so. In The Wisdom of Solomon, death is not made by God but is summoned and befriended by the ungodly, while God made man for incorruption and death entered through the devil's envy. In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, death entered through man, is empowered by sin and the law, and is abolished as the last enemy when the perishable becomes imperishable.

death in The Wisdom of Solomon

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where death first appears

Abstract written by a model

Death is presented as not made by God, who delights not in the perishing of the living; Hades holds no royal dominion on earth. The ungodly summon death, befriend it, and treat it as a final, irreversible end. The narrator counters that God made man for incorruption, and death entered through the devil's envy, afflicting his own.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

Death was not made by God, and God does not delight when the living perish. Hades has no royal dominion upon earth. Ungodly men summon death with their hands and words, regard him as a friend, and make a covenant with him.

Chapter 2

The ungodly view death as a final, irreversible end, sealed so that no one turns it back, after which the person is as though never born. The narrator counters that God made man for incorruption and that death entered the world through the devil's envy, and is experienced by those who belong to the devil.

Abstract written by a model
