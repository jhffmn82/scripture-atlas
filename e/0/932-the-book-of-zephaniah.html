The Book of Zephaniah

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

The Book of Zephaniah, in three chapters, records a prophetic message from the LORD to Zephaniah in the days of Josiah, king of Judah. Its central figures are the LORD, who judges and then restores, Judah and Jerusalem, the surrounding nations, and the humble remnant. Chapter 1 announces sweeping judgment: the LORD will clear everything from the earth, cut off Baal worship and those who turned from him, and punish princes, the king's sons, the violent, and the complacent in Jerusalem. The day of the LORD is portrayed as wrath, darkness, and alarm, when silver and gold cannot save anyone and the land is consumed by the fire of his jealousy. Chapter 2 urges the humble to seek the LORD, righteousness, and humility before his anger arrives. It then pronounces destruction on the Philistine cities, Moab and Ammon, the Cushites, and Assyria, whose carefree capital Nineveh, claiming 'I am, and there is no one besides me,' becomes a desolation. Judah's remnant will inherit the conquered lands. Chapter 3 denounces the rebellious, polluted city and its corrupt princes, judges, prophets, and priests, though the LORD within her is righteous. After he gathers the nations in indignation, he will purify the peoples' lips, remove the proud, and leave a humble remnant living without lies or fear. The book ends with the daughter of Zion told to sing, because the LORD saves, rejoices over her, gathers the scattered, and restores their fortunes with honor and praise.

The Book of Zephaniah / Chapter 1

The word of the LORD comes to Zephaniah in the days of Josiah, king of Judah. The LORD announces he will sweep away everything from the earth, including man, animal, birds and fish, and will stretch out his hand against Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, cutting off the remnant of Baal and those who worship the sky's army, swear by Malcam, or have turned from him. He declares that the day of the LORD is at hand, with a sacrifice prepared, and that he will punish princes, the king's sons, and the violent and deceitful. He will search Jerusalem with lamps to punish those complacent on their dregs, whose wealth will be plundered. The great day of the LORD is described as a day of wrath, darkness, and alarm, when sinful men will be unable to be saved by silver or gold and the whole land will be devoured by the fire of his jealousy.

Chapter 2

Chapter 2 opens with a call for the shameless nation to gather and for the humble of the land to seek the LORD, righteousness, and humility before the day of the LORD's anger arrives. The LORD then announces destruction on the Philistine cities of Gaza, Ashkelon, Ashdod, and Ekron, whose coast will become pasture for the remnant of the house of Judah. Moab and the children of Ammon will become like Sodom and Gomorrah for reproaching and magnifying themselves against the LORD's people, whose remnant will plunder and inherit them. The LORD will also strike the Cushites and stretch out his hand against Assyria, making Nineveh a desolation inhabited by animals. Nineveh is portrayed as the carefree city that claimed 'I am, and there is no one besides me,' and will now be mocked by all who pass.

Chapter 3

Chapter 3 opens with a woe against the rebellious, polluted, oppressing city, whose princes, judges, prophets, and priests are corrupt even though the LORD within her is righteous and has shown her the fate of other nations. The LORD calls on those who wait for him to expect the day when he gathers the nations to pour out his indignation and the fire of his jealousy. After that he will purify the peoples' lips, bring offerings from beyond Cush's rivers, remove the proud, and leave an afflicted and poor remnant of Israel who take refuge in his name and live without lies or fear. The daughter of Zion is told to sing and rejoice because the LORD, a mighty one who saves, is among her, has removed her enemy, and will rejoice over her with singing. He promises to deal with her afflictors, save the lame, gather the driven away, and give them honor and praise when he restores their fortunes.
