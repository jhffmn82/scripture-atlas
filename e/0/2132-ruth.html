Abstract written by a model

Ruth is a Moabite woman, first the wife of Mahlon and daughter-in-law of Naomi, who becomes known for extraordinary loyalty. After her husband's death she vows to stay with Naomi until death, adopting Naomi's people and God, and leaves her parents and homeland to return with her to Bethlehem at the start of barley harvest. A foreigner in Israel, she takes refuge under the LORD's wings. She gleans in Boaz's field, calling herself his servant, stays close to his maidens through barley and wheat harvests, and lives with Naomi. Boaz blesses her, and the city knows her as a worthy woman who does not pursue young men, poor or rich. At Naomi's direction she goes to the threshing floor and asks Boaz, a near kinsman, to spread his garment over her. He purchases her along with the field to raise up the dead man's name and takes her as wife. The LORD enables her to conceive, and she bears Obed. The women say she loves Naomi and is better than seven sons.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

Ruth, a Moabite, married one of Naomi's sons and lived in Moab about ten years. She refused to leave Naomi, vowing that Naomi's people would be her people and Naomi's God her God, and that only death would part them. She came with Naomi to Bethlehem at the beginning of barley harvest.

Chapter 2

Ruth asks Naomi's leave to glean, goes to the field, and happens upon Boaz's portion. She bows before Boaz in gratitude, is praised for leaving her homeland to care for Naomi, eats with the reapers, and gleans about an ephah of barley. She brings it home to Naomi, stays close to Boaz's maidens through the barley and wheat harvests, and lives with Naomi.

Chapter 3

Ruth agrees to do all Naomi says and goes down to the threshing floor, where she uncovers Boaz's feet and lies down. At midnight she identifies herself as his servant and asks him to spread his garment over her because he is a near kinsman. Boaz praises her kindness and worth, she stays until morning and leaves before she can be recognized, carrying six measures of barley. She reports everything to Naomi.

Chapter 4

Ruth the Moabitess, wife of Mahlon, is named by Boaz as something that must be bought along with the field to raise up the name of the dead. Boaz purchases her to be his wife, the LORD enables her to conceive, and she bears a son. The women say she loves Naomi and is better to her than seven sons.
