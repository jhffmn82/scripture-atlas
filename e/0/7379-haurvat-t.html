Haurvatât

deity · 2 works · 12 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Haurvatât, the Amesha-Spenta, Haurvatât, the master, Weal, blessedness, our Weal, weal

Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, Haurvatât is Weal, guarding Mazda-made waters, offered propitiation, and requested from Mazda. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, Haurvatât is an Amesha-Spenta and master of prosperity and holiness, providing help, comforts, and pleasures for the faithful, receiving offerings and worship, and confessing Mithra's religion.

Haurvatât in Yashts (Yasts) I-XII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Haurvatât first appears

Abstract written by a model

Haurvatât is an Amesha-Spenta and master of prosperity and holiness, a reward for holy ones freed from their bodies. It provides help, comforts, and pleasures for the faithful, receives extensive offerings and worship, and confesses Mithra's religion.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

IV. Khordâd Yast.

Haurvatât is an Amesha-Spenta and master who provides help, enjoyments, comforts, and pleasures for the faithful. Sacrifice, prayer, propitiation, glorification, libations, Haoma, meat, baresma, wisdom of the tongue, holy spells, speech, deeds, and rightly-spoken words are offered unto Haurvatât.
