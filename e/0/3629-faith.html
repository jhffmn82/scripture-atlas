Abstract written by a model

In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, faith is the means by which righteousness is accounted to those who believe, justifies believers, gives access into grace, and brings peace with God through Jesus Christ. In The Letter to the Hebrews, faith is the basis for drawing near to God in fullness, living righteously, and attaining the saving of the soul rather than shrinking back to destruction. In The Letter to the Hebrews, faith is defined as assurance of things hoped for and proof of things not seen, and is illustrated through examples from Abel, Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Rahab. In 1 John, faith is named as the victory that has overcome the world, belonging to the one who believes that Jesus is the Son of God. In Jude, the faith is described as once for all delivered to the saints, for which recipients are urged to contend earnestly and upon which they are to build themselves up. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), faith consists of believing in Allah and in what was revealed to Muhammad, Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, the tribes, Moses, Jesus, and the prophets, while turning away leads to schism. In Surah 3: The Family of Imraan (Aal-i-Imraan), belief and disbelief are contrasted through faith in Allah and His revelations, obedience and steadfastness, and the rewards or losses that follow belief or disbelief. In Surah 10: Jonas (Yunus), faith is linked with belief in Allah, good works, trust in Him, and surrender to Him, and Allah guides by faith by His permission.

In Surah 13: The Thunder (Ar-Ra'd), faith is associated with recognizing revelation as truth, remembering Allah, finding rest of heart, and doing right. In Surah 20: Taa-Haa, faith is shown by the magicians who believe in the Lord of Aaron and Moses and by those who come to Allah as believers, who receive high stations for good works. In Surah 21: The Prophets (Al-Anbiyaa), faith is represented by believers whose good works are not rejected and who are saved from anguish and share in Allah’s mercy. In Surah 26: The Poets (Ash-Shu'araa), faith is affirmed by the wizards, the believers, and those who trust Allah, while most of the peoples described reject it despite witnessing signs. In Surah 40: The Forgiver (Al-Ghaafir), faith in Allah is refused by disbelievers, followed by believers, hidden by the believing man of Pharaoh’s family, and unable to benefit disbelievers when they see Allah’s doom. In Surah 49: The Inner Apartments (Al-Hujuraat), faith is described as something Allah endears and beautifies in believers’ hearts, requiring belief in Allah and His messenger without doubt and striving with wealth and lives for Allah’s cause. In Surah 58: The Pleading Woman (Al-Mujaadila), faith is written upon the hearts of those who do not love people opposing Allah and His messenger, and Allah strengthens them with a Spirit from Him. In Surah 59: The Exile (Al-Hashr), faith is shared by those who entered the city before the fugitives and by later believers, who ask Allah to forgive their brethren and remove rancour from their hearts. In Surah 60: She that is to be examined (Al-Mumtahana), faith is examined in believing women who come as fugitives, and believers are told to act in accordance with belief in Allah.

faith in Paul’s Letter to the Romans

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where faith first appears

Abstract written by a model

Faith is the means by which righteousness is accounted to those who believe rather than work. In Chapter 4, Abraham's unweakened faith was counted as righteousness, and because the promise rests on faith, it accords with grace and is sure to all his offspring. In Chapter 5, faith justifies believers, gives access into grace, and brings peace with God through Jesus Christ.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Faith is what was accounted to Abraham for righteousness, and it is likewise accounted as righteousness to the one who does not work but believes in him who justifies the ungodly. Because the promise rests on faith, it is according to grace and sure to all Abraham's offspring. Abraham's faith did not weaken, and he grew strong through faith in the face of his own body's deadness and Sarah's womb.

Chapter 5

Faith is the means by which believers are justified and through which they have access into the grace in which they stand. It leads to peace with God through Jesus Christ.

Faith is defined as assurance of things hoped for and proof of things not seen, and through it the elders obtained approval. Without it, it is impossible to be well pleasing to God.
