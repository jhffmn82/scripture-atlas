Levi

person · 3 works · 13 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: children of Levi, tribe of Levi

Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Levi, son of Jacob and brother of Dinah and Simeon, kills the males of a city with Simeon to avenge Dinah, and Jacob curses their cruel wrath and pronounces that Levi will be divided and scattered in Israel. In 1 Chronicles, Levi is the father of Gershom, Kohath and Merari, with a troop count of 4600; the singers Heman, Asaph and Ethan trace their genealogies back to him. In The Book of Malachi, Levi is depicted as an ideal priest who held a covenant of life and peace with the LORD, revered his name, spoke truth, walked with the LORD in peace and uprightness, and turned many from iniquity.

Levi in Genesis

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Levi first appears

Abstract written by a model

Levi, son of Jacob and brother of Dinah and Simeon, is violent. With Simeon he kills the males of a city, including Hamor and Shechem, to avenge Dinah, defying Jacob's rebuke. Later Jacob curses their cruel wrath and pronounces that Levi will be divided and scattered in Israel.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 34

Levi, a brother of Dinah, takes his sword with Simeon and kills all the males of the city, including Hamor and Shechem. With Simeon he is rebuked by Jacob and answers by asking whether their sister should be treated as a prostitute.

Chapter 49

Jacob names Levi as a brother of Simeon, with swords that are weapons of violence. He says they killed men in anger and hamstrung cattle in self-will, curses their cruel wrath, and says he will divide and scatter them in Israel.

Levi in The Book of Malachi

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Levi first appears

Abstract written by a model

Levi is depicted as an ideal priest who held a covenant of life and peace with the LORD, revered and stood in awe of his name, spoke truth without unrighteousness, walked with the LORD in peace and uprightness, turned many from iniquity, and models a priest whose lips keep knowledge.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Levi had a covenant of life and peace with the LORD, was reverent, and stood in awe of his name. The law of truth was in his mouth and no unrighteousness was in his lips; he walked with the LORD in peace and uprightness and turned many away from iniquity. He serves as the model of a priest whose lips keep knowledge.
