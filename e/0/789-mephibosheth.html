Abstract written by a model

Mephibosheth is the lame-footed son of Jonathan and grandson of Saul, father of a young son, Mica. Living in Lo Debar at Machir's house, he is summoned by David, bows as a 'dead dog', and is promised Saul's land and a permanent place at the king's table in Jerusalem. Later, Ziba reports he is staying in Jerusalem hoping Israel will restore his father's kingdom, so David gives Ziba all his property. When Mephibosheth meets David, unkempt in mourning, he says Ziba deceived and slandered him. David orders the land divided; Mephibosheth says Ziba may take all, since the king has returned in peace.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

Mephibosheth, son of Jonathan and lame in both feet, is living in Lo Debar in the house of Machir when David sends for him. He falls on his face before David, calls himself a dead dog, receives the promise of Saul's land and a place at the king's table, and then lives in Jerusalem eating there like one of the king's sons. He has a young son named Mica.

Chapter 16

Mephibosheth is reported by Ziba to be staying in Jerusalem, saying Israel will restore the kingdom of his father. On the strength of this report, David gives all that belonged to him to Ziba.

Chapter 19

Mephibosheth comes to meet David, having not groomed his feet, trimmed his beard, or washed his clothes since the king left. He says his servant deceived him and slandered him, and David tells him and Ziba to divide the land; Mephibosheth replies that Ziba may take all, since the king has returned in peace.
