Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Job, man is portrayed as frail, mortal, impure, capable of remarkable skill, yet lacking wisdom apart from fearing the Lord and turning from evil. In Psalms, the children of men are examined by the Lord and found to have gone aside, become corrupt, and lack anyone doing good. In Psalms, man is the subject of the psalmist's wonder, made slightly lower than angels, crowned with glory and honor, and given rule over the Lord's works. In Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher, the sons of men are mortal creatures made from dust who share the animals' fate, bear misery, lack power and knowledge, cannot discover God's work, and are urged to enjoy life and labor. In Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher, man is portrayed as frail, limited, vain, sinful, ignorant, mortal, and bound to fear God and keep his commandments. In The Wisdom of Solomon, man is formed by God's wisdom to rule the world in holiness and righteousness, yet has unstable thoughts, fragile plans, and a corruptible body, while wisdom teaches and saves him. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the sons of Adam bear a heavy yoke and great travail from birth to burial, troubled by fear of the future and death. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, man is made by the Lord with free choice and may choose life or death, while the Lord knows every act and commands no one to sin.

man in The Book of Job

author unknown · about 574 BC · the work’s page · read it where man first appears

Abstract written by a model

Man is portrayed as a frail, mortal creature: born of woman, living few days full of trouble, pain and mourning, like a flower cut down or a fleeting shadow. God fixes his days and months within bounds he cannot pass. He is unclean and cannot be righteous before God, a worm and son of man, less pure than the moon and stars. At death he lies down and does not rise, unaware of his sons' honor or lowliness. Yet he is capable of remarkable skill: mining ore, gems and metals, breaking shafts, overturning mountains and bringing hidden things to light. Still, he does not know wisdom's price, which is not found among the living. The account ends with God's answer that fear of the Lord is wisdom and turning from evil is understanding.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

Man is described as born of a woman, of few days, full of trouble, and like a flower cut down or a shadow that does not last. His days and months are determined by God, who has set bounds he cannot pass. When he dies he lies down and does not rise, and in death he does not know whether his sons are honored or brought low.

Chapter 25

Man is described as born of a woman and as a worm, the son of man. He is judged far less bright and pure than the moon and stars, which themselves fall short in God's sight.

Chapter 28

Man is shown as able to mine metals and gems, break open shafts, overturn mountains, and bring hidden things to light. Yet he does not know wisdom's price, and it is not found in the land of the living. God tells him that the fear of the Lord is wisdom and that departing from evil is understanding.

Abstract written by a model

A man responds to failed attachment, rule, or politeness by examining his own benevolence, wisdom, and feeling of respect. He has a correct person, studies to remain in harmony with the ordinances of God, and obtains much happiness.
