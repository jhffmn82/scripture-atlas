Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This collection of Yasts and related blessings presents Ahura Mazda’s teaching to Zarathustra and the worship of powerful holy beings, especially the Fravashis of the faithful. The Fravashis sustain creation, protect families, towns, rulers, waters, plants, animals, and unborn children, defeat Daêvas and enemies, and bless those who honor them with sacrifice, prayer, food, clothing, and devotion. The texts praise the good Law of the worshippers of Mazda, holiness, good thoughts, words, and deeds, and figures including Gaya Maretan, Zarathustra, Vîstâspa, Haoshyangha, Yima, Thraêtaona, Keresâspa, and many holy men and women. Other units invoke Verethraghna, Vayu, Kista, Ashi Vanguhi, the Aryan Glory, kingly Glory, and the star Vanant for victory, health, wealth, strength, wisdom, prosperity, protection, and healing. Verethraghna takes many forms and supplies battle protection; Vayu grants boons while rejecting Azi Dahâka’s destructive request; Ashi Vanguhi gives glory and prosperity but condemns sexual and reproductive wrongdoing. The Glory moves among mountains, rulers, heroes, Zarathustra, and the Aryan nations, supporting Vîstâspa’s advance of the Law and defeat of enemies. The collection repeatedly opposes Angra Mainyu, the Drug, Daêvas, Yâtus, Khrafstras, and wicked people. It looks ahead to Saoshyant and Astvat-ereta, who will raise and restore the bodily world: death, decay, hunger, thirst, the Drug, and Angra Mainyu will perish, and living creatures will reach immortality. Its closing teachings concern worship and moral consequence. Praise of Holiness is the glorification of all Mazda’s good creations, especially when joined to professions of good thoughts, words, and deeds and rejection of evil. After death, faithful souls pass through paradises to Endless Lights, while wicked souls descend through hells to Endless Darkness. Zarathustra’s blessings for Vîstâspa and his teachings to the young king culminate in prosperity, righteous rule, charity, proper worship, the defeat of evil, and entry into Garô-nmâna, the bright abode of the holy Ones.

XIII. Farvardîn Yast.

Ahura Mazda teaches Spitama Zarathustra about the power, glory, and assistance of the Fravashis of the faithful. The Fravashis sustain the sky, waters, earth, plants, animals, and unborn children, and help the faithful overcome the Daêvas and other enemies. They grant victory, health, prosperity, and protection to those who honor them through sacrifice, prayer, and devotion. During the Hamaspathmaêdha, they visit settlements seeking worship, offerings, food, and clothing, and bless those who receive them properly.

XXII.

The unit concerns the Fravashis of the faithful, who are worshipped as powerful beings that aid their kindred, towns, and countries. They seek and bring waters, fight for their own lands, and protect rulers who have honored them. They defend against Angra Mainyu and other dangers, while waters and plants continue to flow and grow through the action of Ahura Mazda. Ahura Mazda’s Fravashi is praised as supreme in holiness and excellence.

XXIII.

The unit worships the good, strong, beneficent Fravashis, including those of the Amesha-Spentas, divine beings, elements, plants, animals, human figures, and holy creations. It honors Gaya Maretan as the first listener to Ahura Mazda’s thought and teaching, and Zarathustra as the first teacher and bearer of the Law. Zarathustra’s birth and growth are described as bringing joy to waters, plants, and the good creations, while the good Law of the worshippers of Mazda spreads through the seven Karshvares. The unit also praises Vîstâspa for driving back the Drug and supporting the law of Ahura and the law of Zarathustra, while describing the Daêvas as defeated and unworthy of sacrifice and prayer.

XXVI.

This unit worships the Fravashis of many named holy men, women, kings, heroes, and peoples, and concludes with invocations of the Fravashis of the faithful and their ancestors. It concerns SAOSHYANT and ASTVAT-ERETA, whose future work will benefit and raise up the bodily world and withstand the Drug and evil. It praises Ahura Mazda, Sraosha, Zarathustra, the law, holiness, sacrifice, prayer, and the blessed destinies of holy men and women. It closes with blessings for houses, worshippers, the Fravashis, and the good powers associated with Ahura Mazda and the Amesha-Spentas.

XIV. Bahrâm Yast.

The unit concerns Verethraghna, made by Ahura, whom Ahura Mazda identifies as the best-armed of the heavenly gods. Verethraghna appears successively as a wind, bull, horse, camel, boar, youth, raven, ram, buck, and armed man, bearing Glory, Strength, and Victory. Zarathustra learns invocations, sacrifices, protective rites, and remedies involving Verethraghna, including the use of a Vârengana feather against curses. The text promises protection and victory in battle to those who properly offer sacrifice and prayer, while condemning Daêvas, their worshippers, and improper recipients of offerings.

XV. Râm Yast.

This unit concerns Vayu, whom Ahura Mazda, Haoshyangha, Takhma Urupa, Yima, Azi Dahâka, Thraêtaona, Keresâspa, Aurvasâra, Hutaosa, and unnamed maids worship through sacrifice and invocation. Vayu grants boons to several worshippers, including victory, protection, glory, prosperity, love, and husbands, but refuses Azi Dahâka’s request to empty the earth of men. Vayu describes many names and powers of himself to Zarathustra and instructs him how to worship, promising health and protection from Angra Mainyu, the Yâtus, Daêvas, and hostile men. The unit repeatedly asks for Vayu’s brightness and glory and concludes with blessings for those who worship him.

XVI. Dîn Yast.

This unit of the Dîn Yast concerns the most right Kista, made by Mazda and holy, and the good Law of the worshippers of Mazda. Zarathustra, Hvôvi, the Âthravans, and the king of the country are described as making sacrifices to Kista for righteousness, lawful conduct, memory, strength, peace, and other benefits. Kista is said to grant bodily strength, health, speed, hearing, and extraordinary sight. The unit ends by blessing Kista and the good Law and asking for brightness, glory, long life, and the blissful abode of the holy Ones.

XVII. Ashi Yast.

This unit concerns Ashi Vanguhi, who is worshipped as a powerful giver of glory, prosperity, health, wisdom, and victory. She grants boons to Haoshyangha, Yima Khshaêta, Thraêtaona, Haoma, Husravah, Zarathustra, and Kavi Vîstâspa. The unit also describes her family, her praise of Zarathustra, her rejection of certain libations, and her three wailings about sexual and reproductive wrongdoing. It concludes with prayers for the strength, glory, health, and blessed abode associated with Ashi Vanguhi.

XVIII. Âstâd Yast.

Âstâd Yast recounts Ahura Mazda's teaching to Spitama Zarathustra about the Aryan Glory and its powers. The Aryan Glory brings food, flocks, wealth, intelligence, money, victory, and protection against enemies and destructive fiends. Ashi Vanguhi enters the families and royal palaces of those who gladden the faithful, while Tistrya, the strong wind made by Mazda, and the Glory of the Aryas bring increase throughout the land. The unit worships holiness, the law of holiness, the healing Words, and the Glory of the Aryas.

XIX. Zamyâd Yast.

The unit praises the awful kingly Glory made by Mazda and recounts its power, movements, and attachment to mountains, rulers, Zarathustra, and the Aryan nations. It names the first mountains and many other heights, including Mount Haraiti Barez and Mount Ushi-dhau Ushi-darena. It describes the future restoration of the world, when the creation becomes deathless and the Drug perishes. It also recounts how the Glory was held or pursued by Yima, Mithra, Thraêtaona, Keresâspa, Âtar, the Son of the Waters, Frangrasyan, Kavi Husravah, and Zarathustra.

XIV.

The unit concerns the awful kingly Glory made by Mazda, its connection with king Vîstâspa, and its future connection with Saoshyant and Astvat-ereta. Vîstâspa follows the Law, advances the holy religion, and defeats enemies and wicked Hvyaonas. Saoshyant will restore the world, causing death, decay, hunger, thirst, the Drug, and Angra Mainyu to perish. Astvat-ereta will rise from Lake Kãsava, deliver living creatures to immortality, and lead friends who follow the good law.

XX. Vanant Yast.

The unit is the XX. Vanant Yast, concerning worship, sacrifice, and prayer to the star Vanant, made by Ahura Mazda. The worshipper identifies himself as a worshipper of Mazda, a follower of Zarathustra, one who hates the Daêvas, and an observer of Ahura’s laws. Vanant is invoked as strong and healing to withstand the accursed Khrafstras of Angra Mainyu. The unit asks for brightness, glory, and a blissful abode for a man.

XXI. Yast Fragment.

Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda which word contains the glorification of all good things made by the good principle. Ahura Mazda answers that it is the praise of Holiness, which praises him, the waters, the earth, the cattle, the plants, and all good things made by Mazda. He explains that its value increases when recited during specific acts and times, especially with professions of good thoughts, words, and deeds and rejection of evil ones. The final recital, made to renounce evil thoughts, words, and deeds, is said to equal all that lies between earth and the heavens, the earth, luminous space, and all good things made by Mazda.

Yast XXII.

Yast XXII concerns Zarathustra’s questions to Ahura Mazda about the souls of the faithful and the wicked after death. The faithful person’s soul remains near the head for three nights, then encounters its conscience and enters successive paradises, ending in the Endless Lights. The wicked person’s soul remains near the skull for three nights, then enters successive hells, ending in the Endless Darkness. The unit also describes the food of the departed, the manifestation of holy souls, and the lying call of Bûshyãsta.

Âfrîn Paighambar Zartûst / XXIII. Âfrîn Paighambar Zartûst.

Âfrîn Paighambar Zartûst presents Zarathustra blessing king Vîstâspa. He wishes Vîstâspa a long, exalted, good life, long-lived people and women, sons, strength, glory, wealth, holiness, victory, welfare, and freedom from sickness and death. He names models for these qualities, including Gâmâspa, Mazda, Thraêtaona, Yima Khshaêta, and Zarathustra Spitama. The blessing concludes by calling for the propagation of good thoughts, good words, and good deeds and for entry into the bright abode of the holy Ones.

XXIV. Vîstâsp Yast.

The unit concerns Zarathustra's teachings and blessings for the young king Vîstâspa, including prosperity, holiness, long life, strength, offspring, and royal power. It describes the Law of the worshippers of Mazda, proper priests and sacrifices, prayers, moral conduct, charity, and the defeat of evil. It teaches that the faithful soul reaches Garô-nmâna through Good Thoughts, Good Words, Good Deeds, and Endless Light, meeting its own conscience along the way. Ahura Mazda and the Amesha-Spentas are presented as granting glory, protection, guidance, and the rewards of holiness.
