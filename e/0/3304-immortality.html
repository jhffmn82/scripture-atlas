Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Solomon, immortality is kin to wisdom and brings closeness to God, with eternal memory for posterity sought through wisdom. In Yasna, Immortality is Ahura Mazda’s abiding, saving, and greatest gift, granted through holy deeds, words, and sacrifice.

Abstract written by a model

Immortality is kin to wisdom and brings closeness to God; in Chapter 8 the speaker expects it, plus eternal memory for posterity, through wisdom, so he seeks her.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

The speaker expects to gain immortality because of wisdom, together with an eternal memory left to those who come after him. He reasons that immortality is in kinship to wisdom, which is part of why he seeks to take her to himself.

Immortality in Yasna

L.H. Mills · the work’s page · read it where Immortality first appears

Abstract written by a model

Immortality is Ahura Mazda’s abiding, saving, and greatest gift, given through holy deeds, words, and sacrifice. A blessing for the righteous and chosen saint, it is granted with Weal to those who heed His teaching, desired in eternal existence, and increased by Âramaiti with Universal Weal and the Righteous Order.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna XXXIV.

Immortality is one of the gifts given by Ahura Mazda through holy deeds, words, and sacrifice. Âramaiti increases it together with Universal Weal and the Righteous Order.

Yasna XLV.

Immortality is granted with Weal to those who heed Ahura Mazda’s teaching. It is also desired by the souls of the righteous in eternal existence.

Yasna XLVII.

Immortality is one of the two greatest gifts that Ahura Mazda gives to His chosen saint through His Bountiful Spirit.
