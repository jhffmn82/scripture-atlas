Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Luke, repentance is required to avoid perishing, and heaven rejoices over a repenting sinner. In The Book of Jarom, repentance is what the prophets, priests, and teachers continually stir the people up unto by pricking their hearts with the word. In The Book of Helaman, repentance is a condition of baptism and the path to salvation, remission of sins, and redemption through Jesus Christ. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, repentance is required for deliverance, salvation, healing, baptism, and receiving the gospel sacrament worthily. In The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record, repentance is required for salvation and for being spared by the Lord, while failure to repent leads to destruction and being cut off. In The Book of Ether, repentance is required to preserve Coriantumr's kingdom and spare the people, and the Lord shows mercy when people repent.

repentance in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where repentance first appears

Abstract written by a model

Repentance is a theme in Jesus' teaching, required to avoid perishing. In Chapter 13, after the Galileans killed by Pilate and the eighteen killed by the Siloam tower, he twice warns that unless hearers repent they will all perish, denying the victims were worse sinners; the fig tree parable offers another year. In Chapter 15, heaven rejoices over one repenting sinner, shown by the returning younger son's confession and welcome.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

Jesus twice says that unless his hearers repent they will all perish in the same way, after mentioning the Galileans killed by Pilate and the eighteen killed by the falling tower in Siloam. He denies that those victims were worse sinners or offenders than others. The fig tree parable, with its extra year to bear fruit, follows.

Chapter 15

Jesus says there is more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous people who need no repentance, and joy in the presence of God's angels over one sinner repenting. In the third parable the younger son's decision to return and confess his sin against heaven and his father shows it, and it is met with welcome and celebration.

repentance in The Book of Helaman

The Book of Mormon · the work’s page · read it where repentance first appears

Abstract written by a model

Repentance is a condition of baptism, a repeated command to turn unto the Lord, and the path by which people receive salvation, remission of sins, and redemption through the power and merits of Jesus Christ. It is joined with faith, humility, hearkening to the Lord, confession of sins, and a change of heart. As the church grows, people are baptized unto repentance and united to the church of God. The Nephites repent after preaching by Moronihah, Nephi, and Lehi; they prosper and regain possessions and lands. Repentance is preached to Lamanites and Nephites, bringing many into humility as followers of God and the Lamb. Warnings repeatedly contrast repentance with destruction: those who repent and hearken receive the Lord God's strength, are blessed, and may be saved, while those who refuse lose cities, lands, salvation, and are destroyed. When famine and destruction threaten, the people humble themselves in sackcloth; Nephi prays, and the Lord turns away his anger. Repentance and returning to the Lord can thus spare the people, prevent everlasting destruction, and keep them from being cast into the fire. In the end, Nephi preaches repentance, and believers in Samuel's words repent and confess their sins.

Chapter 16

repentance in The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman

Mormon · the work’s page · read it where repentance first appears

Abstract written by a model

Repentance is a response to iniquity and a turning from wicked ways, evil doings, wickedness, and abominations. It is required for deliverance, salvation, healing, the kingdom of God, baptism, and receiving the gospel sacrament after unworthiness. Lachoneus teaches the people to repent of all iniquities for deliverance from the Gadianton robbers, and the Nephites later recognize repentance as a reason for their deliverance from everlasting destruction. Nephi preaches repentance and remission of sins, and many are baptized unto repentance. After a great and terrible day, people regret not repenting earlier and are called to repent, return to Jesus Christ, and receive eternal life. Jesus Christ commands all to repent, become as a little child, and seek baptism through those with authority. Those who repent, are baptized in his name, and endure to the end may be filled, held guiltless, and numbered among his people and the house of Israel; those who do not repent are not numbered and may be cut off.

repentance in The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record

Mormon · the work’s page · read it where repentance first appears

Abstract written by a model

Repentance is required for salvation and for being spared by the Lord, and God requires it of the Gentiles. The Nephites begin with sorrow and crying, yet fail to repent through God’s goodness or come to Jesus with broken hearts and contrite spirits. Though the Lord calls them to repent after deliverance, they harden their hearts; destruction and being cut off follow. Mormon laments that repentance could have prevented their fall. The records command people to repent of every sin, turn from evil, humble themselves, resist temptation, and serve God.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

The Nephites begin to repent of their iniquity and cry as Samuel the prophet had prophesied. Their sorrow is not unto repentance through the goodness of God, and they do not come to Jesus with broken hearts and contrite spirits.

Chapter 3

The Lord called the Nephites to repentance, but they did not repent and hardened their hearts. Because they did not repent after being delivered, the Lord declared that they would be cut off from the face of the earth.

Chapter 5

The Nephites do not repent of their iniquities, and Mormon knows the judgments that will come upon them. The Gentiles are commanded to repent, turn from their evil ways, and humble themselves before God.

Chapter 6

Mormon wishes that the Nephites had repented before the great destruction came upon them. He teaches that repentance could have prevented their fall.

Chapter 7

The people must come unto repentance or they cannot be saved. They must repent of all their sins and iniquities.

Chapter 9

The text urges unbelievers to turn to the Lord, strip themselves of uncleanness, yield to no temptation, and serve the true and living God.

Repentance is required for Coriantumr and his household to preserve the kingdom and spare the people. Gentiles and all the ends of the earth are commanded to repent, become clean, and come unto Jesus Christ. Corihor and later the people repent and prosper; prophets call repeatedly, and the Lord shows mercy when people repent. Coriantumr, his household, and the people do not repent.

Chapter 4

The Gentiles must repent of their iniquity and become clean before the Lord before the sealed things will go forth unto them. All the ends of the earth are commanded to repent and come unto Jesus Christ.

Chapter 7

Corihor repents of the many evils he has done, and Shule gives him power in the kingdom. The people later repent of their iniquities and idolatries, after which the Lord spares them and they prosper.

Chapter 11

The prophets repeatedly called the people to repentance. When the people began to repent of their iniquity, the Lord had mercy on them.

Chapter 13

Ether is told to warn Coriantumr that repentance by him and all his household would bring the kingdom and spare the people. Coriantumr, his household, and the people do not repent.
