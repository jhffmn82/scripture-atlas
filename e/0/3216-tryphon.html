Tryphon

person · 1 work · 10 quoted facts · 7 connections

Abstract written by a model

Tryphon is an ambitious usurper, once a supporter of Alexander. He exploits discontent against Demetrius, routs him, and installs the young Antiochus as king, gaining the elephants and control of Antioch. Coveting rule over Asia, he moves against Antiochus and fears Jonathan, whom he deceives at Bethshan and seizes at Ptolemais. He invades Judah with a large army, demands money and hostages without releasing Jonathan, and, turned back by heavy snow, kills him near Bascama. He then deceitfully kills the young Antiochus, takes the crown of Asia, and brings calamity; Simon calls his deeds plunder. Finally, deserted by his forces, he flees to Dor, is besieged, and escapes by ship to Orthosia with Antiochus's forces pursuing.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Tryphon, formerly on Alexander's side, saw that the forces murmured against Demetrius. He persuaded Imalcue the Arabian to hand over the young Antiochus, brought him back to reign, routed Demetrius, and took the elephants and control of Antioch.

Chapter 12

Tryphon sought to reign over Asia and move against king Antiochus, and feared Jonathan would oppose him. At Bethshan, seeing Jonathan's large army, he instead received him honorably and persuaded him to dismiss his forces and come to Ptolemais with a few men. After Jonathan was seized there, Tryphon sent troops and cavalry into Galilee and the Great Plain to destroy Jonathan's men.

Chapter 13

Tryphon musters a mighty army to invade Judah, holding Jonathan prisoner, and deceitfully demands money and hostages without releasing him. Turned back by heavy snow, he goes to Gilead, kills Jonathan near Bascama, and returns to his own land. He then deceives and kills the young King Antiochus, takes the crown of Asia, and brings calamity on the land; Simon later says all he did was plunder.

Chapter 15

Tryphon is left with few men when Antiochus's forces gather against him, and he flees to Dor, knowing his forces have deserted him. Shut up in Dor by the siege, he later escapes by ship to Orthosia while Antiochus pursues him.
