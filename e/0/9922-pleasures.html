Abstract written by a model

Pleasures are portrayed as dangerous worldly desires with a strong hold on people. They lead greedy seekers toward recklessness, delusion, sin, and blamable actions, especially through sensuality and women’s friendship and company. Virtuous men regard them as equal to diseases, while those who follow the Law abstain. Monks are warned that pleasures, possessions, women, vehicles, perfumes, ornaments, and beds can overcome control and abandon discipline. Sexual pleasures are especially blamable; one who loves them should become indifferent. Finally, pleasures procured by sinful acts bring no freedom from misery and should neither be sought nor enjoyed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Second Lecture, Called the Destruction of Karman. / First Chapter. .. Third Chapter.

Pleasures have a strong hold on people and lead greedy, pleasure-seeking men toward recklessness and delusion. Those who follow the Law abstain from pleasures and regard them as equal to diseases.

Third Lecture, Called the Knowledge of Troubles / First Chapter. .. Fourth Chapter.

Worldly desires tempt monks through pleasures, possessions, women, vehicles, perfumes, ornaments, and beds. A monk should not desire life or allow such desires to overcome his control.

Fourth Lecture, Called Knowledge of Women. / First Chapter. .. Second Chapter.

Sexual pleasures are presented as dangerous and blamable, causing monks to abandon discipline and become subject to delusion. A monk who loves them should become indifferent to them again.

Ninth Lecture, Called the Law.

Pleasures obtained through sinful acts do not free people from misery. A monk should not seek or enjoy pleasures, even when they offer themselves.
