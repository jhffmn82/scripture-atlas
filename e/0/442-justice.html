justice

Also called: God's justice, iniquity, right, righteousness, the law, truth, uprightness, wickedness

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Job, God's justice is characterized by impartiality and is defended as free from wickedness and perversion. In Proverbs, justice is presented as something kings may pervert, while Lemuel is instructed to judge righteously and serve the poor and needy. In The Book of Isaiah, justice is portrayed as absent, prompting the LORD to act with his own arm and righteousness.

is_characterized_by impartiality [applies to princes, rich and poor alike] Elihu insists that God's justice is beyond question: far be it from God to do wickedness, and he will not pervert justice. He gives each man the result of his work, shows no partiality between princes, the rich, and the poor, and needs no further inquiry into a man before judging. Elihu asks whether one who hates justice could govern, and says God overturns the wicked and hears the cries of the afflicted.

justice in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where justice first appears

Abstract written by a model

Justice is portrayed as absent: far from the people and turned backward, with truth fallen in the street. Displeased at its absence, the LORD responds by acting himself with his own arm and righteousness.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 59

Justice is described as absent: the people do not know the way of peace, justice is far from them, turned backward, and truth has fallen in the street. The LORD is displeased to see that there is no justice, and responds by acting himself with his own arm and righteousness.
