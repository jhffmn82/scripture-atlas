Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, Aaron's sons are appointed to the priesthood, minister alongside Aaron, and undergo clothing, anointing, consecration, and sanctification. In Leviticus, Aaron's sons serve as consecrated priests carrying out the sacrificial system and observing holiness requirements. In Numbers, the sons of Aaron serve as priests alongside Aaron, blow the silver trumpets, and share priestly duties and portions of holy things. In The Book of Joshua, Aaron's children receive thirteen Levitical cities in the first allotment. In 1 Chronicles, the sons of Aaron form a priestly family set apart for holy things, incense, ministry, blessing, and service by the Levites. In The Second Book of Chronicles, the priests cleanse the temple, carry away uncleanness, slaughter animals, sprinkle blood, and make the sin offering for Israel. In The Second Book of Chronicles, the priests receive tithes and first fruits, are organized into divisions, and perform offerings and Passover duties. In The Book of Ezra, twelve chiefs of the priests receive and safeguard the holy silver, gold, and vessels for delivery in Jerusalem.

Aaron's sons in Exodus

author unknown · about 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Aaron's sons first appears

Abstract written by a model

Aaron's sons (named as Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar) are a group appointed to the priesthood to minister alongside Aaron. They keep the lamp in order from evening to morning in the Tent of Meeting, a statute forever throughout generations. They are given tunics, sashes or belts, headbands, and linen pants covering their flesh so they do not bear iniquity and die. The records unfold from their naming and clothing, to anointing, consecration, and sanctification with Aaron: laying hands on offerings, blood applied to ear, thumb, and toe, and sharing the consecration meal. Their priesthood is a perpetual statute, and one of them will wear Aaron's garments afterward. Finally they are brought to the door, washed, clothed, and anointed as their father was, and wash hands and feet when entering the Tent or approaching the altar.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 27

Aaron's sons, together with Aaron, are to keep the lamp in order from evening to morning before the LORD in the Tent of Meeting. This is a statute forever throughout their generations.

Chapter 28

Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar are named as Aaron's sons who are to minister with him in the priest's office. They are to receive tunics, sashes, and headbands for glory and beauty, and linen pants covering their flesh from waist to thighs. Together with Aaron they are to be anointed, consecrated, and sanctified, and the pants are a statute forever so they do not bear iniquity and die when entering the Tent of Meeting or approaching the altar.

Chapter 29

Aaron's sons are to be brought, given tunics, belts, and headbands, and consecrated along with Aaron. They lay hands on the offerings, receive blood on the right ear, thumb, and toe, and share the wave offering portions and the consecration meal. They will hold the priesthood by a perpetual statute, and one of them will wear Aaron's garments after him.

Chapter 40

Aaron's sons are to be brought to the door, washed, put in tunics, and anointed as their father was, to serve as priests. They wash their hands and feet at the basin, and again when entering the Tent or approaching the altar.

Aaron's sons in Leviticus

author unknown · 539 to 331 BC · the work’s page · read it where Aaron's sons first appears

Abstract written by a model

Aaron's sons are the priests, a consecrated group set apart by the LORD to serve at the altar at the Tent of Meeting. Across Leviticus they carry out the sacrificial system: they sprinkle the blood around the altar, put fire on it, lay wood in order, arrange the pieces, and burn the fat and offerings, including birds and herd offerings. In the meal offering the priest burns a memorial handful, and the remainder belongs to Aaron and his sons as a most holy part of the LORD's fire offerings. They eat it unleavened in the court of the Tent of Meeting. Every male among them has a perpetual portion forever throughout their generations, including the waved breast and, for the one who offers the blood and fat of the peace offerings, the right thigh. Whoever touches these portions becomes holy. In Chapter 8 they are washed, clothed by Moses in tunics, sashes and headbands, lay hands on the bull and rams, receive blood on right ear, thumb and great toe, are sprinkled with oil and blood, eat the consecration meal, and remain at the Tent of Meeting seven days. Holiness is the central demand. They must be holy to their God and not profane his name: no shaving heads or beards, cutting flesh, defiling themselves for the dead beyond near relatives, or marrying a prostitute, profane woman or divorced woman. A priest's daughter who profanes herself through prostitution is to be burned. Finally, they must keep away from holy things while unclean, eating them only after being clean and bathing, or face sin and death, and they must not cause the people's holy things to bear guilt.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Leviticus / Chapter 1

Aaron's sons, the priests, present and sprinkle the blood around on the altar, put fire on the altar, lay wood in order, and arrange the pieces, head, and fat on the wood. The priest burns the whole offering on the altar, and for birds he wrings off the head, drains the blood on the altar's side, and burns the bird.

Chapter 2

Aaron's sons are the priests to whom the meal offering is brought; the priest takes a handful and burns the memorial on the altar. What is left of the meal offering belongs to Aaron and his sons as a most holy part of the offerings of the LORD made by fire.

Chapter 3

Aaron's sons, the priests, sprinkle the blood of each offering around on the altar. They also burn the removed fat on the altar, on the burnt offering that lies on the wood on the fire.

Chapter 6

The sons of Aaron offer the meal offering before the LORD at the altar and eat what remains of it unleavened in the court of the Tent of Meeting. Every male among them has a perpetual portion of the offerings made by fire, and whoever touches them becomes holy. The anointed priest from among his sons offers the wholly burned meal offering.

Chapter 7

The sons of Aaron share in every meal offering, whether mixed with oil or dry, one as well as another. They receive the waved breast, and the one among them who offers the blood and fat of the peace offerings receives the right thigh. Their portion is forever throughout their generations.

Chapter 8

Aaron's sons are washed and clothed with tunics, sashes, and headbands, and they lay their hands on the bull and both rams. They receive blood on their right ear, thumb, and great toe, hold the wave offering, are sprinkled with oil and blood, and are told to eat the consecration meal and stay at the Tent of Meeting seven days.

Chapter 21

The priests are commanded to be holy to their God and not profane his name. They may not defile themselves for the dead except near relatives, may not shave their heads or beards or cut their flesh, and may not marry a prostitute, a profane woman, or a divorced woman. A priest's daughter who profanes herself by prostitution profanes her father and is to be burned with fire.

Chapter 22

Aaron's sons, as priests, must keep away from the holy things while unclean and may eat them only after being clean and bathing. They are warned that profaning the holy things brings sin and death, and they are told not to cause the people's holy things to bear guilt.

sons of Aaron in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · 449 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where sons of Aaron first appears

Abstract written by a model

The sons of Aaron are Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar, a priestly family. Nadab and Abihu died childless before their father, so only Eleazar and Ithamar served as priests. Eleazar's line continues through Phinehas and Zadok down to Jehozadak. They were set apart forever for the most holy things, incense, ministry, and blessing, with Levites serving them. They received thirteen cities, including Hebron and Libnah, with Hebron the Kohathites' first lot.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

The sons of Aaron are named as Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar. The line through Eleazar continues to Phinehas, Zadok, Ahimaaz, and later priests down to Jehozadak. They received thirteen cities from Judah, Simeon, and Benjamin, including Hebron, which was the Kohathites' first lot.

Chapter 23

The sons of Aaron were set apart forever with Aaron for the most holy things, incense, ministry, and blessing. The Levites were to wait on them for the service of the LORD's house and keep their duty.

Chapter 24

The sons of Aaron are named as Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar. Nadab and Abihu died before their father without children, so only Eleazar and Ithamar served as priests, and the Levites cast lots like them.

Abstract written by a model

The priests are a group descended from Aaron's sons who receive tithes and first fruits from Jerusalem's people so they can devote themselves to the law. Under Hezekiah they are organized into divisions for burnt and peace offerings, supplied by genealogy and named men, and have plenty left over. Under Josiah they are set in their offices, sprinkle Passover blood from the Levites, offer burnt offerings until night, and receive livestock from the rulers.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 31

The priests were organized by Hezekiah into divisions according to their service for burnt and peace offerings. Their portion was provided by the people so they could devote themselves to the LORD's law, and they were supplied through listings by genealogy and by named men in their cities. They ate and had enough, with much left over.

Chapter 35

Josiah sets the priests in their offices and encourages them in the service of the LORD's house. At the Passover they sprinkle the blood received from the Levites' hands and are busy offering the burnt offerings and the fat until night. Hilkiah, Zechariah, and Jehiel, rulers of God's house, give them two thousand six hundred small livestock and three hundred head of cattle for the Passover offerings.
