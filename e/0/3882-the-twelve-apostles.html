Abstract written by a model

In The Good News According to Mark, the twelve apostles are appointed by Jesus to be with him, preach, heal sicknesses, and cast out demons. In The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry, the twelve apostles of the Lamb follow the Lamb, judge the twelve tribes of Israel, bear record of truth, and carry the book from the Jews to the Gentiles.

the twelve apostles in The Good News According to Mark

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the twelve apostles first appears

Abstract written by a model

The twelve apostles are a group Jesus appoints on the mountain to be with him, be sent out to preach, and hold authority to heal sicknesses and cast out demons. Named members include Simon Peter, James, John, and Andrew; the list ends with Judas Iscariot, who betrayed him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

Jesus appoints twelve on the mountain so that they might be with him, be sent out to preach, and have authority to heal sicknesses and cast out demons. They are named as Simon (given the name Peter), James and John (called Boanerges, Sons of Thunder), Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Zealot, and Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed him.

Abstract written by a model

The twelve apostles of the Lamb follow the Lamb amid opposition from the multitude of the earth and shall judge the twelve tribes of Israel. They bear record of truth in the Lamb of God, carry the book from the Jews unto the Gentiles, and their record will be established with the records of Nephi's seed.
