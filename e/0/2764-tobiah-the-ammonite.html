Tobiah the Ammonite

person · 1 work · 11 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Tobiah, Tobiah the Ammonite servant, Tobiah’s

Abstract written by a model

Tobiah the Ammonite, called a servant, is a persistent enemy of Nehemiah who opposes the rebuilding of Jerusalem's wall. He begins by being grieved at Israel's welfare, then ridicules the builders alongside Sanballat and Geshem, scoffing that a fox could break the wall, and joins the conspiracy against Jerusalem. After the wall is finished, he hires Shemaiah to frighten Nehemiah and sends intimidating letters. Through marriage ties to Shecaniah and his son Jehohanan's marriage, many in Judah are sworn to him, and nobles correspond with him. Eliashib the priest gives him a temple room, but Nehemiah returns and throws out his belongings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Tobiah, called the Ammonite servant, is grieved exceedingly at the news that someone has come to seek Israel's welfare. He joins Sanballat and Geshem in ridiculing the builders and accusing them of rebelling, and is told he has no portion, right or memorial in Jerusalem.

Chapter 4

Tobiah the Ammonite stands beside Sanballat and scoffs that if a fox climbed the Jews' stone wall, it would break it down. He later joins the others in anger and in the conspiracy against Jerusalem.

Chapter 6

Tobiah is among the enemies who hear the wall is built and, with Sanballat, hires Shemaiah to frighten Nehemiah into sin. Many in Judah are sworn to him, since he is son-in-law of Shecaniah and his son Jehohanan married Meshullam's daughter. Nobles of Judah exchange letters with him, praise his good deeds to Nehemiah, and report Nehemiah's words to him, while he sends letters to put Nehemiah in fear.

Chapter 13

Tobiah was given a great room in the temple courts by his ally Eliashib the priest. When Nehemiah returned, he threw all of Tobiah's household stuff out of the room.
