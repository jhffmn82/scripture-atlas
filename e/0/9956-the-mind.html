the mind

Also called: mind, the unruly, dreadful, bad horse

Abstract written by a model

The mind is an unruly, dreadful, bad horse, governed by Gautama through the Law and knowledge. Attached to pleasures, it is attacked by love and hatred; recognizing desire’s vanity brings indifference. Living simply and subduing senses protects it from Love, while purity makes it proficient in meditation and concentration.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Twenty-Third Lecture.

The mind is compared to an unruly, dreadful, bad horse. Gautama governs it by the discipline of the Law and the bridle of knowledge so that it keeps to the right path.

Thirty-Second Lecture.

The mind of one who lives in unfrequented lodgings, eats low food, and subdues the senses is protected from Love. Objects of the senses and of the mind cause pain to passionate people, while perfect indifference arises when the mind recognizes the vanity of all desires.
