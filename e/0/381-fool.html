Abstract written by a model

The fool is a quarreling figure described in Chapter 26 as unfit for honor, needing a rod for his back, and making parables useless in his mouth. He repeats his folly like a dog returning to its vomit. Advice is paradoxical: answer him, or not, according to his folly. There is more hope for him than for one wise in his own eyes.

Chapter 26

The fool is said to be unfit for honor, to need a rod for his back, and to make a parable useless in his mouth. Advice is given both not to answer him according to his folly and to answer him so he is not wise in his own eyes. Honoring, hiring, or sending a message by him is likened to harmful acts, and he is compared to a dog returning to its vomit when he repeats his folly.
