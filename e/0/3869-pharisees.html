Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Matthew, the Pharisees repeatedly oppose and test Jesus, accuse him and his disciples, conspire against him, and are denounced as hypocrites and blind guides. In The Gospel According to Matthew, the scribes and the Pharisees are religious leaders who fail to practice what they teach, burden others, seek honor, and neglect justice, mercy, and faith. In The Good News According to Mark, the scribes regard Jesus' forgiveness of sins as blasphemy and question his disciples about eating with tax collectors and sinners. In The Good News According to Mark, the Pharisees oppose Jesus through repeated challenges concerning eating, fasting, the Sabbath, traditions, signs, divorce, and taxes, and conspire against him. In The Gospel According to Luke, the Pharisees oppose Jesus, question and challenge his actions, and are rebuked for hypocrisy, greed, and neglect of justice and love. In The Gospel According to John, the Pharisees oppose Jesus, interrogate and expel the man he healed, reject his testimony, and plan with the chief priests to seize and kill him. In The Acts of the Apostles, the Pharisees confess the resurrection, angels, and spirits, while some defend Paul and suggest that a spirit or angel may have spoken to him.

Pharisees in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where Pharisees first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Pharisees are a group portrayed throughout these records chiefly as opponents of Jesus. They are known for fasting often, and they come to Jesus repeatedly, sometimes with scribes from Jerusalem, Sadducees, or Herodians. In Chapter 9 they question his eating with tax collectors and sinners and claim he casts out demons by the prince of the demons. In Chapter 12 they accuse his disciples over the Sabbath, ask about healing on it, conspire to destroy him, attribute his exorcisms to Beelzebul, and ask for a sign; Jesus calls them offspring of vipers. In Chapter 15 they charge his disciples with breaking the elders' tradition; Jesus calls them hypocrites, says they make void God's commandment through tradition, honor him with lips while their hearts are far away, teach men's rules as doctrine, and are blind guides. In Chapter 16 they test him with a request for a sign, and he warns of their teaching. In Chapter 19 they test him on divorce. In Chapter 22 they seek to entrap him over taxes, send a lawyer, and finally cannot answer his question about the Christ.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

The Pharisees ask Jesus' disciples why their teacher eats with tax collectors and sinners, and John's disciples say they fast often along with the Pharisees. After the mute man speaks, the Pharisees claim that Jesus casts out demons by the prince of the demons.

Chapter 12

The Pharisees accuse Jesus's disciples of unlawful Sabbath work, ask whether healing on the Sabbath is lawful in order to accuse him, and then conspire to destroy him. They attribute his casting out of demons to Beelzebul, and together with certain scribes ask him for a sign. Jesus calls them offspring of vipers and an evil and adulterous generation.

Chapter 15

Pharisees, with scribes, come from Jerusalem and accuse Jesus's disciples of disobeying the tradition of the elders over unwashed hands. Jesus calls them hypocrites and applies Isaiah's prophecy to them. The disciples report that they were offended, and Jesus calls them blind guides of the blind who will fall into a pit.

Chapter 16

The Pharisees, with the Sadducees, tested Jesus by asking for a sign from heaven. Jesus called them hypocrites and an evil and adulterous generation, and warned his disciples against their yeast, which is their teaching.

Chapter 19

The Pharisees come to Jesus to test him, asking whether it is lawful to divorce a wife for any reason. When Jesus answers from creation, they object by citing Moses's command to give a certificate of divorce.

Chapter 22

The Pharisees take counsel to entrap Jesus and send their disciples with the Herodians to ask about taxes to Caesar. They marvel at his answer, gather again after he silences the Sadducees, and send a lawyer to test him. Jesus then questions them about the Christ, and they say he is David's son but cannot answer further.

scribes and the Pharisees in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where scribes and the Pharisees first appears

Abstract written by a model

The scribes and Pharisees are a group of religious leaders who sit on Moses' seat of authority yet fail to practice what they teach, binding heavy burdens on others and acting to be seen by men and win honor and titles. Jesus pronounces repeated woes on them as hypocrites: they shut the Kingdom of Heaven against men, make proselytes twice as much sons of Gehenna, and neglect justice, mercy, and faith. Likened to whitened tombs, outwardly beautiful but inwardly unclean, they are called children of the prophets' killers and offspring of vipers, facing judgment upon this generation.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 23

The scribes and Pharisees sit on Moses' seat, but they say and do not do, bind heavy burdens on others, and do their works to be seen by men. Jesus pronounces repeated woes on them as hypocrites for devouring widows' houses, shutting the Kingdom of Heaven against men, making proselytes twice as much sons of Gehenna, swearing falsely, neglecting justice, mercy, and faith, and being clean outside but full of extortion and iniquity within. He says they testify against themselves as children of those who killed the prophets, and that the blood of the righteous and judgment will come upon this generation.

Pharisees in The Good News According to Mark

author unknown · about AD 70 · the work’s page · read it where Pharisees first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Pharisees are a religious group, often joined by scribes, who persistently oppose Jesus. They hold to the tradition of the elders, practice fasting with their disciples, and wash hands and forearms before eating. Jesus calls them hypocrites who set aside God's commandment to keep their tradition, and warns his disciples against their yeast. The account unfolds as a escalating series of confrontations. First they question his eating with tax collectors and sinners, his disciples' lack of fasting, and Sabbath grain-plucking. Then they watch him heal on the Sabbath and conspire with the Herodians to destroy him. Later, scribes from Jerusalem fault his disciples' unwashed hands. They seek a sign from heaven, test him on divorce, and finally, with the Herodians, try to trap him with words about paying taxes to Caesar. Jesus answers them each time, and they marvel at the last.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

The Pharisees, with the scribes, question why Jesus eats with tax collectors and sinners. Their disciples fast, and they ask why Jesus' disciples do not. They also object that his disciples pluck grain on the Sabbath, and Jesus answers by citing David.

Chapter 3

The Pharisees watch Jesus in the synagogue to see if he will heal on the Sabbath so they can accuse him. After he heals the man, they go out and immediately conspire with the Herodians about how to destroy him.

Chapter 7

The Pharisees, with some scribes from Jerusalem, find fault with the disciples for eating with unwashed hands and ask why they don't follow the tradition of the elders. Jesus calls them hypocrites who honor God with their lips while their hearts are far away, and who reject God's commandment to keep their tradition.

Chapter 8

The Pharisees come out and question Jesus, seeking a sign from heaven and testing him. Jesus sighs deeply and says no sign will be given to this generation, then leaves them. He afterward warns his disciples to beware of their yeast.

Chapter 10

The Pharisees come to Jesus to test him, asking whether it is lawful for a man to divorce his wife. Jesus turns them to what Moses commanded and explains that Moses allowed it because of their hardness of heart.

Chapter 12

Some of the Pharisees are sent, with the Herodians, to trap Jesus with words. They ask whether it is lawful to pay taxes to Caesar, and Jesus, knowing their hypocrisy, answers them and they marvel.

Pharisees in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where Pharisees first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Pharisees are a group, often paired with scribes, teachers of the law and lawyers, who have disciples of their own and consistently oppose Jesus. Early on, having come from villages across Galilee, Judea and Jerusalem, they reason that he blasphemes in forgiving sins, murmur at his eating with tax collectors and sinners, question his disciples' fasting, and challenge plucking grain on the Sabbath. They watch him to accuse him, and after he heals a withered hand they are filled with rage. At a Sabbath meal, a Pharisee host marvels that he did not wash, and Jesus rebukes them: they cleanse the cup's outside while full of extortion and wickedness, tithe herbs while bypassing justice and love, love the best seats, and are like hidden graves. They grow angry and lie in wait to catch his words. Later they stay silent when asked about healing on the Sabbath, murmur again about his welcoming sinners, warn him about Herod, and, described as lovers of money, scoff, justifying themselves before men while God knows their hearts.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Pharisees and teachers of the law sit by as Jesus teaches, having come from every village of Galilee, Judea, and Jerusalem. They and the scribes reason that Jesus blasphemes in forgiving sins, and later murmur against his disciples for eating with tax collectors and sinners and ask why Jesus' disciples do not fast.

Chapter 6

Some Pharisees challenge the disciples for plucking grain on the Sabbath. Together with the scribes they watch Jesus to see whether he will heal on the Sabbath so they can accuse him, and after he heals the withered hand they are filled with rage and discuss what to do to him.

Chapter 11

Some of the crowd and a Pharisee host react to Jesus, and the Pharisee marvels that Jesus did not wash before dinner. Jesus rebukes the Pharisees for cleaning the outside of the cup while being full of extortion and wickedness, for tithing herbs while bypassing justice and love, and for loving the best seats. The scribes and Pharisees then grow angry and lie in wait to catch him in his words.

Chapter 14

The lawyers and Pharisees watch Jesus and are asked whether it is lawful to heal on the Sabbath. They are silent and then cannot answer him about pulling a son or ox out of a well.

Chapter 15

The Pharisees and scribes murmur that Jesus welcomes sinners and eats with them. Jesus responds to them by telling the parables of the lost sheep, the lost coin, and the two sons.

Chapter 16

The Pharisees, described as lovers of money, hear Jesus' teaching and scoff at him. Jesus tells them they justify themselves before men, but God knows their hearts.

Pharisees in The Gospel According to John

author unknown · AD 90 to 100 · the work’s page · read it where Pharisees first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Pharisees are a group of religious authorities who consistently oppose Jesus across the records. Acting with the chief priests, they send officers to arrest him, dismiss the crowd as cursed for not knowing the law, and insist no ruler or Pharisee believes in him. They bring the woman taken in adultery to test him, then reject his self-testimony as invalid. In the healing episode they interrogate the healed man and his parents, split over whether Jesus can be from God since he healed on the Sabbath, claim to be disciples of Moses, and expel the man. Jesus tells them their sin remains because they claim to see. Finally, after a report of what Jesus did, they gather a council with the chief priests, fear everyone will believe in him, plan his death, and command that anyone knowing his whereabouts report it so he can be seized.

Chapter 7

The Pharisees hear the multitude murmuring about Jesus and, with the chief priests, send officers to arrest him. When the officers return without him, they ask whether the officers were also led astray and say that no rulers or Pharisees believe in him. They call the multitude, who do not know the law, cursed.

Chapter 8

With the scribes, the Pharisees bring the woman taken in adultery to Jesus and cite Moses' law on stoning to test him and find something to accuse him of. Later they tell Jesus his self-testimony is not valid after he calls himself the light of the world. They ask where his Father is and are told they know neither him nor the Father.

Chapter 9

The Pharisees question the healed man about how he received sight, and they split over whether Jesus can be from God given that he did the healing on the Sabbath. They call his parents, insult the man, claim to be disciples of Moses while saying they don't know where Jesus comes from, and throw the man out. When Jesus says those who see may become blind, some of them ask if they are also blind, and he says their sin remains.

Chapter 11

The Pharisees receive a report from some of the Jews about what Jesus did. With the chief priests they gather a council, fear that everyone will believe in him, and take counsel to put him to death. They also command that anyone who knows where he is must report it so they can seize him.
