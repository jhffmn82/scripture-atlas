In The Second Book of Samuel, Abner serves as captain of Saul's army, installs Ishbosheth as king, fights at Gibeon, kills Asahel, vows to transfer the kingdom to David, persuades Israel's elders, and is killed by Joab.

Abner in The Second Book of Samuel

author unknown · about 950 BC · the work’s page · read it where Abner first appears

Abstract written by a model

Abner, son of Ner, is captain of Saul's army who installs Ishbosheth as king over Gilead, Ephraim, Benjamin and all Israel. He proposes the contest at Gibeon, loses the battle, and kills Asahel after repeated warnings, then appeals to Joab to end the pursuit. Rebuked by Ishbosheth over Rizpah, he vows to transfer the kingdom to David and persuades Israel's elders. After feasting with David in Hebron, Joab kills him; David mourns him as a prince and great man.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Abner, son of Ner and captain of Saul's army, brought Ishbosheth to Mahanaim and made him king over Gilead, Ephraim, Benjamin, and all Israel. He proposed the contest at Gibeon, was beaten in the ensuing battle, and killed Asahel with the back end of his spear after warning him repeatedly to turn aside. He then appealed to Joab to end the pursuit and fled all night with his men to Mahanaim.

Chapter 3

Abner, strong in Saul's house, is rebuked by Ishbosheth over Rizpah, vows to transfer the kingdom to David, and persuades Israel's elders and Benjamin to turn to David. After feasting with David in Hebron and being sent away in peace, he is brought back from the well of Sirah and killed by Joab, and David mourns him as a prince and great man.
