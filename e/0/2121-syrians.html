Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, the Syrians are an enemy force opposing Israel, whose raids are avoided through Elisha's warnings; after being fed in Samaria, they stop raiding, but later besiege Samaria before fleeing their camp and goods when they hear a Lord-caused sound of chariots. In The Second Book of Chronicles, the Syrians are a small army that attacks Judah and Jerusalem, destroys the people's princes, sends plunder to Damascus's king, and seriously wounds Joash because Judah had forsaken the LORD.

Syrians in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Syrians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Syrians are an enemy force opposing Israel. Elisha's warnings let Israel's king avoid their raids; after being fed in Samaria, the bands stop raiding, though Benhadad later besieges Samaria. Finally, hearing a Lord-caused sound of chariots, they panic, flee at twilight, and abandon their camp and goods.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

The Syrians are warned of by Elisha as coming down to a certain place, allowing Israel's king to avoid them. After they are fed in Samaria, the Syrian bands stop raiding Israel's land, though Benhadad later besieges Samaria.

Chapter 7

The Syrians, having heard a sound of chariots and horses that the Lord caused, believed the king of Israel had hired the Hittite and Egyptian kings against them. They fled in the twilight, leaving tents, horses, donkeys, and the camp as it was, and scattering garments and equipment along the path to the Jordan.
