Abstract written by a model

Monks follow Mahâvîra’s austerities, renouncing wishes and plans. They cease from sin, guard themselves, avoid violence and attachment, bear pain, wander homeless, and assure safety to all beings. Living on pure water and abstaining from ablutions, they eat for control, subdue passions, seek liberation, and annihilate Karman through endurance and control.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Sixth Lecture, Called Praise of Mahâvîra.

Monks are described as having Mahâvîra as their flag by his austerities. He is praised as the greatest among monks because he renounced all wishes and plans.

Seventh Lecture, Called Description of the Wicked.

Monks should cease from sin, guard themselves, abstain from violence, avoid attachment, bear pain, wander homeless, and give assurance of safety to all beings. A monk should eat in order to practise control and should subdue the foe of passions.
