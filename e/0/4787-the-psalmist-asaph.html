Abstract written by a model

Asaph, a psalmist who takes refuge in God and is continually with him, nearly slips from envy of the prosperous wicked and doubts his pure living. Silent so as not to betray God's children, he gains understanding in the sanctuary, sees the wicked's end, admits his senselessness, and affirms God upholds him.

Chapter 73

Asaph, author of the psalm, confesses that his feet nearly slipped because he envied the prosperous wicked, and that he felt his pure living was in vain amid daily plague and punishment. He held back from speaking this so as not to betray God's children, and found understanding in God's sanctuary, where he saw the wicked's end. He admits he was senseless like a brute beast, then affirms that God upholds him, that he desires no one but God, and that it is good to be near God and tell of his works.
