Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Job, Sheol is the land of no return, dark as midnight and without order, where Job says he will go when his few days run out. In The Book of Job, Sheol is a place of the dead entered through its gates, which Job envisions as his house and darkness-couch. In Psalms, Sheol is the place of the dead, where no thanks are given to the LORD; the foolish are appointed there, though God redeems the psalmist's soul. In Proverbs, Sheol is the realm of departed spirits, where the foolish woman's guests lie in its depths and which lies open to the LORD's sight; it is listed among things never satisfied. In The Book of Isaiah, Sheol is a place beneath that holds departed spirits and stirs to meet the arriving king of Babylon, whose pomp falls as worms cover him. In The Book of Ezekiel, Sheol is the place of the dead, called the pit and the lower parts of the earth, where the LORD casts the Assyrian and other fallen nations.

Sheol is a place beneath that holds departed spirits and has enlarged desire. It stirs to meet the arriving king of Babylon, raising former rulers, who mock his weakness as his pomp falls and worms cover him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 14

Sheol is portrayed as stirring from beneath to meet the king of Babylon at his coming, raising the departed spirits and the former rulers from their thrones.
