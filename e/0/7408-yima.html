Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, Yima is a brilliant, glorious, sunlike ruler whose herds and people avoid death, whose plants and waters avoid drought, and whose reign lacks imperishable food's scarcity, cold, heat, age, death, and demon-made envy. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII, Yima rules the material world with golden implements, declines to preach Ahura Mazda's law, enlarges the overcrowded earth, and builds the Vara to preserve selected life from fatal winters. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, Yima Khshaêta, the good shepherd, sacrifices to Drvâspa, asks for fatness, flocks, and immortality, and seeks the removal of hunger, thirst, old age, death, and hot and cold winds from the Mazda-created world. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, Yima is a valiant, bright good shepherd whose Fravashi is worshipped against Daêva oppression, drought, and unseen death; he seeks glory and abundance, rules Varena and the seven Karshvares, and loses his kingly Glory after delighting in falsehood.

Abstract written by a model

Yima, son of Vîvanghvant, is a brilliant, glorious, sunlike ruler of the corporeal world and many herds. During his reign, herds and people avoid dying, plants and waters avoid drought, food is imperishable, and cold, heat, age, death, and demon-made envy are absent.

Yima, son of Vîvanghvant, is called brilliant, glorious, and the possessor of many flocks. Under his rule, herds and people were free from dying, plants and waters from drought, and people could eat imperishable food; there was neither cold nor heat, age nor death, nor demon-made envy.

Yima in Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Yima first appears

Abstract written by a model

Yima, son of Vîvanghant, is a valiant, bright good shepherd whose Fravashi is worshipped against Daêva oppression, pasture-destroying drought, and unseen death. He seeks glory and a reign in which animals and men are undying, waters and plants endure, and food never fails. Vayu and Ashi Vanguhi grant his requests, enabling him to bring fatness, flocks, and immortality to the world and remove hunger, thirst, old age, death, heat, and cold. He rules Varena and the seven Karshvares in abundance, with undrying waters and plants and freedom from deprivation. He possesses the awful kingly Glory until he delights in falsehood and untruth; then the Glory departs as a bird and is later seized by Mithra, Thraêtaona, and Keresâspa.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

XXVI.

Yima, the son of Vîvanghant, is described as valiant and as having flocks at his wish. His Fravashi is worshipped to stand against Daêva oppression, drought that destroys pastures, and unseen death.

XV. Râm Yast.

Yima sacrifices to Vayu and asks to become the most glorious of men, make animals and men undying, preserve waters and plants, and make food unfailing. Vayu grants the boon, and Yima’s reign is described as free from cold, heat, old age, death, and Daêva-made envy.

XVII. Ashi Yast.

Yima Khshaêta offers a sacrifice to Ashi Vanguhi from the height Hukairya. He asks to bring fatness, flocks, and immortality to the world and remove hunger, thirst, old age, death, heat, and cold for a thousand years, and Ashi Vanguhi grants the boon.

XIX. Zamyâd Yast.

Yima rules the seven Karshvares and enjoys a reign without cold, heat, old age, death, or failing food and water. After taking delight in falsehood, he loses the Glory, which flees from him in the shape of a bird; Mithra, Thraêtaona, and Keresâspa later seize it.
