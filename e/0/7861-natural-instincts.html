natural instincts

Also called: man's natural instincts, natural instinct

Abstract written by a model

Natural instincts, Heaven-sent and common to all humanity, include clothing and feeding oneself. They once let people live quietly with natural integrity and unity, but man discarded and lost them through worldliness, intellectualism, art, and learning.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IX.

Natural instincts include weaving and clothing oneself and tilling and feeding oneself, and they are described as common to all humanity and Heaven-sent. When they prevailed, people lived quietly and retained natural integrity.

CHAPTER XVI.

Natural instinct characterized man's original condition and the earlier unity of existence. Intellectualism, art, and learning caused man to lose it and prevented his return to it.
