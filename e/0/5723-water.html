Abstract written by a model

In The Tao Teh King, or The Tao and Its Characteristics, water benefits all things, occupies the low place without striving, and effectively attacks firm and strong things. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XV (Wei Ling Kung), water is described as less important to man than virtue, and men have been seen to die from treading on it.

water in The Tao Teh King, or The Tao and Its Characteristics

Lao-Tse · the work’s page · read it where water first appears

Abstract written by a model

Water benefits all things and, without striving, occupies the low place disliked by all men, resembling the highest excellence. Though nothing is more soft and weak, nothing surpasses it in effectually attacking firm and strong things; nothing equally effectual can replace it.

Water is described as very soft and weak, yet nothing takes precedence over it in attacking firm and strong things. It can be changed for nothing that is as effectual.
