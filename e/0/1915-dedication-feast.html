dedication feast

event · 2 works · 6 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: a solemn assembly, a very great assembly, feast, the dedication of the altar, the feast, the feast in the month Ethanim, the feast seven days

Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, the dedication feast is a fourteen-day celebration in Ethanim before the LORD, attended by Solomon and all Israel. In The Second Book of Chronicles, the dedication feast includes seven days dedicating the altar, seven days of feasting, and a solemn assembly on the eighth day.

dedication feast in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where dedication feast first appears

Abstract written by a model

A fourteen-day dedication feast held in Ethanim, the seventh month, before the LORD. Solomon and all Israel, from Hamath's entrance to Egypt's brook, attend; he offers 22,000 cattle and 120,000 sheep as peace offerings, then dismisses the blessing people on the eighth day.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

Solomon and all Israel, from the entrance of Hamath to the brook of Egypt, hold the feast before the LORD for seven days and seven more, fourteen in all. Solomon offers twenty-two thousand cattle and one hundred twenty thousand sheep as peace offerings, and on the eighth day he sends the people away and they bless him.

dedication feast in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where dedication feast first appears

Abstract written by a model

Solomon and all Israel hold a seven-day dedication of the altar and a seven-day feast in the seventh month. Priests sound trumpets and Levites play David's instruments in thanks. A solemn assembly follows on the eighth day; on the twenty-third day the people go home joyful.

Chapter 7

Solomon and all Israel keep the dedication of the altar for seven days and the feast for seven days, with a solemn assembly on the eighth day. Priests sound trumpets and Levites play instruments David made to give thanks. On the twenty-third day of the seventh month the people are sent home joyful and glad of heart.
