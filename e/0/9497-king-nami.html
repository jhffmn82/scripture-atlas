King Nami in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where King Nami first appears

Abstract written by a model

King Nami, a Kshattriya ruler of Videha and royal Seer, remembers his former birth, becomes enlightened as a Svayamsambuddha, and ends delusion. After placing his son on the throne, he retires from the world, relinquishing Mithila, his army, seraglio, retinue, wealth, and pleasures. Directed by Sakra to humble himself, he rejects arguments for royal power, punishment, conquest, and sacrifice, teaching self-control, patience, austerity, and self-conquest before taking Sramanahood.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Ninth Lecture.

King Nami remembers his former birth, becomes a Svayamsambuddha, places his son on the throne, and gives up his kingdom, army, seraglio, and retinue. He rejects Sakra's arguments for royal power, wealth, pleasures, punishment, conquest, and sacrifice, teaching instead the value of self-control, patience, austerity, and conquering oneself. He takes upon him Sramanahood.
