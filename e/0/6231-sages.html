Sages in Chuang Tzŭ

Chuang Tzŭ · the work’s page · read it where Sages first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sages are blamed for abandoning and annihilating Tao through charity, duty, music, ceremonies, wisdom, knowledge, and restrictions. They seek to rectify government, but instead foster competition for gain, trip and fetter people, and enable great robbers. The account ends by calling for the Sages and their systems to disappear, restoring natural integrity and peace.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IX.

Sages are blamed for tripping people up with charity and fettering them with duty to one’s neighbour. They introduced music and ceremonies to rectify government and caused people to develop a taste for knowledge and competition for gain.

CHAPTER X.

The Sages' wisdom and restrictions are said to cause the appearance and protection of great robbers. The unit calls for the Sages and their imposed systems to disappear so that the people may return to natural integrity and the empire may rest in peace.
