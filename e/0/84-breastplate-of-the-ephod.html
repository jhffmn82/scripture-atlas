breastplate (of the ephod)

object · 1 work · 12 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: breastplate, breastplate of judgment, the breastplate

Abstract written by a model

The breastplate is a sacred garment piece made in the same work as the ephod, of gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen. It is square and folded double, a span by a span. Its most important feature is twelve stones, set in gold in four rows of three, each engraved with the name of one of the twelve tribes of Israel, so it represents the children of Israel. The Urim and the Thummim are placed in it. In Chapter 28 it is described as hanging by braided gold chains and rings, bound to the ephod with a blue lace, resting on Aaron's heart as a continual memorial before the LORD. Chapter 39 repeats the design as made, attached to the ephod's shoulder straps and rings so it stays on the woven band and does not come loose.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 28

The breastplate is made like the ephod's work, square, folded double, and a span in length and width. It holds four rows of three stones set in gold, twelve in all, each engraved with a tribe's name, and the Urim and the Thummim are placed in it. It hangs by gold braided chains and rings, bound to the ephod with a blue lace, and rests on Aaron's heart as a memorial before the LORD continually.

Chapter 39

The breastplate is made square and double, a span by a span, in the same work as the ephod, and set with four rows of three stones in gold settings, one for each of the twelve tribes. It is attached by gold chains, gold rings and settings to the ephod's shoulder straps, and bound with a blue lace to the ephod's rings so that it stays on the woven band and does not come loose.
