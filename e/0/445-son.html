son (the addressee) in Proverbs

author unknown · 969 to 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where son (the addressee) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The son is the addressee of a father figure's instruction, repeatedly called 'my son' across Proverbs. He is a learner who receives commands, warnings, and promises. Early on he is told to heed his father's instruction and his mother's teaching, and not to follow enticing sinners. Next he is urged to seek wisdom and understanding, which will bring the fear of the LORD and deliverance from evil men and the strange woman, and to walk the path of the righteous. Then he is told to keep the commandments, trust the LORD rather than his own understanding, honor the LORD, accept discipline, and do good to his neighbor, with promises of long life, peace, favor, health, and plenty. Later he is told to show restraint, accept correction, avoid drunkenness, gluttony, and sexual temptation, and buy truth, with a hope that will not be cut off. Finally he is told to fear the LORD and the king, not to envy evildoers or join rebels, to judge impartially, to refuse revenge, to rescue those led to death, and to prepare his fields before building his house.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Proverbs / Chapter 1

The son is told to listen to his father's instruction and not forsake his mother's teaching, which will be a garland and chains for him. He is warned not to consent to sinners who entice him and not to walk on their path.

Chapter 2

The son is urged to receive the speaker's words, store up his commandments, turn his ear to wisdom and apply his heart to understanding. If he seeks her earnestly, he will understand the fear of the LORD and be delivered from evil men and the strange woman. He is told to walk in the way of good men and keep the paths of the righteous.

Chapter 3

The son is urged to keep his father's teaching and commandments, to bind kindness and truth around his neck, to trust the LORD rather than his own understanding, and to honor the LORD with his substance. He is told not to despise the LORD's discipline, to keep sound wisdom and discretion so he can walk securely and sleep sweetly, to do good to his neighbor without delay, and to avoid causeless strife and envy of violent men. In return he is promised long life, peace, favor, health, plenty, and a life free of fear.

Chapter 23

The son is addressed throughout as the one being instructed. He is told to show restraint, apply his heart to instruction, accept correction, keep his heart on the right path, fear the LORD, avoid heavy drinking and gluttony, buy truth, and shun the prostitute and wayward wife. He is promised a future hope that will not be cut off, and is asked to give his heart to the speaker.

Chapter 24

The son is addressed as 'my son' and told to eat honey, which stands for knowing wisdom, and to fear the LORD and the king. He is told not to envy evildoers, join rebels, show partiality in judgment, witness against his neighbor without cause, or take revenge. He is also told to rescue those led to death and to prepare his fields before building his house.

son (the speaker's son) in Proverbs

author unknown · 969 to 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where son (the speaker's son) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The son is told to pay attention to the speaker's wisdom, keep away from the adulteress, and not come near the door of her house. He is warned that otherwise he will lose his honor, years, and wealth, and will groan at the end, regretting that he despised instruction. He is urged to drink from his own cistern, rejoice in the wife of his youth, and be captivated always by her love.
