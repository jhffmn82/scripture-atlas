purification ritual

topic · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: clean, cleansing, purification, purified, purify, purify him, purify himself, sprinkle, sprinkled, statute of the law, water for impurity

Abstract written by a model

A perpetual statute binding the children of Israel and the resident stranger, governing purification from uncleanness through contact with the dead. The unclean person is sprinkled with the water for impurity on the third and seventh days, washes clothes, bathes, and is clean at evening. Those handling the heifer's ashes also become unclean until evening. Refusal brings being cut off for defiling the sanctuary. After battle, killers and touchers camp outside seven days with captives; garments, skin, goats' hair, and wood are purified, fireproof metals pass through fire and water, others through water, and clothes are washed on day seven.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 19

The unclean person is sprinkled with the water for impurity on the third and seventh days, then washes his clothes and bathes in water and is clean at evening. Those who burn the heifer, gather the ashes, or sprinkle the water must also wash and are unclean until evening, and one who refuses purification is cut off for defiling the sanctuary.

Chapter 31

Anyone who killed or touched the slain must camp outside for seven days and purify themselves on the third and seventh days, along with their captives. Garments and items of skin, goats' hair, and wood are purified, and metals that withstand fire go through the fire and the water for impurity, while other things go through water. The men wash their clothes on the seventh day before entering the camp.
