Guru Nanak

person · 6 works · 60 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: I, Nanak, Nanak, Nanak saith, Nanak's, O Nanak, Saith Nanak, The poet Nanak, slave Nanak

Abstract written by a model

In Japji, Guru Nanak teaches submission to God’s will, rejection of ego, and meditation on the Name as the path toward divine knowledge and emancipation. In So Dar and So Purukh (the Rahiras), Guru Nanak praises God as Creator, teaches remembrance of the true Name, and seeks shelter through the Guru’s instruction. In Sohila (Kirtan Sohila), Guru Nanak teaches remembrance of God, divine unity, cosmic worship, and humble devotion as a servant longing for God’s presence. In Asa ki War (Asa di Var), Guru Nanak teaches divine truth, grace, humility, devotion, and inner purity while condemning hypocrisy, corruption, ritualism, and falsehood. In Hymns of Guru Nanak, Guru Nanak speaks as a humble teacher, minstrel, devotee, and servant who teaches devotion to God, the Guru, the Word, and the Name. In Additional Sloks of Guru Nanak, Guru Nanak teaches rejecting evil, embracing goodness, giving oneself to faithful companions, and accepting the Perfect One’s will.

Nanak in Japji

Max Arthur Macauliffe · the work’s page · read it where Nanak first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nanak is presented as teaching that people should live according to God's preordained will, avoid egoism, and accept whatever pleases God as good. The records describe realms in which divine knowledge brings joy, beauty marks the realm of happiness, and knowledge, wisdom, intellect, understanding, and supernatural skill are fashioned. What occurs there, as well as the continents, worlds, universes, and forms, cannot be completely described. The realm of action contains powerful warriors, heroes, saints, and those in whose hearts God dwells; they rejoice because the True One is in their hearts. God dwells in the true realm, looks on its denizens with favour, and makes them happy. The account closes with Nanak teaching that those who complete their toil and ponder on the Name may gain bright countenances and emancipation in the company of others.

Guru Nanak in So Dar and So Purukh (the Rahiras)

The Sikh Religion · the work’s page · read it where Guru Nanak first appears

Abstract written by a model

Guru Nanak is associated with Rag Asa. In Sodar, he portrays all creation praising God and teaches that remembering the true Name gives life. In So Purukh, he humbly seeks shelter, praises God as eternal Creator, and says God becomes manifest through the Guru's instruction.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Sodar

Guru Nanak describes all creation as singing God’s praises and says that God is eternal, true, sovereign, and beyond complete description. He teaches that remembering the true Name gives life, while forgetting it leaves people spiritually empty.

So Purukh

Guru Nanak calls himself helpless, impure, untrue, unlearned, and foolish, and says that he has sought the shelter of those who do not forget God. He praises God as the eternal Creator and declares that what God pleases and does comes to pass. He says that under the Guru's instruction God becomes manifest.

Guru Nanak in Sohila (Kirtan Sohila)

Max Arthur Macauliffe · the work’s page · read it where Guru Nanak first appears

Abstract written by a model

Guru Nanak is portrayed as a devoted servant focused on God. He teaches remembrance of the Caller, the oneness of God despite many forms, cosmic worship, and God’s light in everything. His account moves from teaching toward intimate devotion: longing for God’s lotus feet, seeking favour to dwell in God’s name, finding happiness there, and calling himself a slave who desires the saints’ feet-dust.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Guru Nanak, Rag Gauri Dipaki

Guru Nanak teaches remembrance of the Caller and says that the approaching day should be remembered.

Guru Nanak, Rag Asa

Guru Nanak teaches that there is one God, although His forms are many.

Guru Nanak, Rag Dhanasari

Guru Nanak describes the cosmic worship offered to God and recognizes God's light in everything. He compares his longing for God's lotus feet to a bumble-bee's attraction to a flower and asks for God's favour so he may dwell in God's name.

Guru Arjan, Rag Gauri Purbi

Nanak calls himself the slave and craves the happiness of being made the dust of the saints' feet.

Guru Nanak in Asa ki War (Asa di Var)

Guru Nanak · the work’s page · read it where Guru Nanak first appears

Abstract written by a model

Guru Nanak is presented as a speaker, teacher, Guru, Commander, and devoted slave of the bright God. Across the records, he teaches that God alone is true, unrivalled, all-powerful, formless, independent, and the maker, ruler, and caretaker of the world. God knows what humans do not, including whether burning or burial is better, ordains regard for people according to their regard for God, gives differing conditions, and causes beings to act. Guru Nanak repeatedly contrasts this divine truth with a world marked by falsehood, pride, corruption, suffering, death, transmigration, and the consequences of deeds. His central teaching is that the true Guru, divine grace, God's name, meditation on the True One, love, devotion, humility, mercy, charity, and divine knowledge lead people toward truth, comfort, honour, purity, and salvation. Without the Guru and grace, even extensive good works, study, bravery, charity, penance, reading, ritual, and outward religious marks are of no avail. He rejects hypocrisy, violence, greed, falsehood, slander, empty observance, arguments, worship of stones, and external purity, insisting instead on God in the heart and inner purity. The account unfolds through praise of God's true order and power, then warnings about the fearful, changing, and false world. It develops a sustained critique of corrupt rulers, priests, pretenders, caste, ritual practice, and the purchased janeu, replacing them with a true discipline of mercy, contentment, continence, truth, and praise of the Name. It ends by affirming longing for God, the gift received when the Lord is pleased, the servant's love for the master, and God's continuing power to make, fill, regenerate, and sustain all beings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

24 sections; each heading opens the text

Slok .. Pauri I

Guru Nanak is praised as one who, through the Guru, makes demigods out of men. He warns that those who consider themselves clever but do not think of the Guru will be abandoned like spurious sesames, bearing fruit and flower but containing ashes within.

Slok II .. Pauri II

Guru Nanak declares God's regions, universes, worlds, creation, acts, thoughts, order, court, command, favour, signs, power, strength, praises, eulogies, and might to be true. He teaches that those who meditate on the True One are true, while those who are born and die are the falsest of the false.

Slok III .. Pauri III

Guru Nanak marvels at the wonders of God's works and says that those who understand them are supremely fortunate. He teaches that everything exists by God's power and that God is unrivalled.

Slok IV .. Pauri IV

Guru Nanak teaches that God, the Formless One, alone is without fear, and that all other beings and movements occur in fear. He also says that divine knowledge is not gained through mere words, skill, or orders.

Slok V .. Pauri V

Guru Nanak describes the world as a stage of performers and turning objects, and teaches that beings dance according to their own acts. He also teaches that those with fear of God have love and that death comes when a person's measure is full.

Slok VI .. Pauri VI

Guru Nanak states that the Creator knows whether it is better for a Musalman's ashes to be burned or buried.

Slok VII .. Pauri VII

Nanak states that the Commander has ordained that as man regards God, so God regards him. Nanak also teaches that through God's grace and the Guru's instruction, trouble caused by pride shall depart.

Slok VIII .. Pauri VIII

Guru Nanak teaches that hundreds of thousands of good deeds, charities, penances, acts of bravery, studies, and readings would be of no avail without the true mark of grace. He states that the Creator who made the world has decreed transmigration.

Slok IX .. Pauri IX

Guru Nanak teaches that only God's name has lasting account, while excessive reading and outward religious practices become senseless or harmful without devotion. He also teaches that meeting the true Guru and fixing God's name in the heart brings comfort and destroys pride.

Slok X .. Pauri X

Guru Nanak teaches that worldly things and the whole world are false except God. He explains how man becomes true through truth, love for the True One, mercy, charity, and instruction from the true Guru.

Slok XI .. Pauri XI

Guru Nanak teaches that truth is scarce and falsehood prevails in the age. He describes the corruption of rulers, priests, religious pretenders, and ordinary people, and states that honour will properly weigh a person. He also says that man's evil is seen by the True One and that caste has no power in the next world.

Slok XII .. Pauri XII

Guru Nanak teaches that pain is medicine and worldly pleasure is a disease, and he declares that God is the Doer while he does nothing. He praises God’s omnipotence and says that God’s light pervades and contains all creatures. He also says that divine knowledge restrains the mind and cannot be obtained without a guru.

Slok XIII .. Pauri XIII

Guru Nanak teaches about the changing qualities of the body and its driver in each age. He explains what the four Veds state and says that love, devotion, and humility lead to salvation. He declares himself a sacrifice to the true Guru.

Slok XIV .. Pauri XIV

Guru Nanak teaches that outward rituals, arguments, worship of stones, falsehoods, and religious markings are vain without knowledge of God's designs and guidance from the true Guru. He also teaches that inner purity matters more than repeated bowing and that people must face the results of their deeds.

Slok XV .. Pauri XV

Guru Nanak teaches that a true janeu consists of mercy, contentment, continence, and truth, and that praising the Name gives honour and a thread fit for God's court. He criticizes the purchased cotton janeu, which does not prevent wrongdoing, falls away at death, and is accompanied by external rituals and mental blindness.

Slok XVI .. Pauri XVI

Guru Nanak condemns hypocrisy, falsehood, violence, and empty ritual observance. He urges people to repeat God's name and meditate on the True One.

Slok XVII .. Pauri XVII

Guru Nanak teaches that only the fruit of a person’s earnings and toil will be obtained in the next world. He also teaches that true purity comes from having God in the heart.

Slok XVIII .. Pauri XVIII

Guru Nanak teaches that impurity is not washed away by physical means but by divine knowledge. He also states that pretended saints who practice greed, falsehood, coveting, or slander shall go bound to hell.

Slok XIX .. Pauri XIX

Guru Nanak teaches that people depend on a vessel for conception, birth, marriage, friendship, worldly life, and replacement after death. He says that only the True God is independent of a vessel and that the face praising Him will be bright in His court.

Slok XX .. Pauri XX

Guru Nanak teaches that evil people are foolish, rejected in God's court, and punished. He also describes the devotion of good people and the judgment awaiting the wicked.

Slok XXI .. Pauri XXI

Nanak says that a person who judges what is good as good and what is bad as bad in this manner is not a true lover. Nanak also says that such a person will not obtain a place in God's court.

Slok XXII .. Pauri XXII

Nanak says that the person who longs for God shall meet Him and that the longing will be acceptable. Nanak also says that pleasure lies in praising God and that commands will not succeed with Him.

Slok XXIII .. Pauri XXIII

Nanak says that the wonderful gift is obtained when the Lord is pleased. He says that a servant is absorbed in the love of the master and that God's end and boundaries are unseen. Nanak also says that God causes people to act and that the one who made the world takes care of it.

Slok XXIV .. Pauri XXIV

Guru Nanak teaches that God makes and fills vessels, gives different beings different conditions, and regenerates those upon whom He looks with favour. He also states that God does everything Himself and that there is no abiding Place except in God alone.

Nanak in Hymns of Guru Nanak

Guru Nanak · the work’s page · read it where Nanak first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nanak is a human being, teacher, minstrel, devotee, and speaker of compositions whose life and teaching center on God, the true Guru, the Word, and God’s Name. He begins as an out-of-work minstrel, then serves by singing the Word, praising God, teaching divine knowledge, and seeking truth through searching, observation, and God. He seeks the pure love and nectar of the Name as the wealth, capital, and necklace of his heart, takes shelter in it, and asks for no other gift. He portrays himself humbly as blind, foolish, a slave of slaves, God’s slave, a dog of God’s court, and one sacrificed ever to God. Though he says he performs no service, he bears his Master’s name, thinks exclusively of God, receives understanding from God, makes his heart a temple, and is not born again because the one word dwells there. His teachings reject pride, worldly wealth, status, ritual, external cleansing, hypocrisy, empty display, bodily life, and attachment without God’s Name. He teaches that the Guru, Name, devotion, humility, truth, contentment, good works, love, and remembrance lead to happiness, honour, deliverance, and union with God; sin, pride, and turning away from God bring suffering. He drinks God’s elixir, remains absorbed in God, and declares that the pious who remain so are perennial. He ends by teaching that the true Guru unites man with God, ending sorrow, sin, and death.

Sri Rag .. Ashtapadi

Nanak teaches that God alone is true and that man obtains happiness, honour, and deliverance through the Guru, the Word, and God's name. He declares that worldly wealth, ritual, pride, and bodily life cannot save man from death.

Majh ki War

Nanak teaches that the world is impermanent, that pride and external cleansing cannot remove inner sin, and that God's Name and the Guru's instruction bring happiness. He serves as a minstrel, singing the Word and receiving true praise and the ambrosial food of the true Name.

Rag Gauri .. Gauri Chhant

Nanak teaches that fear of God, the Guru, the Name, the Word, truth, and contentment lead to salvation and happiness. He says that worldly possessions, pride, learning without self-knowledge, and empty religious display are worthless.

Rag Asa .. Asa Ashtapadi

Nanak teaches that God alone is the Spouse and Lord of all, and that worldly status, ritual, learning, wealth, and family attachment are fruitless without God’s Name. He declares that those who follow the Guru, abandon pride and worldly love, and meditate on the Name receive God’s favour and salvation.

Asa Chhant

Nanak teaches that the young woman who sees God her Spouse is delighted and enraptured. He says that the soul must remember God, that those who lack God's name suffer, and that separated beings meet through the True Word.

Rag Gujari .. Gujari Ashtapadi

Nanak asks for the love of God's pure Name and for the nectar of the Name so that he may sing God's praises. He asks for protection, peace, freedom from avarice and covetousness, and God's Name as the capital of his heart.

Rag Sorath .. Sorath Ashtapadi

Nanak teaches that humanity's sins become chains, while virtues cut those chains off. Nanak praises God, identifies himself as the slave of slaves, and says that the Guru's teaching reveals God and makes a person a perfect Bairagi.

Rag Dhanasari .. Dhanasari Chhant

Nanak calls himself God's slave and an eternal sacrifice to Him. He teaches service to God, remembrance of God's name, acceptance of destiny, and that people receive the fruit of their own actions.

Rag Suhi .. Suhi ki War

Nanak teaches that service, humility, love, remembrance of the Name, and acceptance of the Guru's instruction lead to salvation. He says that God tests accounts, pardons those who love the True One, and blends the liberated with Himself.

Rag Bilawal

Nanak calls himself foolish, blind, and unable to describe God, asking God to give him understanding to sing praises truthfully. He says he thinks of no one but God, has made his heart a temple, and will not be born again because the one word dwells in his heart. Although he performs no service, he still bears his Master's name.

Ramkali .. Ramkali ki War I

Nanak teaches that the Name, devotion to God, good works, truth, and the Guru's instruction lead to salvation. He warns against pride, mammon, worldly love, hypocrisy, and dependence on outward religious practices.

Rag Maru Ashtapadi .. Maru ki War I

Nanak teaches that the Name, the Guru, humility, truthful conduct, and God's love are the true means of salvation. He asks God to grant truth, preserve his honour, and cause people to meet the true Guru.

Bhairo

Nanak stirred God's elixir and drank it.

Rag Basant

Nanak teaches that the pious who continue absorbed in God shall be perennial and never wither.

Sarang ki War

Nanak teaches that a person without a guru has no virtues and that turning away from God makes the mouth impure. He identifies divine knowledge, continence, contentment, alms, justice, meditation, truth, mercy, forgiveness, good deeds, and God's love as proper spiritual practices.

Rag Malar .. Malar ki War

Nanak teaches that the pious are invited to God's palace and will be blended with Him. He calls himself the dog of God's court, longs increasingly for God's love, asks only for God's name, and blesses the paper, pen, ink-bottle, ink, and writer who record the True Name.

Rag Prabhati .. Prabhati Ashtapadi

He teaches that if the true Guru is found, the Guru will unite man with God, so that sorrow, sin, and death will be no more.

Abstract written by a model

Nanak teaches people to examine their goods, reject evil, welcome good, and give themselves wholly to their friends. He declares that the world has lost companionship, love, and faith through mammon, praises those absorbed in God, and says that the pious who accept the Perfect One’s will shall be absorbed in Him.
