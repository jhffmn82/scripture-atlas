Abstract written by a model

In Chuang Tzŭ, ceremonial expresses sincerity ornamentally through ceremonies and music, which should be regulated to distinguish relationships and reform the masses. In The Doctrine of the Mean, ceremonial concerns the proper making of ceremonies by one who has proper virtue and occupies the throne.

Abstract written by a model

Ceremonies and music express sincerity ornamentally and should be regulated to distinguish relationships and reform the masses; the old fisherman says Confucius exceeds his sphere, though poor regulation causes disorder.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXI.

Confucius regulates ceremonies and music in order to distinguish human relationships and reform the masses. The old fisherman presents these activities as evidence that Confucius is acting outside his proper sphere, while also saying that poorly regulated ceremonies and music contribute to disorder.
