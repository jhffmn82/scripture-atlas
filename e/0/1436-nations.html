Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, nations are portrayed as forgetters of God who are rebuked, judged, humbled, and turned back to Sheol. In Psalms, the nations are enemies who invade God's inheritance, defile his temple, ruin Jerusalem, and mock God. In The Book of Jeremiah, nations are portrayed negatively as idolaters who devastate Jacob and do not know the LORD.

Abstract written by a model

The nations are portrayed as forgetters of God, mere humans. The LORD rebukes them and erases their name forever; they fall into their own traps, David asks they be judged and humbled, and they are turned back to Sheol.

Chapter 9

The nations are rebuked by the LORD, and their name is blotted out forever. They have sunk in the pit they made and are caught in the net they hid. David asks that they be judged in the LORD's sight and made to know they are only men, and says those who forget God will be turned back to Sheol.

the nations in Psalms

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the nations first appears

Abstract written by a model

The nations are an enemy group who invade God's inheritance, defile his temple, ruin Jerusalem, leave servants' bodies unburied, devour Jacob, and ignorantly mock God. The record ends with the speaker pleading for wrath and vengeance on them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 79

The nations have come into God's inheritance, defiled his holy temple, laid Jerusalem in heaps, and left his servants' bodies as food for birds and animals. They are said to have devoured Jacob and destroyed his homeland, and they do not know God or call on his name. They mock by asking 'Where is their God?', and the speaker asks that wrath and vengeance be poured out on them.

nations in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where nations first appears

Abstract written by a model

The nations are a group portrayed negatively in Chapter 10. They are dismayed by signs of the sky, follow vain customs, and are called brutish and foolish for trusting idols. They cannot withstand the LORD's indignation. Accused of devouring Jacob and laying waste his habitation, they do not know the LORD, so the speaker asks that his wrath be poured on them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10

The nations are dismayed at the signs of the sky and follow customs the text calls vanity. Their wise men and kings are said to have no one like the LORD, and they are called brutish and foolish for being instructed by idols. They are also accused of devouring Jacob, consuming him and laying waste his habitation, and the speaker asks that the LORD's wrath be poured out on them because they do not know him or call on his name.
