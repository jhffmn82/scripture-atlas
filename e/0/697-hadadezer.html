Hadadezer in The Second Book of Samuel

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Hadadezer first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hadadezer, son of Rehob, is king of Zobah and lord over servant kings, owning the cities Betah and Berothai. He was at war with Toi. David struck him as he sought to recover his dominion at the River, capturing horsemen, footmen, shields and bronze. He then summoned Syrians from beyond the River under Shobach; defeated at Helam, his servant kings made peace with Israel and served them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

Hadadezer, son of Rehob and king of Zobah, was struck by David as he went to recover his dominion at the River. David took 1,700 horsemen and 20,000 footmen, hamstrung the chariot horses while keeping enough for one hundred chariots, and carried off the gold shields of his servants and much bronze from his cities Betah and Berothai. Toi king of Hamath, who had his own wars with Hadadezer, sent his son Joram to bless David for defeating him.

Chapter 10

Hadadezer sent for and brought out the Syrians from beyond the River, who came to Helam under his captain Shobach. After their defeat, the kings who were his servants made peace with Israel and served them.

Hadadezer in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Hadadezer first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hadadezer, king of Zobah, owned cities Tibhath and Cun and warred with Tou of Hamath. David defeated him, destroying his chariots, horsemen and footmen, and seized gold shields and bronze; Tou then sent David greetings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

Hadadezer, king of Zobah, is defeated by David while David is going to establish his dominion by the river Euphrates, and loses one thousand chariots, seven thousand horsemen, and twenty thousand footmen. David takes the gold shields of his servants and much bronze from his cities Tibhath and Cun. Hadadezer had been at war with Tou king of Hamath, who sends greetings to David after the defeat.
