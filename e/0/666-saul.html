Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Samuel, Saul becomes Israel’s first king, is rejected by the LORD, and dies in battle against the Philistines. In The Second Book of Samuel, Saul is remembered as the LORD’s anointed who died at Mount Gilboa and whose descendants and bones were later dealt with. In 1 Chronicles, Saul is remembered as the king whose trespass and death led to his kingdom passing to David. In The Book of Hosea, Saul is represented as the king the LORD gave and then took away in wrath.

Saul in The First Book of Samuel

author unknown · about 930 to 721 BC · the work’s page · read it where Saul first appears

Abstract written by a model

Saul is a Benjamite, son of Kish, of the Matrite family, remembered as Israel's first king. He is introduced as a handsome young man, taller than all the people from the shoulders up, from the smallest tribe, sent to find lost donkeys. Through his servant he meets Samuel, who is told by the LORD that Saul is chosen. Anointed as prince over the LORD's inheritance, he receives another heart, prophesies among the prophets, hides among the baggage when chosen by lot at Mizpah, and is acclaimed king. Worthless fellows despise him, and he holds his peace. Coming in from the field behind his oxen, he is seized by God's Spirit, summons Israel by sending pieces of a yoke of oxen, defeats the Ammonites, shows mercy by refusing executions, and is made king at Gilgal. He reigns forty-two years, starting at thirty, fights enemies on every side including Moab, the Amalekites and above all the Philistines, and recruits valiant men. His family includes his wife Ahinoam, sons Jonathan, Ishvi and Malchishua, daughters Merab and Michal, and his captain Abner, who is also his uncle. The turning point comes when his fear leads him to offer the burnt offering himself instead of waiting for Samuel, and he is told his kingdom will not continue. His rash oath nearly costs Jonathan's life. After sparing Agag and the best livestock of Amalek, he is rejected as king by the LORD. The LORD's Spirit departs and an evil spirit torments him. He takes in David as armor bearer and loves him, but after David's victory over Goliath and the women's song he grows afraid and jealous. He hurls his spear, offers his daughters as snares, and hunts David with chosen men, even ordering the priests of Nob killed through Doeg. Twice David spares him, and Saul weeps, calls David more righteous, and acknowledges David will be king, yet the pursuit resumes until David flees to Gath. Abandoned by the LORD and afraid of the Philistine army, Saul consults the medium at Endor, though he had expelled mediums, and Samuel tells him that he and his sons will die. In the final battle at the Philistines' hands, he loses his sons, and when his armor bearer refuses to kill him, he falls on his own sword. The Philistines behead him and fasten his body to the wall of Beth Shan, until men of Jabesh Gilead retrieve and bury his bones.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

20 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

Saul, a tall and handsome Benjamite and son of Kish, is sent to find his father's lost donkeys and, after a fruitless search, agrees to consult the seer on his servant's advice. Samuel, informed by the LORD, tells him the donkeys are found and that Israel's desire is for him, though Saul protests that he is of the smallest tribe and least family. He is honored at the feast with the reserved portion and, at the end, is told to stand still to hear God's message.

Chapter 10

Saul is anointed, experiences all the promised signs, receives another heart, and prophesies among the prophets, prompting the proverb "Is Saul also among the prophets?" He keeps the kingdom matter secret from his uncle, hides among the baggage when chosen by lot at Mizpah, and stands head and shoulders above the people. He is acclaimed king, returns to Gibeah, and holds his peace when worthless fellows despise him.

Chapter 11

Saul, coming in from the field behind his oxen, hears of Jabesh's plight, is seized by God's Spirit and anger, and summons Israel by sending pieces of a yoke of oxen. He leads the people in three companies to defeat the Ammonites, refuses to let anyone be put to death that day because the LORD rescued Israel, and is made king at Gilgal.

Chapter 12

The king, whom Israel chose and asked for, is described by Samuel as one the LORD has set over them. If people and king fear and serve the LORD, they are followers of the LORD their God; if they keep doing evil, both the people and their king will be consumed.

Chapter 13

Saul, who reigned forty-two years, chose three thousand men and blew the trumpet after Jonathan's attack on the Philistine garrison. At Gilgal he grew afraid as the people scattered, offered the burnt offering himself instead of waiting for Samuel, and was told his kingdom would not continue. He was left with about six hundred men, and he and Jonathan were the only ones with a sword or spear.

Chapter 14

Saul stays at Gibeah with about six hundred men, discovers Jonathan is missing, and then joins the battle. He swears the oath against eating, which leaves his people faint, builds his first altar to the LORD, and, when God gives no answer, uses lots to find the offender and condemns his son Jonathan before the people rescue him. His wars against many enemies, his family, and his habit of taking in valiant men are also described.

Chapter 15

Saul musters 200,000 footmen and 10,000 men of Judah, spares the Kenites, and strikes the Amalekites, but keeps Agag alive and spares the best livestock. He claims to have obeyed, blames the people, then confesses and begs Samuel to honor him before the elders. He is told he is rejected as king, tears Samuel's robe in grasping it, worships the LORD, and returns to Gibeah.

Chapter 16

Saul is the king the LORD has rejected, and Samuel fears he would kill him if he learned of the mission. The LORD's Spirit departs from him and an evil spirit troubles him, so he asks his servants for a skilled player and summons David, who becomes his armor bearer, finds favor in his sight, and relieves him with harp playing.

Chapter 17

Saul camps with Israel in the valley of Elah and is dismayed and greatly afraid at Goliath's challenge. He sends for David, doubts that a youth can fight Goliath, then says 'Go! The LORD will be with you' and dresses David in his own armor, which David removes. After the victory he asks Abner whose son David is and is told by David that he is the son of Jesse the Bethlehemite.

Chapter 18

Saul keeps David with him and sets him over the men of war, but becomes angry and suspicious after the women's song credits David with ten thousands. He twice throws his spear at David, fears him because the LORD has left Saul, and makes him a captain of a thousand. He offers Merab and then Michal in hopes the Philistines will kill David, demands a hundred foreskins as dowry, and ends the chapter as David's continual enemy.

Chapter 19

Saul orders David killed, then swears at Jonathan's urging that David will not die, but an evil spirit from the LORD comes on him and he throws his spear at David. He sends messengers to David's house and later to Naioth to seize him, and finally goes there himself, where God's Spirit overcomes him and he prophesies and lies naked a day and a night.

Chapter 20

Saul sits at the new moon feast, says nothing on the first day of David's absence because he thinks David is unclean, and on the second day asks why the son of Jesse has not come. On hearing Jonathan's excuse he burns with anger, insults Jonathan, declares David shall surely die, and casts his spear at his own son.

Chapter 21

Saul is called the king in David's account of secret business, and David fled for fear of him. One of his servants, Doeg the Edomite, the best of his herdsmen, was at Nob that day. The servants of Achish recall a song that Saul has slain his thousands and David his ten thousands.

Chapter 22

Saul, sitting under the tamarisk tree at Ramah with his spear, accused his servants of conspiring against him with David and his son. He condemned Ahimelech and the priests of Nob to death, and when his guards refused to strike them he ordered Doeg to do it.

Chapter 23

Saul summons the people to besiege David at Keilah, believing God has delivered him into his hand, but abandons the effort when told David has escaped. He seeks David daily, accepts the Ziphites' offer to deliver him up, and pursues him in the wilderness of Maon until a messenger reports a Philistine raid and he turns back.

Chapter 24

Saul, told that David is in the wilderness of En Gedi, takes three thousand chosen men to seek him and enters a cave to relieve himself, unaware David is inside. After David spares him and shows him the skirt, Saul weeps, calls David more righteous than himself, and says he knows David will surely be king. He makes David swear not to cut off his offspring or destroy his name, then goes home.

Chapter 26

Saul goes down to the wilderness of Ziph with three thousand chosen men to seek David and sleeps in his camp with his spear stuck in the ground at his head. David spares him, and after hearing David's appeal Saul confesses that he has sinned and played the fool, promises to do David no more harm, and blesses him before returning to his place.

Chapter 27

Saul is the one David fears will kill him. When told that David has fled to Gath, Saul stops looking for him.

Chapter 28

Saul, afraid at the sight of the Philistine army and unanswered by the LORD, disguises himself and goes by night to the woman at Endor, swearing by the LORD that she will not be punished, and asks her to bring up Samuel. Samuel tells him that the kingdom is torn from him for disobeying over Amalek and that he and his sons will die with Israel's army defeated the next day. Saul falls down terrified and weak from fasting, refuses food at first, then is persuaded to eat and leaves that night.

Chapter 31

Saul was overtaken by the Philistines and gravely distressed by their archers, and he lost his three sons in the battle. When his armor bearer refused to kill him, he fell on his own sword to avoid abuse by the uncircumcised. The Philistines later beheaded him, took his armor, and fastened his body to the wall of Beth Shan, until men of Jabesh Gilead took it down, burned it, and buried the bones.

Saul in The Second Book of Samuel

author unknown · about 950 BC · the work’s page · read it where Saul first appears

Abstract written by a model

Saul, son of Kish and father of Jonathan, is remembered as the LORD's anointed and chosen one. David laments him as lovely, pleasant, swifter than eagles and stronger than lions, whose sword never returned empty. He clothed the daughters of Israel in scarlet and gold, and had a concubine, Rizpah. He died on Mount Gilboa, killed at his own request by an Amalekite, who took his crown and bracelet. Later, his land passes to his grandson Mephibosheth, with Ziba as his servant. Saul is blamed for a famine after killing the Gibeonites despite Israel's oath; seven descendants are hanged in atonement. His bones and Jonathan's are finally buried in Zela in Kish's tomb.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Second Book of Samuel / Chapter 1

Saul is reported dead after the battle on Mount Gilboa. According to the Amalekite's account, he was leaning on his spear with chariots and horsemen close behind, and asked the man to kill him because anguish had taken hold of him. David calls him the LORD's anointed and laments him as lovely and pleasant, swifter than eagles and stronger than lions, whose sword did not return empty.

Chapter 9

Saul is named as the grandfather of Mephibosheth and the former owner of the land and possessions that David gives to Mephibosheth. Ziba is described as Saul's servant.

Chapter 21

Saul is blamed for the famine because he put the Gibeonites to death in his zeal for Israel and Judah, though Israel had sworn to them. Seven of his descendants are hanged in atonement, and his bones, along with Jonathan's, are recovered from the men of Jabesh Gilead and buried in Zela in the tomb of his father Kish.

Saul in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · 449 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Saul first appears

Abstract written by a model

Saul, son of Kish and descended through Ner, fathered Jonathan, Malchishua, Abinadab, and Eshbaal. He is remembered for his trespass against the LORD, notably consulting one with a familiar spirit instead of the LORD. David fled from him, though Benjamin's kinsmen largely kept allegiance to his house until they backed David. In battle at Mount Gilboa, archers overtook him; when his armor bearer refused to kill him, he fell on his own sword. The Philistines stripped his body and displayed his head and armor in their gods' houses, while the men of Jabesh Gilead buried him, and his kingdom turned to David.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

Saul is traced in the line Ner, Kish, Saul. He became the father of Jonathan, Malchishua, Abinadab, and Eshbaal.

Chapter 10

Saul is overtaken by archers in the battle and, after his armor bearer refuses to kill him, falls on his own sword to avoid being abused by the uncircumcised. The Philistines later strip his body, take his head and armor, and display them in the houses of their gods and of Dagon. His body is retrieved and buried by the men of Jabesh Gilead.

Chapter 12

Saul is the one David fled from, the son of Kish. Benjamin, his own relatives, supplied archers and slingers to David, though most of them had kept allegiance to Saul's house until then. The Philistine lords also feared David might desert to his master Saul, and the people came to Hebron to turn Saul's kingdom to David.
