Pharaoh in Exodus

author unknown · about 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pharaoh first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pharaoh is the king of Egypt, who rules the land, its servants, and commands taskmasters, an army, and chariots. He is introduced as a new king who did not know Joseph and who feared the numerous children of Israel might join enemies in war and leave. He set taskmasters over them and ordered newborn Hebrew boys cast into the river. Later Moses is sent to him; God says he will not give permission to go until God strikes Egypt with wonders, and that the LORD will harden his heart. Moses is to tell him to let Israel go or his firstborn son will be killed. Pharaoh says he does not know the LORD, refuses to release the people, calls their desire for a feast and sacrifice idleness, and worsens their burden by withholding straw while keeping the brick count. Through the plagues he is stubborn and unwilling to listen: he asks for a miracle, relies on his wise men and sorcerers, and bargains with offers to let them sacrifice in the land, send only the men, or send the little ones but keep the flocks. Under pressure he confesses sin and promises release, but each time relief comes he hardens his heart again, along with his servants. He finally orders Moses out on pain of death. At midnight his firstborn, who sat on his throne, is struck. Pharaoh rises in the night, calls Moses and Aaron, and tells them to take the Israelites, flocks and herds and go serve the LORD, asking for a blessing. He and his servants then regret the release; his heart is hardened by the LORD, and he takes his army and chariots to pursue Israel to the sea. There he, his chariots, horses and horsemen are overthrown, his chosen captains sunk in the Red Sea, and the LORD gains honor over him. His arc runs from fearful oppressor to defiant refuser, to a brief, coerced yielding, and finally to destruction.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

13 sections; each heading opens the text

Exodus / Chapter 1

A new king arose who did not know Joseph and feared that the more numerous Israelites might join Egypt's enemies in war and leave the land. He set taskmasters over them, then ordered the Hebrew midwives to kill newborn boys. When the midwives defied him and gave an excuse, he commanded all his people to cast every newborn son into the river and keep the daughters alive.

Chapter 3

Pharaoh, called the king of Egypt, is the one Moses is sent to. God says he will not give permission to go, not even by a mighty hand, and will let the people go only after God strikes Egypt with wonders.

Chapter 4

Pharaoh is the one Moses is to confront with the wonders, though the LORD says he will harden Pharaoh's heart so that he will not let the people go. Moses is to tell him that the LORD says to let his son Israel go, and that his own firstborn son will be killed if he refuses.

Chapter 5

Pharaoh says he does not know the LORD and will not let Israel go, and he tells Moses and Aaron to get back to their burdens. He commands that straw no longer be given while the brick count stays the same, calls the people idle, and rejects the officers' appeal.

Chapter 6

Pharaoh is called king of Egypt, and the LORD says that by a strong hand he will let Israel go and drive them out of his land. Moses is commanded to speak to him, and Moses doubts that Pharaoh will listen to him.

Chapter 7

Pharaoh asks for a miracle, calls his wise men and sorcerers when Aaron's rod becomes a serpent, and does not listen to Moses and Aaron. After the river turns to blood and his magicians copy it, he turns and goes into his house without taking it to heart.

Chapter 8

Pharaoh asks Moses to entreat the LORD about the frogs and the flies, promising to let the people go to sacrifice, and offers a compromise of sacrificing in the land and then not going very far. Each time relief comes he hardens his heart and refuses to let the people go, even after the magicians tell him the lice are God's finger.

Chapter 9

Pharaoh sees that none of Israel's livestock died yet remains stubborn, and he refuses to let the people go after the boils. After the hail he confesses, "I have sinned this time," calls the LORD righteous and promises to release Israel. When the hail ceases he sins yet more and hardens his heart again, along with his servants.

Chapter 10

Pharaoh is warned of locusts, offers to let only the men go, and has Moses and Aaron driven out. After the locusts he confesses sin and asks for forgiveness, but his heart is hardened again; after the darkness he offers to let the little ones go but keep the flocks, and finally orders Moses out on pain of death.

Chapter 11

Pharaoh is told that his firstborn, who sits on his throne, will die in the coming plague, and that his servants will bow to Moses and urge Israel out. The LORD says he will not listen to Moses, and hardens his heart so that he does not let the Israelites leave.

Chapter 12

Pharaoh's firstborn, who sat on his throne, is among those struck at midnight. Pharaoh rises in the night, calls for Moses and Aaron, and tells them to take the Israelites, their flocks, and their herds and go serve the LORD, asking also for a blessing.

Chapter 14

Pharaoh, king of Egypt, is told the people have fled, and he and his servants change their minds and regret letting Israel go. His heart is hardened by the LORD, and he prepares his chariot, takes his army, and pursues Israel to the sea. He and his chariots, horses, and horsemen are overthrown there, with the LORD gaining honor over him.

Chapter 15

Pharaoh's chariots and army are cast into the sea, and his horses go in with his chariots and horsemen. The song reports that his chosen captains are sunk in the Red Sea.
