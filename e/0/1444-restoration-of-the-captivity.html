Abstract written by a model

The restoration of the captivity is the LORD's promise to reverse the exile of Judah and Israel. It begins with pledges to gather scattered people from all countries, resettle them safely, give them one heart, and make an everlasting covenant of good. It then moves to concrete signs: fields bought, deeds signed and sealed, and witnesses called across Benjamin and Judah. It closes by returning things to how they were at first, with joy, thanksgiving in the LORD's house, flocks in the cities, and mercy on Jacob's and David's offspring.

Chapter 32

The LORD promises to gather the people from all countries where he drove them and bring them back to dwell safely. He says he will be their God, give them one heart and one way, make an everlasting covenant, and plant them in the land with his whole heart. Just as he brought the evil, he will bring the promised good, and fields will be bought, deeds signed and sealed, and witnesses called throughout Benjamin, Judah, and the surrounding places as captivity is reversed.

Chapter 33

The LORD repeatedly promises to reverse the captivity of the land and of Judah and Israel, so that things will be as at the first. It is tied to renewed joy, thanksgiving in the LORD's house, flocks in the cities, and mercy on the offspring of Jacob and David.
