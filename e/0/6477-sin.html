Abstract written by a model

In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), evil deeds done in ignorance may be forgiven when followed quickly by repentance to Allah, but not when repentance comes at death. In Surah 7: The Heights (Al-A'raaf), sin includes indecency, wrongful oppression, associating partners with Allah, denying revelations, and corrupting the earth, and people are punished for what they earn. In Surah 40: The Forgiver (Al-Ghaafir), Allah is the Forgiver of sin, while every soul is requited for what it has earned. In Surah 41: Explained in detail (Fussilat), evil deeds are not equal to good deeds, are held against their perpetrators, and are to be repelled with what is better. In Surah 42: Consultation (Ash-Shura), sin and indecency are among the worst offenses that believers shun, and Allah forgives much. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII, sin may be removed or atoned for through repentance, confession, and resolving not to repeat it, though some deeds have no atonement.

sin in Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where sin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sin is a wrong that may be removed or atoned for through repentance, confession, and a resolve not to repeat the deed. Under the law of Mazda, confession can remove many sins and penalties, including grave deeds, and may apply even to those not taught in that law. Yet some deeds are stated to have no atonement, including voluntary emission of seed under the stated rule.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard III.

Sin may be removed when a person confesses it and resolves never to commit the forbidden deed again. The law of Mazda takes away many sins and penalties, including breach of trust, murdering one of the faithful, burying a corpse, and deeds for which there is no atonement.

Fargard VIII.

Sin may be removed through confession under the law of Mazda for a person who is not a professor of that law or has not been taught in it. The law is said to remove any sin that may be sinned, while voluntary emission of seed is described as a deed for which there is no atonement for those subject to the stated rule.
