Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, Sraosha is a blessed Yazad identified with Obedience who strikes with victory and receives worship and offerings. In Yasna, Sraosha (Obedience) is a holy and beneficent presence accompanying sacrifice and receiving offerings, homage, and praise. In Yasna, Obedience is Ahura Mazda’s greatest assisting guardian, linked with long life and offered by Zarathustra in deed and speech. In Yasna, Sraosha is Obedience and Ahura Mazda’s messenger who protects creation and battles destructive forces. In Visparad, Sraosha is blessed Obedience who receives offerings and is summoned to aid worship and uphold the Yasna Haptanghâiti. In Afrinagan, Gahs and Miscellaneous Fragments, Sraosha is blessed Obedience who advances settlements, smites victoriously, and receives sacrifice and blessing in Gâh Ushahin. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Sraosha is a holy, strong, tall-formed incarnate Word who serves Fire, wakes Parôdars, questions the Drug, and smites Kunda. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, Sraosha is a holy, strong, fiend-smiting incarnate Word who guards creation and fights the Mâzainya Daêvas.

and 1 more work

In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, Sraosha is a holy, powerful incarnate Word and god of Asha whose assistance protects people in distress.

Sraosha in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Sraosha first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sraosha is a blessed Yazad identified with Obedience. He is good, holy, stately, mighty, and endowed with sanctity; Mãthra is his body. His central action is to smite or strike with victory, especially the blow of victory, thereby furthering or causing settlements to advance. Across the records, he is repeatedly propitiated and worshipped through sacrifice, a Yast, meat offerings, sacred flesh, and the flesh of the Kine of blessed gift. He is also a holy lord of the ritual order and has a daring spear. The descriptions move from his identity and victorious power to repeated ritual offerings and worship.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna / Yasna I.

Sraosha, identified with Obedience, is called blessed and endowed with blessed recompense. He is said to smite with victory and further the settlements.

Yasna III.

Sraosha is identified with Obedience and is described as blessed, holy, victorious in smiting, and causing the settlements to advance. He receives propitiation through offerings including meat and sacred flesh, and is approached with a Yast.

Yasna IV.

Sraosha receives offerings and is called blessed and mighty, with the Mãthra as his body. He is also described as having a daring spear, furthering settlements, and smiting with the blow of victory.

Yasna VI.

Sraosha is described as Obedience, blessed and stately, and as smiting with the blow of victory and making the settlements advance. He is worshipped with the sacrifice as a holy lord of the ritual order.

Yasna VII.

Sraosha is described as blessed, endowed with sanctity, victorious in striking, and causing the settlements to advance. Offerings are made for his propitiation, including the Myazda meat-offering and the flesh of the Kine of blessed gift.

Sraosha in Yasna

L.H. Mills · the work’s page · read it where Sraosha first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sraosha is Obedience, also described as listening or attentive Obedience, and Ahura Mazda’s messenger. He approaches Zarathustra with the Good Mind, questions him, and directs him toward the quiet, better mind’s teaching, while being asked to accompany him with holy recompense and spiritual splendour. Blessed, stately, holy, victorious, and devoted to Ahura Mazda, Sraosha is a ritual lord and chief: first to adore Ahura Mazda, first to worship the Bountiful Immortals, and first to chant Zarathustra’s five Gâthas. Present for worship and worshipped with sacrifice, he receives praises and aids holy creation. His central work is protection and conquest. He furthers and guards settlements, houses, people, Ahura Mazda’s creatures, and the corporeal world, keeping destructive terrors away and defending against unhappy death, Wrath, hostile hosts, and their assaults. Sleepless and vigilant, he battles the Daêvas day and night, slays them and the Lie-demon, smites Aêshema, and hews down Angra Mainyu, Rapine, and the Daêvas of Mazendran. A strong, persistent youth and mighty conqueror, he bears victory-making powers and weapons, including the Ahuna-vairya as an axe of victory and a battle-axe that cleaves Daêvas’ skulls. He travels from India in the East to the West, and by victorious power, wise conduct, and full knowledge causes the Bountiful Immortals to descend upon the earth. Within the sacrificer’s house, he governs and is asked to conquer disobedience.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna XLIII.

Sraosha is identified with Obedience and approaches Zarathustra with the Good Mind. Sraosha questions him, instructs him to let the quiet and better mind teach him, and is asked to accompany him with holy recompense and spiritual splendour.

Yasna LVI (Sp. LV).

Sraosha is identified with listening or attentive Obedience and is repeatedly invoked to be present for worship. Sraosha is associated with Ahura Mazda, the good waters, the Fravashis of the saints and their souls, the good and bountiful Immortals, and Blessedness. Sraosha is described as good, holy, blessed, and stately, as one who smites with victory and furthers settlements in their advance.

Yasna LVII (Sp. LVI).

Sraosha is worshipped as Obedience the blessed, the stately and victorious ritual lord who furthers settlements. He guards Ahura Mazda’s creatures, battles the Daêvas, protects houses and people, and is asked to grant speed, soundness, vigilance, and victory.

Yasna LX (Sp. LIX).

Sraosha, identified as Obedience, is asked to conquer disobedience within the house. He is also described as governing there and as receiving good Yasnas and praises.

Yasna LXX (Sp. LXIX).

He is described as blessed Obedience and is invoked to aid the worship. He is also worshipped with sacrifice.

Sraosha in Yashts (Yasts) I-XII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Sraosha first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sraosha is a good, holy god and master of holiness: tall-formed, strong, fiend-smiting, incarnate Word, and mighty-speared. Invoked with Ahura Mazda’s invocation, he comes for help and joy, and with Vohu-Manô helps those whom Ahura Mazda pleases. He makes the world and material world grow, maintains and watches over them, and is the poor’s best protector. Never sleeping, he guards Mazda’s creation, protects faithful people and houses, and stands with his uplifted club from sunset. Every day and night he fights Mâzainya Daêvas, does not bow before Daêvas, and is linked with Peace, Rashnu Razista, Ashi Vanguhi, and Mithra. Offerings and prayers honor his holiness, brightness, glory, strength, and victorious power through all his moments.

Sraosha is described as holy, tall-formed, strong, fiend-smiting, and the master of holiness.
