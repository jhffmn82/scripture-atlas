Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, it was the Babylonian siege of Jerusalem that lasted until Zedekiah's eleventh year, when famine was severe and a breach was made in the city. In The Book of Jeremiah, it was a Babylonian assault by the Chaldeans under Nebuchadnezzar against Jerusalem and other fortified cities, with siege ramps and a temporary withdrawal before the city was taken and burned. In The Book of Jeremiah, it was Nebuchadnezzar's military blockade of Jerusalem, ending in famine, a breach, the capture of Zedekiah, the burning of the city, and exile of survivors. In The Book of Ezekiel, it was the approach of the king of Babylon to Jerusalem, followed by the city's destruction portrayed through the cauldron parable. In The Book of Ezekiel, it was symbolically enacted on a tile with forts, a mound, camps, battering rams, and an iron pan surrounding Jerusalem.

siege of Jerusalem in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · 629 to 579 BC · the work’s page · read it where siege of Jerusalem first appears

Abstract written by a model

The siege of Jerusalem was a Babylonian assault fought by the Chaldeans, conducted by Nebuchadnezzar's army in Zedekiah's tenth year (Nebuchadnezzar's eighteenth), also against Judah's other fortified cities such as Lachish and Azekah. Siege ramps were built, and Zedekiah hoped the LORD would force a withdrawal. The LORD instead declared he would fight against the city and give it to Babylon to be burned. Jeremiah linked it to sword, famine, and pestilence. When the Chaldeans briefly withdrew on news of Pharaoh's army, the LORD said it was temporary: they would return, take, and burn the city, as bread ran short.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

Nebuchadnezzar makes war against Judah, and the Chaldeans besiege the city outside the walls. Zedekiah hopes the LORD will cause the attackers to withdraw. The LORD instead says he will turn the defenders' weapons back, fight against the city himself, and give it into Babylon's hand to be burned.

Chapter 32

The king of Babylon's army is besieging Jerusalem in the tenth year of Zedekiah and the eighteenth of Nebuchadnezzar, with siege ramps built against the city. Jeremiah says it has come about through sword, famine, and pestilence, and the LORD confirms the city will be taken and burned.

Chapter 34

Nebuchadnezzar's forces are fighting against Jerusalem and the remaining fortified cities of Judah, Lachish and Azekah, as the chapter opens. The LORD declares the city will fall and be burned, and that the army, though it has gone away, will be commanded to return and take it.

Chapter 37

The Chaldeans' siege of Jerusalem is lifted for a time when they withdraw on hearing of Pharaoh's army coming out of Egypt. The LORD says through Jeremiah that this is temporary, as the Chaldeans will return and take and burn the city. The closing mention of the city's bread running out points to the siege's pressure.

siege of Jerusalem in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · 629 to 579 BC · the work’s page · read it where siege of Jerusalem first appears

Abstract written by a model

The siege of Jerusalem was Nebuchadnezzar's military blockade of the city, in which he and his whole army built forts around it. It began on the tenth day of the tenth month of Zedekiah's ninth year and lasted into his eleventh year. By the ninth day of the fourth month famine was severe and bread was gone. A breach was made, the men of war fled by night, and Zedekiah fled and was captured. The houses were burned, walls broken, and survivors taken captive.
