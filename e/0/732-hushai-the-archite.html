Hushai the Archite

Abstract written by a model

Hushai the Archite is a friend of David who appears as a double-agent figure in the records. Meeting David on the Mount of Olives in mourning, he is told he would be a burden and is sent instead to serve Absalom, defeat Ahithophel's counsel, and report to the priests Zadok and Abiathar. He enters the city, pledges allegiance to Absalom, declaring he will serve whom the LORD and people choose. He then argues Ahithophel's advice is poor, urging a full gathering of Israel; Absalom accepts it, and Hushai warns David through the priests.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 15

Hushai met David at the top of the Mount of Olives with torn tunic and earth on his head. David told him he would be a burden if he came along, and instead sent him to offer service to Absalom in order to defeat Ahithophel's counsel and report what he hears to Zadok and Abiathar. Hushai, called David's friend, entered the city.

Chapter 16

Hushai, David's friend, comes to Absalom and cries "Long live the king!" When Absalom asks why he did not go with his friend, Hushai answers that he will serve whomever the LORD, the people and Israel have chosen, and the son as he served the father.

Chapter 17

Hushai told Absalom that Ahithophel's counsel was not good, describing David and his men as fierce mighty men and urging that all Israel be gathered and Absalom go to battle himself. His counsel was accepted, and he then told Zadok and Abiathar the priests to send word quickly to David not to lodge at the fords of the wilderness.
