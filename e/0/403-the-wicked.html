Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the proud and wicked are portrayed as the psalmist’s enemies who slander, mock, persecute, and trap him. In Proverbs, the wicked are portrayed as people defined by habitual evil, deceit, violence, and an end in calamity. In The Book of Ezekiel, the wicked die in their iniquity if they are warned but do not turn from their wicked way.

the proud and wicked (enemies of the psalmist) in Psalms

author unknown · undated · the work’s page · read it where the proud and wicked (enemies of the psalmist) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The proud and wicked are the psalmist's enemies: callous-hearted, far from and forsaking the LORD's law, lacking his salvation. They slander, mock, persecute, and trap the psalmist with pits, snares, and lies. The LORD rebukes them, rejects the straying, and discards them like dross; the psalmist grieves and asks that they be disappointed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 119

The proud and wicked slander, mock, and persecute the psalmist, dig pits and lay snares and ropes for him, and smear him with lies. They forsake and break the law and are far from it, and the psalmist feels indignation and sorrow over them. The LORD is said to have rebuked the proud, to reject those who stray, and to put away the wicked like dross, and the psalmist asks that the proud be disappointed.

the wicked in Proverbs

author unknown · 969 to 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where the wicked first appears

Abstract written by a model

The wicked are portrayed as a class of people defined by habitual evil. They cannot rest unless they do wrong, living on the bread of wickedness and wine of violence, walking a dark path where they stumble unknowingly. Their advice is deceitful, their mercies cruel, their mouths gush evil, and their hearts plot violence and desire plunder. They respond to reproof with abuse. Their sacrifices and ways are an abomination to the LORD, who is far from them, and their income brings trouble. The portrait moves from their habits and darkness, to their deceit and misleading way, to their standing before the LORD, and finally to their end: overthrown by calamity, with no reward and a snuffed-out lamp. The son is warned not to envy them, and whoever calls them righteous is cursed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

The wicked do not sleep unless they do evil, and their sleep is taken away unless they make someone fall. They eat the bread of wickedness and drink the wine of violence. Their way is like darkness, and they do not know what they stumble over.

Chapter 12

The wicked give deceitful advice and speak words that lie in wait for blood. They are overthrown and are no more, and they are not established by wickedness. Their tender mercies are cruel, they desire the plunder of evil men, and they are filled with evil. Their way leads them astray.

Chapter 15

The wicked offer sacrifices and follow ways that are an abomination to the LORD, and their income brings trouble. Their mouths gush out evil, their thoughts are detested, and the LORD is far from them.

Chapter 24

Evil men have hearts that plot violence and lips that talk mischief, and the son is told not to envy them or be with them. Those who plot evil are called schemers, and the wicked are overthrown by calamity, receive no reward, and have their lamp snuffed out. Whoever calls the wicked righteous will be cursed by peoples and abhorred by nations.
