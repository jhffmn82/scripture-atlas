Paul and his fellow ministers (we)

Abstract written by a model

The entity is Paul and his fellow ministers, a group of apostolic servants who write to the Corinthian believers and describe who they are and what they do. They say they need no letters of commendation, since the Corinthians themselves are the letter of Christ. Their confidence and sufficiency come from God, not themselves, and God has made them servants of a new covenant. Most important is their ministry of reconciliation: God gave them the word of reconciliation, and they act as ambassadors for Christ, urging people to be reconciled to God. They preach Jesus Christ as Lord, not themselves, and serve the Corinthians for Jesus' sake. The account unfolds in four movements. First, they speak boldly with unveiled faces, being transformed into the Lord's image from glory to glory by the Spirit. Second, having received mercy, they do not faint and renounce shameful hidden things. They hold treasure in clay vessels, so the power is God's, and though the outward person decays, the inward is renewed daily. Third, they groan in the earthly tent, long for the heavenly habitation, and walk by faith rather than sight, aiming to please Christ and driven by his love. Finally, they commend themselves as God's servants through hardships, beatings, imprisonments, and labors, yet are always rejoicing, poor yet making many rich. They close by declaring an open mouth and enlarged heart toward the Corinthians, speaking as to children and asking for the same openness in return.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

The writers ask whether they are commending themselves again and say they need no letters of commendation. They say they are not sufficient of themselves, but God made them sufficient as servants of a new covenant. Because of their hope they speak with great boldness, unlike Moses, and they, with all believers, are transformed with unveiled faces.

Chapter 4

The ministers say they do not faint because they have received mercy and have this ministry. They have renounced shameful hidden things and deceit, preach Christ Jesus as Lord rather than themselves, and see themselves as servants of the believers for Jesus' sake. Though their outward person decays, their inward person is renewed daily, and they fix their gaze on unseen, eternal things.

Chapter 5

The ministers groan in the tent, long to be clothed with the heavenly habitation, and are confident, walking by faith and not by sight. They aim to please the Lord, persuade men knowing the fear of the Lord, and are compelled by Christ's love. They have been given the ministry and word of reconciliation and act as ambassadors for Christ, begging people to be reconciled to God.

Chapter 6

The speaker and his co-workers entreat the Corinthians not to receive God's grace in vain and give no occasion of stumbling so their service is not blamed. They commend themselves as servants of God through endurance, sufferings, and virtues, and are described by paradoxes such as dying yet living and having nothing yet possessing all things. They say their mouth and heart are open to the Corinthians and ask for the same in return, speaking to them as to children.
