Wisdom

topic · 1 work · 10 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: fulness of knowledge, knowledge, knowledge of the known, knowledge of the unknown, man's knowledge, wisdom, wisdom of Sages

Abstract written by a model

Wisdom is a limited form of knowledge that depends on fulfilment and on what remains unknown, extending toward apprehension of God. It is attained by knowing what God is and what Man is: recognizing divine origin, resting in the known, and awaiting the unknown. Begotten from repose, it originally lacked purpose, then became ornamented with rhetoric and exhausted the empire as learning overwhelmed the mind. Wisdom shines when small wisdom is discarded and arises from an unobstructed mind; unobstructed wisdom yields TÊ. It has no limit in one account, yet its limits appear when even the divine tortoise cannot avoid capture and death.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Knowledge requires fulfilment, which is uncertain during life. A person knows God by recognizing his own divine origin and knows Man by resting in the known while waiting for knowledge of the unknown.

CHAPTER XVI.

Knowledge was begotten out of repose and was not initially applied to any purpose. Later, knowledge was ornamented with rhetoric and used in ways that exhausted the empire, while learning overwhelmed the mind.

Kuan Chung being at the point of death, Duke Huan went to see him.

Human knowledge is limited, yet what is not known supports the extension of knowledge toward the apprehension of God. Knowledge of the great ONE, Negative, Nomenclature, Uniformity, Space, Truth, and Law is called perfection.

CHAPTER XXVI.

Wisdom arises when the mind is unobstructed, and when wisdom itself is unobstructed the result is TÊ. The unit also teaches that wisdom has limits, since even the divine tortoise’s wisdom cannot protect it from capture and death.
