Onias in The Second Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 124 BC · the work’s page · read it where Onias first appears

Abstract written by a model

Onias is Jerusalem's godly high priest, noble, gentle and virtue-trained, who hated wickedness so laws were observed. He disagreed with Simon over the market, protected widows', orphans' and Hyrcanus's deposits from Heliodorus, then sacrificed for Heliodorus's recovery, whose life was granted for his sake.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

Onias, the high priest whose godliness and hatred of wickedness kept Jerusalem's laws well observed, disagreed with Simon over the city market. He told Heliodorus that the treasury held deposits of widows, orphans, and Hyrcanus son of Tobias, and that these could not be wronged. Fearing the king might suspect treachery, he offered a sacrifice for Heliodorus's recovery, and the two young men said Heliodorus was granted life for Onias's sake.

Onias in The Second Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 124 BC · the work’s page · read it where Onias first appears

Abstract written by a model

Onias is a high priest, benefactor of the city, guardian of his countrymen and zealot for the laws. He urged the king to secure the people's good, was supplanted by his brother Jason, withdrew to a sanctuary at Daphne, reproved Menelaus for temple theft, and was lured out by Andronicus and killed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Onias, the high priest, appealed to the king not to accuse his fellow citizens but to secure the good of all the people, since Simon would not cease his madness. He was supplanted by his brother Jason, later withdrew to a sanctuary at Daphne and reproved Menelaus for the theft of temple vessels, and was lured out by Andronicus under oath and killed. He is described as the benefactor of the city, guardian of his countrymen, and zealot for the laws.
