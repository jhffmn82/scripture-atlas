Abstract written by a model

In Mencius, Book I (Liang Hui Wang), king Xuan of Qi is the king of Qi who discusses royal government, pleasures, compassion, conquest, and threats to his kingdom with Mencius. In Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou), king Xuan of Qi respects Mencius, sends aid during his illness, gives him a present, appoints Wang Huan, and later asks him to come to court. In Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou), king Xuan of Qi acknowledges guilt, feels ashamed before Mencius, and is hoped to change, do good, and employ Mencius. In Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang), king Xuan of Qi asks Mencius about high ministers and reacts with alarm before composing himself to ask about ministers of a different surname. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), king Xuan of Qi is described as a king whose distinctive bearing comes from his position, and he is noted as having a son and wanting to shorten mourning.

king Xuan of Qi in Mencius, Book I (Liang Hui Wang)

Mencius · about 299 BC · the work’s page · read it where king Xuan of Qi first appears

Abstract written by a model

King Xuan of Qi is the king of Qi, a ruler who seeks to enlarge his territories and rule the Middle Kingdom, often through war. Across repeated interviews with Mencius, he asks about royal government, royal sway, relations with neighbouring kingdoms, ministers, ability, punishment, conquest, and threats to Qi. He displays compassion toward an ox, which Mencius treats as evidence of compassion sufficient to reach animals, and he is told he can attain royal sway if he extends concern to his people. His tastes and infirmities include present-age music, hunting, valour, wealth, and beauty. Mencius repeatedly connects these pleasures to government: the king can gain royal sway by sharing pleasure with the people and can attain true royal government by enabling them to gratify their own love of wealth and beauty. The king also considers his park, the Hall of Distinction, and pleasure in the Snow palace. Though he can judge that neglectful friends, incapable judges, and unregulated officers should be removed, he evades a question about the lack of good government in his kingdom. Later, he asks how to judge and appoint men, whether ministers may kill sovereigns, and whether Qi should take Yan. Mencius warns that possession of Yan depends on Yan’s people being pleased, and advises restoring captives, ceasing removal of precious vessels, consulting Yan’s people about a ruler, and withdrawing to avert attack and calamity.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

12 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

King Xuan of Qi asks Mencius about Huan of Qi, Wen of Jin, royal government, and the royal sway. He acknowledges that his actions have been misunderstood, states that he desires to enlarge his territories and rule the Middle Kingdom, and asks Mencius to teach him how to proceed.

Chapter 1

The king of Qi tells Mencius that he cannot love the music of the ancient sovereigns and instead loves music suited to the present age. He enjoys music and hunting while the people are distressed, but Mencius says that if he shares pleasure with the people, royal sway awaits him.

Chapter 2

King Xuan of Qi asks Mencius whether king Wen's park contained seventy square li and why his own smaller park is considered large by the people.

Chapter 3

King Xuan of Qi asks Mencius about maintaining intercourse with neighbouring kingdoms. He says that he loves valour, and Mencius urges him to use a greater valour to give repose to all the people of the kingdom.

Chapter 4

King Xuan of Qi had an interview with Mencius in the Snow palace and asked whether men of talents and worth also found pleasure in such things.

Chapter 5

king Xuan of Qi considers whether to pull down the Hall of Distinction and asks Mencius to explain true royal government. He says that he is fond of wealth and beauty.

Chapter 6

The king says that a friend who lets a minister’s wife and children suffer from cold and hunger should be cast off. He says that a chief criminal judge who cannot regulate the officers under him should be dismissed, but he avoids Mencius’s question about the kingdom’s lack of good government by speaking of other matters.

Chapter 7

King Xuan of Qi interviews Mencius and asks how to recognize whether men lack ability so that he can avoid employing them. He is told to investigate widely supported judgments before appointing, dismissing, or executing anyone.

Chapter 8

King Xuan of Qi asks Mencius whether Tang banished Jie and king Wu smote Zhou. He then asks whether a minister may put his sovereign to death.

Chapter 9

King Xuan of Qi is addressed by Mencius about his approach to government. He is described as wanting a man to put aside what he has learned and follow him, despite valuing suitable materials and skilled work in other contexts.

Chapter 10

King Xuan asked whether he should take possession of Yan after Qi conquered it. Mencius advised him to do so only if the people of Yan would be pleased, and warned that otherwise they would revolt.

Chapter 11

King Xuan asked Mencius how to prepare for the princes’ plans to attack Qi. Mencius urged him to restore the captives, stop removing Yan’s precious vessels, appoint a ruler after consulting Yan’s people, and withdraw.

king Xuan of Qi in Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin)

Mencius · about 399 to 200 BC · the work’s page · read it where king Xuan of Qi first appears

Abstract written by a model

King Xuan of Qi is a king whose residence, carriages, horses, and dress resemble other men’s, while his distinctive air and appearance arise from his peculiar position in the wide house of the world. He has a son and later wants to shorten mourning.
