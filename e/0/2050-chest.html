author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where chest (Jehoiada's chest) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jehoiada's chest is a lidded container with a hole he bored, placed beside the altar on the right as one enters the LORD's house. Threshold-keeping priests deposited the temple money into it; once full, the king's scribe and high priest bagged and counted it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 12

Jehoiada took a chest and bored a hole in its lid, then set it beside the altar on the right side as one comes into the LORD's house. The threshold-keeping priests put all the money brought to the house into it. When it held much money, the king's scribe and the high priest came up, bagged and counted the money.

chest in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where chest first appears

Abstract written by a model

A chest made at the king's command and placed outside the gate of the LORD's house to collect Moses' tax. Princes and people filled it; the scribe and chief priest's officer emptied it daily, gathering abundant money.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 24

At the king's command a chest was made and set outside the gate of the LORD's house to receive the tax of Moses. The princes and people filled it, and the king's scribe and the chief priest's officer emptied it and returned it to its place day by day, gathering money in abundance.
