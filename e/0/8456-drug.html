Abstract written by a model

The Demon-of-the-Lie is a rebellious, harmful demon opposed by Righteousness’s friends and inspiring the foes. She gives them no light; the speaker seeks her banishment, delivery to the Righteous Order, death through doctrine, destruction of her evil believers, and smiting through action in accordance with the Righteous Order.

Drug Nasu in Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Drug Nasu first appears

Abstract written by a model

Drug Nasu is a polluting, corpse-associated defiling fiend, appearing as a raging fly. It rushes forth when a person or certain dogs die, comes from and is driven toward northern regions, and spreads infection, pollution, and uncleanness. Its defilement varies with the dead person’s status, affecting nearby beings, people resting together, corpses, bedding, clothing, and water; a priest’s death defiles ten and a warrior’s nine. It pollutes ponds, wells, snow, hail, and running water when a corpse is present, with specified purification. After death it rushes upon a dead man, and it also attacks those who eat corpses or bring corpses with filth into water or fire. Dogs, birds, water, and fiend-smiting words can drive or smite it. In cleansing, water drives it down the body until it leaves through the toes and soles toward the north; whether corpse-eating dogs or birds smote it affects the required number of washes.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard V.

The Drug Nasu rushes forth when a person or certain dogs die and defiles nearby beings according to the stated order. It does not defile any creature when an Urupi dog dies, except the person who smites and kills it.

Fargard VI.

Drug Nasu defiles parts of ponds, wells, snow or hail, and running water when a corpse is present. The extent of defilement and the means of purification are specified for each kind of water.

Fargard VII.

She rushes upon a dead man after death, or at the next watch in certain cases, in the shape of a raging fly. She defiles nearby people, bedding, clothing, and other things, and attacks those who eat corpses or bring corpses with filth into water or fire.

Fargard VIII.

The Drug Nasu is described as flying away to the north in the shape of a stained, raging fly when a suitable dog is led along a corpse-bearing way. During purification it rushes from one body part to another as water reaches each place, finally leaving through the toes and soles toward the north.

VIII.

Nasu is treated as having been or not having been smitten by corpse-eating dogs or birds. This condition determines whether the man who touched the corpse must wash thirty times or fifteen times.

Drug in Vendidad (Vendîdâd)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Drug first appears

Abstract written by a model

Drug is a fiendish, wretched, wicked demon associated with defilement by the dead and identified as Bûiti and the unseen death. It rushes from the dead upon the living, defiling them and seeking to give the living world of the holy spirit to death. It becomes weaker or fades when prescribed Gâthic words and words that smite Angra Mainyu, Aêshma, the Mâzainya fiends, and all fiends are recited. It has progeny through four males: one who withholds treasured riches or refuses a small gift, one who urinates on the upper forepart of his foot, one who emits seed during sleep, and a person over fifteen without the sacred girdle or sacred shirt. Drug rushes to destroy Zarathustra but fears his glory, is dismayed by the Ahuna-Vairya, and flees. It is opposed by Zarathustra and the good creatures, smitten by invoked powers, and commanded to perish and depart with the brood and world of the fiend to the regions of the north, never again to give the living world unto death.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard IX.

The Drug becomes weaker at the words that smite Angra Mainyu, Aêshma, the Mâzainya fiends, and all the fiends. The Drug is also said to fade away when the prescribed Gâthic words are recited.

Fargard X.

The Drug is described as rushing from the dead upon the living and defiling the living. The prescribed words are said to stand against it.

Fargard XI.

Drug is commanded to perish and rush away to the regions of the north, never again giving the living world of the holy spirit unto death.

Fargard XVIII.

The Drug is a wretched and wicked demon who says that four males make her conceive progeny. These are the person who refuses a small gift, the man who lets urine fall along the upper forepart of his foot, the man who emits seed during sleep, and a person over fifteen who goes without the sacred girdle or sacred shirt.

Fargard XIX.

The Drug, identified as the demon Bûiti and the unseen death, rushes to destroy Zarathustra but is dismayed by the Ahuna-Vairya and flees. It later appears as an enemy opposed by Zarathustra and the good creatures.

Fargard XX.

The Drug is to be smitten by the might of the invoked powers. It is commanded to perish and depart to the regions of the north.

Fargard XXI.

The fiendish Drug is commanded to perish, along with the brood and world of the fiend. It is driven away toward the regions of the north so that it will no longer give the living world of the holy spirit to death.
