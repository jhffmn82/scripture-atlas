Hezekiah

person · 4 works · 72 quoted facts · 15 connections
Also called: Hezekiah king of Judah, Hezekiah the king, Hezekiah the son of Ahaz, King Hezekiah, This same Hezekiah, his servant Hezekiah, the king, the prince of my people, the same Hezekiah, your master

Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Hezekiah is a reforming king of Judah who trusts the LORD, resists Assyria, prays during crises, receives fifteen additional years of life, shows Babylonian envoys his treasures, and dies succeeded by Manasseh. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Hezekiah is a faithful king of Judah who reforms worship, leads the Passover, prepares for Sennacherib's invasion, prospers, humbles himself after pride, and dies succeeded by Manasseh. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Hezekiah is a Davidic king who fortified Jerusalem, built waterworks, called on the Lord during Sennacherib's invasion, and had his life prolonged. In The Book of Isaiah, Hezekiah is a king of Judah who prays during Assyria's invasion and his illness, receives deliverance and fifteen more years, and shows Babylonian envoys his treasures before accepting Isaiah's prophecy.

Hezekiah in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hezekiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hezekiah, son of Ahaz and Abi, was a king of Judah who began reigning at twenty-five and ruled twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. He did right in the LORD's eyes as David had: removing high places, breaking pillars, cutting down the Asherah, and breaking the bronze serpent Nehushtan. He trusted the LORD, unmatched among Judah's kings, kept the commandments given to Moses, rebelled against Assyria, and struck the Philistines as far as Gaza. When Sennacherib seized Judah's fortified cities, he admitted offense and paid tribute, stripping the temple and palace treasuries and the temple's gold doors. After Rabshakeh's threats, he tore his clothes, wore sackcloth, sought Isaiah's prayer, spread Sennacherib's letter before the LORD, and prayed; the LORD promised to defend Jerusalem and the Assyrian threat ended. Sick with a boil and near death, he prayed, claiming he walked in truth with a perfect heart, and was granted fifteen more years, with the shadow going back ten steps as the sign. He then showed Babylon's envoys all his treasures and armor; Isaiah prophesied Babylonian plunder, and Hezekiah called it good, hoping for peace in his days. He built a pool and conduit bringing water into the city. He died, and his son Manasseh reigned.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

Hezekiah son of Ahaz began to reign over Judah at twenty-five, reigned twenty-nine years, and did right in the LORD's eyes by removing the high places, breaking the pillars, cutting down the Asherah, and breaking the bronze serpent Nehushtan. He trusted the LORD, prospered, rebelled against Assyria, and struck the Philistines to Gaza. When Sennacherib took Judah's fortified cities, he sent to him at Lachish, admitted offense, and paid three hundred talents of silver and thirty of gold, stripping the temple and palace treasuries and the temple's gold doors and pillars. He then received the report of Rabshakeh's words from his officials.

Chapter 19

Hezekiah tears his clothes, puts on sackcloth, goes to the LORD's house, and sends messengers to Isaiah asking for prayer for the remnant. After receiving Sennacherib's letter, he spreads it before the LORD and prays for deliverance. He is rewarded with the LORD's promise to defend Jerusalem, and the Assyrian threat ends.

Chapter 20

Hezekiah, sick and dying, prays and weeps, and is granted healing and fifteen more years. He chooses the shadow going back ten steps as the sign, then shows Babylon's envoys all his treasures and armor. After hearing Isaiah's prophecy of Babylonian plunder, he calls it good and hopes for peace and truth in his days; he dies and his son Manasseh reigns in his place.

Hezekiah in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hezekiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hezekiah is a king of Judah, son of Ahaz and Abijah's child, who begins to reign at twenty-five and rules twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. He does right in the LORD's eyes, following David his father, and is described as good, right and faithful, seeking his God with all his heart and prospering. In his first year he opens and repairs the temple doors, has the priests and Levites cleanse the house, and leads the offerings and singing. He sends couriers with letters to all Israel and Judah, including Ephraim and Manasseh, to keep the Passover in Jerusalem, after taking counsel with his princes. He prays for the unclean, and the LORD listens. He appoints priests and Levites into divisions, provides the king's portion for burnt offerings, commands the people to give the portions due, and has rooms prepared and overseers appointed. He removes high places and altars so worship is at one altar. Facing Sennacherib, he stops the springs, redirects Gihon, repairs the wall, strengthens Millo, makes weapons, sets captains and encourages the people, praying with Isaiah. After the deliverance he is exalted in the sight of all nations and gains exceedingly great riches from God, with treasuries, storehouses, cities, flocks and herds. He falls ill and is given a sign, becomes proud and brings wrath, then humbles himself; God tests him over the Babylonian ambassadors. He dies and is buried at the ascent to the tombs of the sons of David, and his son Manasseh succeeds him. His acts are recorded in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel and in Isaiah's vision.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 29

Hezekiah begins to reign at twenty-five, reigns twenty-nine years in Jerusalem, and does right in the LORD's eyes like David his father. In his first year he opens and repairs the temple doors, commands the priests and Levites to cleanse the house, and plans to make a covenant with the LORD. He leads the sin offering, commands the burnt offering and the singing, invites the assembly to bring offerings, and rejoices with the people when the service is set in order.

Chapter 30

Hezekiah sent letters to all Israel and Judah, including Ephraim and Manasseh, calling them to keep the Passover in Jerusalem in the second month after taking counsel with his princes and the assembly. He prayed for the unclean who sought God, spoke encouragingly to the Levites, and gave one thousand bulls and seven thousand sheep for offerings, and the LORD listened to him.

Chapter 31

Hezekiah appointed the divisions of the priests and Levites, provided the king's portion for the burnt offerings, and commanded the people of Jerusalem to give the portion due to the priests and Levites. He blessed the LORD and Israel on seeing the heaps, questioned the priests about them, ordered rooms prepared in the LORD's house, and appointed overseers. He is described as doing what was good, right and faithful, and as prospering because he sought God with all his heart.

Chapter 32

Hezekiah prepares Jerusalem against Sennacherib by stopping the springs, repairing the wall, strengthening Millo, making weapons, setting captains, and encouraging the people, and with Isaiah he prays and cries to heaven. After being delivered he is exalted, becomes very wealthy, falls ill and is given a sign, but becomes proud and brings wrath, then humbles himself; God tests him over the Babylonian ambassadors. He dies and is buried with honor, and Manasseh succeeds him.

Hezekiah in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · 739 to 680 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hezekiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hezekiah is a king of Judah, a descendant of David who removed high places and altars and commanded worship before one altar. The records follow him through four crises. In the fourteenth year of his reign, Assyria captures Judah's fortified cities and Rabshakeh mocks his trust in the LORD; Hezekiah tears his clothes, wears sackcloth, goes to the LORD's house, sends to Isaiah, and spreads Sennacherib's letter before the LORD, praying for deliverance. The LORD defends the city for his own sake and David's. When Hezekiah is sick and near death, he prays and weeps; the LORD grants fifteen more years and forgives his sins. He resolves to walk carefully and worship in the LORD's house all his life. Pleased by Babylon's letters and gift, he shows the envoys all his treasures, silver, gold, spices, oil, and armor. Isaiah announces coming loss, yet Hezekiah calls the word good, expecting peace and truth in his days.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 36

Hezekiah is king of Judah in the fourteenth year of his reign, when Assyria captures Judah's fortified cities. Rabshakeh accuses him of removing the LORD's high places and altars and of deceiving the people into trusting the LORD. He commands that Rabshakeh not be answered and receives his officials' report of the Assyrian's words.

Chapter 37

Hezekiah tears his clothes, wears sackcloth, goes to the LORD's house, and sends envoys to Isaiah asking for prayer for the remnant. After receiving Sennacherib's letter, he spreads it before the LORD and prays that the LORD save Judah so all kingdoms may know he alone is the LORD. The LORD answers through Isaiah and defends the city for his own sake and for David's sake.

Chapter 38

Hezekiah, sick and near death, is told by Isaiah that he will die, so he turns his face to the wall, prays, appeals to his faithful walk before the LORD, and weeps bitterly. The LORD grants him fifteen more years and promises to deliver him and the city from the king of Assyria. He recovers, writes a song of lament and thanks, and asks what the sign will be that he will go up to the LORD's house.

Chapter 39

Hezekiah is pleased by the letters and gift from Babylon and shows the envoys everything in his house and dominion, withholding nothing. When Isaiah questions him and announces the LORD's word of coming loss, Hezekiah calls the word good and says there will be peace and truth in his days.
