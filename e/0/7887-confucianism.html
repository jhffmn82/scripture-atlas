Confucianism

topic · 1 work · 5 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Confucianist, Confucianist doctrines

Abstract written by a model

Confucianism is a practical, utilitarian view of TAO centered on action, effort, benevolence, unselfishness, ceremonies, music, worldly wisdom, charity, duty to one’s neighbour, and moral reputation. It is limited to the finite in practice and antagonistic to Taoism, whose mystical emphasis reacts against it. Over time it triumphs and becomes established, leaving Taoists as dissenters. It is then attacked as adding harmful artificial practices to natural life, while Huan’s influence and warnings about Confucius expose conflict, outward show, and false learning.

Confucianism is in one work on this site, Chuang Tzŭ. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

_Note on the Philosophy of Chaps._ i-vii.

Confucianism is described as a practical system concerned with action, effort, benevolence, unselfishness, ceremonies, music, and worldly wisdom. Taoism is presented as reacting against its limitation to the finite.

The triumph of Confucianism in the centuries which intervened between

Confucianism is described as triumphing or becoming established over time, while its practical and utilitarian view of TAO diverges from Taoism's mystical emphasis. Mencius regards Lao Tzŭ as a heresiarch, and Confucianism's dominance leaves Taoists as dissenters.

CHAPTER VIII.

Confucianism is the target of the chapter's violent tirade. Its leading doctrines, especially charity, duty to one's neighbour, ceremonies, music, and moral reputation, are criticized as harmful artificial additions to natural life.

CHAPTER XXXII.

Confucianism is presented through Huan's study and influence, which lead his younger brother toward Mihism and contribute to conflict with their father. Yen Ho also warns that making Confucius a pillar of the state would promote outward show and false learning over what is true.
