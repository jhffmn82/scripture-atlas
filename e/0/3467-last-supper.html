Abstract written by a model

In The Good News According to Mark, the Last Supper meal is the Passover meal where Jesus gives bread as his body and the cup as his blood of the new covenant. In The Gospel According to Luke, the Last Supper is a Passover meal where Jesus gives the bread as his body and the cup as the new covenant in his blood. In The Gospel According to John, the supper is the setting before Passover where Jesus washes the disciples’ feet and identifies Judas as his betrayer. In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, the Lord's Supper is the bread and cup instituted by Jesus, sharing in his body and blood and commemorating his death.

Last Supper in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Last Supper first appears

Abstract written by a model

A Passover meal in a large, furnished upper room with the twelve apostles. Jesus shares a cup, gives bread as his body and the cup as the new covenant in his blood, announces a betrayer, hears a dispute over greatness, and warns Peter of denial.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 22

At the meal Jesus shares a cup, then breaks bread and gives it as his body given for the apostles, telling them to do this in memory of him. After supper he gives the cup as the new covenant in his blood, poured out for them. During the meal he announces that his betrayer is at the table, the apostles dispute who is greatest, and he warns Peter of denial.

Lord's Supper in Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Lord's Supper first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Lord's Supper is the bread and cup that Paul received from the Lord Jesus, instituted on the night he was betrayed. The cup and bread share in Christ's blood and body, making the many one body and excluding demons' table. Paul rebukes the Corinthians' selfish meal as not the Lord's supper, presenting it as a memorial proclaiming his death until he comes, and warns against unworthy eating.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10

The cup of blessing is called a sharing of the blood of Christ, and the broken bread a sharing of his body. Because there is one loaf, the many who partake are one body. Partaking of the Lord's table excludes partaking of the table of demons.

Chapter 11

Paul says the Corinthians' meal is not the Lord's supper, because each eats his own supper first, so one is hungry and another drunken. He recalls the Lord's bread and cup as a memorial that proclaims the Lord's death until he comes, and warns that eating or drinking unworthily without discerning the Lord's body brings guilt and judgment.
