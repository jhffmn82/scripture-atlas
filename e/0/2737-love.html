love

Abstract written by a model

Love is portrayed as a overwhelming force. In Chapter 2 it is a banner over the speaker, who is faint with it, and it is expressed as mutual belonging: 'My beloved is mine, and I am his.' The daughters of Jerusalem are charged not to stir or awaken it before it desires. Chapter 8 climaxes: love is strong as death, fiery like a flame of the LORD, unquenchable by floods, and priceless beyond all wealth.

Chapter 2

Love is the banner over the speaker at the banquet hall, and she is faint with it. It is to be neither stirred up nor awakened until it so desires, as she charges the daughters of Jerusalem. Mutual belonging is stated in the line 'My beloved is mine, and I am his.'

Chapter 8

Love is described as strong as death, with jealousy as cruel as Sheol and flashes like fire, a very flame of the LORD. Many waters cannot quench it nor floods drown it, and a man who offered all the wealth of his house for it would be utterly scorned. The daughters of Jerusalem are adjured not to stir up or awaken it until it so desires.
