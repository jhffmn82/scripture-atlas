Self-control

topic · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: control themselves and subdue their senses, controlling myself, restrained, rules of self-control, self-control, self-restraint, subdued his senses

Abstract written by a model

Self-control is a way of life and a means of subduing oneself, preferable to subjection through fetters and corporal punishment. Applied by one who has heard and believed the Law, it restrains the ascetic, shakes off sinfulness, and requires control of the senses. Saints surpass householders in it; with penance it may lead to high regions or liberation. It includes right exertion, tranquillity, protection by the five Samvaras, rejection of heretical doctrines, austerities, and self-restraint, as practised by Harikêsa, Gaya, and other kings.

Uttaradhyayana Sutra / First Lecture.

Self-control is used to subdue the self and is preferable to being subdued by others with fetters and corporal punishment.

Third Lecture.

Self-control is to be applied by one who has been born as a man, heard the Law, and believed in it. It restrains the ascetic and helps shake off sinfulness.

Fifth Lecture.

Self-control is practised by those who control themselves and subdue their senses. Saints are superior to all householders in self-control, and those trained in self-control and penance may attain high regions or liberation.

Twelfth Lecture

Harikêsa is praised as one who subdues his senses and observes self-control. He teaches that self-control, right exertion, and tranquillity are the oblations of the true sacrifice, and that protection by the five Samvaras leads to victory.

Eighteenth Lecture.

Self-control is the way of life followed by those who reject heretical doctrines, practise austerities, and live according to the true religion. Gaya and other kings renounced the world and practised self-restraint.
