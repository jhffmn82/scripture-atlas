Abstract written by a model

In Proverbs, wisdom is the central subject, portrayed as a prized quality to seek, receive from the LORD, and embrace for protection, honor, life, power, victory, reward, and hope. In Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher, wisdom excels folly like light excels darkness but is ultimately called vanity because the wise and foolish share one fate and are forgotten, while God gives it to those pleasing him. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, wisdom is known by the understanding, caution, gratitude, apt proverbs, learning before speaking, self-examination before judgment, and repentance. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, wisdom is defined as the fear of the Lord and its fulfilment through doing the law, excluding wicked knowledge and sinners’ prudence. In Surah 31: Luqman, wisdom was given to Luqman, who used it to teach gratitude to Allah and proper conduct toward Allah, parents, and other people. In Visparad, religious wisdom resides in sacred words, utterances, ceremonies, and ritual practices that are correctly performed, imparted, and pondered. In The Tao Teh King, or The Tao and Its Characteristics, wisdom appears when the Great Tao ceases and benevolence and righteousness arise, and is presented as something to discard for the people’s benefit. In Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou), wisdom means knowing and remaining devoted to serving parents and obeying elder brothers, and it is examined when one’s government fails.

wisdom in Proverbs

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where wisdom first appears

Abstract written by a model

Wisdom is the central subject of these Proverbs passages: a prized quality that the foolish despise and that is too high for a fool. It is to be sought like silver and hidden treasure, bought and not sold, even at the cost of all one's possessions, with the LORD as its giver. The records open with wisdom and instruction as the proverbs' goal, with parental teaching likened to a garland of grace. Next, when wisdom enters a son's heart, knowledge becomes pleasant and she guards him from evil. She is called supreme; if she is loved, esteemed, embraced and not forsaken, she preserves, keeps, exalts and honors a person, giving a garland of grace and crown of splendor, and she is life itself. Further on, the son must apply heart and ears, accept correction, and a wise heart gladdens his parents. Finally, wisdom builds a house and fills its rooms with treasure, gives a wise man great power, brings victory through many advisors, and is compared to honey, sweet to the soul, bringing a reward and a hope that will not be cut off.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Proverbs / Chapter 1

Wisdom and instruction are the goal of the proverbs, which are meant to help people know and receive them. The foolish despise them, while the father's and mother's teaching is likened to a garland and chains of grace.

Chapter 2

Wisdom is to be sought like silver and hidden treasure, and the LORD is its giver. When it enters the son's heart, knowledge becomes pleasant to his soul, and wisdom with discretion and understanding watches over and keeps him, delivering him from evil.

Chapter 4

Wisdom is called supreme and is to be obtained even at the cost of all one's possessions. If she is not forsaken, loved, esteemed, and embraced, she will preserve and keep a person, exalt and honor him, and give him a garland of grace and a crown of splendor. Instruction, spoken of as her, is described as life itself.

Chapter 23

Wisdom, instruction, knowledge, discipline, and understanding are to be pursued: the son should apply his heart and ears to them, buy truth and not sell it, and accept correction, including the rod for a child, which is said to save his soul from Sheol. A wise heart brings gladness to the speaker and to the son's parents.

Chapter 24

Wisdom is said to build a house, and with understanding and knowledge to fill its rooms with rare and beautiful treasure. A wise man has great power, and wise guidance with many advisors brings victory in war. It is too high for a fool, and is compared to honey that is sweet to the soul, bringing a reward and a hope that will not be cut off.

wisdom in Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher

Abstract written by a model

Wisdom is a topic in Chapter 2: it excels folly as light excels darkness and stays with the narrator, yet the wise and the fool share one fate and are forgotten, so he calls it vanity; God gives wisdom to those pleasing him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

The narrator says wisdom excels folly as light excels darkness, and that his wisdom remained with him through his pursuits. However, he sees that the wise man meets the same event as the fool and is equally forgotten, so he asks why he was more wise and calls this also vanity. God gives wisdom to the man who pleases him.

wisdom in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where wisdom first appears

Abstract written by a model

Wisdom is defined as the fear of the Lord and its fulfilment, containing the doing of the law. Chapter 19 excludes knowledge of wickedness or sinners' prudence from wisdom; Chapter 21 adds that grace lies on the wise's lips, while to a fool wisdom is like a destroyed house.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 19

All wisdom is said to be the fear of the Lord, and in all wisdom is the doing of the law. Knowing wickedness or sinners' prudence is explicitly not wisdom.

Chapter 21

Wisdom is described as the fulfilment of the fear of the Lord, and grace is found on the lips of the wise. To a fool it is like a house that is destroyed.
