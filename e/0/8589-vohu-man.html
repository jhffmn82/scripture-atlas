Abstract written by a model

In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Vohu-manô is a holy being defiled by the Daêvas and cleansed through washing, nine nights under heaven, and fire perfume. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, Vohu-Manô is an Amesha-Spenta who helps those Ahura Mazda pleases, receives worship, and opposes its destroyer.

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Vohu-manô first appears

Abstract written by a model

Vohu-manô, a holy being with a golden seat, is defiled by the Daêvas and cleansed through washing, nine nights under heaven, and fire perfume.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Fargard XIX.

Vohu-manô is directly and indirectly defiled by the Daêvas and must be cleansed. Ahura Mazda prescribes washing Vohu-manô with gômêz and water, laying it under the heavens for nine nights, and perfuming it by the fire.

Vohu-Manô in Yashts (Yasts) I-XII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where Vohu-Manô first appears

Abstract written by a model

Vohu-Manô is a creature of Ahura Mazda and an Amesha-Spenta. Associated with riches for those who work for Mazda and relieve the poor, it comes with Sraosha to help those Ahura Mazda pleases, receives worship, and opposes its destroyer through preaching and smiting.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

I. Ormazd Yast.

Vohu-Manô is one of Ahura Mazda's creatures and is associated with the riches given to those who work for Mazda and relieve the poor. Vohu-Manô is also said to come with Sraosha to help whomsoever Ahura Mazda pleases.

II. Haptân Yast.

Vohu-Manô is named as an Amesha-Spenta and receives sacrifice, prayer, propitiation, and glorification. The destroyer of Vohu-Manô and his words is thrown down with repeated preaching and smiting.
