Ezra in The Book of Ezra

author unknown · 399 to 299 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ezra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ezra is a priest and scribe skilled in the law of Moses, son of Seraiah and a descendant of Aaron through Phinehas and Eleazar. He set his heart to seek and do the LORD's law and to teach it in Israel, and the LORD's hand was upon him. The records follow his course from commission to reform. In the reign of Artaxerxes he goes up from Babylon to Jerusalem, authorized by the king to appoint magistrates and judges for the people beyond the River and to carry offerings. He gathers households at the river Ahava, finds no Levites, sends to Iddo for ministers, proclaims a fast to seek a straight way from God, entrusts the offering to twelve priestly chiefs, and leads the group to Jerusalem, where he delivers the king's commissions. Later, hearing the princes' report of the people's trespass, he tears his garment and robe, pulls out hair, and sits confounded until the evening offering. He then prays and confesses the people's guilt, weeps before God's house, has leaders swear to carry out Shecaniah's proposal, and calls the people to confess and separate from the foreign women.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

Ezra, son of Seraiah and a descendant of Aaron the chief priest, is introduced as a priest and a skilled scribe in the law of Moses. He had set his heart to seek the LORD's law, to do it, and to teach statutes and ordinances in Israel, and the king granted all his requests. He led the journey from Babylon to Jerusalem, was commissioned by the king's letter to appoint judges and carry offerings, and blessed the LORD for the king's favor before gathering chief men of Israel to go with him.

Chapter 8

The text's first-person narrator, who is not named in this unit, lists the households that went up with him from Babylon and gathers them at the river Ahava. He finds no Levites there, sends for ministers, proclaims a fast, entrusts the offering to twelve priestly chiefs, and leads the group to Jerusalem, where he delivers the king's commissions.

Chapter 9

Ezra, speaking in the first person, hears the princes' report, tears his garment and robe, pulls out hair from his head and beard, and sits confounded until the evening offering. He then kneels, spreads his hands to the LORD, and offers a prayer confessing the people's guilt and acknowledging the LORD's righteousness.

Chapter 10

Ezra prays, confesses, and weeps before God's house, then makes the chiefs of the priests, the Levites, and all Israel swear to carry out Shecaniah's proposal. He fasts and mourns in Jehohanan's room, tells the assembled people to confess to the LORD and separate from the peoples of the land and the foreign women, and is set apart with heads of fathers' households to examine the matter.

Ezra in The Book of Nehemiah

author unknown · about 399 to 299 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ezra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ezra is a scribe and priest who publicly reads the law before the assembly from a wooden pulpit, blessing the LORD, then meets household heads, priests, and Levites to study it, continuing daily readings through the feast.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

Ezra the scribe and priest brought the law before the assembly and read it from early morning until midday, standing on a wooden pulpit. He opened the book in sight of all the people and blessed the LORD, and he later met with the household heads, priests, and Levites to study the law. He read from the book daily through the feast.
