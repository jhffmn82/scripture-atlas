Abstract written by a model

In Leviticus, sacrifices are regulated offerings brought to the Tent of Meeting and offered to the LORD through the priest as peace offerings. In Psalms, sacrifices and offerings of Israel are not rebuked, but God says he does not need them and ultimately wants more than offerings. In The Book of Isaiah, sacrifices and offerings are regarded by the LORD as a burden and abomination when accompanied by evil and bloodshed. In The Letter to the Hebrews, sacrifices offered under the law are repeated offerings whose inadequacy is replaced by Christ’s one sacrifice. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, sacrifice is a ritual offering and act of worship made to Ahura Mazda and other holy beings, seeking blessings and protection. In Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV, sacrifice is an act of worship and ritual offering made with prayer and offerings to seek favor and blessings.

sacrifice in Yashts (Yasts) I-XII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where sacrifice first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sacrifice is a ritual offering and act of worship made repeatedly to Ahura Mazda, the Amesha-Spentas, the Sun, the Moon, Drvâspa, Mithra, Sraosha, the worshipper's soul, and other holy beings. It is blessed with prayer, strength, and vigour, and seeks prosperity, well-being, brightness, glory, protection, victory, wisdom, bliss, and good conscience. It may begin with prayer, propitiation, and glorification, and includes libations, Haoma, meat, baresma, holy spells, speech, deeds, wisdom of the tongue, and rightly-spoken or proper words; it may also include cattle, birds, and Haomas. Offerings to the Sun seek resistance to darkness, hostile beings, robbers, and unseen death. Offerings to the Moon address its bulls and moons. Offerings to Mithra ask acceptance, loving gathering, and placement in the Garô-nmâna; a pious priest pleases Mithra, unlike an unholy priest. Sacrifice concludes with the blessing of the sacrifice and prayer of Sraosha.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

II. Haptân Yast.

Sacrifice is offered to Ahura Mazda, the Amesha-Spentas, and many other holy beings. The sacrifice is blessed together with prayer, strength, and vigour, and is associated with requests for prosperity and well-being.

VI. Khôrshêd Yast.

Sacrifice is offered to the undying, shining, swift-horsed Sun to withstand darkness, the Daêvas, robbers and bandits, Yâtus and Pairikas, and unseen death. Such sacrifice is offered to Ahura Mazda, the Amesha-Spentas, and the worshipper's own soul, and it rejoices the heavenly and worldly Yazatas.

VII. Mâh Yast.

Sacrifice is offered to the Moon that keeps in it the seed of the Bull, the new moons, the full moons, the Vîshaptathas, the only-created Bull, and the Bull of many species. The offerings include libations, Haoma and meat, baresma, wisdom of the tongue, holy spells, speech, deeds, and rightly-spoken words.

IX. Gôs Yast.

Sacrifice is repeatedly offered to Drvâspa with prayer, glorification, Haoma, meat, baresma, holy spells, speech, deeds, libations, and rightly-spoken words.

X. Mihir Yast.

Sacrifice is repeatedly offered to Mithra with libations, Haoma, meat, baresma, holy spells, speech, deeds, and rightly spoken words. Mithra is asked to accept these sacrifices and grant riches, strength, victory, good conscience, bliss, wisdom, and protection.

XII.

Sacrifices and libations are repeatedly offered to Mithra with his own name and proper words. Mithra is asked to accept them, gather them together with love, and lay them in the Garô-nmâna.

XXIV.

The unit repeatedly calls for sacrifices to Mithra for his brightness and glory. Sacrifices may be offered with small cattle, black cattle, flying birds, Haomas, baresma, and proper words; a pious priest pleases Mithra, while an unholy priest does not.

XI. Srôsh Yast Hâdhôkht.

Sacrifice is repeatedly offered to Sraosha and to other holy beings for their brightness, glory, strength, and victorious power. The text blesses the sacrifice and prayer of Sraosha at its conclusion.

sacrifice in Yashts (Yasts) XIII-XXIV

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where sacrifice first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sacrifice is an act of worship and ritual offering, made with prayer, propitiation, and glorification to figures including the Fravashis, Verethraghna, Vayu, Kista, the good Law, and Ahura Mazda's creation. It requires rites and offerings such as libations, Haoma, meat, baresma, holy spells, words, deeds, and worthy prayer. Ahura Mazda, Zarathustra, and others offer it to seek favor and particular boons. When properly performed in holiness, it brings blessings, healing, protection, strength, victory, royalty, and advancement; worshippers become good in Ahura Mazda's eyes. Sacrifice during Hamaspathmaêdha brings blessings of animals, people, horses, chariots, and capable leadership. Improper recipients cause its virtues to be withdrawn.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

XIII. Farvardîn Yast.

Sacrifice, together with prayer, propitiation, and glorification, is offered to the Fravashis of the faithful. During the Hamaspathmaêdha, those who offer sacrifice with meat, clothing, and a prayer worthy of bliss receive blessings of animals, people, horses, chariots, and capable leadership.

XXVI.

Sacrifice is offered to the Fravashis and is described as making worshippers good in the eyes of Ahura Mazda. Zarathustra and the holy men and women are called worthy of sacrifice.

XIV. Bahrâm Yast.

Sacrifice and prayer to Verethraghna include libations, baresma, Haoma, meat, sacred spells, speech, deeds, and rightly spoken words. When performed in the perfection of holiness, they bring healing, protection, strength, and victory; improper recipients cause these virtues to be withdrawn.

XV. Râm Yast.

Sacrifice is repeatedly offered to Vayu with libations, Haoma, meat, baresma, holy spells, words, deeds, and well-spoken words. Ahura Mazda and several named figures use sacrifice to seek Vayu’s favor and request boons.

XVI. Dîn Yast.

Sacrifice is offered to Kista and the good Law with libations, Haoma and meat, baresma, wisdom of the tongue, holy spells, words, and deeds. Zarathustra, Hvôvi, the Âthravans, and the king of the country are each said to make sacrifices for particular benefits.

XXIV. Vîstâsp Yast.

A sacrifice to Ahura Mazda's creation must be performed according to the rites, including the use of baresma and Haoma. Proper sacrifice brings royalty and helps protect and advance the worshipper.
