Abstract written by a model

Babylon's destruction is the LORD's vengeance for its pride and treatment of his people. It is announced as already accomplished: bulwarks and walls fall, and the earth trembles at its capture. Fire, flood, and drying fountains follow with sudden force. Its land becomes a perpetual, uninhabited desolation like Sodom and Gomorrah, nations no longer flow to Bel, and the sinking of the book in the Euphrates seals it.

Chapter 50

The fall of Babylon is announced as already taken: its bulwarks fall, its walls are thrown down, and it becomes a desolation among the nations. It is the LORD's vengeance for Babylon's pride and treatment of his people, and its land is left uninhabited like Sodom and Gomorrah. The noise of its capture makes the earth tremble, and the cry is heard among the nations.

Chapter 51

Babylon's destruction is portrayed as the LORD's purposed vengeance: sudden fall, fire on her dwellings and gates, a flood of the sea, and drying of her fountain. Her land becomes a perpetual desolation without inhabitant, and the nations will no longer flow to Bel. It is sealed by the symbolic sinking of the book in the Euphrates.
