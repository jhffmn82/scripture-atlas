sin offering

topic · 1 work · 5 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: It is the sin offering for the assembly, No sin offering, a sin offering, the bull of the sin offering, the law of the sin offering, the sin offering

Abstract written by a model

The sin offering is the prescribed sacrifice for unintentional sins, with the animal varying by the offender's status. The offerer lays a hand on its head, it is killed before the LORD at the place of the burnt offering, its blood is applied to the altar, and its fat is burned. It is most holy: the offering priest and male priests of Aaron's line eat it in a holy place, the court of the Tent of Meeting, and whatever touches its flesh becomes holy. Blood-splashed garments and cooking vessels require washing, scouring, or breaking. If its blood enters the Tent of Meeting for atonement, it is burned, not eaten.

Chapter 4

The sin offering is the prescribed sacrifice for unintentional sins, with the animal varying by the status of the offender. The offerer lays a hand on its head, it is killed, its blood is applied to the altar, and its fat is burned, like the fat of the peace offerings. The text itself calls the goat "a sin offering" and the congregation's bull "the sin offering for the assembly."

Chapter 6

The sin offering is killed before the LORD where the burnt offering is killed, and it is most holy. The offering priest and every male priest eat it in the court of the Tent of Meeting, and whatever touches its flesh becomes holy. Garments splashed with its blood must be washed in a holy place, an earthen vessel used to boil it must be broken, and a bronze one scoured and rinsed. If its blood is brought into the Tent of Meeting to make atonement in the Holy Place, it must be burned with fire instead of eaten.
