Ozias

Also called: Ozias the son of Micah, the house of Ozias

Abstract written by a model

Ozias, son of Micah of the tribe of Simeon, is one of Bethulia's three rulers and elders, alongside Chabris and Charmis. He trusts that God will show mercy. He first hosts Achior the Ammonite in his house and feasts the elders. Later the people blame the rulers for not seeking peace with the Assyrians. Ozias urges courage, asks for five more days, and swears to deliver the city if no help comes. When Judith rebukes him, he acknowledges her wisdom and godliness, explains that the thirsty people compelled the oath, asks her to pray for rain, and blesses her above all women before sending her off in peace.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

Ozias, son of Micah of the tribe of Simeon, is named as one of Bethulia's three rulers. He asks Achior what happened, then takes him from the assembly into his house and makes a feast for the elders.

Chapter 7

Ozias, one of the city's rulers, is confronted by the whole people, who blame him and the other rulers for not seeking peace with the Assyrians. He urges courage and asks for five more days for the Lord to turn his mercy toward them. He promises to do as the people say if no help comes, then disperses them to their posts and sends the women and children home.

Chapter 8

Ozias had sworn to the people that he would deliver the city to the Assyrians after five days. When Judith rebukes him, he acknowledges her wisdom and good heart, explains that the thirsty people compelled the oath, asks her to pray for rain, and then blesses her and sends her off in peace.
