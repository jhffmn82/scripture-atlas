Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of the Maccabees, Judas is Mattathias's son and captain who leads Israel's battles, delivers and defends the Jews, cleanses and fortifies the holy place, defeats their enemies, seeks alliance with Rome, and falls at Elasa. In The Second Book of the Maccabees, Judas Maccabaeus is a noble Jewish military leader who gathers and commands faithful Jews, recovers the temple and city, defeats their enemies, prays for the dead, and defeats Nicanor.

Judas in The First Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Judas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judas, surnamed Maccabaeus, is a son of Mattathias, one of five brothers (including Simon and Jonathan), described as strong and mighty from his youth. Mattathias appoints him captain to fight the people's battle. He rises in his father's place, likened to a lion, hunting the lawless and turning wrath from Israel. The records portray him as a deliverer of Israel, renowned to the utmost part of the earth, with a memory blessed forever. Early on he kills Apollonius and keeps his sword, using it all his days, and routs Seron at Bethhoron. At Mizpeh and Emmaus he appoints captains of thousands, hundreds, fifties and tens, sends home those exempted by the law, and trusts heaven to save by many or by few. He defeats Gorgias' force at Emmaus, prays before meeting Lysias at Bethsura with ten thousand men, leads the cleansing of the holy place, and fortifies Mount Zion and Bethsura. He then defeats the children of Esau, Baean and the Ammonites, and leads eight thousand men with Jonathan into Gilead, taking cities, routing Timotheus, and bringing the Jews safely to Mount Zion. Glorified among Israel and the Gentiles, he strikes Hebron and Azotus, then besieges the citadel garrison in the 150th year. Accused to the king, he distrusts Bacchides, avoids Nicanor's deceit, and defeats Nicanor at Adasa on the thirteenth of Adar. He sends Eupolemus and Jason to Rome to seek friendship and alliance and freedom from the Greek yoke. At Elasa his three thousand chosen men dwindle to about eight hundred. Refusing to flee, he defeats Bacchides's right wing and pursues it to mount Azotus, but falls in battle. He is buried at Modin in his ancestors' tomb and mourned as the savior of Israel, his many other deeds left unwritten.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Judas Maccabaeus is listed as one of Mattathias's five sons. Mattathias declares him strong and mighty from his youth and appoints him captain, to fight the battle of the people.

Chapter 3

Judas, called Maccabaeus, rises in his father's place and is likened to a lion, hunting the lawless and turning wrath from Israel. He kills Apollonius and keeps his sword, routs Seron at Bethhoron, and assures his fearful men that heaven can save by many or by few. At Mizpeh and Emmaus he appoints captains, sends home those exempted by the law, and exhorts his men to fight, leaving the result to heaven's will.

Chapter 4

Judas learns of Gorgias' night march and moves to strike the king's army at Emmaus, then leads three thousand men to victory, urging them to hold off from the spoils until Gorgias' force is dealt with. He prays before meeting Lysias at Bethsura with ten thousand men, later leads the cleansing of the holy place, and fortifies Mount Zion and Bethsura.

Chapter 5

Judas defeats the children of Esau, the children of Baean, and the Ammonites, then leads eight thousand men with Jonathan into Gilead to rescue besieged Jews. He takes Bosora, routs Timotheus, takes Mizpeh and other cities, crosses the brook first to defeat the Gentiles, storms Ephron after its refusal of peaceful passage, and brings the Gilead Jews to Mount Zion without losing any. He is glorified among Israel and the Gentiles, then strikes Hebron and Azotus, pulling down altars and burning images before returning to Judah.

Chapter 6

Judas plans to destroy the garrison in the citadel and gathers the people to besiege it in the 150th year, building mounds and engines of war. He then marches to Bethzacharias near the king's camp and engages the king's army.

Chapter 7

Judas and his kindred were accused to the king of destroying the king's friends and scattering the lawless from the land. He did not trust the deceitful peace words of Bacchides, went out through Judea to take vengeance on deserters, and avoided Nicanor once he learned of his deceit. He prayed at Adasa with three thousand men, and his army defeated Nicanor's on the thirteenth of Adar.

Chapter 8

Judas, also called Maccabaeus, hears of the Romans' fame, conquests, and loyalty to friends. He chooses Eupolemus and Jason and sends them to Rome to establish friendship and alliance and to free the Jews from the Greek yoke.

Chapter 9

Judas, encamped at Elasa with three thousand chosen men, was left with about eight hundred when the rest slipped away. He refused to flee, defeated Bacchides's right wing and pursued it to mount Azotus, but fell in the battle. He was buried at Modin and mourned as the savior of Israel, with his many other deeds left unwritten.

Judas Maccabaeus in The Second Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Judas Maccabaeus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judas Maccabaeus is a Jewish military leader, also called Maccabaeus, presented as a noble, trusting commander and the foremost champion of his fellow citizens in body and soul, loyal to them from youth. He is brother to Simon, Joseph and Jonathan. The records open by naming him, with his brothers, as a subject of the history, including the purification of the temple and the dedication of the altar, and note that he gathered the books scattered by the war. He first withdraws with about nine others to the mountains, eating wild plants to avoid defilement. He then secretly gathers about six thousand faithful Jews, trains them, and wins back key positions by night raids. He urges trust in almighty God, divides his army into four parts under himself and his three brothers, defeats Nicanor, and shares the spoils with the maimed, widows, orphans and aged. Led by the Lord, he recovers the temple and city, attacks Idumaean strongholds, executes bribed traitors, prays before facing Timotheus, is guarded by heavenly horsemen, and besieges Gazara. He accepts Lysias's terms out of care for the common good. He avenges the Joppa murders, burns the harbors at Joppa and Jamnia, makes peace and friendship with an Arab army, and takes several towns. At Adullam he leads prayers for the dead and sends two thousand drachmas to Jerusalem for a sin offering, motivated by belief in the resurrection. Camped at Modin, he gives the watchword 'VICTORY IS GOD'S' and leads a night attack on the king's pavilion. Later Alcimus denounces him as leader of the Hasidaeans and Demetrius orders Nicanor to kill him. After a covenant with Nicanor he is urged to marry and settle, but when Nicanor turns harsh he hides and gathers men. In the final account he trusts unceasingly in the Lord, is encouraged by a dream of Onias and Jeremiah, receives a gold sword from Jeremiah, prays, wins, and has Nicanor's head, hand and arm displayed in Jerusalem.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Judas, along with his brothers, is named as the subject of the history to be abridged, including the purification of the temple and dedication of the altar. Separately, Judas is said to have gathered the books scattered by the war, which remain with the writers.

Chapter 5

Judas, also called Maccabaeus, withdrew with about nine others to the mountains. They lived like wild animals, feeding on what grew wild so as not to share in the defilement.

Chapter 8

Judas, also called Maccabaeus, secretly gathers about six thousand faithful Jews, trains them, and carries out night raids that win back important positions. He exhorts his men to trust in almighty God, divides his army into four parts under himself and his brothers Simon, Joseph and Jonathan, and defeats Nicanor. He then shares the spoils with the maimed, widows, orphans and aged.

Chapter 10

Maccabaeus, with the Lord leading, recovered the temple and city and helped cleanse the sanctuary. He attacked the Idumaean strongholds, left a besieging force at two towers, and on learning of the bribery had the traitors killed and took the towers. He prayed before facing Timotheus, was guarded by heavenly horsemen in battle, and besieged Gazara.

Chapter 11

Maccabaeus, with the people, prayed for a good angel, then took up weapons first and urged others to risk themselves with him, leading them out willingly. After the victory he accepted Lysias's terms out of care for the common good, and the king allowed whatever requests he submitted in writing.

Chapter 12

Judas avenges the Joppa murders by burning the harbor and boats there, then does the same at Jamnia, defeats an Arab army and makes peace with it, and takes Caspin, Carnion and Ephron while routing Timotheus's army. He rushes Gorgias's troops with a battle cry and hymns, leads the prayers for the dead at Adullam, and sends two thousand drachmas to Jerusalem for a sin offering, which the text praises as taking thought for the resurrection.

Chapter 13

Judas, hearing of the king's advance, commanded the people to call on the Lord day and night, and after three days of prayer consulted the elders and camped at Modin. He gave the watchword "VICTORY IS GOD'S" and led a chosen force of the bravest young men in a night attack on the king's pavilion. He also sent necessary supplies to those inside Bethsura, and was later graciously received by the king as Maccabaeus.

Chapter 14

Judas is denounced by Alcimus as the leader of the seditious Hasidaeans, and Demetrius orders Nicanor to kill him. After a covenant with Nicanor he is kept in his company and urged to marry and settle, but when Nicanor turns harsh he gathers men and hides. Nicanor then demands that the priests hand him over.

Chapter 15

Judas trusts unceasingly in the Lord's help, encourages his men with the law, the prophets and a dream of Onias and Jeremiah, and receives a gold sword from Jeremiah in the vision. He prays to the Lord before the battle, citing the angel sent in Hezekiah's time, and after the victory orders Nicanor's head, hand and arm taken to Jerusalem and displayed. He is described as the foremost champion of his fellow citizens, loyal to them since youth.
