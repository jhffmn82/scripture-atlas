Ashemaogha

Also called: Ashemaoghas, that ungodly Ashemaogha, that wicked, two-legged ruffian, the ungodly Ashemaogha, the unholy Ashemaogha, ungodly Ashemaogha, wicked, two-footed ruffian, wicked, ungodly Ashemaogha

Abstract written by a model

In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Fargards I-VIII, Ashemaogha is an ungodly wicked ruffian who while alive defiles creatures of the good spirit by harming water, fire, the cow, and faithful people, but when dead defiles no more. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), Ashemaogha improperly cleanses an unclean person without knowing Mazda’s rites, causing sickness and death, and while alive defiles creatures of the good spirit and deprives the faithful man of necessities until put to death. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, Ashemaogha flees before the Airyaman prayer and is smitten and afflicted by Asha-Vahista.

Abstract written by a model

Ashemaogha is an ungodly wicked two-footed ruffian opposed by man, who while alive directly and indirectly defiles creatures of the good spirit by harming water, fire, the cow, and faithful people; when dead, he defiles no more than a long-dead dried-venom frog.

Fargard V.

An Ashemaogha, while alive, directly and indirectly defiles the creatures of the good spirit by harming water, fire, the cow, and faithful people. When dead, he defiles no more than a frog whose venom is dried up and which has been dead for more than a year.

Ashemaogha in Vendidad (Vendîdâd)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Ashemaogha first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ashemaogha is an ungodly, wicked person who improperly cleanses an unclean one without knowing Mazda’s rites, causing sickness and death. While alive, he directly and indirectly defiles creatures of the good spirit, smites water, blows out fire, carries off the cow, kills or strikes the faithful man, and deprives him of necessities. The affected land and fields lack sweetness, health, healing, fulness, increase, and growth until he is put to death; once dead, he no longer commits these acts.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Fargard IX.

Ashemaogha is the ungodly person who cleanses the unclean without knowing the rites according to the law of Mazda. Until Ashemaogha is put to death, sweetness, health, healing, fulness, increase, and growth do not return to the affected land and fields.

Fargard XII.

The living Ashemaogha directly defiles the creatures of the good spirit and indirectly defiles them. While alive, he smites water, blows out fire, carries off the cow, kills the faithful man, and deprives him of food, clothing, house, bed, and vessels; when dead, he no longer does these things.
