Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Tiglath Pileser conquered Naphtali and neighboring towns, deported their people to Assyria, attacked Damascus at Ahaz's request, took it, deported its people to Kir, killed Rezin, and prompted Ahaz's temple changes. In 1 Chronicles, Tilgath Pilneser carried Beerah the Reubenite prince captive and carried the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh to Halah, Habor, Hara, and the river of Gozan. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Tilgath-pilneser answered Ahaz's call for help but troubled him instead of strengthening him, despite receiving temple and royal treasure.

Tiglath Pileser in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where Tiglath Pileser first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tiglath Pileser is a king of Assyria. In Pekah's days he conquered Naphtali and neighboring towns, deporting their people to Assyria. Later Ahaz, calling himself his servant and son, sent silver and gold; he attacked Damascus, took it, deported its people to Kir, and killed Rezin. Ahaz's temple changes followed because of him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 15

Tiglath Pileser king of Assyria came in the days of Pekah and took Ijon, Abel Beth Maacah, Janoah, Kedesh, Hazor, Gilead, and Galilee, all the land of Naphtali. He carried the people captive to Assyria.

Chapter 16

Tiglath Pileser king of Assyria was called on by Ahaz, who described himself as his servant and son, and was sent a present of silver and gold. He listened, went up against Damascus, took it, carried its people captive to Kir, and killed Rezin. Ahaz met him at Damascus, and Ahaz's later changes to the temple were made because of him.
