Abstract written by a model

In Surah 39: The Groups (Az-Zumar), the soul is received by Allah at death and sleep, with some souls kept for death and others released until an appointed term. In Surah 56: The Inevitable (Al-Waaqia), the soul of the dying person rises to the throat while the Lord of the Worlds is nearer to it than those watching. In Surah 81: The Overthrowing (At-Takwir), souls are reunited and every soul knows what it has made ready. In Surah 82: The Cleaving (Al-Infitaar), the soul will know what it sent before and left behind, and no soul will control another on that day. In Surah 91: The Sun (Ash-Shams), the soul is perfected and inspired with awareness of right and wrong, succeeding when it grows and failing when it is stunted. In The Table-Talk of the Prophet Mohammad (Part 2), the soul leaves the body at death, and a believer’s soul is welcomed into celestial regions while an infidel’s soul is taken by punishing angels. In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I, the Self is a soul born again and again and is the knower or experiencer through which one knows. In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, the absolute (âtman) is an unchangeable entity appearing as the world, bound by possessions and wrongdoing but capable of liberation from Karman.

and 8 more works

In Sutrakritanga, Book II, the soul is debated as either identical with the body or denied as a separate entity, while merit and demerit affect it. In Sutrakritanga, Book II, Âtman is identified with Soul as an entity active within the skin’s surface and lasting only as long as the body. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, the Self is difficult to subdue, but self-control and penance subdue it and bring happiness in this world and the next. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, the soul’s eternal welfare is protected through avoidance of hatred and lust, meditation, discipline, restraint, and freedom from attachment. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, the soul inhabits various bodies, is driven through Samsâra by Karman, and is eternal because it has no corporeal form. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, the Self is the invincible foe that Gautama vanquishes and that advances through spiritual practice to perfection and final beatitude. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, the soul is one of six substances, bound by Karman and turning in the Circle of Births until discipline and freedom from passions release it from Samsâra. In Asa ki War (Asa di Var), the soul belongs to God, passes away after man becomes ashes, and departs at death while receiving a spiritual janeu of mercy, contentment, continence, and truth.

absolute (âtman) in Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where absolute (âtman) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Soul is an unchangeable entity that appears as the world in various forms and is bound by possessions, violence, passions, and wrong conduct. Though some say it ends after death or deny rebirth, the teaching says it experiences pleasure and pain and may become free from Karman. Its benefit, liberation, is sought through truth, restraint, austerities, Guptis, detachment, and subduing the senses; abandoning possessions delivers it from misery.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I / First Book. / First Lecture, Called the Doctrine. / First Chapter. .. Fourth Chapter.

The Soul is subject to bondage through possessions, violence, passions, and wrong conduct. Some doctrines deny its continued existence or rebirth, but the teaching maintains that Soul experiences pleasure and pain and can become free from Karman.

Second Lecture, Called the Destruction of Karman. / First Chapter. .. Third Chapter.

The soul's benefit is pursued through truth, control, austerities, possession of the Guptis, and detachment from worldly ties. Undertakings that kill living beings are said to work the perdition of the soul.

Eleventh Lecture, Called the Path.

A man should guard his soul and subdue his senses. The monk who crosses Samsâra wanders intent on the benefit of his soul.

Abstract written by a model

The Self is the one invincible foe, though Gautama says he has vanquished it with the four cardinal passions and five senses. It advances through desire for the Law, right faith, discipline, knowledge, conduct, concentration, control, austerities, repentance, confession, devotion to the Guru, and freedom from greed and passion. It purifies faith through adoration of the twenty-four Ginas, destroys and cuts off Karman, and gains absolute knowledge and faith. After pure meditation and destroying remaining Karman, it obtains perfection, enlightenment, deliverance, unchecked happiness, and final beatitude. Renouncing its bodies, it reaches the highest region, develops into its natural form, and ends all misery.
