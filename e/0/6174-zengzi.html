Zengzi

person · 2 works · 9 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Zeng, the philosopher Zeng, the philosopher Zeng spoke them

Abstract written by a model

In Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou), the philosopher Zeng values benevolence and righteousness above wealth and nobility, sustains valour through self-examination and uprightness, and is affirmed by Mencius as expressing principle. In Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou), Zengzi nourishes his father, Zeng Xi, with wine and flesh, respectfully asks where leftovers should go, and says some remain, exemplifying filial piety and nourishing the will.

Abstract written by a model

Zengzi nourishes his father, Zeng Xi, with wine and flesh, respectfully asks where leftovers should go, and says some remain. This service exemplifies filial piety and is called nourishing the will.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 19

Zengzi nourishes his father, Zeng Xi, by providing wine and flesh and asking respectfully to whom the leftovers should be given. His practice is called nourishing the will, and serving parents in this way may be accepted as filial piety.
