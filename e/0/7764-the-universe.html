THE UNIVERSE

place · 1 work · 16 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: The universe, all creation, creation, the universe, universe, whole universe

Abstract written by a model

The universe is the whole under the canopy of heaven: a vast smelting-pot with countless contents, regular phenomena, and uniform laws. It results from the harmonious interaction of the perfect Negative and perfect Positive principles, is ordered through Yin, Yang, and the four seasons, and is governed, informed, covered, and supported by TAO. TAO is contained in every thing, including the smallest, so nothing is ultimately separable from the identity established through it. The universe and the speaker came into being together and are one. Though it may pass away, it can be concealed within itself with nowhere left for it to be lost, while apparent destruction leaves immaterial existence. Its scale makes the Four Seas like puddles and the Middle Kingdom like a tare-seed; even the ocean is small within it. It models virtue for the wise ruler, who travels upon it with all creation as his team, and the Sage holds it in his grasp, harmonizes distinctions, and flourishes beyond its passing. Hui Tzŭ claims it has no peer for him, but from its TAO his value is slight.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

The universe and the speaker are said to have come into being together and to be one. Nothing in the universe is ultimately separable from the identity established through TAO.

Of old, the Emperor Yao said to Shun, "I would smite the Tsungs, and

The Sage holds the universe in his grasp and blends its distinctions into harmony. The universe may pass away, but the Sage will still flourish.

CHAPTER VI.

The universe is described as something that can be concealed in the whole universe, leaving no place where it may be lost. It became what can be seen through TAO, which underlies all its phenomena.

CHAPTER XII.

The universe is vast, regular in its phenomena, uniform in the laws governing its contents, and dominated by sovereignty. It is covered and supported by TAO, and the true Sage becomes united with it by following the natural order.

CHAPTER XIII.

The universe is the model upon which the wise ruler’s virtue is formed. It is described as the vehicle upon which the ruler travels, with all creation as his team.

CHAPTER XVII.

The universe gives the ocean its shape and is so vast that the ocean is like a small stone or tree on a mountain within it. The universe is also used to illustrate that opposing principles and conditions are mutually related.

CHAPTER XXII.

Creation is based on absolute principles but does not speak. It is continually informed by TAO, and its apparent destruction leaves immaterial existence; its order is governed by the Yin, the Yang, and the four seasons.

Hui Tzŭ was a man of many ideas. His works would fill five carts. But

The universe was said by Hui Tzŭ to hold no peer of his. Viewed from the perspective of the TAO of the universe, however, Hui Tzŭ's value was compared with the efforts of a mosquito or a gadfly.
