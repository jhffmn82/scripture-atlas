final beatitude

topic · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: final delivery, final liberation

Abstract written by a model

Final beatitude is a goal of the religious path and the ultimate destination for a wandering monk. It is reached by monks after attaining Kêvala, ending misery and completely ceasing from sin. Those instructed in the Law who cultivate virtues, abstain from sins and passions, and conduct themselves well reach it. Unworthy, impure, unholy, or sinful conduct does not lead to final delivery; the monk should improve his chances and wander until reaching liberation.

I say: here in the East, West, North, and South there are some men, viz. Âryas,

Those instructed in the Law by a monk who exert themselves well possess virtues, abstain from sins and passions, conduct themselves well, and reach final beatitude.

Second Lecture, Called on Activity.

Final delivery is not reached through conduct that is unworthy, impure, not holy, not right, and not eradicating sins.

Now the explanation of the second subject, viz. merit, is as follows:

Final beatitude is attained by monks who reach Kêvala and put an end to all misery. It is also taught by the various philosophical schools and is the ultimate result of complete cessation from sin.

Fifth Lecture, Called Freedom from Error.

The monk should improve his chances for final liberation and wander until he reaches it.
