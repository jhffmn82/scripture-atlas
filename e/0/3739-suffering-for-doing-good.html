suffering for doing good

Abstract written by a model

Suffering for doing good is presented as commendable with God when patiently endured, unlike punishment for one's own sin. Believers are called to it, following Christ's sinless, non-retaliating example. It is a fiery trial not to be wondered at, a share in Christ's sufferings in which to rejoice, and should be borne only as a Christian while continuing to do good and entrusting souls to God. Brothers worldwide undergo the same; it lasts a little while, then God perfects, establishes, strengthens, and settles them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Enduring pain and suffering unjustly because of conscience toward God is called commendable, whereas patiently enduring a beating for one's own sin earns no glory. Patiently enduring suffering while doing well is commendable with God. Believers were called to this, following the example of Christ, who suffered for them without sinning or retaliating.

Chapter 4

Suffering in the flesh is tied to ceasing from sin, and Christ's own suffering is the model. Believers are told not to be astonished at the fiery trial, to rejoice as partakers of Christ's sufferings, and to suffer only as Christians, never as murderers, thieves, evildoers, or meddlers. Those who suffer according to God's will should keep doing good and entrust their souls to him.

Chapter 5

Sufferings appear as the author's witnessing of Christ's sufferings and as the same sufferings undergone by the readers' brothers in the world. The readers' own suffering is described as lasting only a little while, after which God will perfect, establish, strengthen, and settle them.
