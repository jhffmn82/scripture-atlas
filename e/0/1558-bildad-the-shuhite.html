Bildad the Shuhite

person · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections

Abstract written by a model

Bildad the Shuhite is one of Job's three friends who come from his own place to comfort him, weeping, tearing his robe, and sitting silently seven days and nights. He then becomes a speaker in the dialogue, answering Job three times. He first rebukes Job's words and defends God's justice, claiming Job's children sinned and urging him to seek God and heed past generations. Later he protests being counted unclean and describes the wicked man's ruin. His last speech is short, stressing God's greatness and that man cannot be just before God. In the end the LORD's wrath falls on him, Eliphaz and Zophar for not speaking rightly, and Bildad does as the LORD commanded.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 2

Bildad the Shuhite is one of Job's three friends who hear of his troubles and come from his own place. He makes an appointment with the others to comfort Job, and joins in weeping, tearing his robe and sitting silently seven days and nights.

Chapter 8

Bildad the Shuhite answers, rebuking the addressee's words as a mighty wind and defending God's justice. He claims that the addressee's children sinned and were delivered into their disobedience, urges seeking God, and appeals to the teaching of past generations, admitting that he and his companions are but of yesterday and know nothing.

Chapter 18

Bildad the Shuhite answers by asking how long his listener will hunt for words and by protesting that he and his companions are counted as unclean animals. He then delivers a long description of the wicked man's ruin, ending with the claim that such is the dwelling of one who does not know God.

Chapter 25

Bildad the Shuhite answers with a short speech about God's greatness. He asks rhetorical questions about God's armies and light, and argues that man cannot be just or clean before God.

Chapter 42

Bildad is included in the LORD's wrath against Eliphaz and his two friends for not speaking rightly of him. He goes with Eliphaz and Zophar and does what the LORD commanded.
