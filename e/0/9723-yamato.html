Yamato

place · 3 works · 3 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Yamato's, the Land of Yamato, this high metropolis of Yamato

Abstract written by a model

In Kojiki (Records of Ancient Matters), Volume I (The Age of the Gods), the Land of Yamato is the destination of the Deity’s departure from Idzumo. In Kojiki, Yamato is described as the most secluded and delightful land in Yamato-take’s Land-Regretting Songs, associated with a Heavenly Sovereign’s august interment, and the place where Empress Jin-Gō returns, deceives its doubtful people, and defeats the opposing forces after the Korean expedition. In Kojiki (Records of Ancient Matters), Volume III (Emperor Nin-toku to Empress Sui-ko), Yamato is described as a land and high metropolis mentioned in songs and journeys, where Princess Kuro’s spouse went, which the Empress named while passing Nara, which a song links to a wild-goose’s egg, where Emperor Ri-Chiu escaped from a burning palace and Midzu-ha-wake followed after killings, and which Emperor Yū-Riyaku called the Island of the Dragon-Fly.

Yamato in Kojiki

Basil Hall Chamberlain · the work’s page · read it where Yamato first appears

Abstract written by a model

Yamato is a land called the most secluded and delightful in Yamato-take’s Land-Regretting Songs, associated with a Heavenly Sovereign’s august interment. After the Korean expedition, Empress Jin-Gō returns there, deceives its doubtful people with a mourning-vessel and false report, and defeats the opposing forces.

Sect. LXXVI.—Empress Kei-Ko (Part I,—Genealogies) .. Sect. XCIII.—Emperor Kei-Kō (Part XVIII. His Age and Place of Burial).

It is called the most secluded and delightful of lands in one of Yamato-take’s Land-Regretting Songs. The songs are also associated with the august interment of a Heavenly Sovereign.

Sect. XCV.—Emperor Chiū-Ai (Part I.—Genealogies). .. Sect. CIII.—Emperor Chiū-Ai (Part IX.—His Death and That of the Empress Jin-Gō).

Yamato is the destination of Empress Jin-Gō's return after the Korean expedition. Because she doubts the disposition of its people, she uses a mourning-vessel and a false report of the child's death before defeating the opposing forces.

Yamato in Kojiki (Records of Ancient Matters), Volume III (Emperor Nin-toku to Empress Sui-ko)

Basil Hall Chamberlain · the work’s page · read it where Yamato first appears

Abstract written by a model

Yamato is a land and high metropolis, mentioned in songs and journeys. Princess Kuro’s spouse went there, the Empress named it while passing Nara, and a song links it to a wild-goose’s egg. Emperor Ri-Chiu escaped there from a burning palace, and Midzu-ha-wake followed after killings. Emperor Yū-Riyaku called it the Island of the Dragon-Fly.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Sect. CXX: Emperor Nin-Toku (Part II.—Various Deeds). .. Sect. CXXX.—Emperor Nin-Toku (Part XI.—His Age and Place of Burial).

Yamato was the land toward which Princess Kuro’s spouse went and which the Empress mentioned while passing Nara. A Song also refers to the land of Yamato in connection with a wild-goose laying an egg.

Sect. CXXXI.—Emperor Ri-Chiu (Part I.—Genealogies). .. Sect. CXXXV.—Emperor Ri-Chiu (Part V.—His Age and Place of Burial).

Yamato was the destination of Emperor Ri-Chiu's escape from the burning palace. His Augustness Midzu-ha-wake also proceeded to Yamato after killing King Sumi-no-ye-no-naka-tsu and Sobakari.

Sect. CL.—Emperor Yū-Riyaku, (I.—Genealogies). .. Sect. CLXI.—Emperor Yū-Riyaku (Part XII.—Songs by the Emperor and Princess Wodo).

Yamato was described in Emperor Yū-Riyaku’s song as the land called the Island of the Dragon-Fly after the dragon-fly ate the horse-fly. The Empress’s song also referred to the high metropolis of Yamato.
