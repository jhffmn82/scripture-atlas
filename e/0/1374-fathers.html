Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the Israelites, the fathers and their descendants, received God’s covenant and commands but were remembered as stubborn and rebellious, though God remembered his covenant when they cried out for rescue. In The Book of Jeremiah, Israel’s ancestors forsook the LORD, followed and worshiped other gods, and broke his law, while the LORD gave their fathers the land and promises to restore the people. In The Book of Ezekiel, the fathers of the children of Israel repeatedly rebelled against the LORD in Egypt and the wilderness, clinging to idols, rejecting ordinances, and profaning Sabbaths; the LORD spared them but barred them from the land. In The Book of Zechariah, the fathers were the object of the LORD’s great displeasure because they did not hear or listen to the former prophets, though they later repented when the LORD’s words overtook them. In The Acts of the Apostles, the Israelite fathers sold Joseph, went to Egypt, multiplied, suffered mistreatment, rejected Moses, worshiped a calf and Moloch, held the testimony tabernacle, and persecuted prophets. In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, the fathers, Israelites in the wilderness, shared the cloud, sea crossing, baptism into Moses, and spiritual food and drink, but most displeased God and fell, serving as examples and warnings for believers. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, the fathers were in bondage and afflicted by hunger and thirst, and God delivered them from Egypt and Jerusalem while their genealogy was preserved on brass plates.

Israelites (the fathers and their offspring) in Psalms

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Israelites (the fathers and their offspring) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Israelites, the fathers and their descendants, are a people given God's covenant and commands to pass to their children, yet remembered as a stubborn, rebellious generation with disloyal hearts. Despite wonders in Egypt, they forgot God, rebelled at the Red Sea, made a calf, joined Baal Peor and sacrificed children to idols. Oppressed by enemies, they cry out; God remembers his covenant, and the psalmist prays for rescue.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 78

The fathers told the teachings to later generations and received God's covenant and commands, which they were to pass on to their children. They are also held up as a warning, a stubborn and rebellious generation whose hearts were not loyal, and later Israel dealt treacherously like them. God did marvelous things in their sight in Egypt.

Chapter 106

The Israelites confess that they sinned with their fathers, who forgot God's wonders, rebelled at the Red Sea, craved in the desert, made a calf, murmured in their tents, joined Baal Peor, mixed with the nations and sacrificed their children to idols. As a result they were ruled and oppressed by their enemies and brought low in iniquity, but God heard their cry and remembered his covenant. The psalmist prays that God will save them and gather them from among the nations.
