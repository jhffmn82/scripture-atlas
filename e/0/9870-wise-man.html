wise man

person · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: A wise man, Wise men, the wise, wise, wise men

Abstract written by a model

A wise man understands that living beings desire happiness and therefore follows the Law through carefulness, nonviolence, wisdom, and restraint from pleasures. He conquers greed and sins, enters the noble path, and uses meditation to cover past wrongdoing while withdrawing from harmful action, senses, Karman, and language. As life approaches its end, he learns a religious death. By avoiding new acts and understanding the world’s sins, wise men pass beyond misery, reach excellence, or become liberated.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Seventh Lecture, Called Description of the Wicked.

A wise man who understands the Law knows that all beings desire happiness. He should light no fire, abstain from violence, conquer pleasures, and live by wisdom rather than outward practices.

Eighth Lecture, Called on Exertion.

A wise man conquers greed, enters the noble path, abstains from sins, and learns the method of dying a religious death when his life draws toward its end. He covers his sins with meditation and withdraws his hands, feet, mind, senses, bad Karman, and bad language.

Fifteenth Lecture, Called the Yamakas.

Wise men know the sins of the world and get beyond misery by avoiding new acts. They use the ends of things and are therefore called makers of an end; some reach excellence and some become liberated.
