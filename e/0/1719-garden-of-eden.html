Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, the Garden of Eden is the place planted by the LORD God where the man is placed to cultivate and keep it, later becoming the setting of the serpent’s temptation, the couple’s hiding, their expulsion, and the cherubim’s guarding of access to the tree of life. In The Book of Ezekiel, Eden is the garden of God whose trees envy the Assyrian cedar and are later brought down to the lower parts of the earth and comforted there.

Garden of Eden in Genesis

author unknown · about 449 BC · the work’s page · read it where Garden of Eden first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Garden of Eden is a place planted by the LORD God eastward in Eden, filled with pleasant, food-bearing trees, including the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil in its middle. A river flowing out of Eden waters it and divides into four rivers. The man is placed there to cultivate and keep it. It later becomes the setting of the serpent's temptation and the couple's hiding. The man is sent out, and cherubim with a flaming sword guard its east against access to the tree of life.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

The LORD God planted the garden eastward, in Eden, with trees pleasant to see and good for food, including the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil in the middle. A river went out of Eden to water it and divided into four rivers (Pishon, Gihon, Hiddekel, and the Euphrates). The LORD God put the man there to cultivate and keep it.

Chapter 3

The garden is the setting where the serpent tempts the woman, the couple hide among its trees, and the LORD God walks in the cool of the day. The man is sent out of it to till the ground. Cherubim and a flaming sword that turns every way are placed at its east to guard the way to the tree of life.
