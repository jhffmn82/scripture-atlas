Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, salvation comes from God alone, who is the psalmist’s rock, salvation, and security. In Psalms, salvation is near those who fear God, and the psalmist asks the LORD to grant it. In Psalms: The LORD's right hand and holy arm have worked salvation for him. In The Acts of the Apostles, salvation through Jesus includes remission of sins and justification for everyone who believes, beyond what Moses’ law could achieve. In The Acts of the Apostles: is_sent_to nations [and they will listen] Paul declares to the Jewish leaders that the salvation of God is sent to the nations, and that they will listen. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, salvation comes to those who confess Jesus as Lord, believe in his resurrection, and call on the Lord’s name. In Paul’s Letter to the Ephesians, salvation through faith means believers are saved by grace as God’s gift rather than by their works. In 1 Timothy: God desires all people to be saved and to come to full knowledge of the truth.

and 19 more works

In 2 Timothy: is_given_in Jesus Christ [before times eternal] God saved and called people with a holy calling, not according to their works but according to his own purpose and grace given in Christ Jesus before times eternal. In Titus, salvation comes through God’s kindness, love, and mercy, through regeneration, renewal by the Holy Spirit, and justification by grace. In The Letter to the Hebrews, salvation is a great matter proclaimed through the Lord and authored by Jesus, who was perfected through suffering. In 1 Peter, salvation is ready to be revealed in the last time and is received through faith in Christ’s redemption. In The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry, salvation is the Lord’s blessing and help, given through his servant as a light to the Gentiles. In The Second Book of Nephi, redemption comes through the Holy Messiah, who sacrifices himself for sin and redeems mankind from the fall. In The Second Book of Nephi, remission of sins comes through Christ, repentance, baptism by water, and baptism of fire and the Holy Ghost. In The Book of Omni, salvation is associated with coming unto Christ, partaking of his salvation, and enduring to the end in fasting and prayer. In The Book of Mosiah: The people receive a remission of their sins after calling on God in faith, and they experience joy and peace of conscience. In The Book of Mosiah, salvation comes through Jesus Christ, repentance, faith, the atonement, and keeping God’s commandments. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, remission of sins is expected through Christ and received from the Lord after a merciful plea. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, salvation is deliverance through faith, repentance, Christ’s atonement, and faithfulness to the end. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, the holy calling was prepared from the world’s foundation and leads faithful recipients to ordination in the high priesthood. In The Book of Helaman, salvation comes through repentance, faith in Jesus Christ, and belief in the Son of God. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, remission of sins comes through faith in Christ, repentance, and baptism. In The Book of Moroni: Salvation in the kingdom of God requires charity, faith, and hope. In The Book of Moroni: Remission of sins comes through the shedding of the blood of Christ in the covenant of the Father, allowing people to become holy and without spot. In The Book of Moroni, salvation requires faith in the name of Jesus Christ, without which there would be no redemption. In Asa ki War (Asa di Var), salvation is obtained through meeting the true Guru, love, devotion, humility, and repeating God’s name.

Abstract written by a model

Salvation comes only through Jesus Christ, through repentance, faith, and the atonement prepared for all mankind. It is everlasting and is received through Christ’s wisdom, power, justice, and mercy. It requires faith continued to the end of life, repentance, and keeping the commandments of God; it does not come through the law alone, because without the atonement people would perish notwithstanding the law of Moses. salvation in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where salvation first appears

Abstract written by a model

Salvation is deliverance promised to the righteous and to those who believe on the name of the Son of God. It requires humility, trust in the true and living God, faith, repentance, and faithfulness until the end; the truly penitent receive it, while those who do not repent are cast off. It cannot come through the law of Moses, nor can people be saved in their sins, because no unclean thing inherits the kingdom of heaven. It comes only through the Lord Jesus Christ, his power and deliverance, the last sacrifice, the atonement, and the plan of mercy. Salvation brings people from death, misery, darkness, and destruction into everlasting light. It is anticipated for righteous people who are slain and go to God, proclaimed as glad tidings, and presented as available now. The ministry prepares people for it, while records and words bring knowledge of God and aid the salvation of souls. God also brings about the salvation of many souls through small means.
