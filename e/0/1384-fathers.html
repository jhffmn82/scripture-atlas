Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the fathers and their descendants received God’s covenant and commands but were remembered as stubborn and rebellious, forgetting God, worshipping idols, and suffering oppression, while God remembered his covenant and the psalmist prayed for rescue. In The Book of Jeremiah, Israel’s fathers forsook the LORD, followed and worshipped other gods, and broke his law; the LORD gave them the land and promises to restore the people, while nations said the fathers inherited lies. In The Book of Ezekiel, the fathers repeatedly rebelled against the LORD in Egypt and the wilderness, clinging to idols, rejecting ordinances, profaning Sabbaths, and later worshipping at high places, though the LORD spared them from destruction but barred them from the land. In The Book of Zechariah, the fathers disobeyed the former prophets who urged them to turn from evil ways, becoming objects of the LORD’s great displeasure before repenting when his words and decrees overtook them. In The Acts of the Apostles, the Israelite fathers sold Joseph, went to Egypt, multiplied, suffered mistreatment, rejected Moses, worshipped a calf and Moloch, preserved the testimony tabernacle into Joshua’s time, and persecuted prophets. In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, the fathers shared the cloud, sea crossing, baptism into Moses, and spiritual food and drink, but most displeased God and fell, serving as examples and warnings for believers.

Israelites (the fathers and their offspring) in Psalms

author unknown · undated · the work’s page · read it where Israelites (the fathers and their offspring) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Israelites, the fathers and their descendants, are a people given God's covenant and commands to pass to their children, yet remembered as a stubborn, rebellious generation with disloyal hearts. Despite wonders in Egypt, they forgot God, rebelled at the Red Sea, made a calf, joined Baal Peor and sacrificed children to idols. Oppressed by enemies, they cry out; God remembers his covenant, and the psalmist prays for rescue.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 78

The fathers told the teachings to later generations and received God's covenant and commands, which they were to pass on to their children. They are also held up as a warning, a stubborn and rebellious generation whose hearts were not loyal, and later Israel dealt treacherously like them. God did marvelous things in their sight in Egypt.

Chapter 106

The Israelites confess that they sinned with their fathers, who forgot God's wonders, rebelled at the Red Sea, craved in the desert, made a calf, murmured in their tents, joined Baal Peor, mixed with the nations and sacrificed their children to idols. As a result they were ruled and oppressed by their enemies and brought low in iniquity, but God heard their cry and remembered his covenant. The psalmist prays that God will save them and gather them from among the nations.

Chapter 7

The fathers sold Joseph, went to Egypt in famine, multiplied there, and were later mistreated and forced to abandon their babies. They rejected Moses, turned back in their hearts to Egypt, made a calf and worshiped it, and took up the tabernacle of Moloch. They had the tabernacle of the testimony, brought it in with Joshua, and persecuted the prophets, as Stephen says his hearers now do.
