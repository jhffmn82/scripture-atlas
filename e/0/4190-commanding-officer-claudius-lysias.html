Abstract written by a model

Claudius Lysias is the commanding officer of the regiment, who commands soldiers and centurions. When Jerusalem erupts in an uproar, he rushes down, stops the beating, arrests Paul, has him chained, and lets him address the crowd. He orders Paul scourged, then becomes afraid on learning Paul is a Roman he had bound; he frees him and convenes the chief priests and council. Fearing Paul would be torn apart, he takes him into the barracks. After hearing of a plot from Paul's nephew, he sends Paul to Felix under heavy armed escort by night, with a letter stating Paul faced no charge worthy of death or imprisonment.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

The commanding officer of the regiment hears that Jerusalem is in an uproar and runs down with soldiers and centurions, ending the beating. He arrests Paul, has him bound with two chains, and has him taken toward the barracks, asking whether he is the Egyptian who led four thousand Assassins into the wilderness. After Paul answers, he permits Paul to speak to the people.

Chapter 22

The commanding officer has Paul brought into the barracks and orders him examined by scourging to learn why the crowd shouts against him. Upon learning Paul is a Roman, he becomes afraid because he had bound him, and the next day he frees Paul and convenes the chief priests and council.

Chapter 23

Fearing Paul would be torn apart, he has soldiers take Paul by force into the barracks. After hearing of the plot from Paul's nephew, he orders two hundred soldiers, seventy horsemen, and two hundred spearmen to take Paul to Felix by night. He writes a letter identified as from Claudius Lysias saying Paul was accused over questions of the law and not charged with anything worthy of death or imprisonment.
