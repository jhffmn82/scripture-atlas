Abstract written by a model

The trespass offering is a most holy offering brought by a guilty person to the LORD, typically a ram without defect from the flock, valued in silver by the sanctuary shekel and brought to the priest. Chapter 5 presents it for sin regarding holy things or unwitting sin, declaring the bringer guilty before the LORD. Chapter 6 adds that the priest makes atonement through it and the offender is forgiven. Chapter 7 gives the procedure: it is killed where the burnt offering is killed, its blood sprinkled around the altar, and its fat, kidneys and liver cover burned by the priest. Male priests eat the rest in a holy place, and the officiating priest owns it, under one law with the sin offering. Chapter 14 shows a different use: a male lamb is waved with oil, followed by sin, burnt and meal offerings, with a poorer person bringing one male lamb plus two doves or pigeons.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

The term covers the offering the guilty person brings to the LORD for his sin, including the lamb, goat, or birds. For unwitting trespass it is specifically a ram without defect valued in silver by the sanctuary shekel. It is declared that the one who brings it is certainly guilty before the LORD.

Chapter 6

The trespass offering is a ram without defect from the flock, valued by estimation and brought to the priest. Through it the priest makes atonement before the LORD and the offender is forgiven. It is also named as most holy, along with the sin offering.

Chapter 7

The trespass offering is most holy and is killed in the place of the burnt offering, with its blood sprinkled around the altar. Its fat, kidneys and liver cover are burned by the priest as an offering made by fire, and male priests eat the rest in a holy place. It has one law with the sin offering, and the priest who makes atonement with it has it.

Chapter 14

On the eighth day the person brings two male lambs, a ewe lamb, fine flour mixed with oil, and a log of oil. One male lamb is the trespass offering and is waved with the oil, and the sin offering, burnt offering, and meal offering follow to make atonement. A poor person brings one male lamb for the trespass offering, a smaller amount of flour and oil, and two turtledoves or young pigeons, one for a sin offering and one for a burnt offering.
