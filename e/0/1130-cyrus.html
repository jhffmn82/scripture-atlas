Cyrus

Also called: Cyrus King of Persia, Cyrus king of Babylon, Cyrus king of Persia, Cyrus the Persian, Cyrus the king, King Cyrus the king of Persia, O king, him, his anointed, king, king (Cyrus), the king, the king’s signet

Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Chronicles, Cyrus is the king of Persia whose spirit the LORD stirs to proclaim the command to build a house in Jerusalem. In The Book of Ezra, Cyrus is the king of Persia and Babylon who commissions the rebuilding of the Jerusalem temple, returns its vessels, and whose decree authorizes its completion. In The Book of Isaiah, Cyrus is the LORD's shepherd and anointed who subdues nations, enables Jerusalem and the temple to be rebuilt, frees exiles, and receives tribute from nations. In Daniel (Greek), Cyrus the Persian receives the kingdom, honors Daniel, destroys Bel's priests, later delivers Daniel to the Babylonians, and after finding him alive praises Daniel's God and punishes his accusers.

Cyrus in The Book of Ezra

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Cyrus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cyrus is a king of Persia, also called king of Babylon, who rules Persia and is said to have been given all the earth's kingdoms by the LORD. In his first year the LORD stirred his spirit, and he issued a written proclamation across his kingdom commissioning him to build a house in Jerusalem. He had the temple vessels counted out to Sheshbazzar, whom he appointed governor, with instructions to place them in the Jerusalem temple. Later, the elders cite his decree, and a scroll found at Achmetha confirms it, ordering expenses paid from the king's house and the vessels restored. His decree is named as an authority behind the completed building.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Ezra / Chapter 1

In his first year, Cyrus's spirit was stirred by the LORD, and he issued a written proclamation throughout his kingdom saying the God of heaven had given him all the kingdoms and commanded him to build a house in Jerusalem. He also brought out the vessels of the LORD's house through Mithredath the treasurer and had them counted out to Sheshbazzar.

Chapter 5

According to the elders, Cyrus, called king of Babylon, made a decree in his first year to build God's house. He took the gold and silver vessels Nebuchadnezzar had carried off from the Babylon temple and delivered them to Sheshbazzar, whom he had made governor, with instructions to put them in the Jerusalem temple.

Chapter 6

A scroll found at Achmetha records that in his first year Cyrus decreed the rebuilding of God's house at Jerusalem. He ordered the expenses paid from the king's house and the vessels taken by Nebuchadnezzar restored. His decree is cited as one of the authorities behind the completed building.

Cyrus in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Cyrus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cyrus is a figure named by the LORD as his shepherd and anointed, called by name though he has not known the LORD. Strengthened to subdue nations and strip kings of armor, he is promised hidden treasures. He will build the LORD's city, enabling Jerusalem's rebuilding and the temple's foundation, and free the exiles without price or reward. Egypt, Ethiopia, and the Sabeans will come to him in chains, acknowledging God is in him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 44

Cyrus is named by the LORD as his shepherd who shall perform all his pleasure. His role is connected to the LORD's word that Jerusalem will be built and the temple's foundation laid.

Chapter 45

Cyrus is addressed as the LORD's anointed, whose right hand the LORD has held to subdue nations and strip kings of their armor. The LORD promises him treasures of darkness and hidden riches, names him though he has not known the LORD, and says he will build the LORD's city and free the exiles without price or reward. Egypt, Ethiopia, and the Sabeans are to come over to him in chains and acknowledge that God is in him.
