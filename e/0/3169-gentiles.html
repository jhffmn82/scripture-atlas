In The First Book of the Maccabees, Gentiles are strong, fortified forces defeated at Emmaus whose reproach is turned away at the sanctuary’s rededication, after which Judas fortifies Zion against them.

Gentiles in The First Book of the Maccabees

Abstract written by a model

The Gentiles are hostile neighbors of the Jews who profaned Jerusalem's holy things and took her spoils. They shelter apostate Jews and, angered by the rebuilt altar, plot to destroy Jacob's race, killing Jews in several regions, but are repeatedly defeated by Judas and Simon, though Gorgias's force wins at Jamnia.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

The Gentiles are described as having profaned Jerusalem's holy things and taken her spoils. Mattathias's company fights to rescue the law from their hand, and apostate Jews flee to them for safety; Mattathias tells his sons to repay them.

Chapter 5

The Gentiles around Israel, angered by the rebuilt altar, plot to destroy the race of Jacob and kill Jews in Gilead, Galilee, and elsewhere. They are repeatedly defeated by Judas and Simon, with cities burned and many killed, and Gorgias's force defeats Joseph and Azarias at Jamnia.
