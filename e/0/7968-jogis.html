Abstract written by a model

In Asa ki War (Asa di Var), Jogis meditate on God the Creator, call God the Unseen and Bright One, and seek divine knowledge. In Hymns of Guru Nanak, Jogis are portrayed critically as ascetics who may remain hypocritical and attached to wives, while those who follow the Guru's instruction dwell peacefully.

Jogis in Hymns of Guru Nanak

Guru Nanak · the work’s page · read it where Jogis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jogis are portrayed critically as ascetics who beg, wear ashes and patched coats, yet may remain hypocritical and attached to wives. Without the Word and the true Guru, their twelve sects, halters, and breath-control lead to ruin. A Jogi who receives the Guru's instruction, meditates, accepts alms of love, and sees all equally instead dwells peacefully.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Ramkali .. Ramkali ki War I

The Jogi is criticized for begging, hypocrisy, worldly attachment, and relying on outward marks such as ashes, patched coats, bags, and earrings. The Jogi who follows the Guru's instruction, practices meditation, and is content with the alms of love can live peacefully and see all people equally.

Rag Prabhati .. Prabhati Ashtapadi

They are said to go to ruin in twelve sects, and the Jogis, Kaprias, and plucked-headed Saravagis without the Word are described as having halters round their necks. Their breath-control practices are declared of no avail without the true Guru.
