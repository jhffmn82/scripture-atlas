Chapter 7

Nicanor, an honorable prince of the king who hated Israel, was sent to destroy the people and came to Jerusalem with a great army. He tried to seize Judas through a deceitful offer of peace, lost about five hundred men at Capharsalama, then mocked the priests at mount Zion and swore to burn the temple. He was the first to fall at Adasa, and his head and right hand were cut off and hung beside Jerusalem.

Nicanor in The Second Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 124 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nicanor first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nicanor, son of Patroclus, is a chief friend of the king and former master of the elephants, described as thrice-accursed, boastful and arrogant. In Chapter 8, Ptolemy appoints him to destroy the Jews and sell captives to raise the king's two-thousand-talent tribute to the Romans; his army is routed and he flees to Antioch, admitting the Jews have One who fights for them. In Chapter 14, as governor of Judea appointed by Demetrius, he is sent to kill Judas but instead befriends him, then, after the king's rebuke, plots against him, threatens the temple, and sends soldiers against Razis. In Chapter 15 he resolves to attack on the Sabbath, claims to be a sovereign on earth, and advances with trumpets and victory songs. He is found dead in full armor; his head, hand and arm are taken to Jerusalem and displayed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

Nicanor, son of Patroclus, is appointed by Ptolemy to destroy the Jewish race and plans to sell captive Jews at seventy per talent to raise the king's tribute to the Romans. His army is routed, and he removes his glorious apparel and flees like a fugitive slave to Antioch. There he declares that the Jews have One who fights for them and are invulnerable while they follow his laws.

Chapter 14

Nicanor, formerly master of the elephants, is made governor of Judea and sent to kill Judas. He instead makes a covenant with Judas and befriends him, but after Demetrius's rebuke he plots by strategy, then threatens to level the temple and build one to Dionysus. He sends over five hundred soldiers against Razis.

Chapter 15

Nicanor resolves to attack Judas on the Sabbath, refuses the Jews' plea to honor the day, and declares himself a sovereign on earth who commands them to bear arms. He plans a monument of victory, advances with trumpets and victory songs, and is found dead in full armor after the battle. His head, hand and arm are cut off and taken to Jerusalem, his tongue is cut out, and his head and shoulder are hung from the citadel.
