Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Isaiah, the inhabitants of Jerusalem rejoiced while their rulers fled, prepared defenses without seeking the LORD, refused mourning, and faced unforgiven iniquity and death. In The Book of Jeremiah, the inhabitants of Jerusalem forsook the LORD, worshiped other gods, refused his words, sacrificed children to Baal, and faced judgment by sword, beasts, and cannibalism. In The Book of Ezekiel, the inhabitants of Jerusalem claimed the land, urged others to stay far from the LORD, desolated the land through unfaithfulness, and faced judgment by fire. In The Book of Zephaniah, the inhabitants of Jerusalem faced the LORD's punishment for idolatry, improper worship, violence, and deceit. In The Book of Zechariah, the inhabitants of Jerusalem were promised abundant, unwalled habitation and protection by the LORD, while those dispersed to the north and Babylon were called to return.

inhabitants of Jerusalem in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · 629 to 579 BC · the work’s page · read it where inhabitants of Jerusalem first appears

Abstract written by a model

The inhabitants of Jerusalem forsook the LORD, burned incense to other gods, refused to hear his words, and sacrificed children to Baal. In Chapter 19 the LORD condemns them with the kings of Judah, foretelling death by sword, corpses as food for beasts, and cannibalism in siege.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 19

Along with the kings of Judah, they are addressed as having forsaken the LORD, defiled the place, burned incense to unknown gods, and filled it with innocent blood. The LORD says they will fall by the sword, be given as food to birds and animals, and eat the flesh of their children and friends in the siege.

inhabitants of Jerusalem in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · about 592 to 570 BC · the work’s page · read it where inhabitants of Jerusalem first appears

Abstract written by a model

The inhabitants of Jerusalem are a group who tell Ezekiel's brothers and Israel to stay far from the LORD, claiming the land as their possession. Their unfaithfulness desolates the land, and they eat in fearfulness. Likened to useless vinewood, they face the LORD's set face and fire, and will know he is the LORD.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

The inhabitants of Jerusalem have told Ezekiel's brothers and all the house of Israel to go far away from the LORD, claiming the land has been given to them as a possession.

Chapter 15

The inhabitants of Jerusalem are likened to the useless vine wood given to the fire. The LORD will set his face against them, and though they go out from the fire, it will still devour them. They will then know that he is the LORD.
