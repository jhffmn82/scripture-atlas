Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Jacob is the younger twin son of Isaac and Rebekah who obtains Isaac’s blessing, serves Laban, fathers twelve sons and Dinah, wrestles with a man and is renamed Israel, reconciles with Esau, and settles in Egypt before dying at 147. In Genesis, Israel is Jacob, an aged father who sends Benjamin to Egypt, is mourned and buried at Machpelah, and is said to have asked Joseph to forgive his brothers. In Psalms, Jacob is the patriarch whose descendants are Israel, the LORD’s chosen generation seeking Him, and to whom the covenant was confirmed. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Jacob is the patriarch in whom Wisdom dwells and whose inheritance is divided among twelve tribes. In The Book of Jeremiah, Jacob, also called Israel, is the LORD’s servant who faces trouble and captivity but is promised salvation, restored dwellings, and measured correction. In The Book of Hosea, Jacob is recalled as grasping his brother’s heel, contending with God and an angel, weeping at Bethel, fleeing to Aram, and serving for a wife. In The Book of Malachi, Jacob is Esau’s brother whom the LORD loved, cited in answer to Israel’s question about divine love. In Surah 12: Joseph (Yusuf), Jacob is Joseph’s father who grieves over his sons, remains patient and trusting in Allah, and regains his sight through Joseph’s shirt.

Jacob in Genesis

author unknown · about 449 BC · the work’s page · read it where Jacob first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jacob is a man, the younger twin son of Isaac and Rebekah, born holding his brother Esau's heel, which gives him his name. A quiet, smooth-skinned man who lives in tents, he buys Esau's birthright for bread and lentil stew and then obtains Isaac's blessing by deceit, wearing Esau's clothes and goat skins at Rebekah's urging. Esau's murderous anger forces him to flee. Blessed and commanded by Isaac not to marry a Canaanite, he goes to Paddan Aram, dreaming of a stairway to heaven at Bethel, where he receives the LORD's promises, sets up a pillar and makes a vow. He serves his uncle Laban twenty years: seven years for Rachel, whom he loves more than Leah, and who is replaced by Leah, then seven more for Rachel, then six for flocks under a wage agreement for speckled, spotted and black animals. Through his wives and their servants he fathers twelve sons and a daughter, Dinah, including Joseph and Benjamin by Rachel, and grows very wealthy in flocks, servants, camels and donkeys. Told by the LORD to return, he flees secretly, makes a covenant with Laban, and meets angels. Fearing Esau, he prays and sends gifts, then wrestles a man until daybreak, is renamed Israel, names Peniel and limps from a strained thigh. He reconciles with Esau, settles at Succoth and Shechem, buys land and erects the altar El Elohe Israel. He rebukes Simeon and Levi after the attack over Dinah, fearing his few numbers, and obeys God's call to Bethel, where God repeats the promise. Rachel dies after bearing Benjamin, and he later returns to bury Isaac with Esau. His favoritism for Joseph, shown by the tunic of many colors, leads to deep mourning when his sons deceive him with the bloodied tunic. Famine sends his sons to Egypt, and he fears for Benjamin. Learning Joseph lives, he goes to Egypt, receiving God's promise at Beersheba, and settles in Goshen. At 130 he tells Pharaoh his years were few and evil. He lives seventeen years in Egypt, adopts Ephraim and Manasseh, giving Joseph an extra portion, blesses his sons, charges them to bury him at Machpelah, and dies at 147, gathered to his people.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

17 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 25

Jacob was born second, holding Esau's heel, and grew into a quiet man living in tents whom Rebekah loved. He boiled stew and made Esau swear to sell him the birthright before giving Esau bread and lentil stew.

Chapter 27

Jacob, the younger and smooth-skinned son, fears being seen as a deceiver but obeys Rebekah, wears Esau's clothes and goat skins, and tells Isaac he is Esau. He receives Isaac's blessing of plenty, rule over nations and his brothers. Esau's murderous plan then forces him to flee to Laban in Haran at Rebekah's command.

Chapter 28

Jacob is blessed and commanded by Isaac not to marry a Canaanite woman, and he obeys his father and mother by going to Paddan Aram. Along the way he dreams of a stairway to heaven and receives the LORD's promises, then sets up his stone as a pillar, names the place Bethel, and makes a vow.

Chapter 29

Jacob journeys to the land of the children of the east, meets Rachel at the well, rolls away the stone to water Laban's flock, and weeps as he kisses her. He serves Laban seven years for Rachel, is given Leah instead, and agrees to serve seven more years to also receive Rachel, whom he loves more than Leah. He fathers Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah with Leah.

Chapter 30

Jacob becomes angry at Rachel's demand, lies with Bilhah, Zilpah, and Leah, and fathers Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, Issachar, Zebulun, Dinah, and Joseph. He then asks Laban to send him away, negotiates a hire of the speckled, spotted, and black animals, and through his rod method becomes exceedingly wealthy with large flocks, servants, camels, and donkeys.

Chapter 31

Jacob hears the complaints of Laban's sons, is told by the LORD to return home, and persuades Rachel and Leah to leave with him. He flees secretly with his family and livestock, and when Laban overtakes him he denies knowing about the stolen teraphim and angrily recounts his twenty years of faithful service. He sets up a pillar, makes a covenant with Laban, swears by the fear of Isaac, and offers a sacrifice.

Chapter 32

Jacob is met by angels, sends messengers to Esau, and becomes greatly afraid on learning Esau approaches with four hundred men. He divides his people into two companies, prays to God, sends a present ahead, and crosses his family over the Jabbok before wrestling alone with a man until daybreak. He refuses to let the man go without a blessing, is renamed Israel, names the place Peniel, and limps away with a strained thigh.

Chapter 33

Jacob arranges his family in order and goes ahead of them, bowing to the ground seven times before Esau. He urges Esau to accept his gift, saying he has seen Esau's face as one sees the face of God, and he declines Esau's offer of an escort, citing the tender children and the flocks. He then travels to Succoth, builds a house and shelters, comes in peace to Shechem, buys the ground where he pitched his tent, and erects the altar El Elohe Israel.

Chapter 34

Jacob hears that Dinah has been defiled and holds his peace until his sons come in from the field. After Simeon and Levi's attack he rebukes them for making him odious among the Canaanites and Perizzites, fearing that, being few in number, he and his house will be destroyed.

Chapter 35

Jacob obeys God's command to go to Bethel, has his household put away their foreign gods and hides them under the oak by Shechem, and builds an altar at Bethel. God renames him Israel and repeats the promise of nation, kings and land; Jacob sets up a stone pillar, pours a drink offering and oil on it, and later sets a pillar on Rachel's grave. He names Rachel's newborn son Benjamin, hears of Reuben's act with Bilhah, and returns to his father Isaac at Mamre, helping Esau bury him.

Chapter 37

Jacob, also called Israel, lives in Canaan and loves Joseph more than his other sons, making him a tunic of many colors. He rebukes Joseph's second dream but keeps it in mind, sends Joseph to check on his brothers, and is deceived by the bloodied tunic into believing Joseph was devoured. He tears his clothes, wears sackcloth, mourns many days, and refuses comfort, saying he will go down to Sheol mourning.

Chapter 42

Jacob tells his sons to go to Egypt to buy grain so that they may live, but he does not send Benjamin, fearing harm to him. When his sons return, he laments that he has been bereaved of Joseph and Simeon and refuses to let Benjamin go down, saying that harm to him would bring his gray hairs down to Sheol with sorrow.

Chapter 45

Jacob, called Israel, is the father Joseph asks about and wants brought down to Egypt. When his sons tell him Joseph is alive, his heart faints and he does not believe them, but when he sees the wagons Joseph sent his spirit revives, and he says he will go see Joseph before he dies.

Chapter 46

Jacob (Israel) goes to Beersheba, offers sacrifices to the God of his father Isaac, and receives God's promise in a night vision to go down to Egypt. He travels there with all his offspring, sends Judah ahead to Goshen, and weeps with Joseph on meeting him, saying he can now die since he has seen Joseph alive.

Chapter 47

Jacob is brought before Pharaoh, blesses him, and says his 130 years of pilgrimage have been few and evil, not reaching the days of his fathers. He lives 17 years in Egypt and dies-to-be at 147, and, called Israel, he makes Joseph swear to carry him out of Egypt and bury him in his fathers' burying place, then bows himself on the bed's head.

Chapter 48

Jacob, also called Israel, strengthens himself and sits up on the bed to receive Joseph, and recounts God Almighty's promise at Luz and Rachel's burial near Ephrath. Though his eyes are dim, he claims Ephraim and Manasseh as his own, blesses them with crossed hands, and refuses Joseph's attempt to correct him. He announces that he is dying, that God will be with Joseph, and that he has given Joseph a portion taken from the Amorite with his sword and bow.

Chapter 49

Jacob, also called Israel, gathers his sons and speaks to each of them about what will happen in the days to come. He then charges them to bury him in the cave of Machpelah, and after finishing, he gathers his feet into the bed, breathes his last, and is gathered to his people.

Abstract written by a model

Israel, also called Jacob, is an aged father who worships God Almighty and has sons by his wife Rachel, including Benjamin, whose life he loves as bound up with his own. In Chapter 43 he reluctantly yields to Judah's pledge and sends Benjamin with a present, double money, and a prayer for mercy, resigning himself to possible bereavement. In Chapter 44, Judah recounts his grief over a lost son and fear that losing another would bring his gray hairs down to Sheol. In Chapter 50 he is embalmed by Joseph's physicians, mourned seventy days by the Egyptians, and buried by his sons at the cave of Machpelah, as he wished. His brothers claim he asked that Joseph forgive them.

Israel is embalmed over forty days by Joseph's physicians, and the Egyptians weep for him seventy days.
