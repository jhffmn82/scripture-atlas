Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the tabernacle is commanded, constructed, furnished, completed, and filled with the LORD’s glory. In Exodus, the Tent of Meeting is the sacred structure and meeting place where the LORD meets and speaks with Moses and Israel. In Leviticus, the Tent of Meeting is the sacred place where the LORD speaks to Moses and dwells with Israel and where offerings and atonement rites are performed. In Numbers, the Tent of Meeting is the sacred tabernacle at the center of Israel’s camp where the LORD meets with Moses and where Levites serve and guard the holy things. In Numbers, the Tabernacle of the Testimony is the sacred tent entrusted to the Levites, whose cloud determines whether Israel camps or travels. In The Second Book of Chronicles, the Tent of Meeting is the sacred wilderness tent located at Gibeon, where Solomon and the assembly go and the bronze altar stands. In The Letter to the Hebrews, the earthly tabernacle Moses made is described as a copy of heavenly things with a Holy Place and Holy of Holies.

tabernacle (Tent of Meeting) in Exodus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where tabernacle (Tent of Meeting) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The tabernacle, also called the Tent of Meeting and the Tabernacle of the Testimony, is a sanctuary the LORD commands the people to make so that he may dwell among them. It and all its furniture must follow the pattern he shows Moses on the mountain. Its structure is ten curtains of fine twined linen with cherubim, coupled into a single unit by gold clasps and loops. Acacia wood boards stand upright on silver sockets and are held by gold-overlaid bars, with bronze pins. It is covered by eleven goats' hair curtains, then rams' skins dyed red, with sea cow hides above. A veil of blue, purple, scarlet and linen divides the holy place from the most holy place, and an embroidered screen hangs at the tent door. Inside, the ark of the covenant stands screened by the veil, the table of showbread on the north side, the lamp stand opposite on the south, and the golden incense altar before the veil. The altar of burnt offering is at the door, with a basin between it and the tent, and a court with a screened gate surrounds it. The account unfolds from command to making to completion. Chapters 25 and 26 give the command and design, and chapter 35 lists the parts and furnishings. In chapter 36 wise-hearted workers build it, and in chapter 38 its materials are counted under Ithamar for the Levites' service, with Bezalel making everything commanded. In chapter 39 the work is declared finished, and the Israelites bring it to Moses, who sees it was done as commanded. In chapter 40 it is raised on the first day of the first month of the second year, furnished and enclosed by the court. Then the cloud covers it and the LORD's glory fills it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 25

The LORD commands the people to make him a sanctuary so that he may dwell among them. It and all its furniture must be made according to the pattern of the tabernacle that he shows Moses.

Chapter 26

The tabernacle is to be made of ten linen curtains with cherubim, coupled into a single unit by gold clasps, and is to be set up according to the pattern shown on the mountain. It is covered by goats' hair curtains, rams' skins and sea cow hides, and is divided by a veil into the holy place and the most holy place.

Chapter 35

The tabernacle is to be made along with its covering, clasps, boards, bars, pillars, sockets, and pins. Its furnishings are also listed: the ark, mercy seat, table, lamp stand, incense altar, altar of burnt offering, basin, and court hangings.

Chapter 36

The tabernacle is made with ten linen curtains with cherubim, joined by loops and gold clasps so it is a unit. It is covered with goats' hair curtains, rams' skins dyed red, and sea cow hides, and is built with acacia boards on silver sockets, gold-covered bars, a veil, and a door screen.

Chapter 38

It is called the Tabernacle of the Testimony, and its materials were counted according to Moses' commandment for the Levites' service under Ithamar son of Aaron the priest. All its pins were of bronze, and Bezalel made everything the LORD commanded Moses.

Chapter 39

The work of the tabernacle of the Tent of Meeting is declared finished, having been done as the LORD commanded Moses. The children of Israel bring it to Moses with its tent, boards, furniture, altars, lamp stand, court hangings and instruments, and Moses sees it was done as commanded.

Chapter 40

The tabernacle is raised on the first day of the first month of the second year, with its sockets, boards, bars, pillars, coverings, and roof, and furnished and surrounded by a court. Afterward the cloud covers it and the LORD's glory fills it.

Tent of Meeting in Exodus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Tent of Meeting first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Tent of Meeting is both a sacred structure and a meeting place where the LORD meets and speaks with Moses and Israel. Its furnishings include the veil, ark of the covenant, table, pure lamp stand with vessels, altar of incense, altar of burnt offering, and basin with its base. The records begin with the lamp kept by Aaron and his sons outside the veil from evening to morning. They then describe its role in consecration: Aaron and his sons are washed, the bull is killed, the consecration meal is eaten, and the daily burnt offering is made at its door, where the LORD promises to meet Israel and sanctify it by his glory. Washing at the basin is required before entering, so they do not die. It is first to be anointed with holy oil, and incense is placed before the covenant where the LORD meets Moses. It is the first thing commanded to be made, with its furniture. Finally, Moses habitually pitches the tent far outside the camp; everyone seeking the LORD goes there, the pillar of cloud stands at its door as the LORD speaks with Moses, and the people worship from their tent doors.

Tent of Meeting in Leviticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Tent of Meeting first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Tent of Meeting is the sacred place where the LORD speaks to Moses and dwells with the children of Israel, even amid their uncleanness. Its door is the central site for worship: offerers bring burnt offerings and sin-offering bulls there, the altar of burnt offering stands before it, and offerings made elsewhere bring blood guilt and being cut off. Inside, the priest sprinkles blood before the veil and on the altar of sweet incense. The records progress from sacrifice procedures (Leviticus 1, 4), to the congregation's assembly and the seven-day ordination of Aaron and his sons (8), to the Day of Atonement, when Aaron alone enters and leaves his linen garments (16), ending with the rule that all offerings must be brought to its door (17).

Tent of Meeting in Numbers

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Tent of Meeting first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Tent of Meeting, also called the Tent of the Testimony, is the sacred tabernacle at the center of Israel's camp, where the LORD meets with Moses. Tribes camp around it at a distance, with Levite clans positioned on three sides and Moses, Aaron, and his sons to the east. It sets out after the second camp when the camps move, with the Levites in the middle. Once set up, anointed, and sanctified by Moses, it becomes the place of Levite service: Levites aged thirty to fifty carry and care for its holy things, furnishings, and coverings, and are presented and cleansed before it. Aaron's rod is laid before the covenant there and kept as a token. Throughout, strangers and ordinary Israelites may not approach it, on pain of death or bearing sin.

tabernacle (Tent of the Testimony) in Numbers

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where tabernacle (Tent of the Testimony) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Tabernacle of the Testimony, also called the Tent of the Testimony, is a sacred tent entrusted to the Levites, who carry its furnishings, take it down, set it up, and encamp around it; any stranger approaching is to be put to death, and their encampment guards the congregation from wrath. When it was raised, the cloud covered it. The cloud resting on or lifting from it decides whether Israel camps or travels. When the cloud lifts, Gershon's and Merari's sons carry it, and others set it up before the Kohathites arrive.

Abstract written by a model

The earthly tabernacle Moses made was a copy of heavenly things. It had two parts: the Holy Place, with lamp stand, table and show bread, and, behind the second veil, the Holy of Holies, with the golden altar of incense and ark. Priests entered the first continually; the high priest alone entered the second yearly. Blood was sprinkled on it and its vessels.
