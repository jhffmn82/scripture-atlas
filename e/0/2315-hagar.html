Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Hagar is an Egyptian servant of Sarai and Abraham who becomes Abram’s wife, bears Ishmael, flees and returns at the LORD’s angel’s direction, and is later sent away with her son before God provides them a well. In Paul’s Letter to the Galatians, Hagar represents the covenant from Mount Sinai in Arabia, bearing children into bondage and corresponding to the present Jerusalem.

Hagar in Genesis

author unknown · about 449 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hagar first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hagar is an Egyptian servant of Sarai (Sarah) and Abraham, given to Abram as a wife. Pregnant, she looks down on Sarai, is treated harshly, and flees; the LORD's angel meets her by a fountain, sends her back, and promises many offspring. She bears Ishmael. Later Abraham sends her and her child away; in the wilderness of Beersheba, as water runs out, God's angel reassures her and God shows her a well. She gets her son an Egyptian wife.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 16

Hagar, Sarai's Egyptian servant, is given to Abram as a wife and conceives, then looks down on Sarai and flees after Sarai's harsh treatment. The LORD's angel finds her by a fountain on the way to Shur, tells her to return and submit, and promises numerous offspring. She names the LORD 'a God who sees,' and bears Abram a son, Ishmael.

Chapter 21

Hagar, the Egyptian servant, is sent away by Abraham with bread, a container of water, and her child, and wanders in the wilderness of Beersheba. When the water is spent she weeps at a distance from the child, and the angel of God tells her not to be afraid; God opens her eyes to a well. She later gets a wife for her son from Egypt.
