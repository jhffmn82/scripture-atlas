Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of the Maccabees, Antiochus is a name borne by kings, including Alexander's young child who is crowned under Tryphon and another Antiochus, son of Demetrius, who claims his father's kingdom and later sends Cendebaeus against Judea. In The Second Book of the Maccabees, King Antiochus, son of the previous Antiochus, restores Jewish religious freedom, the temple, ancestral laws and foods, and grants amnesty.

Antiochus in The First Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 99 BC · the work’s page · read it where Antiochus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Antiochus is a name borne by kings in these records. One is Alexander's young child, raised by Imalcue, crowned under Tryphon, who confirms Jonathan's high priesthood and makes Simon governor. Another, son of Demetrius and called a great king of Asia, claims his fathers' kingdom, courts Simon with tax remissions and coinage rights, then rejects his help, cancels covenants, demands cities or money, and sends Cendebaeus against Judea while pursuing Tryphon. He also pays tribute to Romans.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Antiochus was Alexander's young child, raised by Imalcue the Arabian until Tryphon obtained him. He was crowned king and joined the forces that routed Demetrius. He wrote to Jonathan confirming the high priesthood, appointing him over four districts, granting him golden vessels, purple and a golden buckle, and made Jonathan's brother Simon governor from the Ladder of Tyre to Egypt's borders.

Chapter 15

Antiochus, son of Demetrius, writes to Simon promising tax remissions, coinage rights and great honor, and gathers forces that leave Tryphon with few men. He besieges Dor, then rejects Simon's help, cancels their covenants, and demands the cities or money through Athenobius. Angered by Simon's reply, he appoints Cendebaeus against Judea and pursues Tryphon.

King Antiochus in The Second Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 124 BC · the work’s page · read it where King Antiochus first appears

Abstract written by a model

King Antiochus, son of the previous Antiochus, is a ruler who restores Jewish religious freedom. Following his father's wish to leave subjects undisturbed, he writes to his brother Lysias and the Jewish senate, restoring the temple, permitting ancestral laws and foods, granting amnesty, and sending Menelaus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

King Antiochus wrote to his brother Lysias, noting that his father wished subjects left undisturbed and that the Jews preferred their own law to Greek customs, and so ordered their temple restored and their ancestral way of life allowed. He also wrote to the Jewish senate granting friendship, permission to use their own foods and laws, and amnesty for things done in ignorance, and said he had sent Menelaus to encourage them.
