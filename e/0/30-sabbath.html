Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, the seventh day is when God finished creation, rested, blessed the day, and made it holy. In Exodus, the Sabbath is a holy seventh-day rest, sign, covenant, and commemoration of creation, with commands concerning manna, labor, profaning it, and kindling fire. In Leviticus, the Sabbath is the LORD's solemn rest and holy convocation, a dating reference for festivals, and a commanded observance whose missed days are received by the desolate land. In Deuteronomy, the Sabbath day is a holy seventh-day rest from labor for everyone, commemorating Israel's deliverance from servitude in Egypt. In The Book of Nehemiah, the Sabbath is profaned by work and commerce, so Nehemiah shuts the gates and posts guards to sanctify it. In The First Book of the Maccabees, the Sabbath is a day not to be profaned, and the deaths of refugees who refused to fight lead Mattathias and his friends to permit defensive fighting. In The Book of Isaiah, the Sabbath is the LORD's holy day, whose unprofaned observance and honoring bring blessing, covenant promises, delight, and Jacob's heritage. In The Book of Jeremiah, the Sabbath day is to be made holy by bearing no burden and doing no work; keeping it brings blessing, while breaking it brings unquenched fire.

In The Book of Ezekiel, the Sabbath is when the inner court's east gate is opened and the prince presents a specified burnt offering and meal offering. In The Gospel According to Matthew, the Sabbath is the occasion of disputes over grain-plucking and healing, in which Jesus declares that doing good is lawful and that the Son of Man is Lord of the Sabbath. In The Good News According to Mark, the Sabbath is a day of rest and conflict in which Jesus defends his disciples' grain-plucking, declares himself its lord, and argues that doing good and saving life are lawful. In The Gospel According to Luke, the Sabbath is the setting for Jesus' healings and repeated disputes over plucking grain, healing, and doing good or rescuing life. In The Gospel According to John, the Sabbath is the day Jesus heals the man at Bethesda, prompting persecution because carrying a mat is considered unlawful and Jesus calls God his Father.

Sabbath in Exodus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Sabbath first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Sabbath is the seventh day, a holy day of solemn rest to the LORD. It is presented as a sign between the LORD and Israel throughout generations and as a perpetual covenant. It commemorates creation: the LORD made heaven, earth, and sea in six days, rested on the seventh, blessed it, and made it holy. In Chapter 16 it appears through manna: the people gather twice as much on the sixth day, it does not spoil, none is found on the seventh, and they stay in their places and rest. Chapter 20 commands remembering and keeping it holy, with six days of labor and no work by household, servants, livestock, or the stranger within the gates. Chapter 31 calls it a sign that the LORD sanctifies Israel and makes profaning it punishable by death. Chapter 35 repeats the death penalty for working and forbids kindling fire in any habitation.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 16

The Sabbath is presented as a solemn rest, a holy day to the LORD, on the seventh day. On the sixth day the people gather twice as much manna, which does not spoil when kept, and on the seventh none is found. The LORD tells the people to stay in their places that day, and they rest.

Chapter 20

The Sabbath is commanded to be remembered and kept holy: six days for labor, and the seventh a Sabbath to the LORD on which no one in the household, including servants, livestock, and the stranger within the gates, may work. The reason given is that the LORD made heaven, earth, and sea in six days, rested on the seventh, blessed it, and made it holy.

Chapter 31

The Sabbath is described as a sign between the LORD and Israel forever, marking that he sanctifies them. It is a day of solemn rest, holy to the LORD, on the seventh day after six days of work, rooted in the LORD making heaven and earth in six days and resting on the seventh. Profaning it or working on it is punishable by death.

Chapter 35

The Sabbath is set out as the seventh day, a holy day of solemn rest to the LORD. Anyone who does work on it is to be put to death, and no fire is to be kindled in any habitation.

Sabbath in Leviticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Sabbath first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Sabbath is the LORD's solemn rest and holy convocation on the seventh day, kept in all dwellings after six days of work. In Chapter 23 it is established first, serves as a dating reference for the wave sheaf and counting seven Sabbaths, and names the day of atonement. In Chapter 26 it is commanded, and the desolate land receives its missed Sabbaths.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 23

The Sabbath is set out first: work is done six days, and the seventh is a Sabbath of solemn rest and a holy convocation with no work, kept in all their dwellings. It also serves as a reference point for dating the wave sheaf, which is offered on the day after the Sabbath, and for counting seven Sabbaths. The day of atonement is itself called a Sabbath of solemn rest.

Chapter 26

The Israelites are commanded to keep the LORD's Sabbaths. The land will enjoy its Sabbaths while lying desolate and the people are in enemy lands, receiving the rest it did not have while they lived on it.

The Sabbath is the LORD's holy day. Chapter 56 names keeping it unprofaned, with avoiding evil, as a mark of the blessed, and extends the LORD's promises to eunuchs and foreigners who keep it and hold his covenant. Chapter 58 urges honoring it as a delight, not pursuing one's own pleasure, leading to delighting in the LORD and Jacob's heritage.

Chapter 56

Keeping the Sabbath without profaning it is named as a mark of the blessed person and is paired with keeping one's hand from evil. Both eunuchs and foreigners who keep it and hold fast the LORD's covenant receive his promises.

Chapter 58

The Sabbath is called the LORD's holy day, and the people are told to turn their foot from doing their own pleasure, ways, and words on it. They are to call it a delight and honor it as holy to the LORD. Doing so leads to delighting in the LORD, riding on the high places of the earth, and being fed with the heritage of Jacob.

Sabbath in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Sabbath first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Sabbath is a day on which Jesus healed, and healing on it provoked indignation. Across three chapters it becomes the setting of repeated conflict. In Chapter 6, plucking grain and healing a withered hand lead Jesus to call the Son of Man lord of the Sabbath and ask whether it is lawful to do good or save life. In Chapter 13, a synagogue ruler objects to a bent woman's healing, and Jesus cites watering an ox. In Chapter 14, at a Sabbath meal, observers watch him and he cites rescuing a son or ox from a well.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

The Sabbath is the setting of two conflicts: the plucking of grain and the healing of the withered hand. Jesus declares the Son of Man lord of the Sabbath and asks whether it is lawful on the Sabbath to do good or harm, to save a life or kill.

Chapter 13

Jesus heals the bent woman in a synagogue on the Sabbath, and the ruler of the synagogue objects that healing belongs on the six working days. Jesus replies that people untie their ox or donkey to water it on the Sabbath, so the woman ought to be freed from her bondage on that day.

Chapter 14

The meal takes place on a Sabbath, and the people there watch Jesus. Jesus points out that any of them would immediately pull a son or ox out of a well on a Sabbath day.
