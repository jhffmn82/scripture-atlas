Abstract written by a model

In Leviticus, Eleazar, a surviving son of Aaron, follows Moses' instructions regarding his conduct and duties and is rebuked with Ithamar when the sin offering goat is burned. In Numbers, Eleazar serves as a priest and son of Aaron, oversees sanctuary requirements, performs priestly duties, conducts the census, receives instructions concerning Joshua, handles purification and offerings after battle, succeeds Aaron, and helps divide Canaan. In 1 Chronicles, Eleazar is a priest who survives Nadab and Abihu, heads a line of sixteen chief fathers' houses, and is the ancestor of Zadok.

Eleazar in Numbers

author unknown · 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Eleazar first appears

Abstract written by a model

Eleazar is a priest, a son of Aaron, and a prince of the Levites' princes with oversight of those who keep the sanctuary requirements. His duties include the oil for the light, sweet incense, continual meal offering, anointing oil, and the tabernacle's requirements. He appears early in the account taking the censers from the burning, scattering the fire, and beating the bronze censers into an altar covering as a memorial that no outsider to Aaron's line may burn incense. He then receives the red heifer, sprinkles its blood seven times toward the Tent of Meeting, and watches it burned. On the plains of Moab he conducts the census with Moses, his brothers Nadab and Abihu having died for strange fire. He is designated to inquire by the Urim for Joshua, before whom Joshua is commissioned. After the battle he tells the soldiers the purification statute, receives the LORD's wave offering from the warriors' half, and brings the officers' gold into the Tent of Meeting. He succeeds Aaron, receiving his garments, and is named with Joshua to divide Canaan.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 16

Eleazar, son of Aaron the priest, is told by the LORD to take the censers out of the burning and scatter the fire away from the camp. He beats the bronze censers into a covering for the altar. This serves as a memorial that no stranger outside Aaron's offspring should burn incense before the LORD.

Chapter 19

Eleazar receives the red heifer and brings it outside the camp, where it is killed before him. He sprinkles some of its blood with his finger toward the front of the Tent of Meeting seven times, and the burning takes place in his sight.

Chapter 26

Eleazar the priest is addressed by the LORD along with Moses and conducts the census with him on the plains of Moab by the Jordan at Jericho. He is listed as one of Aaron's sons, with Nadab, Abihu, and Ithamar. Nadab and Abihu are noted as having died when they offered strange fire before the LORD.

Chapter 27

Eleazar the priest was present when the daughters of Zelophehad made their request. He was designated to stand with Joshua, inquiring for him by the judgment of the Urim before the LORD, and Joshua was set before him at his commissioning.

Chapter 31

Eleazar, with Moses and the princes, meets the returning army outside the camp and tells the soldiers the statute for purifying metals and other goods. He receives the LORD's wave offering of the soldiers' half of the plunder and, with Moses, takes the officers' gold into the Tent of Meeting.

Chapter 34

Eleazar the priest is named by the LORD as one of the men who shall divide the land for inheritance. He is listed together with Joshua the son of Nun.
