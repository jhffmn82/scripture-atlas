Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, Elisha is the landowner and plowman whom Elijah appoints as his servant and successor. In The Second Book of Kings, Elisha is the prophet and man of God who succeeds Elijah and performs wonders, gives prophecies, and dies before a man revived by touching his bones. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Elisha is the fearless prophet filled with Elijah's spirit whose buried body prophesied and whose works were marvelous in death.

Elisha in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Elisha first appears

Abstract written by a model

Elisha, son of Shaphat from Abel Meholah, is a landowner plowing with twelve yoke of oxen when Elijah casts his mantle on him. He asks to bid his parents farewell, slaughters the oxen and feeds the people, then leaves to follow and serve Elijah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 19

Elisha, son of Shaphat of Abel Meholah, is found plowing with twelve yoke of oxen, himself with the twelfth, when Elijah puts his mantle on him. He asks to kiss his father and mother first, then kills the yoke of oxen, boils the meat with the oxen's equipment, and gives it to the people to eat. He then goes after Elijah and serves him.

Elisha in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Elisha first appears

Abstract written by a model

Elisha, son of Shaphat, is a prophet of the LORD and man of God who begins as the servant of Elijah, pouring water on his hands and calling him master. He refuses three times to leave Elijah, asks for a double portion of his spirit, sees him taken up, and cries 'My father, my father, the chariots of Israel and its horsemen!' He takes up Elijah's mantle, parts the Jordan, and is recognized by the sons of the prophets as having Elijah's spirit. He then heals Jericho's waters and curses mocking youths, returning via Mount Carmel to Samaria. His ministry is marked by wonders and by the LORD's word, which Jehoshaphat acknowledges. He prophesies water and victory over Moab, multiplies a widow's oil, promises the Shunammite woman a son and revives the boy by prayer, neutralizes poisoned stew, and feeds a hundred men with twenty loaves. He directs Naaman to wash in the Jordan seven times and refuses his gift, swearing by the LORD. He perceives the deceit of his servant Gehazi and passes Naaman's leprosy to him and his offspring. He makes a lost ax head float, reveals the Syrian king's private plans to the king of Israel, prays that his servant's eyes be opened and the Syrian army blinded, then leads them to Samaria and has them fed rather than struck. He foresees a threatening messenger and tells the elders to hold the door shut. He announces cheap flour and barley in Samaria's gate, and the doubting captain sees it but does not eat of it. He warns the Shunammite woman to leave for seven years of famine and tells Hazael that Benhadad will die and that Hazael will be king over Syria, weeping over the violence to come. He sends a young prophet with a vial of oil to anoint Jehu king over Israel. At the end he falls sick with the illness of which he dies. King Joash weeps over him, echoing the same cry he once made for Elijah. Elisha has the king shoot an arrow of victory over Syria, then grows angry when the king strikes the ground only three times. He dies and is buried in a tomb, and a man who touches his bones revives.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Elisha refuses three times to leave Elijah, asks for a double portion of his spirit, and sees him taken up, crying 'My father, my father, the chariots of Israel and its horsemen!' and tearing his clothes. He takes up Elijah's mantle, parts the Jordan, is recognized by the prophets as having Elijah's spirit, heals Jericho's waters, curses the mocking youths, and returns via Mount Carmel to Samaria.

Chapter 3

Elisha son of Shaphat, who had poured water on Elijah's hands, was identified by a servant as available to inquire of the LORD. He rebuked Jehoram, called for a musician, and then prophesied that the valley would fill with water and that Moab would be delivered and its land devastated.

Chapter 4

Elisha tells the widow to borrow containers and fill them from her oil, and later promises the Shunammite woman a son. He prays to the LORD and stretches himself on her dead child until the boy revives. He also renders the poisoned stew harmless with meal and feeds a hundred men with twenty loaves, saying the LORD promised leftovers.

Chapter 5

Elisha, the man of God in Samaria, tells the king of Israel to send Naaman to him and sends a messenger instructing Naaman to wash in the Jordan seven times. He refuses Naaman's gift, swearing by the LORD, and tells Naaman to go in peace. He perceives Gehazi's deceit, rebukes him, and pronounces that Naaman's leprosy will cling to Gehazi and his offspring forever.

Chapter 6

Elisha goes with the sons of the prophets to the Jordan and makes a lost borrowed ax head float with a cut stick. He reveals the Syrian king's plans to the king of Israel, prays that his servant's eyes be opened and that the Syrian army be blinded, then leads them to Samaria and tells the king of Israel to feed them rather than strike them. When the king vows to take his head, Elisha, sitting with the elders, foresees the messenger and tells them to hold the door shut.

Chapter 7

Elisha, called the man of God, announces the Lord's word that a seah of fine flour and two seahs of barley will each sell for a shekel in the gate of Samaria by the same time the next day. He also tells the doubting captain that he will see it but not eat of it, and both statements come true.

Chapter 8

Elisha warns the woman to leave for seven years because of a famine called by the LORD. In Damascus he tells Hazael that Benhadad will surely die, stares at him until he is ashamed, weeps, and foretells the violence Hazael will do to Israel and that Hazael will be king over Syria.

Chapter 9

Elisha the prophet calls one of the sons of the prophets and instructs him to take a vial of oil to Ramoth Gilead, anoint Jehu as king over Israel, and flee at once. He gives detailed instructions about finding Jehu and taking him to an inner room.

Chapter 13

Elisha became sick with the illness of which he died, and King Joash wept over him, calling him "My father, my father, the chariots of Israel and its horsemen!" He had the king shoot an arrow eastward as the LORD's arrow of victory over Syria, then grew angry when the king struck the ground only three times, saying he should have struck five or six. Elisha died and was buried, and a man touching his bones later revived.

Elisha in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Elisha first appears

Abstract written by a model

Elisha is a figure filled with Elijah's spirit when Elijah was taken in a whirlwind; fearless before rulers and subject to no one, nothing was too hard for him, and his buried body prophesied, so his works were marvelous in death.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 48

Elisha was filled with Elijah's spirit when Elijah was wrapped in a whirlwind. In his days he was not moved by fear of any ruler and no one brought him into subjection. Nothing was too hard for him, and when he was buried his body prophesied, so his works were marvelous in death as in life.
