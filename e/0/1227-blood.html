Abstract written by a model

In Leviticus, blood is presented as the life of the flesh, used for atonement on the altar, and forbidden as food. In Leviticus, sacrificial blood from peace offerings is sprinkled around the altar, and the people are forbidden to eat it. In Leviticus, blood from offerings is sprinkled on the altar, while eating any bird or animal blood is forbidden to the children of Israel. In Deuteronomy, blood is identified as life and must not be eaten with meat but poured out on the earth or the LORD's altar. In The Letter to the Hebrews, blood dedicates covenants, sanctifies flesh, remits sins, and Christ's blood cleanses the conscience. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, the blood of Christ cleanses garments from every stain so a person may be saved. In The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record, the blood of the Lamb cleanses those who turn to the Lord from uncleanness at the great and last day.

blood in Leviticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where blood first appears

Abstract written by a model

Blood is presented as the life of the flesh, given by the LORD on the altar to make atonement. Eating it is forbidden to Israelites and resident strangers, on penalty of being cut off. Hunted animals' blood must be poured out and covered with dust; killing without the Tent of Meeting counts as shed blood.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 17

Blood is declared to hold the life of all flesh, and eating it is forbidden to Israelites and strangers on penalty of being cut off. Sacrificial blood is sprinkled on the altar, and the blood of hunted animals or birds is to be poured out and covered with dust. Killing an animal without bringing it to the Tent of Meeting results in blood being imputed to the man as shed blood.

blood in The Letter to the Hebrews

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where blood first appears

Abstract written by a model

Blood is a central element of cleansing and covenant. The first covenant was dedicated with calf and goat blood, and without shedding blood there is no remission. Animal blood sanctifies the flesh, while Christ's blood cleanses the conscience from dead works, and Christ entered the Holy Place through it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

Blood is central to cleansing and covenant: the first covenant was dedicated with the blood of calves and goats, and apart from shedding of blood there is no remission. The blood of goats and bulls sanctifies to the cleanness of the flesh, but the blood of Christ cleanses the conscience from dead works to serve the living God. Christ entered the Holy Place through his own blood, not that of goats and calves.
