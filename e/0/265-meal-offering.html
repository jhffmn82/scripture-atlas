Abstract written by a model

In Leviticus, the meal offering is a most holy offering made of fine flour with oil and frankincense, presented through Aaron's sons, with specified preparations, ingredients, priestly portions, and burning requirements. In Numbers, the meal offering is fine flour mixed with oil and presented alongside burnt offerings, with amounts scaled according to the animal offered. In The Book of Ezekiel, the meal offering accompanies animal offerings in specified amounts, with oil, and is baked by the priests in the holy rooms.

meal offering in Leviticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where meal offering first appears

Abstract written by a model

The meal offering is a most holy offering made of fine flour with oil and frankincense, presented to the LORD through Aaron's sons, the priests. It may be baked in an oven, made on a griddle or in a pan, and a first fruits version uses parched, crushed fresh grain. It must contain no yeast or honey and is seasoned with salt. The priest burns a handful of flour, oil and all the frankincense on the altar as a memorial portion for a pleasant aroma. The remainder belongs to Aaron and his sons, shared equally, and every male eats it unleavened in a holy place. The priest's own meal offering, made on a griddle with oil, is wholly burned.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

The meal offering is to be made of fine flour with oil and frankincense, and may be baked in the oven, made on a griddle, or made in a pan. It must contain no yeast or honey, must be seasoned with salt, and a memorial portion is burned on the altar while the remainder goes to Aaron and his sons. A first fruits version uses fresh heads of grain parched with fire and crushed, with oil and frankincense.

Chapter 6

The meal offering is presented by the sons of Aaron before the altar, where a handful of fine flour, oil, and all the frankincense is burned as a memorial portion for a pleasant aroma. The rest is eaten unleavened by Aaron and his sons in a holy place, and it is most holy. The priest's own meal offering, however, is made with oil on a griddle and wholly burned.

meal offering in Numbers

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where meal offering first appears

Abstract written by a model

The meal offering is fine flour mixed with oil, presented alongside burnt offerings. Amounts scale by animal: one tenth ephah per lamb, two tenths per ram, three tenths per bull; the Sabbath's two lambs take two tenths, and the daily offering uses a fourth of a hin of beaten oil.

Chapter 28

The meal offering is fine flour mixed with oil, offered with the burnt offerings. The amounts are one tenth of an ephah per lamb, two tenths for a ram, and three tenths for a bull, with two tenths for the Sabbath's two lambs; the daily one is mixed with a fourth of a hin of beaten oil.
