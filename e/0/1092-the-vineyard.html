the vineyard

object · 2 works · 15 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: a vineyard, his vineyard, my vineyard, ten acres of vineyard, the vineyard of the LORD of Armies, vineyard, vineyard (the beloved's vineyard)

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Isaiah, the vineyard is carefully cultivated by the LORD of Armies but yields wild grapes, after which its hedge and wall are removed; it is left trampled, overgrown, and without rain, and is identified as the house of Israel and the men of Judah. In The Second Book of Nephi, the vineyard is fenced, cleared, planted with the choicest vine, and equipped with a tower and wine-press, but produces wild grapes before its hedge and wall are removed and it is laid waste.

vineyard (the beloved's vineyard) in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where vineyard (the beloved's vineyard) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The beloved's vineyard, owned by the LORD of Armies, sits on a very fruitful hill, carefully dug, cleared of stones, planted with the choicest vine, and given a tower and wine press. Despite this care it yields wild grapes. The beloved vows to remove its hedge and wall, leaving it trampled, overgrown with briers and thorns, and without rain. It is finally identified allegorically as the house of Israel and the men of Judah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

The beloved's vineyard on a very fruitful hill was dug, cleared of stones, planted with the choicest vine, and given a tower and a wine press, yet it yielded wild grapes. The beloved announces he will take away its hedge and wall so it is eaten up and trampled, make it a wasteland of briers and thorns, and command the clouds not to rain on it. It is then explained to be the house of Israel and the men of Judah.
