Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, Ben Hadad was king of Syria, hired by Asa to attack Israel, fought Ahab, and was spared after promising to restore cities and give streets in Damascus. In The Second Book of Kings, Ben Hadad was king of Syria whose plans against Israel were foiled by Elisha, and who besieged Samaria. In The Second Book of Kings, Benhadad was king of Syria who fell sick, sent Hazael to ask the LORD through Elisha, and was smothered by Hazael. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Ben Hadad was king of Syria who accepted Asa's silver and gold and attacked Israel's northern cities.

Ben Hadad in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ben Hadad first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ben Hadad, son of Tabrimmon, was king of Syria and lived at Damascus. Hired by Asa, he broke his treaty with Baasha and struck Israelite towns and Naphtali. Later he besieged Samaria with thirty-two allied kings, demanded Ahab's wealth and family, and was routed while drunk. He returned at Aphek, was defeated, fled, and was spared by Ahab, promising to restore cities and give streets in Damascus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 15

Ben Hadad, son of Tabrimmon, son of Hezion, was king of Syria and lived at Damascus. Asa sent him silver and gold and asked him to break his treaty with Baasha. He listened, sending his army captains against Israel, where they struck Ijon, Dan, Abel Beth Maacah, all Chinneroth, and the land of Naphtali.

Chapter 20

Ben Hadad, king of Syria, besieges Samaria with thirty-two kings, demands Ahab's wealth, wives, and children, and is routed while drinking himself drunk. He returns a year later to fight at Aphek on the advice of his servants, flees into an inner room after defeat, and is spared by Ahab, promising to restore cities and give Ahab streets in Damascus.

king of Syria in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where king of Syria first appears

Abstract written by a model

Benhadad, king of Syria, is at war with Israel. His plans are foiled because Elisha exposes them; he sends an army to seize Elisha at Dothan, then gathers his whole army and besieges Samaria.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

The king of Syria wages war on Israel, but his camp plans are repeatedly thwarted because Elisha tells the king of Israel. Troubled, he learns from a servant that Elisha reveals even his bedroom words, and sends horses, chariots, and a great army to seize Elisha at Dothan. The text then names Benhadad king of Syria, who gathers his whole army and besieges Samaria.

Abstract written by a model

Ben Hadad, king of Syria living at Damascus, son of an earlier king, accepted Asa's silver and gold and attacked Israel's northern cities; Hanani later rebuked Asa for relying on him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 16

Ben Hadad, king of Syria, who lived at Damascus, listened to Asa after receiving silver and gold. He sent his army captains against Israel, who struck Ijon, Dan, Abel Maim, and all the storage cities of Naphtali. Hanani later said the Syrian army had escaped Asa's hand because Asa relied on Syria's king.
