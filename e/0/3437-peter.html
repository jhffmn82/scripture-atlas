Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Matthew, Peter is a fisherman and apostle who follows Jesus, demonstrates bold faith and failure, receives the name Peter and the keys of the Kingdom, and denies Jesus three times. In The Good News According to Mark, Peter is a fisherman and apostle who follows Jesus, declares him the Christ, misunderstands his suffering, sleeps in Gethsemane, and denies him three times. In The Gospel According to Luke, Peter is a Galilean fisherman and apostle who follows Jesus after a miraculous catch, confesses him as the Christ, and later denies him three times before turning again. In The Gospel According to John, Simon Peter is a disciple renamed Cephas who confesses Jesus, denies him three times, enters the empty tomb, and is commissioned to tend Jesus’ followers. In The Acts of the Apostles, Peter is a leading apostle and witness who preaches Jesus, heals, confronts opponents, receives Gentiles, is delivered from prison, and supports salvation by grace. In Paul’s Letter to the Galatians, Peter, called Cephas, is a reputed pillar entrusted with the Good News for the circumcised whom Paul opposes for withdrawing from Gentile believers. In 1 Peter, Peter is an apostle who writes to chosen foreigners in the Dispersion, blessing God and urging them to endure trials, live in holiness, and love one another. In Peter’s Second Letter, Simon Peter is a servant and apostle who writes to believers, recalls witnessing Christ’s majesty, and promises to preserve their remembrance after his departure.

Peter in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Peter first appears

Abstract written by a model

Peter, also called Simon and Simon Bar Jonah, is a fisherman and the brother of Andrew. Jesus sees him casting a net in the sea of Galilee, promises to make him a fisher of men, and Simon immediately leaves his nets and follows. He becomes a disciple, is listed first among the apostles, and addresses Jesus as Lord. Jesus gives him the name Peter, the rock. His story is one of bold faith mixed with failure. He walks on water but sinks in fear; he declares Jesus the Christ, the Son of the living God, and is promised the keys of the Kingdom of Heaven, yet soon rebukes Jesus and is called a stumbling block. He accompanies Jesus up the high mountain with James and John, is sent to catch a fish with a stater for the temple tax, asks how often to forgive (seventy times seven), and notes that the disciples have left everything. At the end he vows never to stumble, sleeps in Gethsemane, follows Jesus at a distance, denies him three times as foretold, and weeps bitterly when the rooster crows.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Simon, called Peter, is seen by Jesus casting a net into the sea of Galilee with his brother Andrew, for they were fishermen. Jesus says he will make them fishers for men, and Simon immediately leaves his nets and follows him.

Chapter 14

Peter asks Jesus to command him to come to him on the waters, and when told "Come!" he steps out of the boat and walks on the water. Seeing the strong wind, he becomes afraid and begins to sink, cries out "Lord, save me!", and Jesus takes hold of him and rebukes him as one of little faith.

Chapter 16

Simon Peter answered Jesus's question by declaring him the Christ, the Son of the living God, and was blessed, named Peter, and promised the keys of the Kingdom of Heaven. After Jesus foretold his suffering, Peter took him aside and rebuked him, and Jesus called him Satan and a stumbling block for setting his mind on the things of men.

Chapter 17

Peter is taken up the mountain with James and John, and offers to make three tents for Jesus, Moses, and Elijah. In Capernaum he tells the didrachma collectors that Jesus pays, and Jesus then sends him to catch a fish holding a stater to pay for them both.

Chapter 18

Peter asks Jesus how often he should forgive his brother who sins against him, suggesting seven times. Jesus replies that it should be seventy times seven.

Chapter 19

Peter speaks up after the young man leaves, saying that the disciples have left everything to follow Jesus and asking what they will have. Jesus answers with a promise of reward in the regeneration.

Chapter 26

Peter vows never to stumble and to die with Jesus rather than deny him, and is among those taken to Gethsemane, where he is found sleeping. One follower draws a sword and cuts off the high priest's servant's ear at the arrest. Peter follows Jesus at a distance to the high priest's court, denies him three times, and weeps bitterly after the rooster crows.

Peter in The Good News According to Mark

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Peter first appears

Abstract written by a model

Peter, also called Simon, is a Galilean fisherman and brother of Andrew, whose mother-in-law lived in his house. Jesus calls him from his nets, and he immediately follows, becoming a disciple, one of the twelve, and receiving the name Peter from Jesus. He addresses Jesus as Rabbi and declares him the Christ, yet rebukes Jesus over predicted suffering and is rebuked in turn as thinking the things of men. On the mountain with James and John, he fearfully proposes three tents. In Gethsemane he vows never to deny Jesus, even in death, but falls asleep, then follows the arrested Jesus into the high priest's courtyard, denies him three times, and weeps on remembering Jesus' words.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

The Good News According to Mark / Chapter 1

Simon is a fisherman casting a net with his brother Andrew when Jesus calls him to become a fisher for men, and he immediately leaves his nets and follows. Jesus then enters his house, where Simon's wife's mother is healed of a fever, and later Simon and those with him search for Jesus and tell him everyone is looking for him.

Chapter 8

Peter answers Jesus' question about who the disciples say he is by saying, "You are the Christ." When Jesus openly teaches that he must suffer and be killed, Peter takes him aside and begins to rebuke him. Jesus rebukes Peter in turn, saying, "Get behind me, Satan!" because Peter has in mind the things of men, not of God.

Chapter 9

Peter goes up the mountain with Jesus, James, and John and, afraid and not knowing what to say, proposes making three tents for Jesus, Moses, and Elijah. He is one of those who keep the command to tell no one what they saw until the Son of Man has risen.

Chapter 14

Peter insists he will not be offended even if all others are and says he will not deny Jesus even if he must die with him. He is one of three taken deeper into Gethsemane, falls asleep, and later follows the arrested Jesus into the high priest's courtyard, where he denies him three times and weeps when he remembers Jesus' words.

Peter in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Peter first appears

Abstract written by a model

Peter, also called Simon, is a Galilean fisherman who owns boats and has partners James and John. After a miraculous catch at Jesus' word, he calls himself a sinful man, is told he will catch people, and leaves everything to follow Jesus. He becomes one of the twelve and one of the inner three, confesses Jesus as The Christ of God, and speaks unknowingly on the mountain. He vows to die with Jesus, yet after warning of Satan's sifting he denies Jesus three times, weeps bitterly, and is later expected to establish his brothers after turning again.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Simon lends his boat to Jesus and, though he had caught nothing all night, lets down the net at Jesus' word. Seeing the catch he falls at Jesus' knees, calling himself a sinful man, is told he will be catching people alive, and leaves everything to follow Jesus.

Chapter 9

Peter answers Jesus's question with the confession that he is "The Christ of God." On the mountain he proposes making three tents for Jesus, Moses and Elijah, "not knowing what he said."

Jesus warns that Satan sought to sift the apostles, says he prayed for Peter's faith, and predicts that Peter will deny him three times before the rooster crows. Peter follows the arrested Jesus at a distance, denies knowing him three times, and weeps bitterly when the rooster crows and Jesus looks at him.

Simon Peter in The Gospel According to John

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Simon Peter first appears

Abstract written by a model

Simon Peter, son of Jonah and brother of Andrew, is a disciple of Jesus from Bethsaida and one of the twelve. Brought to Jesus by Andrew in Chapter 1, he is renamed Cephas, interpreted Peter. In Chapter 6 he speaks for the twelve, confessing Jesus as the Christ, the Son of the living God, and saying they have nowhere else to go. In Chapter 13 he first objects to having his feet washed, then asks for more washing, and vows to lay down his life for Jesus, who predicts he will deny him three times. In Chapter 18 he draws a sword and cuts off Malchus's right ear, is told to sheathe it, and then in the high priest's court denies being a disciple three times, the last followed by a rooster crowing. In Chapter 20 he is outrun to the tomb but is first to enter and sees the linen cloths. In Chapter 21 he proposes fishing, throws himself into the sea on recognizing the Lord, affirms his affection for Jesus when questioned three times, and is commissioned to feed and tend Jesus' lambs and sheep. Jesus signifies his death will glorify God and tells him to follow.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

The Gospel According to John / Chapter 1

Simon, brother of Andrew, is told by Andrew that the Messiah has been found and is brought to Jesus. Jesus looks at him, calls him Simon the son of Jonah, and says he will be called Cephas, which is interpreted Peter. He is also named as being from Bethsaida, the city of Andrew and Philip.

Chapter 6

Simon Peter, Andrew's brother, answers for the twelve when Jesus asks if they also want to leave. He says they have nowhere else to go because Jesus has the words of eternal life, and confesses him as the Christ, the Son of the living God.

Chapter 13

Simon Peter objects to Jesus washing his feet, then, when told he would have no part with Jesus otherwise, asks to be washed on his hands and head as well. He beckons the disciple leaning on Jesus to ask who the betrayer is. He asks where Jesus is going, says he will lay down his life for him, and is told he will deny Jesus three times before the rooster crows.

Chapter 18

Peter draws a sword and cuts off the right ear of Malchus, the high priest's servant, and Jesus tells him to sheathe it. He follows Jesus to the high priest's court, where he is brought in by another disciple and denies being a disciple three times, the last followed immediately by a rooster crowing.

Chapter 20

Simon Peter hears Mary's report and runs with the other disciple to the tomb. He is outrun, but he is the first to enter and sees the linen cloths and the head cloth rolled up by itself. The disciples then go back to their homes.

Chapter 21

Simon Peter proposes going fishing, and on hearing it is the Lord, wraps his coat around himself and throws himself into the sea. He later draws the net to land and is questioned three times by Jesus about his love, grieving at the third question, and is commissioned to feed Jesus' lambs and sheep. Jesus signifies that Peter will be carried where he doesn't want to go in a death that glorifies God, and tells him to follow.

Peter in The Acts of the Apostles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Peter first appears

Abstract written by a model

Peter, also called Simon, is an apostle and a Jew, listed first among the eleven, who believes in Jesus Christ as Lord and bears witness to his resurrection, often alongside John. He is a man, not to be worshiped, who has no silver or gold and has never eaten anything common or unclean. The records show him as a leading voice of the early believers, chosen by God so the nations would hear the Good News through his mouth. The account begins in the upper room, where Peter stands among about one hundred twenty disciples and says Scripture about Judas must be fulfilled and a witness to the resurrection chosen. In Chapter 2 he stands with the eleven, answers the mockers by pointing to Joel's prophecy, preaches Jesus' death and resurrection, and calls the crowd to repent and be baptized. In Chapter 3 he and John heal a lame man at the temple in Jesus' name, and Peter calls the astonished crowd to repent. In Chapter 4, filled with the Holy Spirit, he tells the rulers that salvation is in no other name. In Chapter 5 he confronts Ananias and Sapphira, who each die after his words; the sick are laid in the streets so his shadow might fall on them; and before the council he says they must obey God rather than men. In Chapter 8 the apostles send him with John from Jerusalem to Samaria, where they lay hands on believers to receive the Holy Spirit, and he rebukes Simon for trying to buy God's gift. In Chapter 9 he heals Aeneas at Lydda, raises Tabitha at Joppa, and stays many days with Simon the tanner. In Chapter 10, after the sheet vision, he goes with Cornelius's men to Caesarea, declares no man is unholy or unclean, and commands that the Gentile hearers be baptized after the Spirit falls on them. In Chapter 11 he defends this in Jerusalem and silences his critics. In Chapter 12 Herod seizes him and holds him under guard, bound by two chains. The church prays constantly, an angel of the Lord delivers him, and he tells the believers, asking them to inform James and the brothers, then departs. In Chapter 15 at the Jerusalem gathering he says God gave the Gentiles the Holy Spirit as to the Jewish believers, calls the law an unbearable yoke, and affirms that all are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

11 sections; each heading opens the text

The Acts of the Apostles / Chapter 1

Peter is listed first among the eleven in the upper room. He stood among about one hundred twenty disciples and said that Scripture about Judas had to be fulfilled. He declared that one of the men who had accompanied them from John's baptism to the ascension must become a witness of the resurrection.

Chapter 2

Peter stands up with the eleven, lifts his voice, and answers the mockers by explaining that the believers are not drunk but are fulfilling Joel's prophecy. He preaches Jesus' death and resurrection, citing David, and tells the cut-to-the-heart crowd to repent and be baptized, urging them to save themselves from this crooked generation.

Chapter 3

Peter goes up to the temple with John, tells the lame man he has no silver or gold, and commands him in Jesus' name to get up and walk, raising him by the right hand. He then addresses the astonished crowd, denying that the healing came from his own power or godliness, accusing them of denying Jesus, and calling them to repent.

Chapter 4

Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, tells the rulers and elders that the crippled man was healed in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom they crucified and God raised. He declares Jesus the rejected stone that became the head of the corner and says there is salvation in no other name.

Chapter 5

Peter confronted Ananias and Sapphira over keeping back part of the price, and each died after his words. Sick people were laid in the streets so that his shadow might overshadow them. Before the council he declared that they must obey God rather than men, and he testified that God raised up Jesus.

Chapter 8

Peter was sent with John from Jerusalem to Samaria, where they prayed for the believers and laid hands on them to receive the Holy Spirit. He sharply rebuked Simon for trying to buy the gift of God, told him to repent, and afterward returned to Jerusalem preaching in many Samaritan villages.

Chapter 9

Peter traveled through the region and came to the saints at Lydda, where he healed Aeneas. He was called to Joppa, sent the mourners out, prayed, and raised Tabitha. He then stayed many days in Joppa with Simon, a tanner.

Chapter 10

Peter, also called Simon, is staying with a tanner named Simon in Joppa when he prays on the housetop, falls into a trance, and sees the sheet vision, at first objecting that he has never eaten anything common or unclean. Directed by the Spirit, he goes with Cornelius's men to Caesarea, declares that God has shown him not to call any man unholy or unclean, and preaches about Jesus. When the Spirit falls on the hearers, he asks whether anyone can forbid their baptism and commands that they be baptized.

Chapter 11

Peter goes up to Jerusalem and defends his visit to uncircumcised men by recounting his vision at Joppa, the Spirit's command, and the Holy Spirit falling on the Gentiles. He concludes that he could not withstand God, and his critics are silenced.

Chapter 12

Peter is seized by Herod and held under guard by four squads of four soldiers, sleeping bound with two chains between two soldiers. An angel wakes him, his chains fall off, and he follows the angel past the guards and through the iron gate, at first thinking it is a vision. He then recognizes that the Lord delivered him, goes to Mary's house, tells the believers how the Lord brought him out, asks them to tell James and the brothers, and departs to another place.

Chapter 15

Peter speaks at the Jerusalem gathering, saying God chose that the nations hear the Good News through his mouth and gave the Gentiles the Holy Spirit just as to the Jewish believers. He calls the law a yoke neither the fathers nor they could bear and says all are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus.
