Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon conquers Judah, captures Jerusalem, deports its leading people, and installs Zedekiah and Gedaliah. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Nebuchadnezzar attacks Judah, takes the temple vessels, brings Jehoiachin to Babylon, and installs Zedekiah as king. In The Book of Jeremiah, the king of Babylon is the feared foreign ruler who takes Judah captive and whose army threatens Jerusalem, while the remnant is told not to fear him. In The Book of Jeremiah, Nebuchadnezzar is Babylon's king and the LORD's servant who conquers Judah, Egypt, and surrounding nations as an instrument of judgment. In Baruch, Nabuchodonosor is Babylon's king who carries Jechonias and the people of Jerusalem into exile. In The Book of Ezekiel, the great eagle symbolizes Babylon's king taking Jerusalem's king and princes and establishing a covenant with royal offspring. In The Book of Ezekiel, Nebuchadnezzar is Babylon's king whose sword and army execute the LORD's judgment against Jerusalem, Tyre, and Egypt. In Daniel (Greek), Nebuchadnezzar is Babylon's king who conquers Jerusalem, rules Babylon, erects a golden image, and is humbled and restored by God.

Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, holds territory from the brook of Egypt to the Euphrates, taken from Egypt's king. He attacks Judah after Jehoiakim's rebellion, captures Jehoiachin, plunders the temple and royal treasures, and deports the leading people, including ten thousand captives. He installs Zedekiah as king. Later he besieges Jerusalem, his captain Nebuzaradan destroys it, and he leaves Gedaliah as governor over those remaining in Judah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 24

Nebuchadnezzar came up against Judah, and Jehoiakim served him three years before rebelling. His servants besieged Jerusalem, and he captured Jehoiachin in the eighth year of his reign, carried off the temple and royal treasures, cut up Solomon's gold vessels, and deported the leading people to Babylon. He then made Mattaniah king in Jehoiachin's place and renamed him Zedekiah.

Chapter 25

In his ninth year Nebuchadnezzar came with all his army against Jerusalem, encamped, and built forts around it. In his nineteenth year his captain Nebuzaradan came to Jerusalem and destroyed it, and Nebuchadnezzar had left Gedaliah as governor over the people remaining in Judah.

Nebuchadnezzar in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · 629 to 579 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nebuchadnezzar first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nebuchadnezzar is the king of Babylon, presented throughout these records as a powerful ruler and as the LORD's servant, an instrument of judgment. He commands a large army that includes the kingdoms and peoples under his dominion, and many nations are said to be under his yoke. They are to serve him, his son, and his son's son until the time of his own land comes, and any nation that refuses is to be punished until consumed by his hand. The records open with him making war on Zedekiah and Jerusalem. The LORD declares he will give Zedekiah and the city into Nebuchadnezzar's hand and that the city will be burned. He is dated against Jehoiakim's reign, and his first year matches Jehoiakim's fourth. In that same fourth year he strikes Pharaoh Necoh's army at Carchemish. He is said to have taken the vessels of the LORD's house to Babylon and to have carried the Judeans away captive. Hananiah falsely claims his yoke will be broken in two years, but the LORD says the nations will serve him. The LORD also says Ahab and Zedekiah son of Maaseiah will be delivered into his hand and killed. He besieges Jerusalem in Zedekiah's ninth year and builds forts around it. At Riblah he pronounces judgment on Zedekiah, killing Zedekiah's sons and all the nobles of Judah. He orders Nebuzaradan to care for Jeremiah and do him no harm. Nebuzaradan enters Jerusalem in his nineteenth year, and deportations are dated by his seventh, eighteenth and twenty-third years. Further prophecies say he struck Kedar and the kingdoms of Hazor. He will set his throne on hidden stones at Tahpanhes and strike Egypt, burn the houses of its gods, and leave in peace. Overall he is a conqueror who carries out the LORD's judgment on Judah, Egypt and the surrounding nations.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

11 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

Nebuchadnezzar is making war against Zedekiah and the city, and his forces besiege it. The LORD says he will give Zedekiah, his servants, and the survivors into Nebuchadnezzar's hand, and that Nebuchadnezzar will burn the city with fire and strike them without sparing, pity, or mercy.

Chapter 25

Nebuchadnezzar is king of Babylon, whose first year matches Jehoiakim's fourth. The LORD calls him "my servant" and says he will send him with the families of the north against Judah, its inhabitants and the surrounding nations.

Chapter 27

Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon is called the LORD's servant, to whom the LORD has given all these lands and the animals of the field. All nations will serve him, his son, and his son's son until the time of his own land comes, and any nation refusing his yoke will be punished until consumed by his hand.

Chapter 28

Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, is said to have taken the vessels of the LORD's house to Babylon. Hananiah falsely claimed his yoke would be broken in two years, but the LORD said all the nations would serve him, and that he was also given the animals of the field.

Chapter 29

Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon is the one who carried the Judeans away captive. Zedekiah king of Judah sends envoys to him in Babylon. The LORD says he will deliver Ahab and Zedekiah son of Maaseiah into his hand, and that he will kill them before the captives' eyes, roasting them in fire.

Chapter 34

Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, with all his army and the kingdoms and peoples under his dominion, is fighting against Jerusalem and all its cities. The LORD declares he will be given Jerusalem and will burn it with fire, and that he will speak with Zedekiah mouth to mouth.

Chapter 39

Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came with all his army against Jerusalem and besieged it, then pronounced judgment on Zedekiah at Riblah, killing Zedekiah's sons and all the nobles of Judah. He also commanded Nebuzaradan to take care of Jeremiah, do him no harm, and do for him whatever he asked.

Chapter 43

Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, is called the LORD's servant, whom the LORD will send for and take. His throne will be set on the hidden stones, and he will spread his royal pavilion over them. The entity is Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon and king of kings, presented as a military ruler whose sword is the LORD's instrument of judgment. He first appears at a parting of two ways, using divination with arrows, teraphim, and liver; his right-hand lot points to Jerusalem, where he is to set battering rams, mounds, and forts, while his sword may go to Rabbah of Ammon instead. The LORD then brings him from the north against Tyre with horses, chariots, horsemen, and a large army, to kill, build forts and a mound, break walls and towers, and enter its gates. His army's great service against Tyre brought no wages, so the LORD gives him Egypt's multitude, plunder, and prey as payment. Finally, he and his people, called the terrible of the nations, draw swords against Egypt; the LORD strengthens his arms and puts the LORD's sword in his hand, so Egypt's multitude falls.

Nebuchadnezzar in Daniel (Greek)

author unknown · about 164 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nebuchadnezzar first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nebuchadnezzar is the king of Babylon, called king of kings, represented in the great image as the head of gold, and father of Belshazzar. He besieged Jerusalem, took golden and silver vessels from the temple there, and had Israelite youths trained at his table, finding them ten times better than his magicians and enchanters. Troubled by a dream he could not remember, he threatened his wise men with death until Daniel revealed and interpreted it. He then worshipped Daniel, called Daniel's God the God of gods and Lord of kings, and made Daniel ruler over the province of Babylon. He later set up a golden image on the plain of Dura and decreed worship of it on pain of the fiery furnace. Enraged by Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, he had them cast in, saw four men walking unharmed, blessed their God, and promoted the three. After boasting of the Babylon he built as his royal dwelling, he was driven from men, ate grass like oxen, and lived like the animals until his understanding returned. Restored, he worshipped and praised the Most High God, who had given him kingdom, greatness, glory, and majesty, and declared that God humbles the proud.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Daniel with Greek Portions / Chapter 1

Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, besieged Jerusalem in Jehoiakim's third year and carried part of the vessels of God's house to the house of his god in Shinar. He ordered selected Israelite youths trained and fed from his table, then talked with the four and found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters in his realm.

Chapter 2

Nebuchadnezzar is troubled by a dream he cannot remember and demands that his wise men tell him both the dream and its interpretation on pain of death. Angered by their failure, he orders all the wise men of Babylon destroyed. After Daniel reveals and interprets the dream, he falls on his face, worships Daniel, declares Daniel's God the God of gods and Lord of kings, and makes Daniel ruler over the province of Babylon.

Chapter 3

Nebuchadnezzar made a golden image, gathered the provincial officials to its dedication, and decreed that all must worship it or be thrown into a burning fiery furnace. Enraged by the refusal of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, he had the furnace heated seven times hotter and had them cast in, but then saw four men walking unharmed in the fire. He blessed their God, decreed that anyone speaking evil against that God be cut in pieces, and promoted the three in Babylon.

Chapter 4

Nebuchadnezzar, at rest and flourishing in his palace, is frightened by a dream that none of his wise men can interpret, so he tells it to Daniel. After boasting of the Babylon he built, he is driven from men, eats grass like oxen, and grows hair like eagles' feathers and nails like birds' claws. When his understanding returns he blesses and praises the Most High, is re-established in his kingdom, and declares that God is able to humble those who walk in pride.

Chapter 5

Nebuchadnezzar is described as Belshazzar's father who took the vessels from the Jerusalem temple and who was given the kingdom, greatness, glory, and majesty by the Most High God. When his heart was lifted up he was deposed and driven to live like the animals until he knew that God rules. He had earlier made Daniel master of the magicians, enchanters, Chaldeans, and soothsayers.
