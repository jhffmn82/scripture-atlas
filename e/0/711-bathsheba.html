Bathsheba in The Second Book of Samuel

author unknown · about 950 BC · the work’s page · read it where Bathsheba first appears

Abstract written by a model

Bathsheba, daughter of Eliam and very beautiful wife of Uriah the Hittite, is taken by David and conceives. After mourning Uriah she becomes David's wife and bears a son, whom the LORD strikes and who dies. David comforts her, and she then bears Solomon.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Bathsheba is identified as the daughter of Eliam and wife of Uriah the Hittite, and is described as very beautiful. David takes her and lies with her, she conceives and tells him, and after mourning her husband she becomes David's wife and bears him a son.

Chapter 12

Bathsheba, referred to as Uriah's wife and the one David took as his wife, bears David a child who is struck by the LORD and dies. David then comforts her as his wife, lies with her, and she bears Solomon.

Bathsheba in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Bathsheba first appears

Abstract written by a model

Bathsheba, mother of Solomon, is warned by Nathan of Adonijah's reign and the danger to her and Solomon. She reminds David of his oath, and he affirms it. Later she carries Adonijah's request for Abishag to Solomon, who honors her but refuses and condemns Adonijah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

Bathsheba, Solomon's mother, is warned by Nathan that Adonijah reigns and that her life and Solomon's are at risk. She goes to David and reminds him of his oath that Solomon would reign, saying she and Solomon will otherwise be considered criminals. David affirms the oath to her, and she bows and wishes him to live forever.

Chapter 2

Bathsheba agrees to carry Adonijah's request for Abishag to King Solomon. Solomon bows to her and seats her at his right hand, but he refuses the request and condemns Adonijah.
