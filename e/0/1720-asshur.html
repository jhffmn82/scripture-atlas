Abstract written by a model

In Judith, Asshur is the Assyrian leader whose threats against Israel are ended by the Almighty Lord through a woman's hand. In Judith, Asshur is the vast army of Nebuchadnezzar led by Holofernes, which besieges Bethulia, flees after Holofernes's death, and is destroyed and plundered by the Israelites. In The Book of Ezekiel, Asshur is a slain group in Sheol whose former power caused terror in the land of the living.

Asshur in Judith

author unknown · AD 900 to 1000 · the work’s page · read it where Asshur first appears

Abstract written by a model

Asshur is the vast army of King Nebuchadnezzar, led by Holofernes. It is described as 120,000 chosen footmen and 12,000 horsemen (elsewhere 170,000 footmen), with camels, donkeys, mules, livestock, rations and treasure, accompanied by a countless company of nations like locusts. It destroys, burns and plunders many lands. It then camps in the valley by the fountain near Bethulia, seizing waters and springs, covering the plain, and surrounding the town for thirty-four days. Its parts include captains, tribunes and the Assyrian camp. The turning point comes when Holofernes' death is discovered: the rulers tear their tunics, a great cry rises, and, as Judith had predicted, fear falls on the camp. The army flees across plain and hill country, is slaughtered past Damascus, and its camp is plundered, enriching the Israelites. Israel's lowly ones shouted and the enemy perished by the army of the Lord, while Persians and Medes quaked at Judith's daring.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

The army consists of 120,000 chosen footmen and 12,000 mounted archers, with camels, donkeys, mules, countless livestock, rations, and gold and silver from the king's house. A countless company of various nations accompanies it like locusts, and it destroys, burns and plunders its way through many lands.

Chapter 7

The army, numbering one hundred seventy thousand footmen and twelve thousand horsemen besides baggage, camps in the valley by the fountain near Bethulia and spreads over a vast area. Part of it, including five thousand children of Asshur who go with the Ammonites, seizes the waters and springs, and the rest covers the plain. It remains around Bethulia for thirty-four days.

Chapter 14

The children of Asshur see the Israelites coming and send word to their leaders, who go to the captains and tribunes and then to Holofernes' tent to ask that he be woken. On learning of his death, the rulers of the army tear their tunics, are exceedingly troubled, and a great cry and noise arises in the camp. Judith had predicted fear would fall on them and that they would flee.

Chapter 15

The camp of Asshur is thrown into trembling and fear when those in the tents hear what has happened, and its people flee into every way of the plain and hill country. Those who had encamped around Bethulia flee, and the enemy is slaughtered past Damascus and its borders. Its camp is plundered and enriches the Israelites exceedingly.

Chapter 16

The army is described as vast, with ten thousands of men and horsemen covering the hills. When Israel's lowly ones shouted, the enemy fled, and the children of slave-girls pierced them through, so they perished by the army of the Lord. The Persians and Medes are said to have quaked at Judith's daring.
