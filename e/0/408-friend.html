friend in Proverbs

Abstract written by a model

A friend is portrayed as one whose wounds are faithful, unlike an enemy's profuse kisses. Friends give earnest counsel that brings joy and sharpen one another like iron. The reader is finally urged never to forsake his friend or his father's friend.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 27

The wounds of a friend are called faithful, in contrast to the profuse kisses of an enemy. Earnest counsel from a friend brings joy like perfume and incense, and a man sharpens his friend's countenance as iron sharpens iron. The reader is told not to forsake his friend or his father's friend.

friend in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where friend first appears

Abstract written by a model

A friend is a relationship that survives a drawn sword or hostile words, allowing reconciliation, but ends through insult, arrogance, disclosed secrets, or treacherous blows, from which one flees. The speaker pledges to shelter a friend without shame, yet warns that evil coming through a friend makes others wary.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 22

A friend may be wronged by a drawn sword or an opened mouth without the relationship being lost. He will flee, however, from insult, arrogance, disclosure of a secret, or a treacherous blow. The speaker declares he will not be ashamed to shelter a friend or hide from his face, though he warns that if evil comes to him through the friend, those who hear of it will beware of him.
