Ch'i

Also called: Ch'i State, State of Ch'i, kingdom, the Ch'i State, the Ch'u, the Ch'u State

Abstract written by a model

In Chuang Tzŭ, Ch'i State is a prosperous state governed by Sages' rules that receives Tzŭ Kao's mission, sends a son to Sung as a door-keeper, is seized by T'ien Ch'êng Tzŭ, later breaks a treaty with Prince Hui of Wei, and is associated with suffering and malefactors' bodies. In The Analects of Confucius, Book VI (Yung Yey), Ch'i is Tsze-hwa's mission destination and a state said to be one change away from becoming like Lu.

Ch'i State in Chuang Tzŭ

Chuang Tzŭ · the work’s page · read it where Ch'i State first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ch'i State is a state containing Ch'ü-yüan and closely placed towns, governed by Sages' rules and prosperous. It receives Tzŭ Kao's mission, sends a son to Sung as a door-keeper, then is seized by T'ien Ch'êng Tzŭ, whose descendants rule twelve generations. It later breaks a treaty with Prince Hui of Wei and is associated with suffering and malefactors' bodies.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

It is the destination of Tzŭ Kao's important mission, where he expects to receive respect but doubts that his hosts will share his interest in the matter.

CHAPTER X.

The State of Ch'i was prosperous and governed according to rules laid down by the Sages. T'ien Ch'êng Tzŭ slew its Prince, stole the kingdom and the Sages' wisdom-tricks, and his descendants ruled Ch'i for twelve generations.

CHAPTER XXV.

The Ch'i State is involved in a broken treaty with Prince Hui of Wei. It is also presented by Poh Chü as a place where the bodies of malefactors lie after rulers' policies have produced crime and suffering.
