Abstract written by a model

Numbers describes vows as promises made to the LORD that must be kept, with a woman's vows subject to her father's or husband's silence or prohibition and a widow's or divorced woman's vows standing. Deuteronomy presents vows to the LORD as obligations that must be paid without delay, while choosing not to vow is not sinful. Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher teaches that a vow to God must be paid without delay and that not vowing is better than vowing and leaving it unpaid.

Abstract written by a model

Vows are promises made to the LORD by a man or woman. A man must keep his word and never break it; a woman's vows stand or fall by her father's or husband's silence or prohibition on the day he hears, while a widow's or divorced woman's stand.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 30

Vows and binding oaths to the LORD must not be broken by a man, who must do all that proceeds out of his mouth. A woman's vows stand or fall depending on whether her father or husband says nothing or forbids them on the day he hears, while those of a widow or divorced woman stand against her.

Abstract written by a model

Vows to the LORD are a topic of obligation: a vow must be paid without delay because the LORD will require it and delay is sin, though choosing not to vow is no sin.
