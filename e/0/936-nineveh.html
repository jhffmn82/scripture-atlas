Abstract written by a model

In Tobit, Nineveh is the Assyrian city where Tobit and his people were held captive, where he buried dead Israelites, and whose destruction Tobit foretells and celebrates. In Jonah, Nineveh is a great wicked city that repents after Jonah warns of its overthrow and is spared when God relents. In The Book of Nahum, Nineveh is portrayed as a bloody city full of lies, robbery, cruelty, prostitution, and witchcraft, whose ruin the LORD of Armies decrees. In The Book of Zephaniah, Nineveh is a joyous, careless city that boasts of its uniqueness and will become a dry desolation.

Nineveh in Tobit

Abstract written by a model

Nineveh is the Assyrian city where Tobit and his people were held captive and where he buried dead Israelites. Jonah's prophecy foretells its overthrow; Tobit warns his family to leave, then rejoices hearing Nebuchadnezzar and Ahasuerus destroyed and captured it.

Tobit / Chapter 1

Nineveh is the Assyrian city to which Tobit, his kindred and his nation were carried captive. Dead Israelites were thrown out on its wall and Tobit buried them there, and it is where Achiacharus later called Tobit back.

Chapter 14

Tobit says Nineveh will be overthrown, as the prophet Jonah spoke, and tells his family not to stay there. Before Tobias's death he hears that Nineveh has been destroyed and taken captive by Nebuchadnezzar and Ahasuerus, and he rejoices over it.

Nineveh in Jonah

author unknown · 399 to 199 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nineveh first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nineveh is a great city, three days' journey across, with over 120,000 people and many animals. Its wickedness comes up before the LORD, who tells Jonah to preach against it, though Jonah does not go at first. Later, warned of overthrow in forty days, the city repents and is spared when God relents. Jonah watches from the east, and God says He should be concerned for it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Jonah / Chapter 1

Nineveh is called a great city whose wickedness has come up before the LORD. The LORD tells Jonah to go there and preach against it, but Jonah does not go in this unit.

Chapter 3

Nineveh is described as an exceedingly great city, three days' journey across. It is warned of overthrow in forty days, repents, and is spared when God relents.

Chapter 4

Nineveh is the city Jonah leaves and watches from the east side, waiting to see what will become of it. God calls it that great city, with more than one hundred twenty thousand persons and many animals, and says He should be concerned for it.

Nineveh in The Book of Nahum

author unknown · 662 to 611 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nineveh first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nineveh is a city, the subject of Nahum's vision, portrayed as a bloody city full of lies, robbery, endless cruelty, prostitution and witchcraft that sell nations and families. Most important is that the LORD of Armies stands against it and decrees its ruin. The records unfold in three stages. First, the book opens with the revelation, and oracles foretell no more descendants bearing its name, its images cut off from its gods' house, and a grave made because it is vile. Second, it is told to prepare defenses but is overwhelmed: river gates open, the palace dissolves, people flee, silver and gold are plundered, and it is left empty, void, and waste, though it was long like a pool of water. Third, its shame is exposed and it becomes a spectacle. Its fortresses fall like ripe figs, its troops are women, its gates stand open, its merchants as numerous as stars and its guards like fleeing locusts cannot save it, and fire and sword devour it with no one to mourn. Its wound is fatal, with no healing.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Nahum / Chapter 1

Nineveh is the subject of the revelation that opens the book. The chapter's closing oracles describe a doomed party addressed as "you": it will have no more descendants to bear its name, its engraved and molten images will be cut off from the house of its gods, and the LORD will make its grave because it is vile. The text does not name Nineveh again after verse 1.

Chapter 2

Nineveh is ordered to prepare its defenses but is overwhelmed: the river gates are opened, the palace dissolves, and the city is uncovered and carried away. Though it has been like a pool of water from of old, its people flee without looking back, its treasures of silver and gold are plundered, and it is left empty, void, and waste. The LORD of Armies declares himself against it.

Chapter 3

Nineveh is called the bloody city, full of lies and robbery, and is accused of prostitution and witchcraft that sell nations and families. The LORD of Armies declares he is against it and will expose its shame and make it a spectacle, while onlookers flee and say it is laid waste with no one to mourn. Its fortresses will fall like ripe figs, its troops are women, its gates are open, and fire and sword will devour it despite its siege preparations.

Nineveh in The Book of Zephaniah

author unknown · 639 to 608 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nineveh first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nineveh is a joyous, careless city that boasted 'I am, and there is no one besides me.' It will be made a dry desolation where animals lie and pelicans lodge, while passersby hiss and shake their fists.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Nineveh will be made a desolation, as dry as the wilderness, with herds and animals lying in its midst, and the pelican and porcupine lodging in its capitals. It is described as the joyous, careless city that said 'I am, and there is no one besides me,' and passersby will hiss and shake their fists at it.
