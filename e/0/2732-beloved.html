Beloved in The Song of Solomon

author unknown · 299 to 199 BC · the work’s page · read it where Beloved first appears

Abstract written by a model

Beloved is a dark yet lovely young woman, the central female voice and subject of these records, who is in a mutual love with the Lover. She says the sun scorched her and that her mother's sons, angry with her, made her keeper of the vineyards while she did not keep her own. She calls the Lover the one her soul loves and likens him to a myrrh sachet between her breasts and a cluster of henna blossoms. The story opens with her longing to be kissed and taken into the king's rooms, and her asking where he grazes his flock. In the next stage the Lover describes her as an apple tree among the trees of the wood, calls her away to come, and asks to see her face and hear her sweet voice, comparing himself to a roe or young deer. He calls her a dove in the clefts of the rock. In the later stage she says he has gone down to his garden and declares that she is her beloved's and he is hers. He praises her as beautiful as Tirzah, lovely as Jerusalem and awesome as an army with banners. He calls her unique, her mother's only daughter and favorite, and says she is blessed by the daughters, queens and concubines. Finally she wishes she could kiss him openly as a brother, imagines leading him to her mother's house, and asks to be set as a seal on his heart and arm. She describes herself as a wall with breasts like towers, says her own vineyard is before her, and ends by urging him to come away like a gazelle or young stag on the mountains of spices.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

The Song of Solomon / Chapter 1

The Beloved asks to be kissed, says his love is better than wine, and asks to be taken away, saying the king has brought her into his rooms. She calls herself dark but lovely, like Kedar's tents and Solomon's curtains, and says the sun scorched her and her mother's sons made her keeper of the vineyards while she did not keep her own. She asks where her lover grazes his flock, likens him to a sachet of myrrh and a cluster of henna blossoms from En Gedi, and calls him beautiful with a verdant couch.

Chapter 2

The Beloved is described as like an apple tree among the trees of the wood, and as a roe or young deer who leaps on the mountains and stands behind the wall, looking through the windows and lattice. He speaks, calling his love, his beautiful one, to come away, and asks to see her face and hear her voice. He calls for the little foxes that plunder the blossoming vineyards to be caught, and is said to browse among the lilies.

Chapter 6

The Beloved is asked where her beloved has gone and answers that he has gone down to his garden, to the beds of spices, to pasture his flock and gather lilies. She declares that she is her beloved's and he is hers. She is praised by the Lover as beautiful as Tirzah and awesome as an army with banners, and is called unique, her mother's only daughter, and blessed by the daughters, queens, and concubines.

Chapter 8

The Beloved wishes the Lover were like her brother so she could kiss him in public without scorn, and she imagines leading him to her mother's house and giving him spiced wine. She asks to be set as a seal on his heart and arm, and says she is a wall with breasts like towers who was in his eyes like one who found peace. She says her own vineyard is before her, gives the thousand to Solomon, and ends by urging her beloved to come away like a gazelle or young stag on the mountains of spices.

beloved (the bride's lover) in The Song of Solomon

author unknown · 299 to 199 BC · the work’s page · read it where beloved (the bride's lover) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The beloved is the bride, also called sister as an endearment and likened to a locked-up garden. She is praised as beautiful and without spot, with detailed features: dove-like eyes, goat-flock hair, shorn-flock teeth, scarlet-thread lips, a tower-like neck, and fawn-like breasts. Her lips drip honey, milk lies under her tongue, and her garments smell of Lebanon. She is called away from Lebanon and said to have ravished the speaker's heart. At the end she invites the winds to blow on her garden and asks her beloved to come and taste its fruits.

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

She is praised as beautiful, with eyes like doves, hair like a flock of goats, teeth like shorn sheep, lips like scarlet thread, and a neck like David's tower. She is told there is no spot in her, is called to come away from Lebanon, and is said to have ravished the speaker's heart. At the end she invites the winds to blow on her garden and asks that her beloved come and taste its fruits.
