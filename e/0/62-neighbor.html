Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the neighbor is a community member who entrusts or receives money, goods, or animals, with protections and restitution obligations determined by the circumstances. In Leviticus, the neighbor is protected from oppression, robbery, endangerment, hatred, and harm, and is to be judged righteously, rebuked appropriately, and loved as oneself. In Deuteronomy, the neighbor who borrows brings the pledge outside to the lender, receives a poor man's garment back at sundown, and blesses the lender. In Proverbs, the neighbor is someone with whom disputes should be settled privately, whose confidences should be kept, whose visits should be infrequent, and against whom false testimony is condemned. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the neighbor is questioned about reported words and reproved before being threatened, allowing the law of the Most High to prevail. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the neighbor who has caused hurt is to be forgiven, not met with anger, and excused for ignorance. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the neighbor receives loans in need, is repaid faithfully, may receive surety, and is helped according to the lender's ability.

neighbor in Exodus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where neighbor first appears

Abstract written by a model

The neighbor is a fellow member of the community who may entrust money, goods, or animals to another for keeping, or do so himself. His protections are set out by circumstance: he receives double from a found thief, is owed restitution for animals let loose in his field, and a borrower must repay an injured animal unless the owner was present. A keeper's oath before the LORD frees him from restitution.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 22

A neighbor may entrust money, goods, or animals to another to keep, and the keeper's responsibility depends on the circumstances. If goods are stolen and the thief is found, the thief pays double; if not, the keeper comes before God to determine whether he took them. A borrower must make good an injured or dead animal unless its owner was with it, and a keeper who swears by the LORD that he did not take an animal that died, was injured, or was driven away unseen is not required to make restitution.

neighbor in Proverbs

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where neighbor first appears

Abstract written by a model

The neighbor is a proverb-style figure: disputes should be argued with him privately, not rushed to court, and his confidences kept lest the betrayer suffer lasting shame. Visits should be infrequent to avoid his hatred, and false testimony against him is likened to a weapon.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 25

The neighbor is someone to debate with directly rather than hastily taking to court, and whose confidence must not be betrayed, lest the betrayer be shamed and gain a lasting bad reputation. One should visit seldom to avoid wearying him into hatred, and false testimony against a neighbor is likened to a club, a sword, or a sharp arrow.
