liberty

topic · 3 works · 7 quoted facts · 12 connections
Also called: a land of liberty, cause of freedom, free government, freedom, freedom from bondage, rights, standard of liberty, that liberty wherewith God has made them free, the cause of our liberty, the liberty of the people, the liberty of thy people

Abstract written by a model

In Paul’s Letter to the Galatians, liberty is the freedom Christ gives believers, in which they must stand firm and serve one another through love rather than use it for the flesh. In The Second Book of Nephi, liberty is available through the Holy Messiah and leads toward eternal life when chosen, while characterizing the land of the Gentiles where no kings raise themselves up. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, liberty is the freedom God gives the sons of Ammon, who stand fast in it by remembering the Lord God and obeying his statutes, judgments, and commandments. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, liberty is God-granted freedom protected by laws, religion, and free government, defended by the Nephites and Moroni against bondage and threats to their government.

liberty in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where liberty first appears

Abstract written by a model

Liberty is a God-granted freedom, protected by statutes, judgments, and commandments and preserved by the Nephites as a remnant of Joseph. It is bound to rights, religion, and a free government maintained by freemen. The Nephites fight and cry to the Lord to preserve it from bondage, while Moroni labors for it, rejoices in it, and acts against threats that could overthrow it. Freemen covenant to maintain it and suppress the king-men’s attempt to overturn their government. Pahoran urges resistance to evil to retain the liberty God made them free in. Finally, Moroni raises the standard of liberty, gathering thousands to defend their freedom.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 43

The Nephites fight to preserve their liberty and freedom from bondage. When they cry unto the Lord for freedom, they gain power and the Lamanites begin to flee.

Chapter 46

Freedom is identified with the rights, religion, and free government that the people covenant to maintain. Moroni gathers the people to defend it, and the people believe God will bless them as they uphold it.

Chapter 48

Liberty is something Moroni and the Nephites labor to support and preserve. Moroni's soul rejoices in the liberty and freedom of his country and brethren from bondage and slavery.

Chapter 50

Nephite liberty is threatened by Morianton's planned migration and possible control of northern lands. Moroni acts to prevent consequences that could overthrow it.

Chapter 51

The freemen covenant to maintain their rights and religious privileges through a free government, and the king-men's attempt to overthrow it is suppressed.

Chapter 61

Pahoran says his soul stands fast in the liberty in which God has made him and his people free. He asks Moroni and others to resist evil to retain that liberty.

Chapter 62

Moroni raised the standard of liberty wherever he went, and thousands gathered to it and took up their swords in defense of their freedom.
