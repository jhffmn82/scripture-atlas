Abstract written by a model

In 2 Corinthians, Titus was Paul's partner and fellow worker, whose coming comforted Paul and whose affection for the Corinthians grew; he was urged to complete their giving and went willingly in Paul's spirit. In Titus, Titus was Paul's true child in a common faith, left in Crete to set things in order, appoint elders, and reprove the Cretans so they might become sound in the faith.

Titus in 2 Corinthians

Paul · AD 55 to 56 · the work’s page · read it where Titus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Titus is a partner and fellow worker of Paul for the Corinthians. His coming comforted Paul and his companions in Macedonia, as he reported the Corinthians' longing, mourning, and zeal. His spirit was refreshed by them, and Paul's boasts about them proved true. His affection for them grows as he remembers their obedience. Urged to complete the giving he had begun among them, he went to them earnestly of his own accord, walking in Paul's spirit.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

Titus's coming comforted the author and his companions in Macedonia, and he reported the recipients' longing, mourning, and zeal. His spirit was refreshed by all of them, and the author rejoiced the more for his joy. The author's boasts about the recipients to Titus proved true, and Titus's affection toward them is abundant as he remembers their obedience.

Chapter 8

Titus was urged to complete among the addressees the grace he had previously begun. He accepted the exhortation, and being very earnest he went out to them of his own accord. The writer calls him his partner and fellow worker for the addressees.

Abstract written by a model

Titus is Paul's true child in a common faith, greeted with grace, mercy and peace. Paul left him in Crete to set in order what was lacking, appoint elders in every city, and sharply reprove the Cretans so they become sound in the faith.

Letter to Titus / Chapter 1

Titus is addressed by Paul as his true child according to a common faith and is greeted with grace, mercy, and peace. He was left in Crete to set in order the things that were lacking and to appoint elders in every city. He is told to reprove the Cretans sharply so that they may be sound in the faith.
