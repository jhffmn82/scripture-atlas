Sakra

person · 3 works · 21 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Indra, god, king of gods, king of the gods, leader and king of the gods, the chief and king of the gods, the chief of kings and gods, the king of gods

Abstract written by a model

In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Sakra creates a divine pavilion and throne, bathes and adorns Mahâvîra, produces Kandraprabhâ, catches his hair, and ends the clamour and music. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, Sakra visits King Nami in disguise, questions him, reveals himself, reveres him, departs, and directs Dasârnabhadra to renounce the world. In Kalpa Sutra, Sakra commands the gods from his celestial abode, perceives Mahâvîra’s conception, and orders the embryo’s transfer to Trisalâ.

Abstract written by a model

Sakra, leader and king of the gods, magically creates a divine pavilion and throne, bathes and adorns Mahâvîra, produces Kandraprabhâ, catches his hair, and ends clamour and music at his holy conduct.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Third Part. / Fifteenth Lecture, Called the Clauses.

Sakra, the leader and king of the gods, prepared a divine pavilion and throne, bathed and adorned Mahâvîra, and produced the palankin Kandraprabhâ. He caught Mahâvîra’s plucked hair in a diamond cup and ordered the clamour and music to cease when Mahâvîra adopted holy conduct.

Sakra in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Sakra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sakra, king of the gods, comes to King Nami disguised as a Brâhmana and questions Mithilâ’s uproar. He urges Nami toward royal and worldly duties, then reveals himself, recognizes Nami as blemishless, praises and reveres him, and flies away; he also directs Dasârnabhadra to renounce the world.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Ninth Lecture.

Sakra comes to Nami in the guise of a Brâhmana and questions him about the uproar in Mithilâ. He repeatedly urges Nami to resume royal duties and worldly pursuits, but after hearing Nami's answers he reveals his true form, praises Nami, pays reverence to him, and flies up through the air.

Sakra in Kalpa Sutra

Bhadrabâhu · the work’s page · read it where Sakra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sakra is chief and king of the gods, commanding them from his celestial abode and throne, riding Airavata and wielding the thunderbolt. After perceiving Mahâvîra’s conception through Avadhi, he orders Harinegamesi to transfer the embryo from Devânandâ to Trisalâ because Tîrthakaras are born in high families.

1 section; each heading opens the text

Kalpa Sutra / Lives of the Ginas. / Life of Mahâvîra.

Sakra, chief and king of the gods, perceived Mahâvîra's conception through Avadhi. He ordered Harinegamesi to transfer Mahâvîra from Devânandâ's womb to Trisalâ's womb because Tîrthakaras are born in high families.
