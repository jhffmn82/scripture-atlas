conscience

Also called: fairest maid, faithful person's conscience, his own conscience, his own conscience were advancing to him, maid, the conscience

Abstract written by a model

The faithful person’s own conscience is a noble, beautiful maiden. At the third-night dawn she appears to the faithful soul, identifies herself, and reflects the person’s moral character: good thoughts, words, deeds, religion, and treatment of the faithful and poor. These make her increasingly lovely, honored, victorious, and free from sorrow.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Yast XXII.

The faithful person’s conscience appears at the third-night dawn as a beautiful maiden and explains that the person’s good thoughts, words, and deeds made her more lovely, fair, desirable, and honored.

XXIV. Vîstâsp Yast.

The conscience of the faithful appears as a beautiful maiden and identifies herself as the person's own conscience. She reflects the person's good thoughts, words, deeds, religion, and treatment of the faithful and the poor.
