Jesus's death and resurrection in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where Jesus's death and resurrection first appears

Abstract written by a model

A prophesied event in which Jesus, as the Son of Man, foretells his own fate. In Chapter 17 he tells the disciples he will suffer at men's hands, be killed, and be raised on the third day, grieving them. In Chapter 20, going up to Jerusalem, he tells the twelve he will be delivered to chief priests and scribes, condemned, handed to Gentiles to be mocked, scourged and crucified, then raised on the third day.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 17

Jesus tells the disciples not to speak of the vision until the Son of Man has risen from the dead. He says he will suffer at the hands of men, be delivered up and killed, and be raised on the third day, which grieves the disciples greatly.

Chapter 20

On the way up to Jerusalem, Jesus takes the twelve aside and says the Son of Man will be delivered to the chief priests and scribes, who will condemn him to death. They will hand him over to the Gentiles to mock, scourge, and crucify him, and on the third day he will be raised up.

Jesus's death and resurrection (predicted) in The Good News According to Mark

Abstract written by a model

Jesus repeatedly predicts his own suffering, death, and resurrection. He says the Son of Man must be rejected by elders, chief priests, and scribes, be killed, and rise on the third day. The teaching begins openly in Chapter 8, where Peter's rebuke is itself rebuked. In Chapter 9 he says the Son of Man will be handed to men, and the disciples do not understand. In Chapter 10, nearing Jerusalem, he adds condemnation, delivery to the Gentiles, mocking, spitting, and scourging before rising.

resurrection of Jesus in The Acts of the Apostles

author unknown · about AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where resurrection of Jesus first appears

Abstract written by a model

The resurrection of Jesus is God raising him from the dead, attested by disciples who accompanied him from Galilee to Jerusalem. Peter argues death could not hold him, citing David's foretelling. Paul later repeats that Jesus was seen many days, fulfilling the fathers' promise, and saw no decay unlike David.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Peter says God raised Jesus up, freeing him from the agony of death because death could not hold him. He argues that David, who died and whose tomb remains, foretold this when he spoke of the Holy One not being left in Hades or seeing decay, and that the apostles are all witnesses.

Chapter 13

Paul declares that God raised Jesus from the dead, that he was seen for many days by witnesses who came with him from Galilee, and that this fulfills the promise to the fathers. He supports it with psalm quotations and contrasts Jesus, who saw no decay, with David, who did.
