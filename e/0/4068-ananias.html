Abstract written by a model

The records gather several different figures named Ananias under one label, so it is not truly a single person. One Ananias, married to Sapphira, sold land, kept back part of the price, and laid the rest at the apostles' feet; Peter said he had lied to God, and he fell dead and was buried. Another, a disciple at Damascus, was devout according to the law and well reported of by the Jews. Told by the Lord in a vision, he went to the blinded Saul, restored his sight, and urged baptism. A third, the high priest, came with elders and Tertullus to inform the governor against Paul.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Ananias sold a possession, kept back part of the price, and laid the remainder at the apostles' feet. Peter told him he had lied to God, not to men, and Ananias fell down and died upon hearing this. The young men wrapped him up, carried him out, and buried him.

Chapter 9

Ananias, a disciple at Damascus, was told by the Lord in a vision to go to Saul on the street called Straight. He voiced concern about Saul's evil toward the saints but obeyed, laid hands on Saul, and told him he would receive sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit.

Chapter 22

Ananias is described as a devout man according to the law, well reported of by all the Jews in Damascus. He comes to the blinded Saul, restores his sight, tells him he is appointed to know God's will and be a witness to all men, and urges him to be baptized and call on the Lord's name.

Chapter 24

Ananias, the high priest, comes down after five days with certain elders and the orator Tertullus. Together they inform the governor against Paul.
