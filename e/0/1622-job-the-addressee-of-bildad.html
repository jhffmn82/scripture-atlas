Abstract written by a model

Job is the sufferer addressed by Bildad in two speeches. In Chapter 8, Bildad rebukes his words as a mighty wind, suggests his children sinned, urges him to seek God, and promises restoration, laughter, and shame for his haters if he is pure. In Chapter 18, Bildad accuses him of hunting for words and self-tearing anger, resenting being regarded as animals.

Chapter 8

Job is addressed by Bildad, who rebukes his speech as a mighty wind and suggests his children sinned against God. He is urged to seek God and supplicate the Almighty, and is told that if he were pure and upright, God would restore him so that his small beginning would be followed by great increase. Bildad promises that God will fill his mouth with laughter and that those who hate him will be clothed with shame.

Chapter 18

Job is addressed by Bildad as one who hunts for words and who tears himself in his anger. Bildad complains that he and his companions are regarded as animals in Job's sight, and asks whether the earth will be forsaken or the rock removed for Job's sake.
