Abstract written by a model

Balak, son of Zippor, is king of Moab and an enemy of Israel. Fearing them, he sends messengers, then more honorable princes, to bring Balaam from Aram to curse Israel, promising great honor. He meets Balaam, sacrifices, and builds altars at high places, then moves him to Pisgah and Peor hoping for a different outcome. Balaam blesses instead each time. Balak finally says neither curse nor bless, then angrily strikes his hands together, tells Balaam to flee, says the LORD kept him from honor, and after Balaam's last oracles goes his own way.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 22

Balak, king of Moab, fears Israel and sends messengers, then a second and more honorable group of princes, to bring Balaam to curse them, promising great honor. He believes that whomever Balaam blesses is blessed and whomever he curses is cursed. He meets Balaam at the City of Moab, sacrifices cattle and sheep, and takes Balaam up to the high places of Baal to see part of Israel.

Chapter 23

Balak, king of Moab, asks Balaam to curse Jacob and Israel, and builds the altars and offers the sacrifices Balaam requests. When Balaam blesses instead, Balak objects and moves him to Pisgah and then to Peor, hoping a different place will let him curse them. At last he tells Balaam to neither curse nor bless them.

Chapter 24

Balak is angry that Balaam has blessed rather than cursed his enemies three times, and he strikes his hands together. He tells Balaam to flee, saying he had meant to promote him to great honor but the LORD has kept him back from it. After Balaam's final oracles, Balak goes his own way.
