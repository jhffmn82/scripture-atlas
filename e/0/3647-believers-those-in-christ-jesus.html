Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Matthew, little ones who believe in Jesus are valued and protected: causing them to stumble merits severe punishment, they must not be despised, their angels see the Father's face, and the Father does not will any to perish. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, believers, those in Christ Jesus, are justified by faith through Christ's blood, reconciled to God, given peace, grace, hope, salvation from wrath, and no condemnation; led by the Spirit, they are children and heirs of God, more than conquerors, and inseparable from God's love. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, the children of the promise, unlike the children of the flesh, are counted as heirs and children of God through Isaac and Jacob. In Titus, believers, the readers and 'us', are reminded of their former foolishness and disobedience, justified by grace and made heirs of eternal life, and urged to obey rulers, do good works, and avoid foolish controversies and factious people.

believers (those in Christ Jesus) in Paul’s Letter to the Romans

author unknown · about AD 57 · the work’s page · read it where believers (those in Christ Jesus) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Believers, those in Christ Jesus, are a group defined by being justified by faith through Christ's blood. Once weak, ungodly sinners and enemies, they are reconciled to God through the death of his Son. They have peace with God, access into grace, and rejoice in hope of God's glory, even amid suffering. They are saved from wrath and face no condemnation. Led by the Spirit, they are children and heirs of God, joint heirs with Christ if they suffer with him. They hold the Spirit's first fruits and await adoption and bodily redemption. They are more than conquerors, and nothing can separate them from God's love.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Believers, justified by faith, have peace with God, access into grace, and rejoice in hope of God's glory and in their sufferings. They were weak, ungodly, sinners and enemies when Christ died for them, and they have now received reconciliation. They will be saved from wrath, and those who receive the abundance of grace and the gift of righteousness will reign in life through Jesus Christ.

Chapter 8

Believers face no condemnation, are freed from the law of sin and death, and are children of God, heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ if they suffer with him. They groan and wait in hope for adoption and the redemption of their bodies, are helped by the Spirit, are called and justified, and are more than conquerors through Christ. Nothing can separate them from God's love in Christ Jesus.

believers (the readers and 'us') in Titus

Paul · about AD 63 · the work’s page · read it where believers (the readers and 'us') first appears

Abstract written by a model

The believers are a group addressed in Chapter 3, reminded they were once foolish, disobedient and deceived, but are now justified by grace and made heirs of eternal life. They are urged to obey rulers, be gentle and humble, do good works, shun foolish controversies, and avoid a factious man after two warnings. The passage ends with greetings and a blessing of grace.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

The believers are told to be subject to rulers and authorities, obedient, gentle, humble toward all, and ready for every good work. They are reminded that they were once foolish, disobedient, deceived, and hateful, but were saved by God's mercy and made heirs of eternal life. They are told to shun foolish disputes, avoid a factious man after two warnings, and learn to maintain good works to meet necessary needs so they are not unfruitful. They send greetings and receive a blessing of grace.
