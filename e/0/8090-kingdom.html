Also called: The great state, a great state, great state, the kingdom

Abstract written by a model

The kingdom is governed according to Tao and made its own through freedom from action and purpose. Its ruler must be grave and still, never lightly asserting mastery through arms; delight in slaughter cannot achieve one’s will there. Excessive prohibitions and legislation bring poverty, disorder, and theft. Governing a great state requires the care of cooking small fish. Like a low-lying, down-flowing stream, the great state draws small states by condescending and abasing itself, gaining them in order to unite and nourish people.

The great state is compared to a low-lying, down-flowing stream that becomes the center toward which the small states tend. By condescending to small states, it gains them for itself, and it seeks to unite and nourish people. It must learn to abase itself.
