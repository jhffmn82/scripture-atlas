Abstract written by a model

These passages from Japji describe a spiritual journey through divine realms and the limits of human speech about God. The first presents God, the True One, as the source of order, gifts, creation, judgment, knowledge, virtue, and salvation. Hearing and contemplating God’s Name, obeying God, and receiving the Guru’s instruction bring divine knowledge and spiritual benefit. It introduces the realms of righteousness, knowledge, and happiness; in the realm of knowledge divine light shines, songs yield countless joys, and incomparable beauty, wisdom, intellect, understanding, and supernatural skill are fashioned beyond adequate description. The next passage turns to the realm of action, marked by incomparable force, powerful warriors and heroes filled with Ram’s might, and many Sitas of indescribable beauty. Those with God in their hearts neither die nor go astray, while saints rejoice with the True One within them. God dwells in the true realm, favors its people, and beholds innumerable continents, worlds, universes, and forms performing their functions by God’s order. Nanak concludes that describing this vastness is impossible and emphasizes love for and meditation on God’s Name as a path to emancipation.

Japji .. 36 In the realm of knowledge the light of divine knowledge is resplendent. There are heard songs from which millions of joys and pleasures proceed. Beauty is the attribute of the realm of happiness. There things are fashioned in an incomparable manner. What is done there cannot be described. Whoever endeavoureth to describe it shall afterwards repent. There are fashioned knowledge, wisdom, intellect, and understanding; And there too is fashioned the skill of demigods and men of supernatural power.

This unit concerns God, the True One, and how people know, praise, hear, and obey Him. It describes God's order, gifts, creation, judgment, and the limits of human power to describe Him. It explains the benefits of hearing God's Name, obeying God, receiving the Guru's instruction, and attaining divine knowledge, virtue, and salvation. It also describes the realm of righteousness, the realm of knowledge, and the realm of happiness.

37 Force is the attribute of the realm of action. Incomparable are they who dwell therein. There are very powerful warriors and heroes They are filled with the might of Ram.- There are many Sitas in the midst of greatness, Their beauty cannot be described They die not, neither are they led astray In whose hearts God dwelleth. There dwell congregations of saints They rejoice; the True One is in their hearts. God dwelleth in the true realm. He looketh on its denizens with an eye of favour, and rendereth them happy. There are continents, worlds, and universes. Whoever trieth to describe them shall never arrive at an end. There are worlds upon worlds and forms upon forms. They fulfil their functions according to God's orders: God beholding and contemplating them is pleased. Nanak, to describe them would be impossible. .. The unit describes the realm of action, whose inhabitants include powerful warriors, heroes, and many Sitas, and whose force is incomparable. It describes congregations of saints who rejoice with the True One in their hearts and the true realm where God dwells. It explains that God favors its inhabitants and is pleased while beholding the continents, worlds, universes, and their forms. Nanak teaches that divine practice involves melting God's name in divine love, and that those who ponder on the Name may be emancipated.
