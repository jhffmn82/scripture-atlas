molten sea in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where molten sea first appears

Abstract written by a model

The molten sea is a large basin, ten cubits brim to brim, five cubits high, thirty cubits around, with a lily-like brim, holding two thousand baths. It rests on twelve oxen, three facing each of four directions, and is placed on the house's right side, eastward toward the south.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

The molten sea was made ten cubits from brim to brim and five cubits high, with a thirty-cubit circumference, a hand width thick, and a brim like a lily flower, and it held two thousand baths. It stood on twelve oxen facing the four directions, three each, and was placed on the right side of the house eastward toward the south.

molten sea in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where molten sea first appears

Abstract written by a model

The molten sea is a large round basin, ten cubits across brim to brim, five cubits high, thirty cubits around, a handbreadth thick, with a cup- or lily-shaped brim. It holds three thousand baths and serves the priests for washing. It rests on twelve oxen facing the four directions, three each, and is placed on the right side of God's house, eastward toward the south.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

The molten sea was ten cubits from brim to brim, five cubits high, and thirty cubits in circumference, with a thickness of a handbreadth and a brim like a cup or lily flower. It stood on twelve oxen facing the four directions, three each, with their hindquarters inward, held three thousand baths, and was for the priests to wash in. It was set on the right side of the house, eastward toward the south.
