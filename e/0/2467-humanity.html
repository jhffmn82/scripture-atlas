Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, men multiply, become continually evil in heart, and are destined for destruction by the LORD, with their lifespan limited because they are flesh. In Psalms, humanity is portrayed as frail and short-lived, troubled by God's anger and asking to be taught to count its days. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, mankind is portrayed as short-lived and perplexed, yet shown patience, mercy, and forgiveness by the Lord. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, humanity is described as condemned to death through Adam's trespass yet justified to life through Jesus Christ's righteousness.

humanity in Psalms

author unknown · undated · the work’s page · read it where humanity first appears

Abstract written by a model

Humanity is portrayed as frail and short-lived, living seventy to eighty years of labor and sorrow, compared to grass that sprouts in the morning and withers by evening. Consumed and troubled by God's anger, with sins exposed, people end like a sigh and ask to be taught to count their days.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 90

Humanity is told to return and is described as swept away, like new grass that sprouts in the morning and is withered by evening. People are consumed and troubled in the Lord's anger, their sins are exposed before him, and their lives of labor and sorrow end like a sigh. They ask to be taught to count their days and to be made glad.

mankind in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · about 179 BC · the work’s page · read it where mankind first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mankind is a short-lived collective, living at most a hundred years, likened to a drop of water or pebble against eternity. Questioned about purpose, good and evil, it faces an evil end, yet the Lord shows patience, mercy and forgiveness; inquiry into God's works leaves man perplexed at the beginning.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

Mankind is questioned as to purpose, good, and evil, and is said to live at most a hundred years, which is like a drop of water or a pebble in the day of eternity. The Lord sees that their end is evil, yet is patient with them and pours out mercy and forgiveness. When a man finishes inquiring into God's works he is only at the beginning and is perplexed.
