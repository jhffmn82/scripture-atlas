Lu

place · 7 works · 4 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: State of Lu

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book V (Kung-Ye Ch'Ang), Lu is a place where virtuous men exist, whose presence helped Tsze-chien acquire his character. In The Analects of Confucius, Book VI (Yung Yey), Lu would come to a State where true principles predominated by one change. In The Analects of Confucius, Book IX (Tsze Han), Lu was the place to which the Master returned. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XIII: Tsze-Lu, Lu has a government that is a brother to the government of Wei. In Mencius, Book I (Liang Hui Wang), Lu is involved in a brush with Zou. In Mencius, Book VI (Gaozi), Lu is a five-hundred-li-square territory including Nan Yang as potentially taken from Qi, to be governed through benevolence and perhaps diminished by a true royal ruler. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), Lu appeared small to Confucius after he ascended the eastern hill.

Part II / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 13

Lu is described as having five times 100 li square, more than the territory originally assigned to Zhou Gong. Mencius says a true royal ruler might diminish Lu rather than increase it and argues that its government should be directed toward benevolence.
