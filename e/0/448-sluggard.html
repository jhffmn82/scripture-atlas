sluggard

Also called: The sluggard, the man void of understanding, the sluggard, you sluggard

Abstract written by a model

The sluggard is a habitually idle, oversleeping man who lacks understanding and offers excuses, such as claiming a lion is outside. He is told to consider the ant. His field and vineyard are neglected. Across both chapters, a little sleep and folded hands bring poverty and want, arriving suddenly like a robber or an armed man.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

The sluggard is told to consider the ant and is asked how long he will sleep. His little sleep and folding of the hands will bring poverty on him like a robber and scarcity like an armed man.

Chapter 24

The sluggard is a man void of understanding, whose field and vineyard the narrator passes by. The narrator learns from this that a little sleep, slumber, and folding of the hands will bring poverty like a robber and want like an armed man.
