the wicked in The Book of Job

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the wicked first appears

Abstract written by a model

The wicked, as described in Chapter 21 and observed by Job, are a group who prosper rather than suffer: they live long, grow mighty, enjoy safe houses, thriving livestock and children who dance and sing. They reject God, telling him to depart and seeing no profit in serving him. They die suddenly, go down to Sheol, and are carried to a watched tomb, unchallenged and unrepaid, though reserved for the day of calamity and wrath.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

The wicked are described as living, growing old, and becoming mighty, with children established before their eyes, safe houses, and children who dance and sing. They tell God to depart and ask what profit there is in serving the Almighty. They go down to Sheol in an instant, are carried to the grave and watched over at the tomb, and no one declares their way to their face or repays them.
