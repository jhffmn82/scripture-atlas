Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the holy garments are made for Aaron and his sons to minister, consecrate, and serve in the priestly office. In Leviticus, the priestly garments clothe Aaron and his sons and are sprinkled with oil and blood to sanctify them. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the priestly garments comprise Aaron's elaborate holy vestments, worn perpetually by his descendants.

holy garments (for Aaron and his sons) in Exodus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where holy garments (for Aaron and his sons) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The holy garments are the priestly vestments made for Aaron to minister in the priest's office, giving glory and beauty and sanctifying him. They include the breastplate of judgment, ephod, robe, fitted tunic, turban, and sash, with a gold plate of the holy crown inscribed "HOLY TO THE LORD" tied to the turban. Wise-hearted workers, filled by the LORD with wisdom, made them from gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine linen. Aaron's sons also receive tunics, sashes, headbands, and linen pants. Aaron is clothed in them for consecration, and they then pass to his sons for anointing; the successor priest wears them seven days upon entering the Tent of Meeting. The finished garments are brought to Moses with the tabernacle work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 28

The holy garments are a breastplate, ephod, robe, fitted tunic, turban, and sash for Aaron, made by wise-hearted workers from gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine linen. They sanctify Aaron for ministry and give glory and beauty. Aaron's sons receive tunics, sashes, headbands, and linen pants, and the robe's bells and the forehead plate are added to Aaron's attire.

Chapter 29

Aaron's holy garments are the tunic, robe of the ephod, ephod, breastplate, woven band, turban, and holy crown, put on him for consecration. They are to pass to his sons after him, to be anointed and consecrated in them. The son who is priest in his place is to wear them seven days when he enters the Tent of Meeting to minister.

Chapter 39

The holy garments are made of blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen as finely worked garments for ministering in the holy place, including the tunics, turban, headbands, trousers and sash. A gold plate of the holy crown is inscribed "HOLY TO THE LORD" and tied to the turban with a blue lace. They are brought to Moses with the tabernacle work for the priest's office.

priestly garments (Aaron's garments) in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where priestly garments (Aaron's garments) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Aaron's priestly garments comprise linen trousers, a long robe, the ephod, and a gold, blue, purple and scarlet holy garment, with pomegranates and golden bells, Urim and Thummim, engraved tribal stones set in gold, and a gold mitre crown engraved "HOLINESS". Unprecedented in kind, they are worn perpetually only by Aaron's sons and offspring; the bells remind the people.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 45

Aaron's garments included linen trousers, a long robe, the ephod, pomegranates with golden bells, and a holy garment of gold, blue, purple, and twisted scarlet. They also featured Urim and Thummim, precious stones engraved like a signet for the tribes of Israel, and a gold crown on the mitre engraved "HOLINESS". Nothing like them had existed before, and only Aaron's sons and offspring may wear them, with the bells serving as a reminder to the people.
