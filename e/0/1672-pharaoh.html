Pharaoh king of Egypt in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · about 592 to 570 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pharaoh king of Egypt first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pharaoh, king of Egypt, is portrayed as a great monster lying in his rivers, boasting that the rivers are his own. The LORD stands against him in judgment. In the first account he is hooked, pulled out, and cast into the wilderness as food for animals and birds. Next he is compared in greatness to the proud Assyrian cedar and told he will be brought down with the trees of Eden to Sheol, lying among the uncircumcised slain by the sword. Finally he is lamented as a young lion of the nations and a monster in the seas who troubled the waters. He will be netted and laid among the slain, seeing other fallen nations and being comforted, since the LORD has put his terror in the land of the living.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 29

Pharaoh is addressed as the great monster lying in his rivers, who claimed the river as his own and made it for himself. The LORD declares he is against him, will put hooks in his jaws, pull him out with the fish sticking to his scales, and cast him into the open wilderness as food for animals and birds.

Chapter 31

Pharaoh king of Egypt is addressed by the LORD through the prophet and asked whom he is like in his greatness. He is compared to the proud Assyrian cedar, and is told he will be brought down with the trees of Eden to the lower parts of the earth. He will lie among the uncircumcised and those slain by the sword.

Chapter 32

Pharaoh is the subject of the lamentation, likened to a young lion of the nations yet a monster in the seas who troubled the waters. The LORD says he will net him, cast him on the open field, and lay him among the uncircumcised slain by the sword. Pharaoh will see the other fallen nations and be comforted over his multitude, because the LORD has put his terror in the land of the living.
