Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Melchizedek is king of Salem and priest of God Most High who brings bread and wine, blesses Abram, and receives a tenth of all from him. In The Letter to the Hebrews, Melchizedek is king of Salem and priest of God Most High who blesses Abraham, receives a tenth of the plunder, and is described as a priest continually, made like the Son of God.

Melchizedek in The Letter to the Hebrews

author unknown · about AD 64 to 69 · the work’s page · read it where Melchizedek first appears

Abstract written by a model

Melchizedek is king of Salem and priest of God Most High, whose name means king of righteousness and king of peace. He meets Abraham returning from battle, blesses him, and receives a tenth of the plunder, showing he is greater than Abraham. He is described as without father, mother, or genealogy, with no beginning of days or end of life, made like the Son of God, and a priest continually.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings, blessed him, and received a tenth from him. His name is interpreted as king of righteousness and king of peace, and he is described as without father, mother, or genealogy, with neither beginning of days nor end of life, made like the Son of God and remaining a priest continually. He is shown to be greater than Abraham because he blessed him.
