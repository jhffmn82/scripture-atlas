Abstract written by a model

The twelve tribes in the Dispersion are the addressees of a letter greeted by James. Called brothers and given an honorable name, they are oppressed by the rich, who drag them before the courts, and they will be judged by the law of freedom; teachers among them face heavier judgment. The letter opens by urging them to count trials as joy, ask God for wisdom, resist temptation, and be doers of the word. It then warns against partiality and dishonoring the poor, and calls for mercy and works along with faith. It cautions about teaching and the tongue, and urges gentle, wise conduct over jealousy and selfish ambition. It then rebukes fighting, coveting, and double-mindedness, calls them adulterers and enemies of God if they befriend the world, and tells them to submit to God, resist the devil, draw near, and humble themselves. It closes by commanding patience until the Lord's coming, avoiding oaths, praying, confessing sins to one another, and turning back any who wander from the truth.

The Letter from James / Chapter 1

The twelve tribes in the Dispersion are the addressees of the letter, greeted by James. They are called brothers and told to count trials as joy, ask God for wisdom, resist temptation, and be doers of the word.

Chapter 2

The brothers are told not to hold the faith with partiality, and are accused of dishonoring the poor man and of becoming judges with evil thoughts. They are reminded that the rich oppress them and drag them before the courts, and they are urged to fulfill the royal law, to speak and act as those judged by the law of freedom, and to show mercy and works along with their faith.

Chapter 3

The brothers are told that not many of them should be teachers, since teachers will receive heavier judgment, and that all stumble in many things. They are told that blessing and cursing from the same mouth ought not to be so, and are asked who among them is wise and understanding. They are urged to show good conduct in gentleness and not to boast or lie against the truth if they harbor bitter jealousy and selfish ambition.

Chapter 4

The readers are accused of fighting, lusting, murdering, and coveting, and of asking with wrong motives so they do not receive. They are called adulterers and adulteresses, sinners, and double-minded, and are told to submit to God, resist the devil, draw near to God, cleanse their hands, purify their hearts, mourn, and humble themselves. They are also warned against speaking against brothers, judging, and boasting about future plans.

Chapter 5

The brothers are urged to be patient until the coming of the Lord, to establish their hearts, and not to grumble against one another. They are told to follow the prophets and Job as examples, to avoid oaths, to pray, sing, call the elders when sick, confess sins to one another, and turn back any who wander from the truth.
