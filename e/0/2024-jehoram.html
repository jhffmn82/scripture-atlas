Jehoram in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Jehoram first appears

Abstract written by a model

The records merge two kings named Jehoram (also Joram): the son of Ahab, who ruled Israel from Samaria for twelve years, and the son of Jehoshaphat, who ruled Judah from Jerusalem for eight years. Israel's Jehoram did evil, though less than his parents, removed Baal's pillar, and kept Jeroboam's sins. He campaigned against Moab with Jehoshaphat and Edom's king, was rebuked by Elisha, heeded Elisha's warnings, spared captured Syrians with a feast, and wore sackcloth during the siege. Judah's Jehoram married Ahab's daughter, followed the ways of Israel's kings and Ahab's house, did evil, struck Edomites at Zair but could not end their revolt, was buried in David's city, and was succeeded by Ahaziah.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

Jehoram son of Ahab began a twelve-year reign over Israel in Samaria, doing evil but removing the pillar of Baal and holding to the sins of Jeroboam. He mustered Israel against rebellious Moab, allied with Jehoshaphat and the king of Edom, and despaired when the army lacked water. Elisha rebuked him, yet the campaign succeeded until Israel departed from Moab after Mesha's sacrifice.

Chapter 6

The king of Israel heeds Elisha's warnings and saves himself more than once. When the blinded Syrians are brought to Samaria he asks Elisha whether to strike them, and obeys by feeding them a great feast and sending them home. During the siege he hears the woman's complaint, tears his clothes to reveal sackcloth beneath, and vows Elisha's head will not stay on him that day.

Chapter 8

Jehoram (also called Joram), son of Jehoshaphat, begins to reign over Judah in the fifth year of Joram of Israel, at age thirty-two, and reigns eight years. He walks in the way of Israel's kings and Ahab's house, having married Ahab's daughter, and does evil. He strikes the Edomites at Zair by night but cannot end their revolt, and he is buried in David's city with Ahaziah succeeding him.

Jehoram in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Jehoram first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jehoram was Jehoshaphat's firstborn son, who received the kingdom and reigned eight years in Jerusalem as king of Judah, beginning at thirty-two. After strengthening himself he killed his brothers, married Ahab's daughter, followed the evil ways of Israel's kings, built high places, and led Judah astray. Having forsaken the LORD, he lost Libnah, saw his sons taken except Jehoahaz, and was struck with illness. He died unregretted and was buried in David's city, not the kings' tombs.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

Jehoram, Jehoshaphat's firstborn, became king at thirty-two and reigned eight years in Jerusalem. After strengthening himself he killed all his brothers and some princes of Israel, walked in the ways of Ahab's house (his wife was Ahab's daughter), built high places, and led Judah astray. He lost Edom and Libnah, saw his sons and wives carried off, died of an incurable bowel disease, and was buried in David's city but not in the kings' tombs.
