Yen Ho

person · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 5 connections

Abstract written by a model

Yen Ho is a philosopher from Lu who has attained TAO and lives in poverty, indifferent to wealth and power. He foresees horses failing under excessive burdens, opposes Confucius, and seeks guidance before tutoring Prince Ling’s dangerous eldest son. He later declines Lu’s presents and disappears.

Yen Ho is in one work on this site, Chuang Tzŭ. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

He is about to tutor the eldest son of Prince Ling of the Wei State and asks Chü Poh Yü how to deal with a naturally low and dangerous disposition.

CHAPTER XXVIII.

Yen Ho lived in poverty while tending oxen and declined the presents sent by the prince of Lu. He disappeared before the messengers returned, illustrating indifference to wealth and power.
