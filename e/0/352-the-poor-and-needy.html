Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Job, the poor and needy are oppressed people who hide from the wicked, scavenge for their children, endure exposure and toil, and end with some killed and the wounded groaning. In The Book of Job, the poor and needy have hope because God saves the needy from the sword of their mouth and the hand of the mighty. In Psalms, the weak and needy are oppressed and groan, prompting the LORD to arise and set the oppressed one in safety. In Psalms, the poor and needy are raised by the LORD from the dust and ash heap and set with princes. In Psalms, the poor and needy are judged with justice by the king, who saves their children, delivers them when they cry out, pities them, and redeems them from oppression and violence. In Psalms, the oppressed are promised that the LORD will be a high tower for them in trouble, and that the needy and poor will not always be forgotten. In Psalms, the weak and poor are those whom the gods are commanded to defend, whose rights are to be maintained, and who are to be rescued from the wicked. In Proverbs, poor and needy people are shunned by neighbors, while pitying them brings blessing and oppressing them scorns their Maker.

and 3 more works

In The Book of Isaiah, the oppressed and needy are those whom the people should free, feed, house, and cover, with care for their afflicted souls tied to the people’s blessing. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), the poor are intended charity recipients who are constrained in Allah’s cause, unable to travel for trade, and not persistent beggars; concealed charity to them is better. In Surah 107: Almsgiving (Al-Maa'un), the needy are not fed because the one who belies religion does not urge their feeding.

the poor and needy in The Book of Job

Abstract written by a model

The poor and needy are oppressed people who hide from the wicked, scavenge like wild donkeys for their children, and endure nakedness, cold, hunger and thirst while laboring for others. The account moves from hiding and scavenging to exposure and toil, ending with some killed and the wounded groaning.

Chapter 24

They are turned out of the way and hide themselves, scavenge like wild donkeys for food for their children, and glean the vineyard of the wicked. They lie naked in the cold, are wet with mountain showers, embrace the rock for shelter, carry sheaves while hungry, and tread wine presses while thirsty. Some are killed by the murderer, and those wounded groan and cry out.
