Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of the Maccabees, Timotheus leads the Ammonite and Gentile armies against Judas, whose forces defeat them. In The Second Book of the Maccabees, Timotheus is a governor who commands large foreign forces and Asian cavalry against the Jews, then is captured and killed.

Timotheus in The Second Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 124 BC · the work’s page · read it where Timotheus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Timotheus is a governor of a district who refused to let the Jews live in peace. Previously beaten, he gathered large foreign forces and Asian cavalry, commanding 120,000 infantry and 2,500 cavalry, but they fled in terror before Judas. He sent his family and baggage to Carnion, tricked Dositheus and Sosipater into freeing him, then was found hidden in a cistern at Gazara and killed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10

Timotheus, previously defeated by the Jews, gathered large foreign forces and Asian cavalry to take Judea by force. He was routed in battle and fled to the fortress of Gazara, where Chaereas commanded. He was found hidden in a cistern and killed.

Chapter 12

Timotheus is one of the governors who would not let the Jews live in peace. He sends his women, children and baggage to Carnion, and his army of 120,000 infantry and 2,500 cavalry flees in terror before Judas. He tricks Dositheus and Sosipater into releasing him by claiming to hold their relatives, and then escapes.
