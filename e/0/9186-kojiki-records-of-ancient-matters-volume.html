Kojiki (Records of Ancient Matters), Volume III (Emperor Nin-toku to Empress Sui-ko)

Abstract written by a model

This portion of the Kojiki traces imperial succession from Emperor Nin-Toku through Empress Sui-Ko, combining genealogies, reigns, conflicts, marriages, songs, public acts, deaths, and burials. Nin-Toku rules from Takatsu in Naniha, undertakes public works, remits and later restores taxes and forced labour, and faces domestic conflict involving Empress Iha-no-hime, Princess Kuro, Yata-no-waki-iratsume, Queen Medori, and King Hayabusa-wake. He dies at eighty-three and is buried at Mozu. His successors include Ri-Chiu, saved from a palace fire set by King Sumi-no-ye-no-naka-tsu; Han-Zei, noted for extraordinary height; and in-Giyō, who is cured by a Korean physician and reforms surnames and gentile names. In-Giyō’s son King Karu falls after violating Karu-no-oho-iratsume and dies with Queen So-tohoshi. Later dynastic violence includes an-Kō’s killing of King Oho-kusaka, King Ma-yowa’s murder of an-Kō, and Prince Oho-hatsuse’s killings, which force Kings Ohoke and Woke into hiding. Yū-Riyaku’s reign includes marriages, clan foundations, encounters, progresses, and songs. After childless Sei-Nei dies, Ohoke and Woke are found in Harima; Woke defeats Grandee Shibi and receives the Empire after Ohoke yields it. Ken-Zō recovers his father King Ichinobe’s bones and avenges him, followed by Nin-Ken and childless Mu-Retsu. Ohodo becomes Kei-Tai, whose forces kill the rebellious Ihawi. The narrative then gives successions and offspring for Kan-an, Sen-Kuwa, Kim-Mei, Bi-Datsu, Yōmei, and Su-Jun, ending with Empress Sui-Ko’s thirty-seven-year reign and the removal of her mausoleum to Shinaga.

Kojiki (Records of Ancient Matters), Volume III (Emperor Nin-toku to Empress Sui-ko) / Sect. CXIX.—Emperor Nin-Toku (Par

This unit of the Kojiki concerns His Augustness Oho-sazaki, Emperor Nin-Toku, who dwelt at Takatsu in Naniha and ruled the Empire. He married Empress Her Augustness Iha-no-hime, Princess Kami-naga, and his younger half-sisters Yata-no-waki-iratsume and Uji-no-waki-iratsume. His marriages produced six august children in all, including His Augustness Ohoye-no-izaho-wake, His Augustness Midzu-ha-wake of Tajihi, and His Augustness the Noble Wo-asadzuma-no-waku-go, who later ruled the Empire.

Sect. CXX: Emperor Nin-Toku (Part II.—Various Deeds). .. Sect. CXXX.—Emperor Nin-Toku (Part XI.—His Age and Place of Burial).

This unit concerns Emperor Nin-Toku’s reign, including public works, remission and later restoration of taxes and forced labour, and his relationships with Princess Kuro, Yata-no-waki-iratsume, and Queen Medori. The Empress Iha-no-hime’s jealousy leads her to withdraw to Yamashiro, while Emperor Nin-Toku follows her through Kuchiko and Nurinomi at Tsutsuki. King Hayabusa-wake and Queen Medori marry, flee to Mount Kurahashi, and are slain by the Imperial army. Emperor Nin-Toku is said to have lived eighty-three years and to have been buried on the Ear-Moor of Mozu.

Sect. CXXXI.—Emperor Ri-Chiu (Part I.—Genealogies). .. Sect. CXXXV.—Emperor Ri-Chiu (Part V.—His Age and Place of Burial).

Emperor Ri-Chiu ruled the Empire from the palace of Wakasakura at Ihare and fathered King Oshiha, King Mima, and Awomi-no-iratsume. While Emperor Ri-Chiu was asleep after a feast at the palace of Naniha, King Sumi-no-ye-no-naka-tsu set fire to the great palace, and the Suzerain of Achi secretly carried the Emperor into Yamato. His Augustness Midzu-ha-wake later killed King Sumi-no-ye-no-naka-tsu and, after appointing Sobakari Prime Minister, killed Sobakari before proceeding to serve Emperor Ri-Chiu. Emperor Ri-Chiu died at age sixty-four and was buried at Mozu.

Sect. CXXXV.—Emperor Han-Zei.

Emperor Han-Zei, also called Midzu-ha-wake, dwells at the palace of Shibakaki at Tajihi and rules the Empire. He is described as nine feet two inches and a half in height, with precisely corresponding teeth. He marries the Lady of Tsunu and Oto-hime, both daughters of Kogoto, and has four children. He dies at age sixty and is buried on the Moor of Mozu.

Sect. CXXXVII.—Emperor in-Giyō (Part I—Genealogies). .. Sect. CXLIII.—Emperor in-Giyō (Part VII.—Death of Prince Karu and Princess So-Tohoshi).

This unit concerns Emperor in-Giyō, his family, his rule, and the events following his death. He was cured of sickness by a Korean physician, rectified the surnames and gentile names of the people, and was buried at Naga-ye near Wega. King Karu of Kinashi, having debauched his younger sister Karu-no-oho-iratsume, lost the support of the Empire and was captured after conflict with His Augustness Anaho. King Karu and Queen So-tohoshi were banished and later killed themselves together.

Sect. CXLIV.—Emperor an-Kō (Part I.—He Slays King Oho-Kusaka). .. Sect. CXLIX.—Emperor an-Kō (Part VI.—Flight of Princes Ohoke and Woke)].

Emperor an-Kō sent Grandee of Ne to ask King Oho-kusaka for Queen Waka-kusaka for Prince Oho-hatsuse, but Grandee of Ne slandered King Oho-kusaka, leading Emperor an-Kō to slay him and take Nagata-no-oho-iratsume as Empress. King Ma-yowa overheard Emperor an-Kō confess fear that he would avenge his father and later slew Emperor an-Kō while he slept. Prince Oho-hatsuse then slew King Kurobiko and King Shiro-biko, and later died with Grandee Tsubura after besieging Tsubura’s house. Prince Oho-hatsuse also slew King Ichi-no-be-no-oshiha, causing Kings Ohoke and Woke to flee through Yamashiro to Harima, where they hid and worked as grooms and cow-herds.

Sect. CL.—Emperor Yū-Riyaku, (I.—Genealogies). .. Sect. CLXI.—Emperor Yū-Riyaku (Part XII.—Songs by the Emperor and Princess Wodo).

This unit concerns Emperor Yū-Riyaku, his genealogies, deeds, songs, encounters, progresses, and final age and burial place. It records his marriages to Queen Waka-kusaka-be and Princess Wodo, his child Shiraka, the establishment of clans, and several episodes involving Akawi-ko, the Great Deity of Kadzuraki, Lord of One Word, and a female attendant from Mihe. It preserves songs associated with the Emperor, Princess Wodo, Queen Waka-kusaka-be, Akawi-ko, and others, including songs about the dragon-fly and the great august liquor. The Heavenly Sovereign’s august years were one hundred and twenty-four, and his august mausoleum was at Takawashi in Tajihi in Kafuchi.

Sect. CLXIII.—Emperor Sei-Nei (Part I.—Search for a Successor to Him). .. Sect. CLXVI.—Emperor Sei-Nei (Part IV.—Prince Ohoke Cedes the Empire to Prince Woke).

His Augustness Shiraka-no-oho-yamato-ne-ko ruled the Empire without an Empress or august children, and after his death a search began for a successor. Oshinumi-no-iratsume, also called Princess Ihi-toyo, was found, while Wodate discovered the princes Ohoke and Woke in Harima and brought them to the palace. Grandee Shibi fought with Prince Woke through the Song-Hedge and was later besieged and slain by the two princes. Prince Ohoke ceded the Empire to Prince Woke, who ruled it first.

Sect. CLXVII.—Emperor Ken-Zō (Part I.—The Old Woman Oki-Me). .. Sect. CLXX.—Emperor Ken-Zō (Part IV.—His Age and Place of Burial). / Sect. CLXXI.—Emperor Nin-Ken.

Emperor Ken-Zō ruled for eight years from the palace of Chika-tsu-Asuka, had no children, and recovered the august bones of his father, King Ichinobe, with the help of the old woman Oki-me. He punished the old boar-herd who had seized his provisions and disfigured the mausoleum of Emperor Yū-Riyaku as revenge for his father's death. After Emperor Ken-Zō died at the age of thirty-eight, His Augustness Ohoke succeeded him and became Emperor Nin-Ken. Emperor Nin-Ken ruled from Hirataka at Isono-kami and had seven august children, including His Grandeur Wo-hatsuse-no-waka-sazaki.

Sect. CLXXII—Emperor Mu-Retsu.

Emperor Mu-Retsu, also called Wo-hatsuse-no-waka-sazaki, dwelt at the palace of Namiki at Hatsuse and ruled the Empire for eight years. He had no august children, so the Wo-hatsuse Tribe was established as his august proxy. His august mausoleum was on the mound of Ihatsuki at Karawoka. After his death, Ohodo was sent for from Afumi, married to Tashiraka, and presented with the Empire.

Sect. CLXXIII.—Emperor Kei-Tai.

Emperor Kei-Tai, also called His Augustness Ohodo, dwelt at the Palace of Tamaho at Ihare and ruled the Empire. The unit records his nineteen children, including His Augustness Ame-kuni-oshi-haruki-hiro-niha, His Augustness Hiro-kuni-oshi-taka-kana-hi, and Queen Sasage. During his reign, Ihawi, Lord of Tsukushi, disobeyed the Imperial Decrees and was exceedingly disrespectful. Mononobe-no-arakawi and Ohotomo-no-kanamura were sent to slay Ihawi, and Emperor Kei-Tai's reign lasted forty-three years.

Sect. CXXXIV.—Emperor Kan-an.

Emperor Kan-an, whose personal name is Hiro-kuni-oshi-take-kana-hi, dwelt in the Palace of Kanahashi at Magari and ruled the Empire. This Heavenly Monarch had no august children. His august grave is at the village of Takaya in Furuchi in Kafuchi.

Sect. CLXXV.—Emperor Sen-Kuwa.

Emperor Sen-Kuwa dwelt in the Palace of Ihorinu at Hinokuma and ruled the Empire. By Her Augustness Tachi-bana-no-naka-tsu-hime, he had three august children: Her Augustness Ishi-hime, Her Augustness Wo-ishi-hime, and King Kura-no-waka-ye. By Kafuchi-no-waku-go-hime, he had King Honoho and King Weha, making five august children in all; King Honoho became ancestor of the Dukes Shihida, and Prince Weha became ancestor of the Dukes of Wina and Tajihi.

Sect. CLXXVI.—Emperor Kim-Mei.

Emperor Kim-Mei, also called the Heavenly Sovereign Ame-kuni-oshi-haruki-hiro-niha, dwelt in the Great Palace of Shikishima and ruled the Empire. He had twenty-five august children by five wives, including Nu-na-kura-tama-shiki, His Augustness Tachibana-no-toyo-hi, Toyo-mike-kashiki-ya-hime, and His Augustness Hatsuse-be-no-waka-sazaki. Nu-na-kura-tama-shiki, His Augustness Tachibana-no-toyo-hi, Toyo-mike-kashiki-ya-hime, and His Augustness Hatsuse-be-no-waka-sazaki successively ruled the Empire after him.

Sect. CLXXVII.—Emperor Bi-Datsu.

Nuna-kura-futo-tama-shiki dwelt in the Palace of Wosada and ruled the Empire for fourteen years. His wife and half-sister Toyo-mike-kashiki-ya-hime bore him eight children, and his other wives bore additional children. Hiko-hito and his wife and half-sister Tamura had three children, including the Heavenly Sovereign that ruled the Empire from the Palace of Wokamoto. The august mausoleum of Nuna-kura-futo-tama-shiki is at Shinaga in Kafuchi.

Sect. CLXXVIII.—Emperor Yōmei.

Emperor Yōmei dwelt in the Palace of Ikenobe and ruled the Empire for three years. His children included King Tame, four children by Princess Hashi-bito-no-anaho-be, and King Tagima and Sugashiroko-no-iratsume by Ihime-no-ko. His august mausoleum was first by the borders of Lake Ihare and was later removed to the middle sepulchre of Shinaga.

Sect. CLXXIX.—Emperor Su-Jun.

Emperor Su-Jun, also called the Heavenly Sovereign Hatsuse-be-no-waka-sazaki, dwelt at the Palace of Shibabaki at Kurahashi. He ruled the Empire for four years. His august mausoleum is on the mound of Kurahashi.

Sect. CLXXX.—Empress Sui-Ko.

Empress Sui-Ko, also called Toyo-mike-kashiki-ya-hime, dwelt at the Palace of Wohorida. She ruled the Empire for thirty seven years. Her august mausoleum was initially on the mound of Ohonu and was later removed to the great sepulchre at Shinaga.
