Tsze-hsia

person · 4 works · 17 quoted facts · 2 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book VI (Yung Yey), Tsze-hsia is told by the Master to be a scholar after the style of the superior man rather than the mean man. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XII (Yen Yuan), Tsze-hsia hears a saying and instructs others, consoles Sze-ma Niu about fate, status, and brotherhood, and explains the Master's rich teaching to Fan Ch'ih. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XIII: Tsze-Lu, Tsze-hsia, as governor of Chu-fu, asks the Master about government. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XIX: Tsze-Chang, Tsze-hsia teaches about the value and limits of inferior studies, the requirements of learning, the precedence of confidence before difficult labors or remonstrance, and the proper limit of mourning.

Tsze-hsia in The Analects of Confucius, Book XII (Yen Yuan)

author unknown · about 478 to 220 BC · the work’s page · read it where Tsze-hsia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tsze-hsia is a disciple who hears a saying and instructs others. He consoles Sze-ma Niu that fate and status rest with determined appointments and Heaven, while proper conduct creates brotherhood. He also explains the Master's rich teaching to Fan Ch'ih through virtuous appointments that remove those without virtue.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. V. 1. Sze-ma Niu, full of anxiety, said, 'Other men all

Tsze-hsia tells Sze-ma Niu that death and life have determined appointments and that riches and honours depend upon Heaven. He teaches that proper conduct, respect for others, and observance of propriety make all within the four seas the superior man's brothers.

CHAP. XXII. 1. Fan Ch'ih asked about benevolence. The Master

Tsze-hsia tells Fan Ch'ih that the Master's saying is truly rich. He explains it by referring to Shun's employment of Kao-yao and T'ang's employment of I Yin, after which those devoid of virtue disappeared.

Tsze-hsia in The Analects of Confucius, Book XIX: Tsze-Chang

author unknown · about 450 BC · the work’s page · read it where Tsze-hsia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tsze-hsia teaches that inferior studies may have value but should not be extended into remote applications. He says that learning requires daily recognition of what is not yet attained, remembrance of what has been gained, extensive study, sincere purpose, earnest inquiry, and self-applied reflection. He also teaches that confidence should precede imposing labors or remonstrating, and that mourning should stop when grief has reached its utmost degree.
