Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Isaiah, Sidon is described as a coastal seafaring place whose merchants replenished the coast's inhabitants, and its daughter is told she will be ashamed, rejoice no more, find no rest, and flee to Kittim. In The Book of Ezekiel, Sidon is declared against by the LORD, with pestilence, blood in its streets, and sword on every side announced against it.

Sidon in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · 739 to 680 BC · the work’s page · read it where Sidon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sidon is a coastal seafaring place whose merchants once replenished the coast's inhabitants. It is told to be ashamed, and its daughter, an oppressed virgin, is told she will rejoice no more and find no rest, even fleeing to Kittim.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 23

Sidon is told to be ashamed because the sea, the stronghold of the sea, speaks of never having travailed or borne children. Its merchants are said to have replenished the coast dwellers. The LORD addresses its daughter as an oppressed virgin who will rejoice no more and will find no rest even if she flees to Kittim.

Sidon in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · about 592 to 570 BC · the work’s page · read it where Sidon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sidon is a place the LORD declares himself against; the prophet is told to prophesy against it, announcing pestilence, blood in its streets, and sword on every side, so they will know the LORD.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 28

The prophet is told to set his face toward Sidon and prophesy against it. The LORD declares he is against Sidon and will send pestilence, blood in its streets, and the sword on every side, so that they will know he is the LORD.
