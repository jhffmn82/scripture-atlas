David's men in The First Book of Samuel

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where David's men first appears

Abstract written by a model

David's men are a company of about six hundred who follow David through his years as a fugitive. They fear going to Keilah yet fight and save its people, then accompany him in the wilderness of Maon and into the cave's innermost parts, where they urge him to seize his enemy Saul, but he restrains them. They go with their households to Achish at Gath, settle in Ziklag, and raid neighboring peoples. Finding Ziklag burned and their families taken, they grieve and speak of stoning David. Four hundred pursue the Amalekites, recover everything, and David overrules those objecting to sharing the plunder.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 23

David's men voice fear about going to Keilah against the Philistines, but go with David, fight, and save the inhabitants. About six hundred of them leave Keilah with David and later are with him in the wilderness of Maon when Saul nearly surrounds them.

Chapter 24

David's men are staying with him in the innermost parts of the cave and urge him to act on the day the LORD spoke of, when his enemy is delivered into his hand. David checks them with his words and does not allow them to rise against Saul. They later go up to the stronghold with David.

Chapter 27

The six hundred men go with David to Achish at Gath, each with his household, and later live in Ziklag. They go up with David to raid the Geshurites, Girzites, and Amalekites.

Chapter 30

The six hundred men weep until they have no strength when they find Ziklag burned and their families taken, and in their grief speak of stoning David. Four hundred cross the brook Besor and pursue the Amalekites to victory, recovering their families and goods. Some among them argue against sharing the plunder with those left behind, but David overrules them.
