Abstract written by a model

In 1 Thessalonians, the assembly of the Thessalonians is addressed as a community of believers commended for its faith, love, hope, conversion, endurance, and holy conduct. In Paul’s Second Letter to the Thessalonians, the assembly of the Thessalonians is addressed as a faithful community enduring persecution, urged to stand firm, pray, imitate proper conduct, and admonish the disobedient.

assembly of the Thessalonians in 1 Thessalonians

Paul · AD 50 to 51 · the work’s page · read it where assembly of the Thessalonians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The assembly of the Thessalonians is a community of believers addressed as standing in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, loved and chosen by God. It is remembered by Paul, Silvanus and Timothy for its work of faith, labor of love and perseverance of hope. Its most important features are its conversion and endurance: it turned from idols to serve a living and true God, waits for his Son from heaven, and is appointed to salvation rather than wrath. It received the word amid affliction with the joy of the Holy Spirit, imitated Paul and the Lord, and became an example to believers in Macedonia and Achaia. The records unfold through the letter's chapters. Chapter 1 recalls its faith, imitation and turning to God. Chapter 2 shows it receiving the message as God's word, suffering as the assemblies in Judea did, and being called the authors' hope, joy and crown at the Lord's coming. Chapter 3 tells of Timothy being sent to establish and comfort it, its steadfastness, its good memories of the authors and longing to see them, and prayer that its love would abound. Chapter 4 exhorts it to please God, live in sanctification, lead a quiet life, and praises its love for all the brothers in Macedonia; it is told to comfort one another about those who have fallen asleep and about the Lord's coming. Chapter 5 calls it children of light and of the day, not of darkness, urges watchfulness, faith, love and hope of salvation, respect for leaders and peace, and closes with requests for prayer, greetings and that the letter be read to all the holy brothers.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

1 Thessalonians / Chapter 1

The assembly is addressed as being in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, and is remembered for its work of faith, labor of love, and perseverance of hope. It became imitators of the writers and of the Lord, receiving the word in much affliction with joy of the Holy Spirit, and became an example to all believers in Macedonia and Achaia. Its faith toward God has spread everywhere, and it turned from idols to serve a living and true God and to wait for his Son from heaven.

Chapter 2

The believers received the message as God's word rather than men's, and it works in them. They became imitators of the assemblies in Judea by suffering the same things from their own countrymen. The speakers call them their hope, joy, crown of rejoicing, and glory before the Lord Jesus at his coming.

Chapter 3

The Thessalonians were sent Timothy to be established and comforted, and they were told beforehand that affliction would come. They have remained steadfast in faith and love, remember the authors well, and long to see them. The authors pray that they will increase and abound in love toward one another and all people.

Chapter 4

The brothers are exhorted to abound more and more in pleasing God, to live in sanctification, and to lead a quiet life, doing their own business and working with their own hands. They are praised for loving all the brothers in Macedonia, and are told not to be ignorant about those who have fallen asleep so they don't grieve like those without hope. They are told to comfort one another with the words about the Lord's coming.

Chapter 5

The brothers are called children of light and of the day, not of the night or darkness, and are urged to be watchful and sober, putting on the breastplate of faith and love and the helmet of the hope of salvation. They are told to respect their leaders, live at peace, and follow a list of exhortations. The writer asks for their prayers, tells them to greet one another with a holy kiss, and commands that the letter be read to all the holy brothers.

assembly of the Thessalonians in Paul’s Second Letter to the Thessalonians

Paul · AD 51 to 52 · the work’s page · read it where assembly of the Thessalonians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The assembly of the Thessalonians is a community of believers addressed as being in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, loved by the Lord, chosen by God for salvation, and called through the Good News to obtain Christ's glory. Its faith and love are praised, and it endures persecutions and afflictions with perseverance, suffering for God's Kingdom and promised relief when Jesus is revealed. It is then urged not to be shaken by claims that the day of Christ has come, to stand firm in the traditions taught, and finally to pray for Paul, imitate his work, withdraw from rebellious brothers, not grow weary in doing right, and admonish the disobedient as a brother.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Paul’s Second Letter to the Thessalonians / Chapter 1

The assembly is addressed as being in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, and is praised for faith that grows exceedingly and for abounding love. It endures persecutions and afflictions, is promised relief when the Lord Jesus is revealed, and is prayed for so that it may be counted worthy of its calling and glorify the name of Jesus.

Chapter 2

The brothers are asked not to be quickly shaken or troubled by any spirit, word or letter claiming the day of Christ has come, and are told not to let anyone deceive them. They are described as loved by the Lord and chosen by God from the beginning for salvation. They are told to stand firm and hold the traditions taught by word or letter, and the writer prays that their hearts be comforted and established.

Chapter 3

The brothers are asked to pray for Paul, are credited with doing and continuing to do what is commanded, and are told to withdraw from rebellious brothers and to imitate Paul's example of work. They are urged not to grow weary in doing right and to admonish a disobedient brother as a brother, not as an enemy.
