Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Solomon, idols are lifeless images made by mortal hands that lack bodily senses and lead fools into lust. In The Wisdom of Solomon, idol worship is condemned as deception and devotion to dead images and hateful creatures. In The Book of Jeremiah, idols are lifeless objects made from wood and adorned with precious materials, unable to speak, breathe, or act, and destined to perish. In Baruch, Babylonian idols are powerless images of wood, silver, and gold that cannot move, speak, breathe, or save themselves, and are declared no gods. In The Book of Ezekiel, idols are objects of the sisters' adulterous worship and are associated with child sacrifice and guilt. In The Book of Ezekiel, Israel's idols are objects of adulterous worship that will be broken, while the slain and scattered bones lie among them. In The Book of Hosea, idols are wooden objects consulted and worshiped through sacrifices and incense, leading the people astray. In Habakkuk, idols are mute, breathless images of gold, silver, wood, and stone that teach lies and are condemned as worthless.

Idols are lifeless objects made by mortal hands from earth, lacking eyes, nostrils, ears, fingers, and feet. Enemies of God's people and the nations consider them gods. Their maker surpasses them, having lived while they never did, and their painted forms lead fools into lust.

idols in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · 629 to 579 BC · the work’s page · read it where idols first appears

Abstract written by a model

Idols are lifeless objects: wood cut from the forest, shaped with an ax by skillful goldsmiths, adorned with silver, gold, blue and purple, and nailed down so they cannot move. They cannot speak, breathe, or do good or evil, and are called falsehood and delusion. The text concludes they will perish in the time of their visitation.

Idols are described as wood cut from the forest, worked by an ax, decked with silver from Tarshish and gold from Uphaz, clothed in blue and purple, and fastened with nails so they cannot move. They cannot speak, do evil, or do good, have no breath, and are falsehood and delusion. The text says they will perish in the time of their visitation, as will the gods that did not make the heavens and the earth.

Babylonian idols in Baruch

author unknown · about 299 BC · the work’s page · read it where Babylonian idols first appears

Abstract written by a model

Babylonian idols are lifeless images of wood overlaid with silver and gold, fashioned by carpenters and goldsmiths and housed in temples. The Letter of Jeremiah shows them as powerless: they cannot speak, breathe, move unless carried, or save themselves from war, plague, or thieves. Inferior to sun, moon, and stars, they are declared no gods, so captives should not fear them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

The Letter of Jeremiah

The idols are described as gods of wood overlaid with silver and gold, made by carpenters and goldsmiths, and unable to speak, see, feel, or move without being carried. They cannot save themselves from rust, dust, thieves, war, plague, or fire, cannot bless or curse kings, give rain, or help the widow and the weak. They are compared to scarecrows, beams, and dead bodies, and are declared to be no gods, so the captives should not fear them.
