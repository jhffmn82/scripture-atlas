Elihu

person · 1 work · 19 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite, Elihu the son of Barachel, the Buzite, of the family of Ram, I, my lips, my tongue, speaker (Elihu)

Abstract written by a model

Elihu is a Buzite, son of Barachel of the family of Ram, and the youngest voice in the records: younger than Job's three friends. He waited to speak out of deference to their age, then grew angry at Job for justifying himself rather than God, and at the three friends for condemning Job without finding an answer. He holds that understanding comes from the Spirit of the Almighty, not from age, and that God made him from clay, giving him the same standing before God as Job. He claims sincerity, refuses flattery and partiality, and says he fears his Maker. Across five chapters his speech unfolds. In chapter 32 he explains his delay and resolves to give his opinion. In chapter 33 he addresses Job directly, says God is greater than man, and desires to justify Job or teach him wisdom. In chapter 34 he calls on wise men to judge, argues that God does no wickedness, and accuses Job of speaking without knowledge and multiplying words against God. In chapter 35 he answers Job and his companions, arguing that human righteousness does not affect God and that Job speaks empty words. In chapter 36 he asks patience, claims to speak on God's behalf as one perfect in knowledge, defends God's justice, and ends praising God's power over the weather.

Elihu is in one work on this site, The Book of Job. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Elihu in The Book of Job

author unknown · about 574 BC · the work’s page · read it where Elihu first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 32

Elihu waited to speak because the three friends were older, then became angry when they had no answer for Job. He says he is young but that the Spirit of the Almighty gives understanding, and he resolves to give his own opinion. He claims he is full of words, will speak impartially, and will not flatter anyone, for fear that his Maker would take him away.

Chapter 33

The speaker opens by asking Job to hear his words, saying they come from the uprightness of his heart and are spoken sincerely. He says that the Spirit of God made him and that he is formed out of clay like Job, so his terror and pressure need not weigh on Job. He answers Job's claims by declaring that God is greater than man, describes how God deals with people, and ends by saying he desires to justify Job or else teach him wisdom.

Chapter 34

Elihu calls on the wise men to judge his words and then argues at length that God is just, quoting and rebuking Job's statements. He accuses Job of speaking without knowledge, of adding rebellion to his sin, and of multiplying words against God, and wishes that Job were tried to the end.

Chapter 35

Elihu answers Job and his companions, questioning whether Job thinks it right to claim that his righteousness is more than God's. He argues that human sin and righteousness do not affect God, that God does not hear an empty cry, and that Job opens his mouth with empty talk and words without knowledge.

Chapter 36

Elihu continues speaking, asking for a little more patience because he still has words on God's behalf. He claims his knowledge comes from afar, that his words are not false, and that one perfect in knowledge is with his listener. He argues that God is just, warns his addressee, and ends by praising God's power over the weather.
