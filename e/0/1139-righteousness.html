righteousness

Also called: all righteousness, my justice, my righteousness, my salvation, righteous, righteousness and salvation, salvation

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Isaiah, righteousness is near with salvation, serves as justice and light to the peoples, and endures forever without being abolished. In The Second Book of Nephi, righteousness is everlasting, opposed to wickedness, made possible through law and opposition, and connected with salvation through the Messiah’s intercession and righteousness.

righteousness in The Second Book of Nephi

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where righteousness first appears

Abstract written by a model

Righteousness is everlasting and opposed to wickedness, made possible through law and opposition. Believers in the Messiah may be saved through his intercession and righteousness. The Lord seeks it from Judah, sanctifies himself in it, and assures those who follow it that it will remain forever.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Righteousness is possible because there is law and opposition. Those who believe in the Messiah may be saved through his intercession and righteousness.

Chapter 8

The Lord's righteousness is near and will be forever; it will not be abolished. Those who follow righteousness and know it are told not to fear the reproach of men.

Chapter 15

Righteousness is what the Lord of Hosts looks for from the men of Judah, but a cry is found instead. The Lord of Hosts shall be sanctified in righteousness.
