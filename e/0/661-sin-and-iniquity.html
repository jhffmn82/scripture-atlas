sin and iniquity

Abstract written by a model

In Judith, it leads Israel to destruction in battle, captivity, the razing of its temple, and the taking of its cities. In Psalms, it is a record no one could withstand if the LORD kept it, but from which he promises to redeem Israel. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, it is known to the Lord, who repays people for their deeds and urges them to forsake it. In The Book of Isaiah, it separates the people from God, hides his face, and is answered by repayment according to deeds and a Redeemer for those who turn away. In The Book of Isaiah, it carries away the speakers like the wind, and the LORD has consumed them by means of it. In The Book of Jeremiah, it has grown great and caused the LORD to wound and chastise Israel. In The Lamentations of Jeremiah, it is confessed by the speakers and borne from their fathers, alongside their suffering and fallen crown. In Daniel (Greek), it is Israel's rebellion against God's law, bringing the written curse and great evil upon Jerusalem.

and 1 more work

In The Book of Hosea, it includes divided loyalty, false covenants, multiplying altars, and persistent wrongdoing, yielding judgment.

Israelite sin and its consequences in Judith

author unknown · AD 900 to 1000 · the work’s page · read it where Israelite sin and its consequences first appears

Abstract written by a model

results_in destruction in battle and captivity [when they departed from God's way] Achior teaches that Israel prospered while it did not sin before its God, because God who hates iniquity was with them. When they departed from the way he appointed, they were destroyed in many severe battles, led captive into a land not theirs, their temple was razed, and their cities were taken. He proposes that Holofernes find out whether they now stumble in sin before attacking, and pass by if there is no lawlessness among them.

iniquity and sin in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · 739 to 680 BC · the work’s page · read it where iniquity and sin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Iniquities and sins are said to separate the people from God and hide his face so that he will not hear. They are described as bloody hands, lies, conceived mischief, adders' eggs and spider webs that yield no covering, and the people acknowledge that their transgressions testify against them. The LORD's response includes repaying according to deeds, and a Redeemer for those who turn from disobedience.

Israel's sin in Daniel (Greek)

author unknown · about 164 BC · the work’s page · read it where Israel's sin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Israel's sin is rebellion against God's law: all Israel transgressed, ignored the prophets, and did not turn from iniquity. It brought the written curse and great evil on Jerusalem, and Daniel acknowledges God's righteousness in sending it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

Israel's sin is described as perverse, wicked rebellion: turning aside from God's precepts, not listening to the prophets, and not entreating God's favor or turning from iniquity. Daniel acknowledges it brought the curse written in the law of Moses, and that God was righteous in bringing the evil.
