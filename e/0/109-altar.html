altar of burnt offering in Exodus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where altar of burnt offering first appears

Abstract written by a model

The altar of burnt offering is a square, hollow acacia-wood structure, five cubits by five cubits and three cubits high, overlaid with bronze. It has one-piece horns on its four corners. It was fitted with a bronze grating, rings and bronze-covered carrying poles, plus bronze vessels. Its sockets, grating and vessels came from the offering bronze.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 38

Made of acacia wood, square at five cubits by five cubits and three cubits high, with horns of one piece with it, all overlaid with bronze. It received bronze vessels, a bronze network grating, four rings, and acacia poles overlaid with bronze for carrying, and was made hollow with planks. Its sockets, grating and vessels were made from the offering bronze.

altar in Leviticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where altar first appears

Abstract written by a model

The altar of burnt offering stands at the door of the Tent of Meeting. In Leviticus 1 it receives sprinkled blood, fire and wood, and burnt offerings; a flock animal is killed on its north side, and a bird's crop and feathers are cast beside it on the east, where the ashes go. In chapter 3 Aaron's sons sprinkle offering blood on it and burn fat portions there. In chapter 4 sin offering blood is put on its horns or poured at its base, and the fat is burned on it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Leviticus / Chapter 1

The altar stands at the door of the Tent of Meeting. Blood is sprinkled around on it, fire and wood are laid on it, and the burnt offerings are burned on it. A flock animal is killed on its north side, and the crop and feathers of a bird are cast beside it on the east part, in the place of the ashes.

Chapter 3

The altar is where the blood of the offerings is sprinkled around by Aaron's sons. The fat portions are burned on it, on the burnt offering on the wood on the fire.

Chapter 4

The altar of burnt offering is at the door of the Tent of Meeting. The rest of the blood of each sin offering is poured out at its base, and the blood of the ruler's and common person's offerings is put on its horns. The fat of the sin offerings is burned on it.

altar in Leviticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where altar first appears

Abstract written by a model

The altar is a sacred place of offering. It keeps the burnt offering burning all night, receives the meal offering, and has its ashes set beside it. Moses sanctifies it and its vessels by anointing oil and blood on its horns and base, then burns offerings on it. Aaron later makes offerings there, putting blood on its horns and base, and fire from before the LORD consumes the burnt offering and fat.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

The altar holds the burnt offering on its hearth all night, and the priest removes ashes from it and sets them beside it. The handful of the meal offering is burned there, and the meal offering is presented before it.

Chapter 8

Moses sprinkles anointing oil on the altar seven times and anoints it and its vessels, the basin, and its base to sanctify them. He purifies it with the bull's blood on its horns and at its base, sprinkles ram's blood around it, and burns the fat, the burnt offering ram, and the consecration offerings on it.

Chapter 9

Aaron draws near to the altar to make the offerings. He puts blood on its horns, pours blood at its base, sprinkles blood around it, and burns the fat, burnt offering pieces, and meal offering on it. Fire from before the LORD finally consumes the burnt offering and fat upon it.
