Gilgal

place · 3 works · 5 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: the camp at Gilgal, the camp to Gilgal

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Joshua, Gilgal was where Israel encamped after crossing the Jordan, where Joshua set up twelve memorial stones, and where Passover was kept. In The First Book of Samuel, Gilgal was where the people gathered after Saul, where Saul waited for Samuel, and where Saul offered the burnt offering and was rebuked. In The Book of Amos, Gilgal was named as a place Israel must not seek, enter, or pass through, and it was declared to go into captivity.

Gilgal in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · about 600 BC · the work’s page · read it where Gilgal first appears

Abstract written by a model

Gilgal is a place on the east border of Jericho, in the plains of Jericho, where Israel encamped after crossing the Jordan. Joshua set up twelve river stones there as a memorial for future generations. Passover was kept there. It received its name when the LORD said he had rolled away the reproach of Egypt, a name said to endure.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Gilgal, on the east border of Jericho, is where Israel encamped after coming up out of the Jordan on the tenth day of the first month. Joshua set up there the twelve stones taken from the river. It is the place where the stones serve as a memorial for future generations.

Chapter 5

Gilgal is the place where the Israelites encamp, and it receives its name after the LORD says he has rolled away the reproach of Egypt from them. The name is said to endure to the time of writing.
