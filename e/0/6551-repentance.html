Abstract written by a model

In Surah 3: The Family of Imraan (Aal-i-Imraan), forgiveness is sought from Allah for sins and granted to those who repent, remember Allah, seek forgiveness, and do not knowingly repeat wrongs. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), repentance is accepted for those who do evil in ignorance and then turn quickly to Allah. In Surah 11: Hud, repentance is required from people after they seek Allah’s forgiveness, and Allah is described as Merciful, Loving, Nigh, and Responsive to those who repent. In Surah 25: The Criterion (Al-Furqaan), repentance is accepted from the one who turns away from wrongdoing, believes, and does righteous work. In Surah 26: The Poets (Ash-Shu'araa), repentance follows the hamstringing of the she-camel, while Abraham and the wizards seek forgiveness for sins. In Surah 39: The Groups (Az-Zumar), repentance involves turning to Allah, putting away false gods, and surrendering to Him before doom arrives. In Surah 40: The Forgiver (Al-Ghaafir), repentance is accepted by Allah, and those who repent and follow His way are included in the angels’ prayers for forgiveness and protection from hell. In Surah 40: The Forgiver (Al-Ghaafir), forgiveness is sought for those who believe, repent, and follow Allah’s way, and Muhammad is instructed to ask forgiveness of his sin.

and 8 more works

In Surah 66: The Prohibition (At-Tahrim), sincere repentance is commanded for believers and may lead to remission of evil deeds and entry into Gardens beneath which rivers flow. In The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry, repentance is required to come unto Jesus Christ and brings well-being to repentant Gentiles. In The Second Book of Nephi, repentance is commanded of all men and is necessary for salvation in the kingdom of God. In The Book of Mosiah, repentance is required for remission of sins, salvation through Christ, baptism, and forgiveness. In The Book of Mosiah, forgiveness is freely granted by the Lord to believers who confess and sincerely repent, and people are required to forgive a repentant neighbor. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, repentance is required for salvation, eternal life, mercy, cleansing, being received by God, inheriting the kingdom of heaven, and avoiding destruction and the Lord’s fierce anger. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, repentance is necessary for salvation, inheriting the kingdom of God, avoiding destruction, condemnation, and perishing, and entering the rest of God or the Lord. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, God forgives the people of Anti-Nephi-Lehi for their many sins and murders through the merits of His Son.

repentance in Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where repentance first appears

Abstract written by a model

Repentance is accepted for those who do evil in ignorance and then turn quickly to Allah. It is incumbent on Allah for them. The account contrasts this with those who continue ill-deeds until death attends them and then repent, and with those who die as disbelievers; neither receives the stated forgiveness, and painful doom is prepared for the latter.

Abstract written by a model

Repentance is required for salvation, eternal life, mercy, cleansing, being received by God, inheriting the kingdom of heaven, and avoiding destruction and the Lord’s fierce anger. It is to be declared with the plan of redemption and preached to every people. Alma repeatedly urges people to repent quickly, beginning in this life rather than delaying until death; procrastination leaves the wicked subject to the spirit of the devil. Those who repent, find mercy, and endure to the end are saved, and repentance can bring knowledge of the Lord, birth of God, and joy. Alma, Amulek, the sons of Mosiah, and others preach it throughout the land, including to Ammonihah, the Lamanites, Zoramites, and individuals. Ammonihah rejects repentance and faces anger, while many Zoramites repent but are driven from their land and receive an inheritance in Jershon. Anti-Nephi-Lehi experience sore repentance and fear taking up arms. Alma labors to bring souls to repentance and joy, and a son is commanded to repent, forsake sins, return, and acknowledge his faults.

repentance in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

Abstract written by a model

Repentance is presented as necessary for salvation, inheriting the kingdom of God, avoiding destruction, condemnation, and perishing, and entering the rest of God or the Lord. Through faith in Jesus Christ, repentance can bring redemption, remission of sins, mercy, sanctification, righteous fruit, knowledge of God’s mysteries, release from darkness and the pains of hell, and hope of eternal life. It is commanded and preached by Helaman, Alma, Melchizedek, Aaron, and Ammon. Aaron teaches the king to repent of all sins and call on God in faith, despite an Amalekite’s challenge. The people of Anti-Nephi-Lehi repent of sins and murders, while many Lamanites repent after witnessing a massacre. Repentance is granted in the probationary state, claimed by mercy, and requires sin and punishment; people are urged not to delay it or harden their hearts.
