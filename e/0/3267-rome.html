Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of the Maccabees, Rome receives Jewish envoys, renews friendship and alliance with Jonathan and Simon, and is known as calling the Jews friends, allies and kindred. In The Acts of the Apostles, Rome is where Paul arrives as a prisoner, meets Jewish leaders, and lives two years in a rented house. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, Rome is the city where the letter’s recipients live, and where Paul hopes to preach the Good News.

Rome in The First Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 99 BC · the work’s page · read it where Rome first appears

Abstract written by a model

Rome is a distant power whose senate receives Jewish envoys. Jonathan's envoys secure renewed friendship and alliance, with letters guaranteeing safe conduct. After Jonathan's death, Rome grieves, then renews the pact with Simon, who sends a gold shield to confirm it. Rome is thereby known as calling the Jews friends, allies and kindred.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 12

Jonathan's chosen envoys entered the Roman senate house and asked to renew the friendship and alliance as in former time. The Romans gave them letters to men in every place, requiring safe conduct for them on their way to the land of Judah.

Chapter 14

Rome heard that Jonathan was dead and was exceedingly grieved, then on learning Simon was high priest wrote to renew the friendship and alliance made with Judas and Jonathan. Simon sent Numenius to Rome with a great gold shield weighing one thousand minas to confirm the alliance, and Demetrius heard that Rome called the Jews friends, allies and kindred.
