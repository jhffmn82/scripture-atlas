Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, wealth cannot buy life or redemption, is left to others at death, and cannot be carried away by its owner. In Proverbs, wealth and riches can ransom a man's life yet deceive, while dishonest gain dwindles, riches lack profit in wrath, and sinners' wealth is stored for the righteous. In Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher, riches and wealth are vanity because love of silver never satisfies, goods multiply consumers, hoarded wealth perishes, and nothing is carried away at death. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, riches and poverty come from the Lord; diligence may bring wealth, but goods are left to others at death and the Lord can swiftly enrich the poor. In The Letter from James, riches of oppressors are corrupted, their garments are moth-eaten, and their gold and silver corrode to testify against them and consume them like fire. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), wealth may belong to orphans, be traded by mutual consent, be hoarded, or be spent, with guidance to maintain orphans’ wealth and spend wealth rightly. In Surah 17: The Night Journey (Al-Israa), wealth is to be given in due measure to kinsmen, the needy, and the wayfarer without squandering, and an orphan’s wealth is not to be approached except beneficially. In Surah 64: Mutual Disillusion (At-Taghaabun), wealth is a temptation, and believers are told to spend because spending is better for their souls.

and 11 more works

In Surah 92: The Night (Al-Lail), the righteous give wealth to grow in goodness, while the riches of the hoarding and self-sufficient person cannot save him at death. In Surah 104: The Traducer (Al-Humaza), the slandering traducer gathers and arranges wealth, thinking it will make him immortal. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, riches are sought through false preaching, while church members may become rich without setting their hearts on riches and give liberally to those in need. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, the Zoramites set their hearts on riches and fine goods, including gold, silver, costly apparel, and ornaments, alongside boasting and worship. In The Book of Helaman, riches bring abundance but cause the Nephites and Lamanites to set their hearts on wealth, seek elevation, forget the Lord, and suffer pride, curses, and slipperiness. In The Book of Helaman, treasures hidden in the earth by the righteous are concealed, while others are lost during flight from enemies and the wicked cannot find them because of the land’s curse. In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I, wealth and treasures provoke injury and violence and may be divided among heirs, stolen, seized by the king, destroyed by fire, or otherwise lost. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, wealth acquired through evil deeds is lost and cannot protect a careless person in this world or the next. In Hymns of Guru Nanak, wealth and possessions are impermanent, entangling, and lost at death, while God's name is pure wealth. In Yasna, wealth is sought as prosperity and power connected with waters, Kine, herds, tillers, and Ahura Mazda’s blessings under Sovereign Power. In The Great Learning, wealth is the result and virtue the root; accumulating wealth scatters the people, while distributing it among them gathers them.

wealth in Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where wealth first appears

Abstract written by a model

Wealth is property that may belong to orphans, be exchanged in trade by mutual consent, be hoarded, or be spent. Orphans’ wealth must be maintained, kept separate from guardians’ wealth, and delivered when appropriate; those kept from managing it are still to be fed, clothed, and addressed kindly. Among believers, wealth must not be squandered in vanity, though mutually consenting trade is permitted. Some disbelievers hoard wealth, encourage avarice, conceal what Allah bestowed, or spend it to be seen by people. The records contrast these practices with spending Allah’s wealth aright.
