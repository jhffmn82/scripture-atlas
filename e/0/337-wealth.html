Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, wealth cannot buy life or redemption, is left to others at its owner's death, and cannot be carried away. In Proverbs, wealth and riches can ransom a man's life, deceive, diminish through dishonest gain, lack profit on the day of wrath, and be stored for the righteous. In Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher, riches and wealth are vanity because love of silver never satisfies, goods multiply consumers, hoarded wealth perishes, and nothing is carried away at death. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, riches and poverty come from the Lord, and wealth gained through diligence may soon be left to others at death. In The Letter from James, the riches of oppressors are corrupted, their garments are moth-eaten, and their gold and silver corrode as testimony against them. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), wealth belonging to orphans is maintained separately, delivered appropriately, and not squandered or misused. In Surah 17: The Night Journey (Al-Israa), wealth is given in due measure to kinsmen, the needy, and the wayfarer, without squandering, and an orphan's wealth is approached only beneficially. In Surah 64: Mutual Disillusion (At-Taghaabun), wealth is a temptation, and believers are told to spend because spending is better for their souls.

and 5 more works

In Surah 92: The Night (Al-Lail), wealth is given by the righteous to grow in goodness, while the riches of the hoarding self-sufficient person cannot save him. In Surah 104: The Traducer (Al-Humaza), wealth is gathered and arranged by the slandering traducer, who thinks it will make him immortal. In Hymns of Guru Nanak, wealth and possessions are impermanent, entangling, and ending at death, while God's name is pure wealth. In Yasna, wealth is sought as prosperity that gives power and is granted and preserved under Sovereign Power, linked with herds, tillers, and Ahura Mazda's blessings. In The Great Learning, wealth is the result while virtue is the root, and its accumulation scatters the people rather than constituting the state's true prosperity.

wealth in Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where wealth first appears

Abstract written by a model

Wealth is property that may belong to orphans, be exchanged in trade by mutual consent, be hoarded, or be spent. Orphans’ wealth must be maintained, kept separate from guardians’ wealth, and delivered when appropriate; those kept from managing it are still to be fed, clothed, and addressed kindly. Among believers, wealth must not be squandered in vanity, though mutually consenting trade is permitted. Some disbelievers hoard wealth, encourage avarice, conceal what Allah bestowed, or spend it to be seen by people. The records contrast these practices with spending Allah’s wealth aright.
