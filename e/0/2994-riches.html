riches

Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, riches are portrayed as dangerous and destructive, causing ruin, anxiety, and injustice. In The Good News According to Mark, riches are described as an obstacle to entering God's Kingdom, though all things are possible with God. In 1 Timothy, riches and the love of money are presented as sources of temptation, evil, ruin, and sorrow.

riches in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where riches first appears

Abstract written by a model

Riches are portrayed as dangerous: they destroy many, turn kings' hearts away, and cause wakefulness and anxiety that consume the flesh. Love of gold is a stumbling block leading fools to ruin, leaving its lover unjustified.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 31

Wakefulness from riches consumes the flesh, and anxiety about them takes away sleep. Love of gold is called a stumbling block that has given many over to ruin and that takes every fool, so the one who loves it will not be justified.

Abstract written by a model

The love of money is called a root of all kinds of evil. Those determined to be rich fall into temptation, snares, and harmful lusts leading to ruin; in greed some stray from the faith and pierce themselves with sorrows.
