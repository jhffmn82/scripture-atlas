Abstract written by a model

Discipleship is the topic of what following Jesus demands. Its conditions are self-denial, bearing one's own cross, renouncing all one has, and disregarding family and one's own life. In Chapter 9, Jesus tells followers to deny themselves, take up the cross, and lose their lives to save them, adding that the least is greatest, and answering three would-be followers: no place to lay one's head, no delay for burying a father, no looking back. In Chapter 14, he tells the multitudes these conditions are required, ending with the tasteless salt saying.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

Jesus teaches that anyone who would follow him must deny himself, take up his cross, and lose his life for his sake in order to save it. He teaches that the least is the greatest, tells the disciples not to forbid one who is not against them, and answers three would-be followers, telling them that following him means no place to lay one's head, no delay for burying a father, and no looking back.

Chapter 14

Jesus tells the multitudes that a person cannot be his disciple unless he disregards family and his own life, bears his own cross and comes after him, and renounces all he has. He ends with the saying that salt which has become tasteless is fit for neither soil nor manure pile and is thrown out.
