house of the LORD (the temple Solomon built) in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where house of the LORD (the temple Solomon built) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The house of the LORD is the temple built by Solomon in Jerusalem, called by the LORD's name and built to fulfill his word to David that his son would build it. It measured sixty cubits long, twenty wide and thirty high, with a porch, lattice windows and side rooms on three floors. It was built of stone prepared at the quarry, covered and lined with cedar, and overlaid with gold. What matters most is that it is the place where the LORD puts his name and presence. The account begins with preparations: timber from Hiram and costly worked stones, prepared by Solomon's builders, Hiram's builders and the Gebalites. The foundation was laid in Ziv of the fourth year and the work finished in Bul of the eleventh year. Hiram's bronze work, including the pillars Jachin and Boaz, the molten sea, bases and basins, was installed, along with gold furnishings and the things David dedicated. The ark was placed in the most holy place, a cloud and the LORD's glory filled the house, and Solomon and all Israel dedicated it with sacrifices. The LORD then declared it holy forever, but warned that if Israel turned away he would cast it out and it would become an object of astonishment.

LORD's house in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where LORD's house first appears

Abstract written by a model

The LORD's house in Jerusalem is the place where the LORD put his name, promised to David and Solomon. It held King David's spears and shields. Early on it sheltered Joash for six years, hosted the covenant and his crowning, and was guarded by appointed officers. Later it was repaired with collected money, though its treasures were once sent to Hazael. Manasseh defiled it with altars for the army of the sky and an Asherah image in its courts. During another repair Hilkiah found the book of the law there. It ends in ruin: Nebuzaradan burned it, and the Chaldeans broke up its bronze pillars, sea and bases and carried them and its vessels to Babylon.

LORD's temple in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where LORD's temple first appears

Abstract written by a model

The LORD's temple is the house Solomon built in Jerusalem for the name of the LORD, on Mount Moriah at the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite, where the LORD had appeared to David. Solomon planned it as a great and wonderful house, greater because God is greater than all gods, and gathered workers, craftsmen, timber, and provisions. Its purposes were burning incense, the continual show bread, and burnt offerings on mornings, evenings, Sabbaths, new moons, and set feasts. Its foundation measured sixty by twenty cubits, with a porch across the front. Beams, thresholds, walls, and doors were overlaid with gold and engraved with cherubim, and the larger room had a cypress ceiling overlaid with fine gold, ornamented with palm trees and chains. Precious stones and gold from Parvaim adorned it. A veil of blue, purple, crimson, and fine linen, ornamented with cherubim, marked the most holy place. Furnishings included ten gold lamp stands, ten tables, the sea, basins, and the golden altar; the inner doors were gold, and the courts had bronze-overlaid doors. Treasuries held the dedicated silver, gold, and vessels. When the work was completed from foundation to finish, the ark was installed in the inner sanctuary and a cloud of the LORD's glory filled the house so that the priests could not enter. The king and people dedicated it. Solomon admitted that even heaven cannot contain God, yet asked that God's eyes be open toward it and that prayers of Israelites and foreigners directed there be heard. The LORD said he had chosen and made it holy so his name would be there forever, but warned that if the people turned away he would cast it out of his sight and make it a byword. The later records show that decline. Uzziah entered to burn incense and was struck with leprosy and cut off from the house. Manasseh set altars and an idol in its courts, then after his restoration removed them. Josiah repaired it with money gathered by the Levites, and the book of the law was found there. Finally, under Nebuchadnezzar, its vessels were carried off to Babylon in stages, the people polluted it, and the Chaldeans killed young men in the sanctuary, took its treasures, and burned it. The records end with Cyrus commanded to build a house for the LORD in Jerusalem.

The temple in Jerusalem is where Jesus finds people selling oxen, sheep, and doves and changing money at the Passover, and he drives them out, calling it his Father's house. Jesus says that if this temple is destroyed he will raise it in three days, and the text says he spoke of the temple of his body. The Jews note it took forty-six years to build.
