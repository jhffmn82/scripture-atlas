Abstract written by a model

In Leviticus, the high priest is consecrated, wears sacred garments, follows strict restrictions, and marries only a virgin of his own people. In The Letter to the Hebrews, the high priest is appointed from among men to offer gifts and sacrifices for sins, deals gently with the ignorant and wayward, offers for his own sins, and is called by God. In The Letter to the Hebrews, the high priest (Levitical) enters the Holy of Holies once yearly with blood not his own to offer for himself and the people's errors.

high priest in Leviticus

author unknown · 539 to 331 BC · the work’s page · read it where high priest first appears

Abstract written by a model

The high priest is a consecrated figure whose head is anointed with oil and who wears the sacred garments. He is bound by strict restrictions: no loose hair, torn clothes, contact with dead bodies (even parents), or leaving the sanctuary. He must marry only a virgin of his own people to avoid profaning offspring.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

The high priest, whose head is anointed with oil and who is consecrated to wear the garments, may not let his hair hang loose or tear his clothes. He may not go in to any dead body, even for his father or mother, and may not leave the sanctuary. He must marry a virgin of his own people, not a widow, a divorced woman, a defiled woman, or a prostitute, so that he does not profane his offspring.

high priest in The Letter to the Hebrews

author unknown · about AD 64 to 69 · the work’s page · read it where high priest first appears

Abstract written by a model

The high priest is a man taken from among men and appointed for them in things pertaining to God, offering gifts and sacrifices for sins. Being surrounded by weakness, he deals gently with the ignorant and wayward, and must offer for his own sins too. He does not claim the honor but is called by God, as Aaron was.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Every high priest is taken from among men and appointed for men in things pertaining to God, to offer gifts and sacrifices for sins. Because he is himself surrounded with weakness, he can deal gently with the ignorant and those going astray, and he must offer sacrifices for his own sins as well as the people's. He does not take the honor himself but is called by God, as Aaron was.

high priest (Levitical) in The Letter to the Hebrews

Abstract written by a model

The Levitical high priest alone enters the Holy of Holies once yearly, bringing blood not his own, offering it for himself and the people's errors; his repeated annual entry contrasts with Christ's single offering of himself.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 9

The Levitical high priest alone entered the second tabernacle, once in the year, not without blood, which he offered for himself and for the errors of the people. He enters the holy place year by year with blood not his own. His repeated entry contrasts with Christ's single offering of himself.
