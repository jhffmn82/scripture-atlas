Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, Death shepherds the foolish appointed as a flock for Sheol, while the upright have dominion over them in the morning. In Revelation, Death rides a pale horse, follows Hades, and receives authority over one fourth of the earth to kill by sword, famine, death, and wild animals. In The Table-Talk of the Prophet Mohammad (Part 2), Death is described as a dreadful event that people should not wish for because repentance and good works remain possible. In The Second Book of Nephi, death is the death of body and spirit that passes upon all men, while Christ provides deliverance through resurrection. In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I, death is unavoidable for mortal people, arises in the cycle of delusion and Karman, and is sought as dissolution of the body by the sage who has reached the other side. In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, death repeatedly overtakes all living beings caught in wrong doctrines, passions, and Karman, and ends only through the true Law. In Sutrakritanga, Book II, death ends a being’s current life, after which Karman governs rebirth as a movable or immovable being with an evil or happy lot. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, Death harasses mankind and compels an ascetic who recognises Karman’s cause to adopt the Law immediately.

and 5 more works

In Asa ki War (Asa di Var), death is ordained to take every man after life, regardless of greatness or wealth, though people forget it amid pleasure until old age. In Hymns of Guru Nanak, Death ends embodied life, separates body and soul, reduces the body to dust, overtakes all regardless of status, punishes sin, and is defeated by remembering God’s Name and following the Guru’s teaching. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, Death is opposed by Sun sacrifice, flees Airyaman prayer, is smitten by Asha-Vahista, and is embodied by Angra Mainyu. In Chuang Tzŭ, death is a destiny-governed transformation and the tail of existence, accepted by pure men as dissolution and surpassed by the Perfect Man. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), death sustained in duties may be Heaven’s appointment, while death under handcuffs and fetters cannot be so ascribed.

death in Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where death first appears

Abstract written by a model

Death is an unavoidable, inescapable condition for mortal people, with nothing secure from it. It may come before religious life and is remembered calmly to overcome carelessness and worldly pleasure. It arises within a sequence linked to delusion, conception, hell, animal existence, pain, and repeated birth among those seized by Time and karman. Religious people seek liberation from death, while the sage who has reached the other side desires it as the dissolution of the body.

Death in Hymns of Guru Nanak

Guru Nanak · the work’s page · read it where Death first appears

Abstract written by a model

Death is the universal force that ends embodied life, separating body and soul and reducing the body to dust. It overtakes every person regardless of learning, rank, wealth, ritual, family, authority, or possessions, and it destroys bodies, kings, wealth, and created beings. It acts as a tax-gatherer, binding people according to deeds, punishing sin with its mace, noose, agents, and difficult road. The deceitful and those attached to mammon are led captive to Death’s gate, where regret, blows, and transmigration follow. Those ignorant, attached to worldly desire, lacking the Word, forgetting God, or failing to repeat God’s praises face fear and punishment. Yet Death cannot espy a person whose heart holds God’s true Name. Remembering and praising God’s Name, receiving God’s favour, and following the Guru’s teaching defeat Death and protect the person from its tortures.

Death overtakes man regardless of learning, rank, wealth, ritual, family, or worldly authority. The body returns to dust, and only God's name and God's favour provide deliverance.

death in Chuang Tzŭ

Chuang Tzŭ · the work’s page · read it where death first appears

Abstract written by a model

Death is a destiny-governed transformation and the tail of existence, arriving when a person’s allotted time comes. Once unknown in primeval man’s day, it is portrayed as God cutting down a man suspended in the air and as night or the changing seasons. The ancients held that excessive mourning violates eternal principles; pure men neither loved life nor hated death, accepting dissolution. Death lifts the veil on life’s bounds in immortality, to which the soul reverts, and frees one from mortal troubles, rulers, distinctions, and a huge tumour. In TAO, life and death are one; the Perfect Man passes beyond death’s power. Though life has distinctions, death makes all equal and may leave the dead regretting their attachment to life.

Death comes when it is a person’s time to die and is described as God cutting down a man suspended in the air. The ancients regarded excessive lamentation and sorrow at death as violations of eternal principles.

CHAPTER VI.

Death lifts the veil and reveals that life is bounded at each end by immortality to which the soul finally reverts. The pure men of old neither loved life nor hated death and accepted dissolution as part of their allotted course.

Nan Po Tzŭ K'uei

Death is described as the tail of existence and as a transformation governed by the same law that grants life. In TAO, death and life are one, and death releases the living from a huge tumour.

CHAPTER XVIII.

Death is compared with night and with the natural transformations of the seasons. It is described as free from the sovereigns, subjects, and troubles of mortal life.

CHAPTER XXVII.

Yen Ch'êng Tzŭ Yu said that after nine years of development, life and death existed for him no more. He questioned how death could have an origin while life had none and observed that life has distinctions but death makes all equal.
