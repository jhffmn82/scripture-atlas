soul

topic · 1 work · 10 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: a soul, his soul, immortal soul, it, some soul, the mind, the soul, this soul

Abstract written by a model

The soul is an unseen power from God that uses and quickens the physical body, producing life and bringing emotions, bodily functions, and God-given talents into play. Its earthly mandate ends as the mortal body is exhausted, so it should be nourished rather than worn out in mortal pursuits. Properly nourished, it may become immortal and return beatified to the Great Unknown. The immortal soul endures life and death and survives the collapse of Heaven and Earth without losing its constitution, though it can change other things. Valued by Sages above money, fame, and resolution, it returns after its brief earthly career to eternity.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

The soul is presented as the possible power that brings emotions and bodily functions into play, although its form cannot be seen. Its mandate ends with the exhaustion of the mortal body.

CHAPTER III.

The soul is what informs life and should be nourished rather than worn out with the body in mortal pursuits. If properly nourished, it may become immortal and return beatified to the Great Unknown.

CHAPTER V.

The immortal soul is described as enduring when life and death pass away and when Heaven and Earth collapse. It can cause other things to change while preserving its own constitution intact.

CHAPTER XV.

The soul is valued by Sages above money, fame, and resolution. It returns after its brief career on earth to the eternity from which it came.

CHAPTER XIX.

The soul is described as coming from God and as the principle that quickens the body. United with the body, it constitutes life.
