Abstract written by a model

In Leviticus, offerings and bread of God have the status of most holy. In Leviticus, offerings are in addition to Sabbaths, gifts, vows, and free will offerings. In Numbers, offerings by fire produce a pleasant aroma to the LORD. In Deuteronomy, offerings and sacrifices must be eaten at the chosen place of worship. In The Book of Micah, offerings and sacrifices are presented as unable to win the LORD's favor.

offerings by fire (burnt offerings, sacrifices) in Numbers

author unknown · 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where offerings by fire (burnt offerings, sacrifices) first appears

Abstract written by a model

produces pleasant aroma to the LORD Offerings of the herd or flock, made as burnt offerings, sacrifices, vows, free will offerings, or at set feasts, must be accompanied by fine flour mixed with oil and a wine drink offering. The amounts scale by animal: one tenth of an ephah of flour with a fourth of a hin of oil and wine for a lamb, two tenths with a third of a hin for a ram, and three tenths with half a hin for a bull. They are described as a pleasant aroma to the LORD.
