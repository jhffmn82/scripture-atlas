Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Canaan is inhabited by Canaanites and Perizzites, entered and traveled through by Abram, later afflicted by famine, and the place from which Jacob's family journeys toward Joseph. In Genesis, the land is the territory promised to Abram's offspring from the river of Egypt to the Euphrates, with possession delayed until the Amorites' iniquity is full. In Genesis, the land promised to Jacob is the land on which he lies and travels, pledged to him and his offspring and promised as their inheritance. In Exodus, Canaan is the good, large land flowing with milk and honey promised to Israel, inhabited by nations that are to be driven out. In Leviticus, the land of Canaan is brought to Israel after being defiled by other nations, but it will vomit out Israel if they likewise defile it. In Leviticus, the land responds to Israel's conduct with abundance or desolation, rests during exile, and is remembered by the LORD. In Leviticus, the land is the LORD's possession given to Israel to inherit, flowing with milk and honey but capable of vomiting out those who disobey. In Numbers, Canaan is the promised inheritance flowing with milk and honey, rejected by Israel's adults but to be entered by the younger generation, with its borders later established.

Canaan in Genesis

author unknown · about 449 BC · the work’s page · read it where Canaan first appears

Abstract written by a model

Canaan is a land inhabited by Canaanites and Perizzites. Abram, Sarai, and Lot enter it, and Abram travels through Shechem, Bethel, and toward the South. After Lot departs, the LORD promises Abram and his offspring all the land he sees. Later, famine strikes Canaan, afflicting Jacob's family and sending the brothers to buy food. Finally, the brothers return with Pharaoh's wagons and provisions, telling Jacob that Joseph is alive, and Jacob resolves to go see him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 12

Abram, Sarai, and Lot enter the land of Canaan, where Canaanites are living at that time. Abram passes through to Shechem and the oak of Moreh, then to the mountain east of Bethel, and travels on toward the South.

Chapter 13

Abram lives in the land of Canaan after Lot separates from him and goes to the cities of the plain. The Canaanites and Perizzites are said to be living in the land at that time. The LORD promises Abram and his offspring all the land he can see, and tells him to walk through it in its length and width.

Chapter 42

Canaan is the land where the famine afflicts Jacob's family and from which the brothers travel to buy food. Jacob and Benjamin remain there, and the brothers return to report what happened.

Chapter 45

Canaan is the land where Jacob lives and to which the brothers travel with Pharaoh's wagons and Joseph's provisions. There they tell Jacob that Joseph is alive, and Jacob decides to go see him.

Abstract written by a model

Canaan is the promised land lying across the Jordan, in the land of the Canaanites, which the LORD swore to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob and gives to Israel as a possession and inheritance. It is described as a good land flowing with milk and honey, with hills and valleys that drink rain from the sky. Its borders run from the wilderness and Lebanon to the Euphrates in the east and to the western sea. It holds great and good cities, houses, cisterns, vineyards, and olive trees that Israel did not build or plant, along with brooks, springs, and underground water. It produces wheat, barley, vines, figs, pomegranates, olives, and honey, and has stones of iron and hills yielding copper, so nothing is lacking. The records unfold as a progression of promise, conditions, and warning. Early on, the land is set before Israel, but the unbelieving generation will not see it except Caleb, while Joshua and the little ones will enter. Moses will see it from Mount Nebo but not enter. Israel is to possess it by driving out nations greater and mightier, gradually, and this is due to the nations' wickedness and the LORD's oath, not Israel's righteousness. The land is the place to keep the commandments; long life there depends on faithfulness, honoring parents, and loving and obeying the LORD. Israel must not forget the LORD when full, and idolatry would mean removal. It is the site of the blessing on Mount Gerizim and the curse on Mount Ebal near Gilgal. The worshipper offers its first fruit and confesses the LORD's gift. Obedience brings blessing in barns and work, while disobedience turns the sky to bronze and earth to iron, with produce eaten by locusts and worms, and the people plucked from it. Finally, the scattered Israelites are promised return to possess it again, and Israel is told obeying the law will prolong their days there.

Its border is described as running from the wilderness and Lebanon to the Euphrates, including all the land of the Hittites, and to the great sea in the west. Joshua is given every place the sole of his foot will tread.
