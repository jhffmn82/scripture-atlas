Shun

person · 10 works · 71 quoted facts · 26 connections
Also called: Shuns, an individual, great Shun, him, his, sovereign, the sage Shun

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book VIII (T'ai-po), Shun governs the empire well with five ministers and holds it with majestic detachment. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XV (Wei Ling Kung), Shun governs efficiently without exertion while reverently occupying the royal seat. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XX (Yao Yueh), Shun receives Yao’s succession and charge, holds fast the Mean, and passes the charge to Yu. In The Doctrine of the Mean, Shun is a greatly wise, filial sage who studies others and employs the Mean in governing the people. In Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou), Shun is an emperor who delights in goodness, learns from others, and follows their virtuous ways. In Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong), Shun is a sage exemplar who receives the kingdom from Yao, regulates disorder, teaches human relations, and seeks able ministers. In Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou), Shun is an ancient king and exemplary minister who values his parents’ accord above the kingdom and practices sage principles. In Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang), Shun is a filial son who becomes sovereign, mourns his parents’ lack of accord, marries without informing them, and welcomes Xiang.

and 2 more works

In Mencius, Book VI (Gaozi), Shun is a perfectly filial man who rises from the channelled fields to prominence. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), Shun is a sovereign guided by benevolence and righteousness who responds to goodness and would abandon the kingdom to care for Gu Sou.

Abstract written by a model

Shun is a greatly wise, filial sage whose central practice is questioning others, studying their words, concealing bad qualities, displaying good ones, and determining and employing the Mean in governing the people. Shun in Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou)

Mencius · the work’s page · read it where Shun first appears

Abstract written by a model

Shun is an ancient king, born in Zhu Feng, removed to Fu Xia, and dying in Ming Tiao. He serves Yao with exemplary respect and is presented as a model for ministers. He marries without telling his parents, fearing no posterity, an act superior men regard as equivalent to informing them. Though the kingdom joyfully submits to him, he values it little beside gaining his parents’ hearts and complete accord with them. He fully serves Gu Sou until Gu Sou delights in good, carries sage principles into practice in the Middle Kingdom, and is the same as other men.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Shun is presented as the model whom a minister should imitate. Shun served Yao in a way that demonstrates respect for a sovereign.

Chapter 26

Shun married without informing his parents because he feared having no posterity. Superior men consider his action equivalent to informing them.

Chapter 28

Shun regarded gaining the whole kingdom’s joyful submission as no more than a bundle of grass. He believed that a person could not be considered a man without gaining his parents’ hearts or a son without reaching complete accord with them, and he fully served Gu Sou until Gu Sou found delight in what was good.

Shun in Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang)

Mencius · the work’s page · read it where Shun first appears

Abstract written by a model

Shun is a filial son who assists Yao for twenty-eight years, receives Heaven’s mandate and the people’s approval, and becomes sovereign of the Middle Kingdom. Despite beauty, wealth, honours, and acceptance, he weeps over lacking his parents’ accord. He marries without informing them, and sincerely welcomes Xiang despite Xiang’s attempt to kill him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang) / Part I / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 7

Shun wept because, despite receiving beauty, wealth, honours, and the kingdom’s approval, he lacked the accord of his parents. He married without informing his parents because informing them would have prevented the marriage, and he sincerely welcomed Xiang despite knowing Xiang had sought to kill him. Shun in Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin)

Mencius · the work’s page · read it where Shun first appears

Abstract written by a model

Shun is a sovereign whose natural benevolence and righteousness guide a life of earnest attention to virtue and what is important. In retirement he lives amid mountains, trees, rocks, deer, and swine, seeming little different from remote-hill inhabitants, yet responds irresistibly to even one good word or action. His wisdom does not extend to everything, but he concentrates on what matters. Faced with Gu Sou’s apprehension, he would not interfere; he would abandon the kingdom like a worn-out sandal, carry Gu Sou into concealment by the sea, forget the kingdom, and live there cheerfully for life.
