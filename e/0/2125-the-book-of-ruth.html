This document is the biblical Book of Ruth, a four-chapter narrative set in the days of the judges, centered on Naomi, her Moabite daughter-in-law Ruth, and Boaz, a wealthy relative of Naomi's late husband Elimelech. During a famine Elimelech takes his wife Naomi and sons Mahlon and Chilion from Bethlehem to Moab. There Elimelech dies, the sons marry Orpah and Ruth, and after about ten years both sons die. Naomi decides to return to Judah; Orpah turns back, but Ruth clings to her and vows that Naomi's people and God will be hers. In Bethlehem at the start of barley harvest, Naomi asks to be called Mara, reflecting her bitterness. Ruth gleans in the field of Boaz, who praises her loyalty, protects her, feeds her, and has extra grain left for her. Naomi blesses him and names him a near kinsman. Following Naomi's instructions, Ruth goes to the threshing floor at night and asks Boaz to spread his garment over her. Boaz blesses her, calls her a worthy woman, and says a nearer kinsman exists, but promises to act himself if that man will not. He sends her home before dawn with six measures of barley. At the city gate, before ten elders, Boaz offers the nearer kinsman Elimelech's land together with Ruth. The man first agrees, then declines for fear of harming his own inheritance, and confirms it by removing his sandal. Boaz declares he has bought all that belonged to Elimelech, Chilion, and Mahlon, and has purchased Ruth as his wife. The people bless him. Ruth bears a son, the women praise the LORD with Naomi, who nurses the child, and he is named Obed, father of Jesse and grandfather of David. The text closes with the generations from Perez to David.

Chapter 1

During a famine in the days of the judges, Elimelech took his wife Naomi and their two sons, Mahlon and Chilion, from Bethlehem Judah to Moab. Elimelech died, the sons married Orpah and Ruth, and after about ten years both sons died, leaving Naomi bereaved. Hearing that the LORD had given his people bread, Naomi set out to return to Judah and urged her daughters-in-law to go back to their mothers' houses. Orpah kissed her and went back, but Ruth clung to Naomi and vowed that Naomi's people and God would be hers. The two arrived in Bethlehem at the start of barley harvest, where Naomi asked to be called Mara because of the bitterness the Almighty had brought her.

Chapter 2

Ruth the Moabitess goes to glean in the fields and happens upon the portion belonging to Boaz, a wealthy relative of Naomi's husband from the family of Elimelech. Boaz learns who she is, praises her loyalty to her mother-in-law, tells her to stay close to his maidens, and orders his young men not to touch or rebuke her. He feeds her at mealtime and instructs his workers to pull out extra grain for her. Ruth gleans until evening, gathers about an ephah of barley, and brings it and leftover food to Naomi. Naomi blesses Boaz, identifies him as a near kinsman, and Ruth stays with Boaz's maidens through the barley and wheat harvests while living with Naomi.

Chapter 3

Naomi, Ruth's mother-in-law, tells Ruth to wash, anoint herself, dress, and go to the threshing floor where their kinsman Boaz will be winnowing barley, then to lie at his feet once he has eaten and drunk. Ruth does all that Naomi says, and at midnight Boaz is startled to find her there; she identifies herself and asks him to spread his garment over her because he is a near kinsman. Boaz blesses her, tells her not to be afraid, and says the city knows she is a worthy woman, but he explains that a kinsman nearer than he exists. He promises that if that kinsman will not perform the kinsman's duty, he himself will, and he lets her stay until morning, then sends her away before she can be recognized, giving her six measures of barley. Ruth returns to Naomi and reports everything, and Naomi tells her to wait, since Boaz will not rest until he has settled the matter that day.

Chapter 4

Boaz goes to the city gate, calls the near kinsman over, and gathers ten elders as witnesses. He tells the kinsman that Naomi is selling Elimelech's parcel of land and that Ruth the Moabitess must also be acquired with it to raise up the name of the dead. The kinsman at first agrees to redeem the land but then declines, fearing he would endanger his own inheritance, and confirms the transfer by taking off his sandal. Boaz declares before the elders and people that he has bought all that belonged to Elimelech, Chilion and Mahlon and has purchased Ruth to be his wife. The people bless him, Ruth conceives and bears a son, the women praise the LORD with Naomi, who nurses the child, and he is named Obed, father of Jesse and grandfather of David, followed by the generations from Perez to David.
