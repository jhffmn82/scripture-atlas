Jonathan

Abstract written by a model

Jonathan is a man who rises from prince and leader of the Jews to high priest, governor and chief friend of the king, and who dies as a captive. He is chosen by the friends of Judas after Judas's death. He flees Bacchides, avenges his brother John, fortifies Bethbasi, then makes peace, settles at Michmash and judges the people, destroying the ungodly. Courted by rival kings, he accepts Alexander's appointment as high priest, wears the holy garments, and rebuilds Jerusalem's walls and Mount Zion. He captures Joppa, wins at Azotus, burns Dagon's temple, and receives Ekron and a gold buckle. Demetrius and later Antiochus confirm his priesthood and grant exemptions, golden vessels and purple clothing. He besieges the Jerusalem citadel, takes Gaza's hostages, and at Hazor turns a fleeing army into a rout after tearing his clothes and praying. He seeks friendship with Rome and claims kinship with the Spartans. He strengthens Jerusalem's walls and isolates the citadel. Trusting Tryphon at Bethshan, he goes to Ptolemais with a thousand men and is seized there. Tryphon holds him on a claim of debt, then kills him near Bascama after being paid. Israel mourns. His brother Simon later moves his bones to Modin and buries him there.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

Jonathan was chosen by Judas's friends as prince and leader after Judas died. He fled Bacchides, avenged his brother John on the children of Jambri, swam the Jordan to escape, and fortified Bethbasi. He then made peace with Bacchides, settled at Michmash, began to judge the people, and destroyed the ungodly out of Israel.

Chapter 10

Jonathan received Demetrius's letter, gained the hostages, and rebuilt Jerusalem's walls and Mount Zion. He accepted Alexander's appointment as high priest, wore the holy garments at the feast of tabernacles, rejected Demetrius's later offers, and was honored by Alexander at Ptolemais. He later took Joppa, won at Azotus, burned Dagon's temple, and received a gold buckle and Ekron.

Chapter 11

Jonathan met Ptolemy at Joppa, besieged the citadel at Jerusalem, and won Demetrius' favor at Ptolemais with gifts, gaining confirmation of the high priesthood and a charter of exemptions. He sent three thousand men to save Demetrius, was mistreated by him afterward, and received honors from the young Antiochus. He took Gaza's hostages, and at Hazor, after his army fled, he tore his clothes, prayed, and turned the battle to a rout of the foreigners before returning to Jerusalem.

Chapter 12

Jonathan the high priest sent envoys to Rome and letters to the Spartans to renew friendship, and pursued Demetrius' princes, struck the Zabadaean Arabs, and traveled through Damascus. He had Jerusalem's walls strengthened and the citadel isolated, then trusted Tryphon at Bethshan, sent most of his forces home, and went to Ptolemais with a thousand men. There he was seized, and all of Israel mourned him.

Chapter 13

Jonathan, brother of Simon, is taken along under guard by Tryphon and is detained on the claim that he owes money to the king's treasury. Tryphon kills him near Bascama after being paid, and he is buried there. Simon later retrieves his bones and buries him at Modin, and all Israel mourns him many days.
