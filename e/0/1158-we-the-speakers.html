we (the speakers)

Abstract written by a model

The speakers are a confessing people who describe themselves as sheep gone astray, each turning to his own way, unclean, long in sin, and with no one calling on the LORD's name. They also claim to be the LORD's people and the work of his hand. In Chapter 53 they recognize that the servant, once despised, bore their sickness and transgressions, bringing them peace and healing. In Chapter 64 they confess their polluted righteousness, fading like leaves, and appeal to the LORD as Father, asking whether they will be saved.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 53

The speakers ask who has believed their message, admit they did not desire or respect the servant, and confess they considered him plagued and struck by God. They come to see that he bore their sickness and was pierced for their transgressions, and that they, like sheep, had all gone astray, each turning to his own way. Through his punishment and wounds they receive peace and healing.

Chapter 64

The speakers confess that they sinned and have been in sin a long time, that they are all like one unclean, and that their righteousness is like a polluted garment. They fade like a leaf and are carried off by their iniquities, and none calls on God's name or stirs himself to take hold of him. They appeal to the LORD as their Father, call themselves clay and the work of his hand and his people, and ask whether they shall be saved.
