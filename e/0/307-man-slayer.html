man slayer

Also called: every man slayer, him who has fled to his city of refuge, man slayer (unintentional killer), the man slayer, the striker, whoever kills any person unintentionally, whoever kills his neighbor unintentionally

Abstract written by a model

In Numbers, the man slayer is someone who kills a person unwittingly and may flee to a city of refuge until the high priest dies. In Deuteronomy, the man slayer unintentionally kills his neighbor without prior hatred and may flee to a city for protection from pursuit. In The Book of Joshua, the man slayer is a person who kills accidentally or unintentionally and may dwell in a city of refuge until judgment and the high priest’s death.

man slayer in Numbers

author unknown · 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where man slayer first appears

Abstract written by a model

The man slayer is someone who kills a person unwittingly and may flee to a city of refuge to avoid dying before judgment. If the congregation finds no intent, he must stay there until the high priest dies; outside its border the avenger may kill him without guilt. Afterward he may return to his land.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 35

The man slayer is one who kills a person unwittingly and may flee to a city of refuge so as not to die before judgment. If the congregation finds him innocent of intent, he lives in the city until the high priest dies, and if he leaves its border and is killed by the avenger, the avenger is not guilty. After the high priest's death he may return to his own land.

man slayer (unintentional killer) in Deuteronomy

author unknown · 624 BC · the work’s page · read it where man slayer (unintentional killer) first appears

Abstract written by a model

A man slayer unintentionally kills his neighbor without prior hatred, like an ax head slipping from its handle. Since he is not worthy of death, he may flee to one of the cities and live, protected from pursuit.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 19

The man slayer who kills his neighbor unintentionally, having not hated him before, may flee to one of the cities and live, as in the example of an ax head slipping from its handle while chopping wood. This protects him from being pursued and killed though he is not worthy of death.

Abstract written by a model

A man slayer is a person who kills accidentally or unintentionally. He may flee to a city of refuge, declare his case to the elders at the gate, and receive a place to live. He dwells there until he stands before the congregation for judgment and the high priest dies, then returns to his own city and house.
