Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the Ten Commandments are God’s commandments governing duties toward God and neighbors. In Deuteronomy, the Ten Commandments are the LORD’s words spoken to Israel, written on stone tablets, and given to Moses.

Ten Commandments in Deuteronomy

author unknown · 624 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ten Commandments first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Ten Commandments are words the LORD spoke to the whole assembly of Israel from the fire on the mountain, written on two stone tablets and given to Moses. They forbid other gods, images, misusing the LORD's name, murder, adultery, stealing, false testimony and coveting, and require Sabbath observance and honoring parents. They are recited first, then rewritten on new tablets matching the first.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

The words are recited here: no other gods, no carved image, no bowing to or serving images, no misuse of the LORD's name, Sabbath observance, honoring father and mother, and not murdering, committing adultery, stealing, giving false testimony, or coveting. The LORD spoke them to the whole assembly with a great voice, added no more, and wrote them on two stone tablets given to Moses.

Chapter 10

The LORD wrote the ten commandments on the new tablets, according to the first writing. They are the words the LORD spoke to Israel from the middle of the fire on the mountain on the day of the assembly, and the LORD gave them to Moses.
