Ishmael the son of Nethaniah in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · 629 to 579 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ishmael the son of Nethaniah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ishmael, son of Nethaniah, is a royal descendant and chief officer who appears among the captains coming to Gedaliah at Mizpah. Johanan warns that Baalis of Ammon sent him to kill Gedaliah, but Gedaliah disbelieves it. Ishmael then arrives with ten men, eats with Gedaliah, and murders him, along with others at Mizpah. He deceives and kills eighty pilgrims and takes captives toward Ammon. Johanan overtakes him at Gibeon, frees the captives, and Ishmael escapes with eight men.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 40

Ishmael is among the captains who come to Gedaliah at Mizpah. Johanan alleges that Baalis, king of Ammon, has sent him to take Gedaliah's life, but Gedaliah rejects the claim as false.

Chapter 41

Ishmael, a royal descendant and chief officer of the king, came to Mizpah with ten men, ate with Gedaliah, and then killed him and the Jews and Chaldean soldiers there. He later deceived and killed eighty pilgrims, spared ten for their hidden stores, and took the remaining people of Mizpah captive toward the Ammonites. Johanan overtook him at Gibeon, the captives were taken back, and Ishmael escaped with eight men to the Ammonites.
