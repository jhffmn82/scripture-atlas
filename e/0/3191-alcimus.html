Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of the Maccabees, Alcimus sought and held the high priesthood, accused Judas, betrayed the Hasidaeans, ordered the sanctuary wall demolished, and died in torment. In The Second Book of the Maccabees, Alcimus opposed Judas Maccabaeus, accused him and the Hasidaeans of sedition, sought the high priesthood, and falsely accused Nicanor.

Alcimus in The First Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Alcimus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Alcimus is an ungodly man who sought the high priesthood and led lawless Israelites in accusing Judas to the king. Demetrius appointed him high priest and sent him with Bacchides. He swore peace to the Hasidaeans, yet sixty of them were killed. Struggling to keep his office, he returned to the king with evil accusations, prompting Nicanor's mission. Later he ordered the sanctuary's inner court wall pulled down, was struck with palsy, lost speech, and died in torment.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

Alcimus led the lawless men of Israel who accused Judas to the king, desiring to be high priest, and Demetrius made him high priest and sent him with Bacchides. He swore peace to the Hasidaeans, after which sixty of them were killed, and Bacchides left him in charge of the country. He struggled to maintain his priesthood, and when he saw Judas had grown strong he returned to the king with evil accusations, which led to Nicanor's mission.

Chapter 9

Alcimus was sent with Bacchides into Judah and in the one hundred fifty-third year ordered the wall of the sanctuary's inner court pulled down, along with the works of the prophets. He was struck with palsy, lost the ability to speak, and died in great torment.

Alcimus in The Second Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Alcimus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Alcimus, a former high priest called wicked, opposes Judas Maccabaeus. He brings Demetrius gifts and accuses Judas and the Hasidaeans of sedition, is to be installed as high priest, then falsely claims Nicanor is disloyal for naming Judas successor.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

Alcimus, a former high priest who had willfully polluted himself, brings Demetrius a gold crown, a palm and olive boughs and accuses Judas and the Hasidaeans of sedition. He is to be set up as high priest, and he later falsely tells Demetrius that Nicanor is disloyal for naming Judas his successor, using the covenants as evidence. The text calls him a most wicked man.
