Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, they are idols and images forbidden by the Ten Commandments, including gods of silver or gold. In Deuteronomy, they are carved images and objects of idolatrous worship that bring destruction, scattering, and exile. In The Second Book of Kings, they are involved in forbidden idolatry and high-place worship, including pillars, Asherah poles, and child sacrifice. In The Book of Isaiah, they are shown to be nothing, unable to declare events, do good or evil, answer, save, or speak. In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), they are false deities sought as judges by those who pretend to believe in revelation. In Surah 39: The Groups (Az-Zumar), they are rejected as powerless and unintelligent, unable to remove hurt or restrain mercy. In Surah 71: Noah (Nooh), they are gods that Noah's people are told not to forsake, including Wadd, Suwa', Yaghuth, Ya'uq, and Nasr.

idolatry and high-place worship in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where idolatry and high-place worship first appears

Abstract written by a model

Idolatry and high-place worship, forbidden by the LORD, involves pillars and Asherah poles on every hill and under every tree. Israel practiced it widely, including child sacrifice, which the text gives as the reason for its exile; foreign settlers then repeat it while fearing the LORD.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 17

Israel built high places in all its cities, set up pillars and Asherah poles, burned incense, served idols, made molten calves, worshiped the sky's army and Baal, and sacrificed children, all of which the text gives as the reason for its exile. The foreign settlers repeat the pattern by placing their own gods in the high-place houses and appointing priests for them, while also fearing the LORD.

idols and false gods in The Book of Isaiah

Abstract written by a model

Idols and false gods are shown to be nothing: in Chapter 41 the LORD challenges them to declare former and coming things or do good or evil. They cannot answer, save, or speak, so they and their works, wind and confusion, are pronounced nothing.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 41

The LORD challenges the idols to produce their cause, declare former things and things to come, and do good or evil so that they can be recognized as gods. He pronounces them and their work nothing, and whoever chooses them an abomination. No one among them declares, shows, hears or answers, and their molten images are wind and confusion.
