forgiveness

topic · 3 works · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Forgive your neighbor the hurt that he has done, confirm your love toward him, debts, forgave, forgive, forgive us our debts, forgive your brother from your hearts, forgiven, overlook ignorance, trespasses

Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, forgiving a neighbor's hurt is the condition for having one's own sins pardoned when praying. In The Gospel According to Matthew, forgiveness is reciprocal: people forgive debtors as they ask forgiveness, and the Father forgives those who forgive others while withholding forgiveness from those who do not. In The Gospel According to Matthew, Jesus requires disciples to forgive from the heart, seventy times seven, showing mercy to others because they have been forgiven. In 2 Corinthians, Paul urges the recipients to forgive, comfort, and reaffirm their love for the offender, and says he forgives for their sake in Christ's presence.

Chapter 6

Forgiveness appears in the prayer, 'Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.' The text then states that if people forgive others their trespasses the heavenly Father will forgive them, but if they do not, neither will the Father forgive their trespasses.
