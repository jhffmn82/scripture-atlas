forgiveness of sins

topic · 4 works · 5 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Your sins are forgiven, Your sins are forgiven you, covered, forgave, forgive sins, forgiven, forgiveness, forgives all your sins, iniquities, removed our transgressions from us, sins, your sins are forgiven you

Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, forgiveness of sins is shown by the LORD forgiving all sins, not dealing with people according to their sins or repaying their iniquities, and removing transgressions as far as the east is from the west. In Psalms, forgiveness is said to be with the LORD, explaining why the LORD is feared and answering the possibility of the LORD keeping a record of sins. In Psalms, forgiveness is presented as the source of blessing for the one whose disobedience is forgiven and whose sin is covered; David experienced it when he confessed his transgressions and the LORD forgave the iniquity of his sin. In The Gospel According to Matthew, forgiveness of sins is declared by Jesus for the paralytic, and Jesus says the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins, leading the multitudes to glorify God for giving such authority to men. In The Good News According to Mark, forgiveness of sins is attributed to God according to the scribes, while Jesus says the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins when he heals the paralytic. In The Gospel According to Luke, forgiveness of sins is regarded by the Pharisees as belonging to God alone; Jesus forgives a paralyzed man and heals him to show the Son of Man's authority on earth to forgive, and links forgiveness to love through the two debtors parable before forgiving the woman.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Jesus declares the paralyzed man's sins forgiven, and the scribes and Pharisees object that only God can forgive sins. Jesus heals the man so they may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins.

Chapter 7

Jesus teaches through the parable of the two debtors that the one forgiven more loves more, and applies it to the woman, saying her many sins are forgiven because she loved much while one forgiven little loves little. He tells her directly that her sins are forgiven, and those at the table ask among themselves who this is who even forgives sins.
