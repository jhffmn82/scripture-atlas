Also called: Every one, The people, men, the people

Abstract written by a model

The people are portrayed as living ordinary lives yet thoughtlessly indulging in them, without fearing death or what they ought to fear. They are warned that this failure will bring their great dread and are urged not to grow weary of the conditions sustaining life. Though they know that softness overcomes hardness and weakness strength, they cannot put this into practice. Their condition also worsens as prohibitive enactments multiply, making them poor. Under the sage’s purposeless action, stillness, lack of trouble, and lack of ambition, they are said to become correct, rich, and primitively simple.

57. 1. A state may be ruled by (measures of) correction; weapons of

The people become poor as prohibitive enactments multiply. According to the sage, they will become correct, rich, and attain primitive simplicity when he does nothing of purpose, keeps still, takes no trouble, and manifests no ambition.

72. 1. When the people do not fear what they ought to fear, that which

The people are warned that if they do not fear what they ought to fear, their great dread will come upon them. They are urged not to indulge thoughtlessly in their ordinary life or act as though weary of what that life depends on.

74. 1. The people do not fear death; to what purpose is it to (try to)

The people do not fear death. The passage suggests that if they were always in awe of death, they might be deterred from doing wrong.
