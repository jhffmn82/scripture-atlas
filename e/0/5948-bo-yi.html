Bo Yi

person · 4 works · 22 quoted facts · 4 connections

Abstract written by a model

In Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou), Bo Yi serves only a ruler he esteems, takes office under good government, refuses bad princes and unapproved gifts, avoids a bad prince’s court, and is described as narrow-minded. In Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou), Bo Yi avoids Zhou on the northern sea coast, then follows king Wen after hearing of his rise and that he nourishes the old. In Mencius, Book V (Wan Zhang), Bo Yi avoids bad sights and sounds, serves only approved princes, commands only esteemed people, takes office in good government, retires in confusion, and reforms the corrupt and strengthens the weak by the North sea during Zhou. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), Bo Yi avoids Zhou on the northern sea coast, then follows king Wen after hearing of his rise and his care for the old.

Bo Yi in Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou)

Abstract written by a model

Bo Yi serves only a ruler he esteems and takes office under good government. He refuses unapproved or bad princes, unesteemed or bad men, and gifts that conflict with his purity; he avoids a bad prince’s court. He is described as narrow-minded.

Chapter 9

Bo Yi would not serve a prince he did not approve of or associate with a friend he did not esteem. He refused bad princes, bad men, and gifts from princes when accepting them would conflict with his purity.
