Chapter 22

Friendship is dissolved by insults, as a stone cast at birds scares them away, and insults precede bloodshed as smoke precedes fire. Reconciliation remains possible after a sword drawn or words spoken against a friend, but not after insult, arrogance, disclosure of a secret, or a treacherous blow. Faithfulness to a neighbor in poverty and affliction is urged so that one may share his gladness and inheritance in prosperity.
