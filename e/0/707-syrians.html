Syrians

group · 3 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Syria, the Syrians, the Syrians who were beyond the River, the king of Syria

Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Samuel, the Syrians were hired by the Ammonites, fled before Joab, regrouped under Hadadezer, were defeated by David, and afterward were afraid to help the Ammonites. In The First Book of Kings, the Syrians mistakenly believed the LORD was only a god of the hills, regrouped at Aphek after fleeing Israel, suffered heavy losses, later held Ramoth Gilead, and a Syrian archer's random shot struck Ahab. In 1 Chronicles, the Syrians fled before Joab, regrouped under Shophach, were defeated by David, and later made peace with and served him.

Syrians in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Syrians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Syrians are a group who mistakenly believe the LORD is only a god of the hills. After fleeing Israel at Samaria, they regroup on the plain at Aphek, where huge numbers die and a wall falls. Later, at peace with Israel, they hold Ramoth Gilead; a Syrian archer's random shot strikes Ahab.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 20

The Syrians flee before Israel's attack at Samaria, then reason that Israel's god is a god of the hills and regroup to fight in the plain. At Aphek one hundred thousand footmen are killed in a day, and a wall falls on twenty-seven thousand of the rest.

Chapter 22

Syria holds Ramoth Gilead and has been at peace with Israel for three years. Its king orders his thirty-two chariot captains to fight only the king of Israel. The captains chase Jehoshaphat by mistake, then turn back, and a Syrian archer's random shot strikes Ahab.
