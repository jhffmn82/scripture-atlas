eating the forbidden fruit (the Fall)

event · 3 works · 6 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Adam should fall, eat of it, eaten from the tree, fall of Adam, partaking of the forbidden fruit, the Fall of Adam, the fall, the fall of Adam, the transgression of Adam, their fall

Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, the woman eats the forbidden fruit and gives some to Adam, leading to their awareness of nakedness, God's curses, and expulsion from the garden. In The Book of Mosiah, the Fall of Adam is Adam's partaking of the forbidden fruit, which causes mankind to become carnal, sensual, and devilish and naturally opposed to God. In The Book of Helaman, the fall of Adam cuts all mankind off from the presence of the Lord, causing spiritual and temporal death.

Abstract written by a model

The Fall: persuaded by the serpent, the woman eats the fruit first and gives some to her husband Adam, who eats. Their eyes open to their nakedness; they sew fig leaves and hide, then God questions them, pronounces curses, and expels the man from the garden.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

The woman eats the fruit after the serpent's persuasion and gives some to her husband, who also eats. Their eyes are opened and they know they are naked, so they sew fig leaves for coverings and hide from God. The act leads God to question them, pronounce curses, and send the man out of the garden.

the Fall of Adam in The Book of Mosiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the Fall of Adam first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Fall of Adam is Adam’s partaking of the forbidden fruit after the old serpent beguiled the first parents. It causes people to fall by nature, makes the natural man an enemy to God, and leaves all mankind carnal, sensual, and devilish.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

The Fall of Adam causes people to fall by nature and provides the condition from which the natural man is an enemy to God. It is compared with Adam’s partaking of the forbidden fruit, which justice could not deny.

Chapter 16

The fall was caused by the old serpent beguiling the first parents. It was the cause of all mankind becoming carnal, sensual, and devilish.
