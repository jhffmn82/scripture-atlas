Also called: the liberation from existence, the road of salvation, what is beyond and above (this world, viz. liberation)

Abstract written by a model

Liberation is the desired goal beyond the world: escape from existence, the Wheel of Births, and bondage. A son of the wise seeks it through discipline, conquering the will, watchfulness, faith, renunciation, the Law, penance, self-control, and absence of passion. It unfolds from desire and pursuit to choosing it over worldly objects, acting accordingly, and sustaining the body only to annihilate Karman.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Uttaradhyayana Sutra / First Lecture.

A son of the wise who desires liberation should pursue discipline and will not be turned away from anywhere.

Fourth Lecture.

Liberation is reached by conquering the will and by remaining watchful, especially during one's young years.

Fifth Lecture.

Liberation is obtained by absence of passion. Those trained in self-control and penance, whether monks or householders, can attain liberation.

Sixth Lecture.

Liberation is acknowledged as the opposite of bondage, but talking about it without acting accordingly does not bring salvation. One should choose liberation over worldly objects and sustain the body only to annihilate Karman.

Fourteenth Lecture.

Liberation is the desired escape from existence and the Wheel of Births. It is pursued through faith, renunciation, the Law, penance, and self-control.
