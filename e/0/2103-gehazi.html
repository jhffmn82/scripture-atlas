Gehazi

Abstract written by a model

Gehazi is the servant of Elisha. In Chapter 4 he acts as Elisha's messenger to the Shunammite woman: relaying Elisha's offer, noting she has no son, trying to push her away at Carmel, carrying Elisha's staff to the dead child, and reporting no awakening. In Chapter 5 he deceitfully takes two talents of silver and clothing from Naaman after Elisha refused a gift, lies to Elisha, and is cursed with leprosy, white as snow, passing to his offspring forever. In Chapter 8 he tells the king of Elisha's deeds and identifies the woman and her son as those Elisha restored to life.

Gehazi is in one work on this site, The Second Book of Kings. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Gehazi, Elisha's servant, calls the Shunammite woman and relays Elisha's offer, then tells Elisha she has no son and her husband is old. He tries to thrust her away at Carmel, is sent with Elisha's staff to lay it on the dead child's face, and reports that the child has not awakened. He later calls the Shunammite again to take up her son, and questions how twenty loaves could feed a hundred men.

Chapter 5

Gehazi, Elisha's servant, resolves to take something from Naaman after his master refused a gift, and runs after him. He falsely claims that Elisha sent him to ask for a talent of silver and two changes of clothing for two young prophets, receives two talents and two changes of clothing, and hides them in the house. He lies to Elisha, saying he went nowhere, is rebuked, and leaves Elisha's presence a leper, white as snow.

Chapter 8

Gehazi is talking with the king, telling him of Elisha's great deeds, including restoring the dead to life. When the woman appears, he identifies her and her son to the king as the ones Elisha restored to life.
