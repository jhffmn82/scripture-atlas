Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, the Gâthas are sacred, bounteous objects of homage and leading chants that receive sacrifice and offerings and provide protection, nourishment, reward, health, progress, growth, sanctity, and gifts. In Visparad, Gâtha Ustavaiti is announced, completed, praised, addressed with brilliant Mazda-made mountains, and approached again with praise. In Afrinagan, Gahs and Miscellaneous Fragments, the lofty Gâthas are bountiful and holy ritual powers ruling in the ritual order, with each of the five Gâthas receiving sacrifice. In Vendidad (Vendîdâd), the Gâthas are holy and beneficent chants ruling over the ratus, containing words recited aloud two, three, or four times.

Gâthas in Yasna

L.H. Mills · the work’s page · read it where Gâthas first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Gâthas are sacred, bounteous objects of homage that rule supreme in the holy ritual as leading chants. They receive sacrifice and offerings encompassing riches, persons, bodily and spiritual elements. Worshippers honor them as sources of protection, nourishment, reward, health, progress, growth, sanctity, and gifts. They are the five Gâthas of Zarathustra, whose metres, words, Zand, questions, and answers Sraosha first chanted. The account ends with their holy worship, sacrifice to their hymns, and Ustavaiti intoned as a salvation hail.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna LIV (Sp. LIII).

The Gâthas are described as bounteous and as ruling supreme in the holy and august ritual. They receive sacrifice.

Yasna LV (Sp. LIV).

The Gâthas receive as an offering all landed riches, persons, bones and tissues, forms and forces, consciousness, soul, and Fravashi. They are worshipped as bountiful, ruling as the leading chants within the holy ritual, and as sources of protection, nourishment, rewards, health, progress, growth, sanctity, and gifts.

Yasna LVII (Sp. LVI).

The Gâthas are the five Gâthas of Zarathustra, whose metres, words, Zand, questions, and answers Sraosha is said to have first chanted.

Yasna LXXI (Sp. LXX).

The five Gâthas are worshipped as holy and bountiful. Their ending words and hymns are sacrificed to, and the Gâtha Ustavaiti is to be intoned as a salvation hail.
