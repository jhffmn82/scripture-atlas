Simon in The Second Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Simon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Simon is a man of the tribe of Benjamin and a guardian of the temple. After disagreeing with Onias over the ruling of the market, he failed to prevail and informed Apollonius of Tarsus that the treasury held untold sums open to the king's seizure; Onias called him impious and false. Later, left by Maccabaeus to besiege two towers, he took a bribe of seventy thousand drachmas and let some enemies escape, so Maccabaeus had him killed as a traitor.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

Simon, of the tribe of Benjamin and guardian of the temple, disagreed with Onias about the ruling of the market in the city. Unable to overcome Onias, he went to Apollonius of Tarsus and reported that the treasury held untold sums that could fall under the king's power. Onias called him impious and said his claims were false.

Chapter 10

Simon was left by Maccabaeus, with Joseph, Zacchaeus, and their men, to besiege the two towers. Yielding to covetousness, he and his men accepted a bribe and let some of the besieged escape. Maccabaeus accused them of selling their kindred and had them killed as traitors.

Abstract written by a model

Simon, an informer who earlier gave information about the money against his country, slanders Onias as inciting Heliodorus. His hatred escalates into murders by an approved agent, while Apollonius increases malice. Menelaus is his brother.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Simon, who had earlier given information about the money against his country, slandered Onias as the one who had incited Heliodorus and caused the evils. His hatred grew until murders were committed through one of his approved agents, and Apollonius the governor was increasing his malice. His brother is Menelaus.
