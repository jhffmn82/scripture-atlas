Abstract written by a model

In The Acts of the Apostles, baptism and repentance are taught as the response Peter urges for forgiveness of sins and the gift of the Holy Spirit, with about three thousand people gladly receiving baptism. In The Book of Moroni, baptism is taught as being for accountable people who truly repent, bring forth worthy fruit, have broken hearts and contrite spirits, witness remission of sins, and determine to serve Christ to the end; baptizing little children is condemned as solemn mockery before God and a denial of Christ’s mercies.

Chapter 2

Asked what to do, Peter tells every one to repent and be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of sins and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit, with the promise extending to their children and to all who are far off. Those who gladly receive his word are baptized, about three thousand that day.

baptism in The Book of Moroni

Abstract written by a model

Baptism is for accountable people who truly repent, bring forth worthy fruit, have broken hearts and contrite spirits, witness remission of sins, and determine to serve Christ to the end. It fulfills commandments; baptizing little children is solemn mockery before God and denies Christ’s mercies.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

Baptism is given only to those who bring forth worthy fruit, have a broken heart and contrite spirit, witness that they have truly repented of all their sins, and determine to serve Christ to the end.

Chapter 8

Baptism is taught as belonging to repentance and the fulfillment of commandments for those accountable and capable of committing sin. Baptizing little children is called solemn mockery before God and a denial of Christ’s mercies.
