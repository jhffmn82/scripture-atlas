Abstract written by a model

Householders possess morality of various character; some surpass monks in self-control. Faithful householders should practise rules of conduct and observe the Pôsaha fast in both fortnights. Living piously under discipline, quitting flesh and bones, they may share Yakshas and obtain liberation through self-control, penance, and absence of passion. They prepare food for their own consumption, while monks do not pursue them for earthly gain.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Fifth Lecture.

Householders have morality of various character, and some householders are superior to some monks in self-control. A faithful householder should follow the rules of conduct and observe the Pôsaha fast in both fortnights; living piously, he may share the world of the Yakshas.

Fifteenth Lecture.

The monk does not improve his acquaintance with householders for earthly gain. He takes bed, lodging, drink, food, or dainties from householders only when they give them themselves, and he remains free from anger when they do not.
