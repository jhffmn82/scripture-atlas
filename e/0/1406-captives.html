Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, the captives of Jerusalem, including princes, mighty men, craftsmen, and smiths, are carried away to Babylon by Nebuchadnezzar. In The Book of Ezra, the captives are brought up from Babylon to Jerusalem, along with the vessels of the LORD's house. In The Book of Jeremiah, the captives of Judah are regarded as good, and the LORD promises to restore them and make them his people. In Baruch, the captives led to Babylon are warned against imitating foreigners or fearing idols and are promised peaceful release after seven generations.

Abstract written by a model

The captives of Judah, sent by the LORD into the land of the Chaldeans, are regarded as good, likened to good figs. The LORD promises to watch over them for good, return them to the land, build and plant them, and give them a heart to know him, making them his people.

Chapter 24

The captives of Judah, sent out of this place into the land of the Chaldeans, are likened to the good figs and regarded as good. The LORD promises to set his eyes on them for good, bring them back to the land, build and plant them, and give them a heart to know him, so that they become his people and return to him with their whole heart.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

The Letter of Jeremiah

The captives are told they will be led to Babylon by Nabuchodonosor because of their sins, and will remain there for seven generations before God brings them out peacefully. They are warned not to become like the foreigners, not to fear the idols when they see crowds worshiping them, and to say in their hearts, "O Lord, we must worship you."
