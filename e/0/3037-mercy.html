mercy in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · about 179 BC · the work’s page · read it where mercy first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mercy is contrasted by scope: human mercy reaches a neighbor, while the Lord's covers all flesh through reproving, chastening, teaching, and shepherd-like return. Prompted by human brevity and evil, he multiplies forgiveness, favoring those who accept chastening and seek his judgments.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

The mercy of a man is on his neighbor, while the mercy of the Lord is on all flesh, expressed through reproving, chastening, teaching, and bringing back as a shepherd does his flock. The Lord poured out his mercy and multiplied forgiveness because of human brevity and evil. He has mercy on those who accept chastening and diligently seek his judgments.
