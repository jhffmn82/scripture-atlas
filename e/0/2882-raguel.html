Raguel

person · 1 work · 15 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Tobit my cousin, father-in-law, her father, his father-in-law, your father and your mother, your father-in-law

Abstract written by a model

Raguel is a man living in Ecbatana, husband of Edna, cousin of Tobit, and father of an only daughter, Sarah. Bound by the law of Moses, he may marry Sarah only to Tobias, her kinsman with the inheritance, on pain of death. He receives Tobias, recognizes his resemblance to Tobit, and weeps at Tobit's blindness. He admits seven earlier husbands died in the night, yet gives Sarah to Tobias and seals a contract. Fearing Tobias will die, he digs a grave, but after learning he lives he blesses God, has it filled, and holds a fourteen-day wedding feast. He swears Tobias must stay until it ends. He promises half his goods, the rest after his and his wife's deaths. At departure he yields to Tobias's insistence, gives Sarah and half his goods, servants, cattle and money, blesses them, instructs Sarah to honor her in-laws, and kisses her.

Raguel in Tobit

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

Raguel is named as a kinsman of Tobias with whom they will lodge near Rages, and as the father of an only daughter, Sarah. The angel says that under the law of Moses Raguel may not marry her to anyone else, or he would be liable to death, because the inheritance belongs to Tobias.

Chapter 7

Raguel receives Tobias and his companion at his house in Ecbatana, notices that Tobias resembles his cousin Tobit, and weeps on learning Tobit is blind. He discloses that seven relatives who married his daughter died in the night, yet agrees to give Sarah to Tobias under the law of Moses, blesses them, and writes and seals a contract.

Chapter 8

Raguel digs a grave in the night, fearing that Tobias will die. After a maidservant reports that Tobias is alive, he blesses God for his mercy, orders the grave filled, and keeps a fourteen-day wedding feast. He swears Tobias must stay until the feast ends, then take half his goods to his father, with the rest to follow when Raguel and his wife die.

Chapter 10

Raguel, who had sworn that Tobias should spend fourteen days of wedding feast there, first asks him to stay and offers to send word to Tobias's father, but yields when Tobias insists. He gives Tobias his wife Sarah and half his goods, servants, cattle and money, blesses them, and tells his daughter to honor her father-in-law and mother-in-law, then kisses her.
