Duke Ai

person · 3 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Duke, Duke Ai of Lu, Duke Ai of the Lu State, The Duke Ai, the Duke, the duke

Abstract written by a model

In Chuang Tzŭ, Duke Ai, ruler of the Lu State, asks Confucius why Ai T'ai T'o attracts followers despite leprosy, learns virtue transcends appearance, fears his rule has harmed the State, and seeks Confucius as a moral friend. In Chuang Tzŭ, Duke Ai of Lu, ruler of Lu, claims many scholars based on robes; challenged by Chuang Tzŭ, he threatens false wearers, leaving one old man. In The Analects of Confucius, Book VI (Yung Yey), Duke Ai asks Confucius which of the disciples loved to learn. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XII (Yen Yuan), Duke Ai asks Yu Zo what should be done during a year of scarcity when returns for expenditure are insufficient, says that taking two tenths is not enough, and questions how one tenth could work.

CHAPTER V.

Duke Ai asks Confucius about Ai T'ai T'o, a loathsome leper whom men and women nevertheless seek out. After hearing Confucius's explanation of perfect talents and virtue without outward form, Duke Ai becomes concerned that his own rule has harmed his State and regards Confucius as a moral friend.

CHAPTER XXI.

Duke Ai of Lu claims that Lu has many scholars because many people wear scholars' robes. After Chuang Tzŭ challenges this claim, he orders that death be the punishment for wearing the robes without TAO, and only one old man continues to wear them and withstands his questions.
