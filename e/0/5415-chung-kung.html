Chung-kung

person · 3 works · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book VI (Yung Yey), Chung-kung asks about Tsze-sang Po-tsze and argues that overlooking small matters is acceptable when joined with reverential attention to business, but excessive when practiced as ease. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XII (Yen Yuan), Chung-kung asks about perfect virtue, acknowledges deficient intelligence and vigour, and resolves to practise the Master's lesson. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XIII: Tsze-Lu, Chung-kung serves as chief minister to the Head of the Chi family and asks the Master about government and identifying virtuous and talented men for office.
