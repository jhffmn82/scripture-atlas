death

Abstract written by a model

In The Tao Teh King, or The Tao and Its Characteristics, death is presented as distinct from perishing, grievous yet not necessarily feared, subject to One's authority, and marking the end of people remaining in their own place without intercourse with neighbours. In The Analects of Confucius, Book VI (Yung Yey), death is the danger from which a man may escape after losing his uprightness, but only by mere good fortune.

death in The Tao Teh King, or The Tao and Its Characteristics

Lao-Tse · the work’s page · read it where death first appears

Abstract written by a model

Death is presented as distinct from perishing, and as a grievous reality that people may nevertheless not fear. Its infliction belongs to One who presides over it; those who try to inflict it in One's place risk harm. In a small state, people remain in their own place without intercourse with neighbours until death.
