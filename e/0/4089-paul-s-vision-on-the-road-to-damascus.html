Paul's vision on the road to Damascus

event · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Paul's conversion vision, Saul's conversion vision on the road, a great light, a light from the sky, a voice, that light, the heavenly vision, the light, the voice

Abstract written by a model

Paul's heavenly vision on the road to Damascus was a noon encounter in which a light from the sky, brighter than the sun, shone around Saul and his companions. He fell to the earth and heard Jesus ask why he persecuted him, while companions heard the sound but saw no one. Left blind, he was led into Damascus to learn what to do. The vision commissioned him as servant and witness to all, including Gentiles, and Paul says he was obedient to it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

Near Damascus a light from the sky shone around Saul, he fell to the earth, and heard Jesus ask why he persecuted him. Jesus told him to enter the city to learn what to do, while his companions stood speechless, hearing the sound but seeing no one.

Chapter 22

Paul recounts a noon light from the sky, a voice identifying itself as Jesus of Nazareth, and his companions seeing the light but not understanding the voice. It left him blind, led into Damascus, and began his commission to be a witness to all men.

Chapter 26

At noon on the way to Damascus, Paul saw a light from the sky brighter than the sun, and he and his companions fell to the earth. He heard Jesus speak in Hebrew, rebuking his persecution and appointing him a servant and witness to the Gentiles. Paul says he was not disobedient to this vision.
