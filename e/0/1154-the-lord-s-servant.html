the suffering servant (he) in The Book of Isaiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the suffering servant (he) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The suffering servant is a figure described as the LORD's righteous servant. He grows up as a tender plant without beauty or majesty, despised and rejected, a man acquainted with disease. He is pierced for the speakers' transgressions, bears their sickness, and heals them by his wounds. Silent like a lamb led to slaughter, though free of violence or deceit, he is buried with the wicked and a rich man. Afterward he sees offspring, prolongs his days, justifies many, bears their iniquities, and intercedes for transgressors, counted among them.

Chapter 53

The servant grew up as a tender plant and a root out of dry ground, without beauty or majesty, and was despised, rejected, and acquainted with suffering. He was pierced, crushed, oppressed, and silent like a lamb, was cut off, and was buried with the wicked and a rich man though he had done no violence and spoken no deceit. He will see offspring, prolong his days, see the light and be satisfied, justify many, bear their iniquities, and receive a portion with the great as one who interceded for transgressors.

the Lord's servant in The Book of Mosiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the Lord's servant first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Lord’s servant grows before the Lord as a tender plant and root from dry ground, without form or comeliness. Despised, rejected, sorrowful, oppressed, and afflicted, he silently goes to slaughter. He bears griefs, sorrows, transgressions, iniquities, and sins for others; is cut off, buried with the wicked and rich, yet has done no evil or deceit. His soul becomes an offering for sin, after which he has seed and prolonged days. By his knowledge he justifies many, intercedes for transgressors, and receives a portion with the great.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

The Lord’s servant grows up as a tender plant and is despised, rejected, wounded, bruised, oppressed, and afflicted. He bears the griefs, sorrows, iniquities, and sins of many, opens not his mouth before slaughter, makes intercession for transgressors, and is ultimately given a portion with the great.
