Abstract written by a model

In Deuteronomy, Manasseh received the rest of Gilead and all Bashan, including the region of Argob. In The Book of Joshua, Manasseh received inheritances on both sides of the Jordan, though its children failed to drive out the Canaanites. In The Book of Joshua, the half-tribe of Manasseh received land east of the Jordan, helped its brothers conquer Canaan, and later returned to its inheritance. In 1 Chronicles, Manasseh is a patriarchal figure whose descendants are traced through Machir and Gilead. In 1 Chronicles, Manasseh is a half-tribe that settled east of the Jordan, aided David, committed idolatry, and was carried into Assyrian captivity.

half-tribe of Manasseh in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · about 600 BC · the work’s page · read it where half-tribe of Manasseh first appears

Abstract written by a model

The half-tribe of Manasseh is one portion of Manasseh that Moses settled east of the Jordan, in Bashan, Og's former kingdom, including Jair's sixty villages and part of Gilead shared with Reuben and Gad. Joshua addresses it with Reuben and Gad, and its fighting men pledge obedience and agree to cross the Jordan armed to help their brothers. The other half receives land west of the river. Afterward Joshua dismisses it with a blessing; it returns east, helps build the great altar, and is cleared of trespass after explaining the altar's purpose.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Joshua / Chapter 1

The half-tribe of Manasseh is addressed by Joshua together with the Reubenites and Gadites. Its fighting men are to cross the Jordan armed to help their brothers before returning to their possession east of the Jordan. They join in promising to obey Joshua.

Chapter 13

Moses gave one half of Manasseh an inheritance east of the Jordan, while the other half is to receive land west of it under Joshua's division. Its territory ran from Mahanaim through all Bashan and Og's kingdom, including Jair's sixty villages, with half of Gilead, Ashtaroth and Edrei going to the children of Machir.

Chapter 22

The half-tribe of Manasseh is sent away by Joshua with a blessing; its other half already held land in Bashan from Moses, while this half was given land west of the Jordan. They return with Reuben and Gad, help build the great altar, and are cleared of the charge of trespass after explaining its purpose.

Manasseh in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · 449 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Manasseh first appears

Abstract written by a model

Manasseh is a half-tribe that settled and multiplied from Bashan to Baal Hermon, Senir, and Mount Hermon, led by mighty heads of fathers' houses. Early on it joined war against the Hagrites, but then committed idolatry, following the gods of the land's peoples. Elsewhere, captains of thousands such as Adnah and Zillethai aided David at Ziklag, and eighteen thousand came to Hebron to make him king. Because of its trespass, the tribe was carried off to Assyrian captivity at Halah, Habor, Hara, and Gozan.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

The half-tribe of Manasseh lived in the land and increased from Bashan to Baal Hermon, Senir, and Mount Hermon, with seven named heads of fathers' houses described as mighty men of valor. They joined the war against the Hagrites, then trespassed against the God of their fathers by following the gods of the land's peoples. For this they were carried away to Halah, Habor, Hara, and the river of Gozan.

Chapter 12

Some of Manasseh joined David as he went to Ziklag after the Philistine lords sent him away from the battle against Saul. Seven named captains of thousands helped him against the band of raiders. At Hebron eighteen thousand of the half-tribe came to make David king, and the eastern half was included in the one hundred twenty thousand across the Jordan.
