Abstract written by a model

In Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong), Chen Zhong lives austerely, refuses Dai’s unrighteous emolument and house, and is questioned over accepting goose flesh and dwelling in Wu Ling. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), Chen Zhong would refuse the kingdom of Qi if offered contrary to righteousness and is esteemed for declining humble portions of food.

Chen Zhong in Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong)

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Chen Zhong first appears

Abstract written by a model

Chen Zhong, of Qi's ancient noble family, lives in Wu Ling, makes hemp sandals, and eats millet. He refuses Dai's unrighteous emolument and house, enduring starvation until worm-eaten plums revive him. Yet his self-denying purity is questioned because he accepts goose flesh from his wife and still dwells in Wu Ling.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10

Chen Zhong lived in Wu Ling and went three days without food until he could neither hear nor see, then recovered after eating three mouthfuls of worm-eaten plums. He rejects his brother Dai's emolument and house as unrighteous, yet eats food given by his wife and lives in Wu Ling.
