disbelief

topic · 4 works · 3 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Their disbelief, disbelieve, disbelieveth, his disbelief

Abstract written by a model

In Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa), disbelief brings Allah’s curse, blocks belief except for a few, and leads to punishment in the Fire; those who persist in increasing disbelief are neither pardoned nor guided. In Surah 35: The Originator (Faatir), disbelief harms the disbeliever and increases abhorrence and loss for disbelievers in their Lord’s sight, while those who disbelieve face awful doom and hell. In Surah 39: The Groups (Az-Zumar), disbelief brings warning, humiliation, loss, and the Fire, and Muhammad is told to let disbelievers take brief pleasure because they are among the owners of the Fire. In Surah 40: The Forgiver (Al-Ghaafir), disbelief includes rejecting Allah when He alone is invoked while believing when a partner is ascribed to Him, and it leads to argument, punishment, the Fire, and ruin.

disbelief in Surah 4: The Women (An-Nisaa)

author unknown · undated · the work’s page · read it where disbelief first appears

Abstract written by a model

Disbelief is presented as a condition that brings Allah’s curse, blocks belief except for a few, and leads to punishment in the Fire for rejecting His revelations. Hypocrites are cast back into disbelief because of what they earned; they want believers to disbelieve as they do and become equal with them. The account culminates in a repeated pattern of believing, then disbelieving, then believing and disbelieving again, with increasing disbelief. Those who persist in that increase are neither pardoned by Allah nor guided to a way.
