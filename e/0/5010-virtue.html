virtue

topic · 2 works · 20 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: Good acts, Good deeds, good acts, good works, the virtues of the virtuous, virtues, virtuous, what is not virtuous, what was not virtuous

Abstract written by a model

In Hymns of Guru Nanak, virtue brings happiness, purity, knowledge of God, and union with the Perfect One; it leads to God, drives vice away, cuts off chains, and is necessary for heaven, salvation, honour, and freedom from punishment, unfolding as a tree of spiritual growth. In The Analects of Confucius, Book IV (Le Jin), virtue is the basis of an excellent neighborhood and the condition for rightly loving or hating others; the virtuous rest in it, cleave to it in hardship and danger, and gain neighbors through practicing it.

virtue in Hymns of Guru Nanak

Guru Nanak · the work’s page · read it where virtue first appears

Abstract written by a model

Virtue brings happiness, purity, knowledge of God, and union with the Perfect One; it is required for happiness and leads to God when embraced. It abides with the person who embraces it, drives vice away, and cuts off chains. Good works are necessary for heaven, salvation, honour, and freedom from punishment, unlike sin, hypocrisy, and empty observances. Virtue unfolds as a tree: God's name forms branches, religion flowers, divine knowledge fruit, attainment of God leaves, and mental pride dense shade.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Sri Rag .. Ashtapadi

Virtue brings happiness, purity, and union with the Perfect One. Those who abandon evil under the Guru's instruction become absorbed in God, while those without virtue lose human life.

Asa Chhant

Virtue brings knowledge of God and abides with the person who embraces it. It drives vice away.

Ramkali .. Ramkali ki War I

Good works are necessary for heaven, salvation, honour, and freedom from punishment. They are contrasted with sin, hypocrisy, and empty religious observances.

Rag Basant

Good acts are described as the tree in a metaphor connecting spiritual practice with God's name, religion, divine knowledge, and attainment of God.
