perfect virtue

topic · 1 work · 10 quoted facts · 3 connections

Abstract written by a model

Perfect virtue is subduing oneself and returning to propriety. Attained through self-subjugation, it requires avoiding improper sights, sounds, speech, and movements; practicing it for one day leads all under heaven to ascribe it to the person. It unfolds into treating everyone as a great guest, employing people as at a great sacrifice, refusing to do to others what one would not wish done to oneself, and leaving no murmuring in country or family. It also appears as cautious, slow speech arising from awareness of the difficulty of doing.

CHAP. I. 1. Yen Yuan asked about perfect virtue. The Master

Perfect virtue is described as subduing oneself and returning to propriety. If a person practices this for one day, all under heaven will ascribe perfect virtue to that person.

CHAP. II. Chung-kung asked about perfect virtue. The Master

Perfect virtue is described as treating everyone as a great guest, employing people as if assisting at a great sacrifice, not doing to others what one would not wish done to oneself, and having no murmuring against one in the country or family.

CHAP. III. 1. Sze-ma Niu asked about perfect virtue.

Perfect virtue is associated with being cautious and slow in speech. The unit connects this manner of speaking with feeling the difficulty of doing.
