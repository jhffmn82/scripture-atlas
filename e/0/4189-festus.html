Festus

Also called: Porcius Festus, he himself, most excellent Festus, the governor

Abstract written by a model

Festus is the Roman governor who succeeds Felix. Arriving in the province, he goes to Jerusalem, refuses the Jews' request to move Paul there, and hears the case at Caesarea. Hoping to please the Jews, he asks Paul about trial in Jerusalem, then sends him to Caesar. He presents Paul to King Agrippa, calls Paul crazy during his defense, and hears Agrippa say Paul could have been freed.

Chapter 25

Festus goes up to Jerusalem three days after arriving in the province, refuses the Jews' request to bring Paul there, and hears the case at Caesarea. Seeking to gain favor with the Jews, he asks Paul about being judged in Jerusalem, then sends Paul to Caesar after conferring with the council. He lays the case before King Agrippa and presents Paul to him, wanting something certain to write to the emperor about the charges.

Chapter 26

Festus interrupts Paul's defense with a loud voice, saying Paul is crazy and that great learning is driving him insane. Paul answers him respectfully as most excellent Festus, and Festus later withdraws with the others and hears Agrippa's remark that Paul could have been freed had he not appealed to Caesar.
