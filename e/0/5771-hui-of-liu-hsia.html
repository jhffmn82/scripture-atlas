Hui of Liu-hsia

person · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book XV (Wei Ling Kung), Hui of Liu-hsia is said to possess virtue and talents known to Tsang Wan, who did not procure that Hui should stand with him in court. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XVIII: Wei Tsze, Hui of Liu-hsia is thrice dismissed from his office as chief criminal judge, explains why serving uprightly leads to dismissal while serving crookedly gives him no reason to leave his parents' country, and is later described as having surrendered his will and submitted to taint, with words corresponding to reason and admirable actions.

Hui of Liu-hsia in The Analects of Confucius, Book XVIII: Wei Tsze

author unknown · about 478 to 220 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hui of Liu-hsia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hui of Liu-hsia is thrice dismissed from his office as chief criminal judge. He says that serving men uprightly leaves him nowhere to go without facing repeated dismissal, while serving crookedly gives him no reason to leave his parents' country. He is later described as having surrendered his will and submitted to taint, while his words corresponded with reason and his actions were admirable.
