Teng

Also called: kingdom, small State, territory of Teng

Abstract written by a model

In Mencius, Book I (Liang Hui Wang), Teng is a small kingdom between Qi and Chu whose defenses are important because it suffers from larger neighboring kingdoms and must prepare for attack. In Mencius, Book II (Gong Sun Chou), Teng is the destination of Mencius's mission of condolence from Qi. In Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong), Teng is a small State urged toward excellence through defined boundaries, regulated land and allowances, mutual aid, and benevolent government.

Teng in Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong)

author unknown · 399 to 300 BC · the work’s page · read it where Teng first appears

Abstract written by a model

Teng is a small State, about fifty li in length and breadth, yet sufficient to become good. Though narrow, it has both superior men and country-men. Mencius urges its ruler toward excellence through defined boundaries, regulated land and allowances, and mutual aid. Teng later receives Xu Xing and his disciples; its prince is praised for benevolent government.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Mencius, Book III (Teng Wen Gong) / Part I / Chapter 1

Teng is described by Mencius as small, amounting to about fifty li in length and breadth, but still sufficient to make a good State. Mencius encourages its ruler to pursue the one path to excellence.

Chapter 3

Teng is described as a narrow and small territory that nevertheless contains both men of a superior grade and country-men. Mencius says its boundaries should be correctly defined and its land and allowances regulated according to a system of mutual aid and adjusted by the prince and his officials.

Chapter 4

Teng receives Xu Xing and his disciples, and its prince is praised as a worthy ruler who practices benevolent government.
