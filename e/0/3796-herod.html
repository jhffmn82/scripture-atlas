Herod

person · 3 works · 20 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Herod himself, Herod the tetrarch, King Herod, The king, that fox, the king, the king of Judea

Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Matthew, Herod imprisons John the Baptizer for Herodias's sake, fears the multitude, then beheads him because of a birthday oath and believes John has risen from the dead. In The Good News According to Mark, Herod imprisons and fears John the Baptizer, then reluctantly beheads him after swearing an oath at his birthday supper and later thinks Jesus is John risen. In The Gospel According to Luke, Herod the tetrarch of Galilee does evil, takes his brother's wife Herodias, is reproved by John, then imprisons John and, by his own statement, beheads him. In The Gospel According to Luke, Herod, king of Judea with jurisdiction over Galilee, is called a fox by Jesus, questions Jesus, mocks him, robes him luxuriously, returns him to Pilate, and becomes Pilate's friend.

Herod the tetrarch in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where Herod the tetrarch first appears

Abstract written by a model

Herod, a tetrarch also called king, brother of Philip, imprisoned John the Baptizer for Herodias's sake, feared the multitude, then grievously beheaded him because of a birthday oath; hearing of Jesus, he believes John has risen from the dead.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

Herod hears the report about Jesus and says to his servants that this is John the Baptizer risen from the dead. He had arrested and imprisoned John for the sake of Herodias, wanted to kill him but feared the multitude, and then, bound by an oath made at his birthday, grievedly commanded that John be beheaded. He is also called the king in the account.

Herod in The Good News According to Mark

author unknown · about AD 70 · the work’s page · read it where Herod first appears

Abstract written by a model

Herod is a king married to Herodias, his brother Philip's former wife. He imprisoned John the Baptizer for her sake yet feared him as righteous and holy. At his birthday supper he swore an oath, then reluctantly beheaded John, and later thought Jesus was John risen.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

King Herod hears of Jesus and says John the Baptizer, whom he beheaded, has risen from the dead. The text explains that he had arrested John for the sake of Herodias, feared him as a righteous and holy man, and heard him gladly. At his birthday supper he swore to give the young lady whatever she asked and, though exceedingly sorry, ordered John's beheading to honor his oaths and his guests.

Herod in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where Herod first appears

Abstract written by a model

Herod, king of Judea with jurisdiction over Galilee, called a fox by Jesus. Long wanting to see a miracle, he gladly receives Jesus, questions him unanswered, mocks him with soldiers, robes him luxuriously, and returns him to Pilate, becoming Pilate's friend that day.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 23

Herod, in Jerusalem at the time, is glad to see Jesus, having long wanted to see a miracle from him. He questions him at length without answer, mocks him with his soldiers, dresses him in luxurious clothing, and sends him back to Pilate. He and Pilate, formerly enemies, become friends that day.
