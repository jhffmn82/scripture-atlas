living beings

topic · 1 work · 5 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: a creature, all sorts of living beings, creatures, eggs and living beings, eggs, few living beings, eggs, living beings, few eggs or few living beings, few living beings, many living beings, movable and immovable (beings)

Abstract written by a model

Living beings are sentient entities that can affect food, liquor, and lodging places. They experience all kinds of sensation, temporarily reside in one of four states of being, and include six classes such as earth-body and animals. They may be harmed during food fetching, vessel breaking, or tending fire; such actions, and sinful conduct toward them, make food unacceptable. The account ends by requiring that they not be killed, injured, displaced, or treated with attachment or aversion.

Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book II, translated by Hermann Jacobi / Second Book / First Part. / First Lecture, Call

Living beings are among the impurities that can affect food and make it unacceptable. Food preparation or acquisition that acts sinfully toward living beings also makes the food unacceptable.

Seventh Lesson.

Living beings may be killed, frightened, bruised, smashed, crushed, afflicted, pained, or dislocated when a layman fetches food from above. They may also be injured when an earthen vessel is broken or when fire is stirred or brightened.

Thirteenth Lecture.

All sorts of living beings experience sensation. Living bulbs, roots, rind, and sprouts are described as living beings or living substances that may be used in an attempted cure of a sick monk.

The Venerable Ascetic Mahâvîra endowed with the highest knowledge and intuition

Living beings are classified into six classes, including earth-body and animals. They must not be killed, injured, displaced, or treated with attachment or aversion.
