Habakkuk in Habakkuk

author unknown · 606 to 597 BC · the work’s page · read it where Habakkuk first appears

Abstract written by a model

Habakkuk is a prophet who calls the LORD his God. He begins by crying out over violence, injustice, and the failure of justice, then questions why the LORD tolerates treachery and whether the oppressor will keep killing nations. He stands at his watch on the ramparts awaiting the LORD's reply and is told to write the vision plainly on tablets. In his closing prayer he stands in awe of the LORD's deeds, trembles in body and lips, and waits quietly for the day of trouble. He nevertheless resolves to rejoice in the LORD even amid famine, and is given deer's feet to go in high places.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Habakkuk / Chapter 1

Habakkuk the prophet sees a revelation and cries to the LORD about violence, iniquity, and the failure of justice. After the LORD's answer, he affirms that the LORD is everlasting and has appointed the Chaldeans for judgment, but he questions why the LORD tolerates treachery and stays silent, and asks whether the oppressor will keep killing nations without mercy.

Chapter 2

The speaker stands at his watch on the ramparts, looking out to see what the LORD will say to him and how he should answer concerning his complaint. He receives the LORD's reply and is told to write the vision plainly on tablets.

Chapter 3

Habakkuk, called the prophet, offers this prayer, stands in awe of the LORD's deeds, and asks him to renew his work. He trembles in body and lips, feels rottenness in his bones, and must wait quietly for the day of trouble. He nevertheless resolves to rejoice in the LORD and declares that God makes his feet like deer's feet and lets him go in high places.
