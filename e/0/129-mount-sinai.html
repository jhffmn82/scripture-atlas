Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, Mount Sinai is holy ground where the LORD meets Moses, later becoming the place where Israel camps, bounds are set, the LORD descends in fire and cloud, Moses builds an altar, enters the cloud, receives the tablets, and descends from the mountain. In Deuteronomy, Horeb is the mountain where the LORD addresses Israel, marks the start of the journey to Kadesh Barnea, burns with fire, and speaks his covenant and the ten commandments. In The First Book of Kings, Horeb is the site of the LORD's covenant with Israel, has a cave, and is where Elijah arrives after forty days and the LORD passes by. In Paul’s Letter to the Galatians, Mount Sinai in Arabia is the source of one of the two covenants, which bears children to bondage, and is equated with Hagar. In The Letter to the Hebrews, Sinai is the touchable mountain that burned with fire and was surrounded by blackness, darkness, storm, trumpet sound, and a voice of words, but which the readers have not come to. In Surah 7: The Heights (Al-A'raaf), the mountain is where Allah tells Moses to gaze as a condition for seeing Him, and it crashes down when Allah reveals His glory to it. In Surah 95: The Fig (At-Tin), Mount Sinai is invoked in an oath.

Mount Sinai in Exodus

author unknown · about 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Mount Sinai first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mount Sinai, also called Horeb and God's Mountain, is holy ground where the LORD meets Moses. It first appears when Moses arrives with the flock and God promises that Israel will serve him there after leaving Egypt. Later Israel camps below; bounds are set, and touching the mountain means death. The LORD descends in fire and cloud as it smokes and quakes. Moses builds an altar with twelve pillars at its base, enters the cloud, and stays forty days and nights. He is later told to present himself alone on the top, where the LORD proclaims his name, and Moses descends with the tablets.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

Horeb is called God's mountain, where Moses arrives while leading the flock to the back of the wilderness. It is made holy ground by God's presence. God says that after the people leave Egypt they will serve him on this mountain, as a token to Moses.

Chapter 19

Mount Sinai is where Israel camps and where the LORD calls to Moses. It is to have bounds set around it, and anyone who touches it, man or animal, is to be put to death. On the third day it is covered by a thick cloud, smokes like a furnace, and quakes greatly as the LORD descends on it in fire, and Moses is called to its top.

Chapter 24

At the base of the mountain Moses builds an altar with twelve pillars. The LORD's glory settles on Mount Sinai, and a cloud covers it for six days. Moses goes up into the cloud and stays there forty days and forty nights.

Chapter 34

Mount Sinai is where Moses is told to present himself on the top, with no one else and no flocks or herds nearby. The LORD descends there in a cloud, proclaims his name, and Moses stays forty days and nights before coming down with the tablets.

Horeb in Deuteronomy

author unknown · 624 BC · the work’s page · read it where Horeb first appears

Abstract written by a model

Horeb is a mountain where the LORD addressed Israel. In Chapter 1 he tells them they have stayed long enough and must set out to possess the land; Horeb is the start of an eleven-day journey to Kadesh Barnea via Mount Seir. In Chapter 4 the mountain burns with fire, and the LORD speaks from it, declaring his covenant and the ten commandments on two stone tablets.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

Horeb is where the LORD spoke to Israel, telling them they had lived long enough at the mountain and must journey to possess the land. It is also the starting point of an eleven days' journey to Kadesh Barnea by way of Mount Seir.

Chapter 4

Horeb is where Israel stood before the LORD when the mountain burned with fire amid darkness, cloud, and thick darkness. The LORD spoke from the fire, and the people heard a voice but saw no form. There he declared his covenant, the ten commandments, and wrote them on two stone tablets.

mountain (Sinai, that burned with fire) in The Letter to the Hebrews

author unknown · about AD 64 to 69 · the work’s page · read it where mountain (Sinai, that burned with fire) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sinai is a touchable mountain that burned with fire, blackness, darkness, storm, a trumpet sound, and a voice of words. Its rule that even an animal touching it be stoned made listeners beg for no more words, and Moses trembled in terror. Readers are told they have not come to it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 12

This mountain is described as one that might be touched and that burned with fire, with blackness, darkness, storm, a trumpet sound, and a voice of words. Those who heard begged for no more words, since they could not stand the command that even an animal touching it be stoned. The appearance was so fearful that Moses said he was terrified and trembling. The readers are told they have not come to this mountain.
