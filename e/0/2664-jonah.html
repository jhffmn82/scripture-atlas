Jonah

Abstract written by a model

In Jonah, Jonah flees the LORD's commission to Nineveh, is swallowed by a huge fish, prays from its belly, preaches Nineveh's overthrow, and becomes angry when God relents. In The Gospel According to Luke, Jonah is cited as a prophet whose preaching led the Ninevites to repent and as a sign to that generation. In Surah 10: Jonas (Yunus), Jonas is associated with a people who believed, had the torment of disgrace removed, and were given comfort for a while.

Jonah in Jonah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Jonah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jonah, son of Amittai, is a self-identified Hebrew who fears the LORD. Commissioned to preach against Nineveh, he flees toward Tarshish to avoid the mission. During a storm he is singled out by lot, tells the mariners to throw him into the sea, and is swallowed by a huge fish, staying three days and three nights in its belly. From there he prays, recalling his affliction, being brought up from the pit, and vowing sacrifice and thanksgiving, and he looks toward the holy temple. The fish vomits him onto dry land. Receiving the LORD's word a second time, he goes to Nineveh and proclaims its overthrow in forty days. He then becomes displeased and angry, explaining that he fled because he knew God is gracious, merciful, slow to anger and relenting of harm. He sits in a booth he made east of the city to watch, is glad for a vine, faints in the heat, and twice asks to die, insisting his anger is right.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Jonah / Chapter 1

Jonah, son of Amittai, is told by the LORD to go preach against Nineveh but flees toward Tarshish by ship, sleeping in the ship's innermost parts during the storm. Singled out by lot, he identifies himself as a Hebrew who fears the LORD and tells the mariners to throw him into the sea. He is thrown overboard and swallowed by a huge fish, in whose belly he stays three days and three nights.

Chapter 2

Jonah prays to the LORD, his God, from the fish's belly, describing his affliction, his descent into the depths, and his being brought up from the pit. He remembers the LORD when his soul faints, vows to sacrifice with thanksgiving and pay what he has vowed, and declares that salvation belongs to the LORD. He is then vomited out by the fish onto dry land.

Chapter 3

Jonah receives the LORD's word a second time, arises, and goes to Nineveh as commanded. He enters the city a day's journey and cries out that Nineveh will be overthrown in forty days.

Chapter 4

Jonah is displeased and angry, prays that the LORD take his life, and explains that he fled to Tarshish because he knew God is gracious and merciful. He sits in a booth east of the city to watch what becomes of Nineveh, is glad for the vine, then faints in the heat and again asks to die, insisting he is right to be angry even to death.
