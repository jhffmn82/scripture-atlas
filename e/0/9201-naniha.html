Also called: great passage of Naniha, point of wave-beaten Naniha

Abstract written by a model

Naniha is a place associated with imperial rule and dramatic events. Emperor Nin-Toku dwelt there in the palace of Takatsu; its great passage, wave-beaten point, and channel figure in an Empress’s returning voyage and news of his marriage. Later, King Sumi-no-ye-no-naka-tsu set Emperor Ri-Chiu’s palace there on fire, which Ri-Chiu saw from the Pass of Hanifu.

Naniha is in one work on this site, Kojiki (Records of Ancient Matters), Volume III (Emperor Nin-toku to Empress Sui-ko). This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

It was the place where Emperor Nin-Toku dwelt in the palace of Takatsu while ruling the Empire.

Sect. CXX: Emperor Nin-Toku (Part II.—Various Deeds). .. Sect. CXXX.—Emperor Nin-Toku (Part XI.—His Age and Place of Burial).

Naniha had a great passage where the Empress’s returning vessel met a lady of the train who reported Emperor Nin-Toku’s marriage to Yata-no-waki-iratsume. Naniha also had a wave-beaten point and a channel mentioned in the account.

Sect. CXXXI.—Emperor Ri-Chiu (Part I.—Genealogies). .. Sect. CXXXV.—Emperor Ri-Chiu (Part V.—His Age and Place of Burial).

Naniha was the place where Emperor Ri-Chiu's palace was set on fire by King Sumi-no-ye-no-naka-tsu. The Emperor later gazed toward Naniha from the Pass of Hanifu while the fire was still bright.
