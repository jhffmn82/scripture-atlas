Benaiah

person · 3 works · 20 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Benaiah the son of Jehoiada

Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Samuel, Benaiah commands the Cherethites and Pelethites, performs great feats, and is set by David over his guard. In The First Book of Kings, Benaiah executes Solomon's orders to kill Adonijah, Joab, and Shimei, then replaces Joab as army commander over Israel. In 1 Chronicles, Benaiah is a valiant warrior and priest's son who performed great feats, was set by David over his guard, led the Cherethites and Pelethites, and commanded the third monthly division.

Benaiah the son of Jehoiada in The Second Book of Samuel

author unknown · about 950 BC · the work’s page · read it where Benaiah the son of Jehoiada first appears

Abstract written by a model

Benaiah, son of Jehoiada and of a valiant man of Kabzeel, commanded the Cherethites and Pelethites. He killed Ariel of Moab's two sons, a lion in a pit, and a huge Egyptian with the man's own spear. Ranked above the thirty but below the three, he was set by David over his guard.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 23

Benaiah, son of Jehoiada and of a valiant man of Kabzeel, killed the two sons of Ariel of Moab and a lion in a pit in a time of snow. He killed a huge Egyptian by taking the spear from his hand with a staff and using it against him. He was more honorable than the thirty but did not attain to the three, and David set him over his guard.

Benaiah in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · 449 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Benaiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Benaiah, son of Jehoiada of Kabzeel, is a valiant warrior and priest's son. He killed the two sons of Ariel of Moab, a lion in a pit on a snowy day, and a five-cubit Egyptian with the Egyptian's own spear. Renowned among the thirty yet not attaining to the three, he was set by David over his guard, leading the Cherethites and Pelethites, and commanded the third monthly division of twelve.
