Jeroboam the son of Nebat

person · 3 works · 38 quoted facts · 12 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, Jeroboam the son of Nebat became king of Israel and led Israel into idolatry, and his dynasty was destroyed. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Jeroboam became king of Israel, established unauthorized worship, was defeated by Abijah, and was struck dead by the LORD. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Jeroboam son of Nebat is described as having made Israel sin and given Ephraim a way of sin.

Jeroboam the son of Nebat in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Jeroboam the son of Nebat first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jeroboam the son of Nebat was an Ephraimite from Zeredah, son of Nebat and a widowed mother, Zeruah. A mighty man of valor, he served Solomon, who put him in charge of the labor of the house of Joseph. Ahijah promised him ten tribes and a sure house if he walked in the LORD's ways. After he lifted his hand against the king and Solomon sought to kill him, he fled to Shishak in Egypt until Solomon died. Recalled, he spoke for the assembly to Rehoboam and was made king over all Israel. He built Shechem and Penuel, then, fearing the people would return to Rehoboam, made two golden calves for Bethel and Dan, appointed non-Levite priests of the high places, and devised a feast on the fifteenth day of the eighth month. At the Bethel altar he ordered a man of God seized and his hand dried up, restored only through the man's intercession; he offered a meal and reward, which was refused, yet did not turn from his evil way. When his son Abijah fell sick, he sent his wife in disguise to Ahijah, and was condemned for doing evil above all before him, making other gods and molten images. He reigned twenty-two years and was continually at war with Rehoboam, and war continued with Abijam. His son Nadab succeeded him, but his house was cut off, destroyed by Baasha according to the LORD's word through Ahijah. His sin, by which he made Israel sin, became the standard for later kings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Jeroboam, an Ephraimite and servant of Solomon, was a mighty man of valor whom Solomon put in charge of the labor of the house of Joseph. Ahijah told him he would receive ten tribes and reign over Israel, with a sure house if he walked in the LORD's ways. He lifted his hand against the king, and when Solomon sought to kill him he fled to Shishak in Egypt until Solomon died.

Chapter 12

Jeroboam, who had fled to Egypt from Solomon, was called back and spoke for the assembly to Rehoboam, then was made king over all Israel. He built Shechem and Penuel, and fearing the people would return to Rehoboam, made two golden calves, set them in Bethel and Dan, appointed non-Levite priests, and ordained a feast of his own devising.

Chapter 13

Jeroboam stood at the Bethel altar to burn incense and ordered the man of God seized, whereupon his hand dried up until the man of God interceded and it was restored. He offered the man of God a meal and a reward, which was refused. Afterward he did not turn from his evil way and kept making priests of the high places from any who wanted it, a sin that doomed his house to be cut off and destroyed.

Chapter 14

Jeroboam sends his wife in disguise to the prophet Ahijah to learn what will become of his sick son Abijah. He is condemned through Ahijah for doing evil above all before him, making other gods and molten images, and casting the LORD behind his back. He reigns twenty-two years, sleeps with his fathers, and is succeeded by his son Nadab, and he is at continual war with Rehoboam.

Chapter 15

Jeroboam was king of Israel when Abijam began to reign in his eighteenth year and when Asa began in his twentieth year. War continued between him and Abijam. His sins, by which he made Israel sin, were the standard for his son Nadab and for Baasha, and his whole house was destroyed by Baasha according to the LORD's word through Ahijah.

Jeroboam in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Jeroboam first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jeroboam, son of Nebat and former servant of Solomon, rebelled against his lord and fled to Egypt. He returned when Israel sent for him, and with all Israel asked Rehoboam for a lighter yoke, fulfilling God's word spoken through Ahijah the Shilonite. As king of Israel, he cast off the Levites and priests, appointed his own priests for the high places, and made goat and golden calf idols as gods. He later set 800,000 chosen men against Abijah, but God struck Israel, costing him 500,000 men and several cities. He never regained strength, and the LORD struck him dead.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10

Jeroboam, who had fled to Egypt from King Solomon, returns when Israel sends for him. He and all Israel speak to Rehoboam asking for a lighter yoke and come back on the third day. The text notes that God's word spoken to him by Ahijah the Shilonite was being established through these events.

Chapter 11

Jeroboam is the one against whom Rehoboam's men turn back from going. He and his sons cast off the Levites from the priest's office to the LORD, and he appoints his own priests for the high places and for the male goat and calf idols he made.

Chapter 13

Jeroboam, son of Nebat and former servant of Solomon, is described by Abijah as having rebelled against his lord and made golden calves for Israel. He set 800,000 chosen men against Abijah and sent an ambush behind Judah, but God struck him and Israel, and he lost 500,000 men and several cities. He never recovered strength in Abijah's days, and the LORD struck him and he died.
