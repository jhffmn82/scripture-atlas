Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Job, Leviathan is portrayed as an invulnerable, fearsome creature that cannot be subdued and is king over all the sons of pride. In The Book of Isaiah, Leviathan is described as the fleeing and twisted serpent, or dragon in the sea, whom the LORD will punish and kill.

leviathan in The Book of Job

author unknown · about 574 BC · the work’s page · read it where leviathan first appears

Abstract written by a model

Leviathan is a fearsome creature described in Chapter 41 who cannot be caught, bound, tamed, traded, or made a servant, not even by Job. His sealed strong scales, stone-hard heart, strong neck, and terrifying teeth make him invulnerable, as swords, spears, arrows, and clubs count as nothing. He stirs the deep to boil, leaves trails like a threshing sledge, and is fearless and unequaled on earth, ending as king over all the sons of pride.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 41

Leviathan is described as a creature that cannot be caught with a hook, cord, or rope, will not plead or make a covenant, and cannot be tamed or traded. His strong scales, flashing eyes, fiery breath, stone-hard heart, and terrifying teeth are detailed, and swords, spears, arrows, and clubs are counted as nothing to him. He stirs the deep to boil, has no equal on earth, is made without fear, and is king over all the sons of pride.
