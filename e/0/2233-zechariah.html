Zechariah in The Book of Zechariah

Abstract written by a model

Zechariah is a prophet, son of Berechiah and grandson of Iddo, who receives the LORD's word. In the first chapter he is given the word in Darius's second year and sees night visions of horses, four horns and four craftsmen, asking the angel to explain them. Later he sees a flying scroll and an ephah basket carried by winged women. In Darius's fourth year he relays the LORD's rebuke to the people and priests, commanding true judgment and kindness.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

Zechariah, the prophet son of Berechiah and son of Iddo, receives the LORD's word in the eighth month of Darius's second year and again on the twenty-fourth day of the eleventh month. He sees a night vision of a man on a red horse, horses, four horns, and four craftsmen, and asks the angel who talked with him to explain them.

Chapter 5

The narrator lifts his eyes and sees the flying scroll, describing its dimensions when asked. He then sees the ephah basket, the woman inside it, and the two winged women lifting it. He asks the angel where it is being carried.

Chapter 7

Zechariah receives the LORD's word in the fourth year of King Darius, on the fourth day of Chislev. He relays the LORD's questions and rebuke to the people of the land and the priests, and the command to execute true judgment and show kindness.

Abstract written by a model

The narrator is a shepherd who speaks for the LORD. He feeds the oppressed flock destined for slaughter, takes staffs Favor and Union, and cuts off three shepherds in a month. He then quits, breaks both staffs, receives thirty silver pieces, throws them to the potter at the LORD's command, and is told to take a foolish shepherd's equipment.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

The narrator feeds the flock to be slaughtered, takes two staffs called Favor and Union, and cuts off three shepherds in one month. He refuses to keep feeding the flock, breaks both staffs, is paid thirty pieces of silver, and throws them to the potter in the LORD's house at the LORD's command. He is then told to take the equipment of a foolish shepherd.
