Hsü Yu

person · 1 work · 7 quoted facts · 5 connections

Abstract written by a model

Hsü Yu, tutored by Yeh Ch'üeh, rejects reputation as reality's shadow and refuses Yao's offers of the empire. Avoiding and fleeing Yao over Yao's charity, he argues against governing through charity, duty, and artificial schemes, likening takeover of government to a corpse impersonator usurping a cook's task. He is presented as a model of conduct superior to self-important ordinary men.

Hsü Yu is in one work on this site, Chuang Tzŭ. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER I.

Hsü Yu refuses Emperor Yao's offer of the throne because reputation is only the shadow of reality. He compares taking over government to a corpse impersonator stepping over sacrificial food to do a cook's work.

Kuan Chung being at the point of death, Duke Huan went to see him.

Hsü Yu says he is fleeing Yao because Yao thinks only of charity and may become a laughing-stock. He criticizes attempts to benefit the whole empire through charity, duty, and artificial schemes.

CHAPTER XXVIII.

Hsü Yu declined Yao's offer of the empire.
