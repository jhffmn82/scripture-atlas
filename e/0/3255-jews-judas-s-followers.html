Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of the Maccabees, Judas's kindred and followers support Judas Maccabaeus, fight for Israel, defeat Seron's army, fast and pray, and organize their forces. In The First Book of the Maccabees, Judas's followers fight Israel's ungodly enemies, defeat Gentile and Lysias's forces, cleanse and fortify the temple, and suffer from famine and deception. In The Second Book of the Maccabees, Maccabaeus's men destroy foreign altars, cleanse the sanctuary, defeat their enemies, capture Gazara, and praise the Lord. In The Second Book of the Maccabees, Judas's company attacks Nicanor's force with prayer, kills thirty-five thousand, and returns rejoicing after finding Nicanor dead.

Jews (Judas's followers) in The First Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Jews (Judas's followers) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Judas's followers are a Jewish army opposed by ungodly men of Israel. Though only three thousand and lacking desired armor and swords, they sound trumpets, rout the Gentile force, plunder its camp, and give thanks. Ten thousand later defeat Lysias' army at Bethsura, and they cleanse the temple. They fortify the sanctuary and Bethsura and burn the king's engines, but famine weakens them and the king's broken oath deceives them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Judas' army of three thousand men lacks the armor and swords they desired, yet sounds trumpets, defeats the Gentile force, and pursues it to Gazara, Idumaea, Azotus, and Jamnia. They burn and plunder the camp, taking gold, silver, blue, and sea purple, and sing thanksgiving. Later ten thousand men defeat Lysias' army at Bethsura, and the army goes up to cleanse the temple.

Chapter 6

The Jews defeated earlier armies, took plunder, and fortified the sanctuary and Bethsura. They fight bravely, burn the king's engines at Bethsura, and counter his engines at the sanctuary, but are weakened by famine, and are then deceived by the king's oath, which is broken.
