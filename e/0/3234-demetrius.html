Demetrius in The First Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 99 BC · the work’s page · read it where Demetrius first appears

Abstract written by a model

Demetrius, son of Seleucus, is a king who comes out of Rome with few men to a coastal city and reigns there. He refuses to see Antiochus and Lysias, whom the army kills, and takes his throne. He sends Bacchides, Alcimus and Nicanor against Israel. Rival of Alexander Epiphanes, he gathers forces and offers Jonathan concessions, which are distrusted because of past evils. He dies in battle against Alexander.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

Demetrius, son of Seleucus, came out of Rome with a few men to a city by the sea and reigned there. He refused to see the faces of Antiochus and Lysias, so the army killed them, and he took his throne. He then sent Bacchides and Alcimus to take vengeance on Israel, and later sent Nicanor to destroy the people.

Chapter 10

King Demetrius gathered great forces against Alexander and sent Jonathan a peace letter, then a larger offer of tax releases, control of the citadel, and funds. Jonathan and the people did not trust him because of earlier evils he had done. He fell in battle against Alexander, though the text first says Alexander's army fled.

author unknown · about 99 BC · the work’s page · read it where Demetrius first appears

Abstract written by a model

Demetrius is a king who married Ptolemy's daughter and won Jonathan over with gifts and tax relief. After dismissing most troops, who hated him, he was saved by Jonathan's men, then lied and treated Jonathan harshly, and was finally routed by Tryphon and Antiochus and fled.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Demetrius was offered Ptolemy's daughter and became king in the 167th year. He won Jonathan over with gifts, granted the Jews tax relief and territory, and then dismissed most of his troops, who came to hate him. After Jonathan's men saved him in Antioch he lied, estranged himself from Jonathan and treated him harshly, and was then routed by Tryphon and Antiochus and fled.

King Demetrius in The First Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 99 BC · the work’s page · read it where King Demetrius first appears

Abstract written by a model

King Demetrius is a king who answers Simon's request with a friendly letter, accepting his golden crown and palm branch, confirming earlier grants and the strongholds Simon built, forgiving faults and the crown tax, releasing the Jews from tribute, and offering court enrollment and peace. He later goes into Media against Tryphon, is seized by Arsaces' forces, and is held captive under guard.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

King Demetrius answers Simon's request with a letter addressed to Simon as high priest and friend of kings. He accepts the golden crown and palm branch, confirms earlier grants and the strongholds Simon built, forgives past faults and the crown tax, releases the Jews from tribute, and offers enrollment in his court and peace.

Chapter 14

King Demetrius gathers his forces and goes into Media for help against Tryphon, but Arsaces' prince strikes his army, seizes him, and Arsaces puts him under guard. The people's decree also notes that he had confirmed Simon in the high priesthood, made him one of his friends, and honored him greatly.

Demetrius in The Second Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 124 BC · the work’s page · read it where Demetrius first appears

Abstract written by a model

Demetrius, a king and son of Seleucus, lands at Tripolis with army and fleet, eliminates Antiochus and Lysias, then is persuaded by Alcimus to send Nicanor against Judas, later ordering Judas sent to Antioch as prisoner.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

Demetrius sails into the harbor of Tripolis with an army and fleet, takes possession of the country, and has made away with Antiochus and Lysias. Persuaded by Alcimus and his friends, he sends Nicanor to kill Judas and install Alcimus, and later, enraged by Alcimus's accusations, orders Nicanor to send Judas to Antioch as a prisoner.
