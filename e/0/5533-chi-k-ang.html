Chi K'ang

person · 3 works · 5 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: K'ang, Sir, sir, you

Abstract written by a model

In The Analects of Confucius, Book VI (Yung Yey), Chi K'ang asks Confucius whether Chung-yu, Ts'ze, and Ch'iu are fit to be employed as officers of government. In The Analects of Confucius, Book X: Heang Tang, Chi K'ang sent Confucius a present of physic. In The Analects of Confucius, Book XII (Yen Yuan), Chi K'ang repeatedly consults Confucius about government and asks how to eliminate thieves, whether killing the unprincipled benefits the principled, and how to promote what is good.

Chi K'ang in The Analects of Confucius, Book XII (Yen Yuan)

Abstract written by a model

Chi K'ang repeatedly consults Confucius about government. Distressed by many thieves in his state, he asks how to eliminate them and is told that his own lack of covetousness would stop theft. He also asks whether killing the unprincipled would benefit the principled, showing a desire for what is good.

Chi K'ang was distressed about the number of thieves in the state and asked Confucius how to do away with them. Confucius told him that if he were not covetous, the thieves would not steal even if rewarded to do so.
