Abstract written by a model

In Deuteronomy, the covenant of the LORD is made in Moab to establish Israel as his people and binds present and future generations. In The Book of Joshua, the covenant of the LORD is commanded to Israel, whose disobedience by serving other gods kindles the LORD's anger and causes them to perish from the land. In The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record, the covenant of the Lord is God's covenant with Abraham and the house of Israel, remembered after their scattering.

covenant of Moab in Deuteronomy

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where covenant of Moab first appears

Abstract written by a model

The covenant of Moab is a covenant made in Moab, in addition to Horeb's, sworn by the LORD to establish Israel as his people as promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. It binds present and future generations; keeping it brings prosperity, while abandoning it explains the land's ruin.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 29

This covenant is made in the land of Moab in addition to the one made at Horeb, and is sworn by the LORD to establish Israel as his people as he swore to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. It binds those present and those not present. Its words are to be kept so Israel may prosper, and abandoning it is the stated reason later nations will give for the land's ruin.

A covenant made by the Father with Abraham and the house of Israel. After Israel is driven, scattered, and long dispersed among the Gentiles, the Lord remembers it; the text asks God to remember and bless them forever through faith on Jesus Christ.
