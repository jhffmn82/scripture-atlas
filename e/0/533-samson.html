Samson

Abstract written by a model

Samson is the son of Manoah and his wife, promised before conception as a Nazirite to God from the womb, bound by a vow forbidding a razor on his head, and destined to begin saving Israel from the Philistines. Blessed by the LORD and moved by the LORD's Spirit, he is marked by great strength and quick anger. He sees a Philistine woman in Timnah and insists on marrying her, kills a lion bare-handed, and poses a riddle at his wedding feast to thirty companions. When his wife betrays the answer, he kills thirty men at Ashkelon, returns angrily to his father's house, and loses her to his companion. Refused entry when he later visits her, he burns the Philistines' crops with foxes and strikes them hip and thigh. Bound by the men of Judah, he breaks free and kills a thousand men with a donkey's jawbone, then is revived by water God provides. He judges Israel twenty years. In Gaza he carries off the city gate, then loves Delilah. After deceiving her three times, he reveals the secret of his hair, is shaved, blinded, bound in bronze fetters and made to grind at the prison mill. He prays to the LORD, brings down the house of Dagon on himself and the Philistines, and is buried in his father's burial site between Zorah and Eshtaol.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

Samson was promised before his conception as a Nazirite to God from the womb who would begin to save Israel from the Philistines. He was born to Manoah's wife and named Samson, grew, and was blessed by the LORD. The LORD's Spirit began to move him in Mahaneh Dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol.

Chapter 14

Samson sees a Philistine woman in Timnah and insists his parents get her for him as wife. He kills a lion bare-handed, eats honey from its carcass, and poses a riddle at his feast. After his wife betrays the answer, he kills thirty men at Ashkelon to pay his wager and returns angrily to his father's house, losing his wife to his companion.

Chapter 15

Samson visits his wife at wheat harvest with a young goat but is refused entry, then burns the Philistines' crops with foxes and strikes them hip and thigh in revenge for her death. He is bound by the men of Judah, breaks free when the LORD's Spirit comes on him, kills a thousand men with a donkey's jawbone, and is revived by water God provides. He is said to have judged Israel twenty years in the days of the Philistines.

Chapter 16

Samson goes in to a prostitute in Gaza, escapes an ambush by carrying off the city gate, and loves Delilah. After deceiving her three times, he tells her the secret of his Nazirite hair, is shaved, blinded, bound in bronze fetters and made to grind at the prison mill. He later prays to the LORD, brings down the house of Dagon on himself and the Philistines, and is buried in his father's burial site, having judged Israel twenty years.
