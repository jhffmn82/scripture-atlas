Abstract written by a model

In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I, Some Brâhmanas are described as arguing that living beings may be harmed without wrong. In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Brâhmanas are described as recipients of food prepared by householders for many persons. In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, Brâhmanas are described as religious practitioners who dispute truth and teach various views about liberation. In Sutrakritanga, Book II, Brâhmanas are described as sometimes acquiring property or killing living beings, with nonviolent teachers avoiding suffering and wandering in Samsâra. In Asa ki War (Asa di Var), Brahmans are described as a group with religious duties and condemned practices. In Asa ki War (Asa di Var), the Brahman is described as a Hindu guru who performs rituals, accepts payment, and reads fate papers. In Hymns of Guru Nanak, the Brahman is condemned for taking life and is contrasted with true Brahmanhood founded on reflection upon God. In Additional Sloks of Guru Nanak, a Brahman is described as one who knows God and attains salvation through devotion and self-restraint.

Brâhmanas are religious practitioners and Kriyâvâdins who perform customary acts and dispute truth. Some explain the world through Brahman or a primeval egg, while others deny true words or the Law and suffer misery. Brâhmanas in Sutrakritanga, Book II

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Brâhmanas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Brâhmanas are depicted both as acquirers of property and, in some cases, killers of living beings. They may retain or relapse into such habits after becoming monks. They can be robbed, insulted, or refused alms and should understand the thirteen kinds of committing sins. Those teaching violence face suffering and wandering in Samsâra; those teaching nonviolence avoid these consequences.

Brahmans in Asa ki War (Asa di Var)

Guru Nanak · the work’s page · read it where Brahmans first appears

Abstract written by a model

Brahmans are a group said to have a duty to read Veds, sound shells, perform Hindu worship, and enjoy viands. They bear sacrificial marks, reddish dhotis, blue clothes, and knives. The records condemn their capital, dealings, maintenance, bodies, actions, and hearts as false or defiled, calling them the world’s butchers. They take bread from malechhas, eat he-goats, and tightly exclude others from cooking squares while performing ablutions after meals.

Brahmans sound shells, perform Hindu worship, and enjoy viands, but the text calls their capital and dealings false. They wear sacrificial marks and reddish dhotis, carry knives, and maintain strict cooking boundaries while their bodies, actions, and hearts are described as defiled or false.

the Brahman in Asa ki War (Asa di Var)

Guru Nanak · the work’s page · read it where the Brahman first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Brahman is a guru of the Hindus. He buys and wears a cotton janeu, declares his role, twists and places neck strings on others, accepts hire for marriages, and reads fate papers. He purifies himself, receives untouched food, and eats while reading Sanskrit verses.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Slok XV .. Pauri XV

The Brahman purchases and puts on a cotton janeu, whispers that the Brahman is the guru of the Hindus, and later twists strings for the neck and puts them on others. The Brahman takes hire for marrying and shows a paper about the fate of a wedded pair, while wandering astray without moral strings.

Slok XIX .. Pauri XIX

The Brahman purifies himself, sits in a purified square, receives purified food that no one may touch, and eats while reading Sanskrit verses.
