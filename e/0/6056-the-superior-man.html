the superior man

Abstract written by a model

The superior man distinguishes his relations: kind but not loving to inferior creatures, loving but not affectionate to people generally, and affectionate to parents. He cultivates himself, follows righteousness without seeking reward, rejects treating natural pleasures as his nature or moral conduct as fate, despises rulers’ pomp, and seeks to restore an unchanging standard.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 45

The superior man is kind to inferior creatures but not loving toward them. He is loving toward people generally but not affectionate, and he is affectionate to his parents.

Part II / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 38

The superior man does not treat the pursuit of natural pleasures as simply his nature, nor moral conduct as merely the appointment of Heaven. He cultivates himself, follows the law of right without seeking emolument, despises the pomp of great rulers, and seeks to restore an unchanging standard.
