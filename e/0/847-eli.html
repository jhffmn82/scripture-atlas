Eli

person · 1 work · 15 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: Eli the priest, her father-in-law, my lord

Abstract written by a model

Eli is an elderly priest of the LORD at the temple in Shiloh who judged Israel for forty years and fathered Hophni and Phinehas. He first appears misreading Hannah's silent prayer as drunkenness, then blesses her and asks God to grant her petition; she later brings the child Samuel to him. Very old, Eli hears of his sons' misdeeds and rebukes them, but they do not listen. A man of God announces that his house will be cut off and both sons will die in one day. With his eyesight dim, Eli realizes the LORD is calling Samuel and instructs him how to answer. He accepts the judgment Samuel reports, saying it is the LORD's will. At ninety-eight and blind, he waits by the road, trembling for the ark. Told that his sons are dead and the ark captured, he falls backward from his seat, breaks his neck, and dies.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

1 Samuel / Chapter 1

Eli the priest sat by the doorpost of the LORD's temple and saw Hannah's moving lips, thinking she was drunk and telling her to get rid of her wine. After she explained her sorrow, he told her to go in peace and asked that the God of Israel grant her petition. Hannah later brought the child Samuel to him.

Chapter 2

Eli, the priest, is very old and hears of his sons' misdeeds. He rebukes them, asking who will intercede for a man who sins against the LORD, but they do not listen. He blesses Elkanah and Hannah, then receives a message from a man of God that his house will be cut off and his two sons will die in one day.

Chapter 3

Eli, whose eyes have grown dim, is woken three times by Samuel and realizes the third time that the LORD is calling the child. He tells Samuel to answer 'Speak, LORD; for your servant hears.' After Samuel reports the judgment against his house, Eli accepts it, saying 'It is the LORD. Let him do what seems good to him.'

Chapter 4

Eli was ninety-eight years old and blind, sitting by the road watching because his heart trembled for the ark. When the man told him that his sons were dead and the ark captured, he fell backward from his seat, broke his neck and died, having judged Israel forty years.
