Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, evildoers are portrayed as enemies of the LORD who plot against the righteous, attack the poor and needy, and ultimately wither and are destroyed. In Proverbs, wicked people are portrayed as perverse people who despise the LORD, stray, are repaid for their ways, and are brought down in calamity as their house is overthrown. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, sinners are portrayed as lacking true counsel and suffering calamities, while their goods and children prove fragile. In Habakkuk, the wicked are portrayed as surrounding the righteous, perverting justice, swallowing up the more righteous, and oppressing people for profit.

evildoers in Psalms

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where evildoers first appears

Abstract written by a model

Evildoers, also called the wicked, are enemies of the LORD who habitually plot against the righteous, borrow without repaying, and draw sword and bow against the poor and needy. The records urge not envying them: their weapons will turn on themselves, they wither like grass, and their future is cut off and destroyed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 37

Evildoers, also called the wicked, are not to be fretted over or envied, since they will soon wither like grass and vanish like smoke. They plot against the just, gnash their teeth, and draw the sword and bow against the poor and needy, but their swords will pierce their own hearts and their bows will break. They borrow and do not repay, and one who once spread like a green tree passed away and could not be found. Their future is cut off and transgressors are destroyed together.

The wicked are perverse people who despise the LORD, yet bow at the gates of the righteous. Made for the day of evil, they go astray, are repaid for their ways, and finally are brought down in calamity as their house is overthrown.

sinners in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where sinners first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sinners are portrayed as lacking true counsel despite their prudence. They suffer sevenfold more calamities than all creatures, such as death, strife, famine and plague, which were created for the wicked and caused the flood; their goods and children prove fragile.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 40

Sinners suffer sevenfold more than other creatures from death, bloodshed, strife, sword, calamities, famine, suffering, and plague. These things were created for the wicked, and the flood came because of them; the goods of the unjust dry up like a river and the children of the ungodly are like unhealthy roots on a sheer rock.
