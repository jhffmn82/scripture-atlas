Purim

Abstract written by a model

Purim, meaning "lots," is the observance of the fourteenth and fifteenth of Adar, named for the lots Haman cast and for the Jews' suffering. Mordecai and Esther confirmed it by letter as a memorial kept forever in every generation, with feasting, gladness, gifts to friends and portions to the poor. It closes with assembly, joy and gladness before God, and with the letter being brought by Dositheus and his son Ptolemeus in the fourth year of Ptolemeus and Cleopatra.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 9

Purim is the name given to the days of the fourteenth and fifteenth of Adar because of the lots Haman cast, and because of the words of Mordecai's letter and all the Jews suffered. It was established as a memorial to be kept forever in every generation, city, family, and province, with feasting, gladness, gifts to friends and portions to the poor. Mordecai and Esther confirmed it by letter and command.

Chapter 10

The days of observance are set for the fourteenth and fifteenth of Adar, with assembly, joy and gladness before God, forever among Israel. In the fourth year of Ptolemeus and Cleopatra, Dositheus, who said he was a priest and Levite, and his son Ptolemeus brought this letter of Purim, which they said was authentic and which Lysimachus son of Ptolemeus in Jerusalem had interpreted.
