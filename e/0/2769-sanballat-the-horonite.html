Sanballat the Horonite

person · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Sanballat

Abstract written by a model

Sanballat the Horonite is an adversary of Nehemiah and of the rebuilding of Jerusalem's wall. He is grieved when someone arrives to seek Israel's welfare, then ridicules the builders with Tobiah and Geshem, and is told he has no portion, right or memorial in Jerusalem. Growing angry, he mocks the Jews before the army of Samaria and joins Tobiah, Arabians, Ammonites and Ashdodites in conspiring to attack Jerusalem. He finally tries to lure Nehemiah to Ono four times, sends an open letter alleging rebellion, and hires Shemaiah to frighten him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Sanballat is greatly grieved when he hears that a man has come to seek the welfare of the children of Israel. With Tobiah and Geshem the Arabian he ridicules and despises the builders and asks whether they will rebel against the king, and the narrator tells him he has no portion, right or memorial in Jerusalem.

Chapter 4

Sanballat hears of the wall-building, becomes angry and indignant, and mocks the Jews before his brothers and the army of Samaria. Later he joins Tobiah, the Arabians, the Ammonites, and the Ashdodites in becoming very angry and conspiring to fight against Jerusalem.

Chapter 6

Sanballat, with Geshem, sends to Nehemiah four times inviting him to Ono with intent to harm him. The fifth time he sends a servant with an open letter accusing Nehemiah of planning rebellion and kingship. He and Tobiah had hired Shemaiah to frighten Nehemiah, and Nehemiah asks God to remember him for these works.
