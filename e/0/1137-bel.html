Bel

idol · 2 works · 6 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: an idol called Bel, idols made with hands

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Isaiah, Bel is shown bowing down with Nebo as a helpless idol carried into captivity. In Daniel (Greek), Bel is a Babylonian idol worshiped by King Cyrus, but Daniel proves it does not eat or drink, then overthrows it and its temple.

Bel in Daniel (Greek)

author unknown · about 164 BC · the work’s page · read it where Bel first appears

Abstract written by a model

Bel is a Babylonian idol of clay and brass, worshiped daily by King Cyrus with large offerings of flour, sheep, and wine. Daniel proves it never eats or drinks, and the king gives it to him; he overthrows it and its temple.

Chapter 14

Bel is a Babylonian idol, clay inside and brass outside, to which large amounts of flour, sheep, and wine are given daily. Daniel shows that it never eats or drinks, and the king hands it over to Daniel, who overthrows it and its temple.
