Taoism

Also called: Taoist, Taoist refutation, Taoists, ancient Taoism, modern Taoism

Abstract written by a model

Taoism is a dissenting, mystical view of TAO, contrasted with Confucianism’s worldly wisdom and practical duty by seeking the Absolute, Infinite, and Eternal. Antagonistic in writings of Chuang Tzŭ and Lieh Tzŭ, it develops mysticism and protest as Confucianism becomes established, including antinomianism. Its later modern form has TAO style, Buddhistic ceremonial, and is described as hybrid superstition.

_Note on the Philosophy of Chaps._ i-vii.

Taoism is contrasted with Confucianism as a system seeking the Absolute, Infinite, and Eternal rather than worldly wisdom and practical duty. Its mysticism is said to have developed as an opposition to established Confucianism, while its later debased form is mentioned without further explanation.

The triumph of Confucianism in the centuries which intervened between

Taoism is presented as antagonistic toward Confucianism in the writings of Chuang Tzŭ and Lieh Tzŭ, while sometimes using Confucius to advance Taoist arguments. It increasingly becomes mystical and adopts a policy of protest as Confucianism becomes established.
