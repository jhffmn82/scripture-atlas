Mihists

group · 1 work · 5 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Mihists of the south, ages of Mihists to come, the Mihists

Abstract written by a model

The Mihists are one of five competing schools, arising beside the Confucianists amid rivalry, fraud, and coercive confusion. They preach charity, duty to one’s neighbour, and universal love, even amid cangues and fetters. Following Mih Tzŭ and Yü’s toil, they practise self-mortification and relentless work, but later divide into disputing groups that call one another schismatics.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XI.

The Mihists arise alongside the Confucianists during a period of rivalry, fraud, recrimination, slander, and decadence. They also preach amid cangues and fetters after coercion has brought confusion into the empire.

CHAPTER XXIV.

The Mihists are one of the schools arguing with Hui Tzŭ about moral standards. Chuang Tzŭ groups them with the Confucianists and the followers of Yang and Ping as competing voices.

CHAPTER XXXIII.

Mihists followed Mih Tzŭ by wearing short serge jackets and straw sandals and working day and night in imitation of Yü. Different Mihist groups disputed doctrine, called one another schismatics, and quarrelled over philosophical questions.
