Jason

person · 1 work · 10 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: He who had driven many from their own country into exile, He who had thrown out a multitude unburied, Jason and his company, the vile Jason

Abstract written by a model

Jason, brother of Onias, is portrayed as an ungodly, false high priest. He supplants Onias by promising the king silver, builds a gymnasium under the citadel, and pushes his people toward the Greek way of life. Menelaus then outbids him, and he flees as a fugitive to the Ammonites. Later, believing a false rumor of the king's death, he attacks Jerusalem with a thousand men and slaughters his own citizens, yet fails to seize the government. He flees again, is imprisoned by Aretas, hated as a rebel against the laws and pursued from city to city. Seeking shelter among kin Lacedaemonians, he dies in exile, unmourned and unburied.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Jason, Onias's brother, supplanted him by promising the king three hundred sixty talents of silver plus eighty more, and offered a further one hundred fifty for a gymnasium and Antiochian citizenship for Jerusalem's inhabitants. He moved his people to the Greek way of life, sent money to the sacrifice of Hercules at Tyre that the bearers diverted to triremes, and received the king magnificently. After Menelaus outbid him he was supplanted and driven as a fugitive into the country of the Ammonites.

Chapter 5

Jason, on a false rumor of Antiochus's death, attacked Jerusalem with a thousand men and slaughtered his own citizens without mercy. He did not win control of the government and fled to the Ammonites. He then ended miserably, imprisoned by Aretas, hated and pursued, and died in exile after seeking shelter among the Lacedaemonians, unmourned and unburied.
