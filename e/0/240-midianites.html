Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, the Midianites are merchants who draw Joseph from the pit, sell him to the Ishmaelites, and are said to sell him in Egypt to Potiphar. In Numbers, the Midianites are attacked by Israel at the LORD’s command for deceiving Israel at Peor, with their people, cities, livestock, and goods destroyed or captured. In The Book of Judges, Midian is a group of invaders that oppressed Israel for seven years before being defeated and subdued by Gideon.

Midianites in Numbers

Abstract written by a model

The Midianites are a people whom the LORD commanded Moses to harass and strike for deceiving Israel at Peor and through Cozbi, who was slain with Zimri. Israel later attacks them, killing every male including five kings, burning cities and encampments, and taking women, children, livestock, and goods.

Chapter 25

The LORD commanded Moses to harass and strike the Midianites because they harassed Israel with wiles and deceived them in the matter of Peor and in the incident regarding Cozbi, daughter of the prince of Midian. The Midianite woman Cozbi was slain with Zimri.

Chapter 31

The Midianites are attacked by Israel, and every male is killed, including their five kings, Evi, Rekem, Zur, Hur, and Reba. Their cities and encampments are burned, and their women, children, livestock, and goods are taken. Moses says their women caused Israel to trespass at Peor through Balaam's counsel.

Midian in The Book of Judges

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Midian first appears

Abstract written by a model

Midian is a group of invaders that oppressed Israel for seven years, allied with the Amalekites and the children of the east. With countless men and camels like locusts, they destroyed the land's produce as far as Gaza and camped in the valley of Jezreel, at Moreh. The LORD promised Gideon he would strike them as one man. Terrified by trumpets, shouts and torches, they fled as their swords turned on each other, and their princes Oreb and Zeeb were killed. Gideon then struck the remaining fifteen thousand and captured and killed kings Zebah and Zalmunna. Midian was subdued and lifted its head no more, and the land had rest forty years.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

Midian, together with the Amalekites and the children of the east, prevailed over Israel, coming up with livestock, tents and camels like locusts and destroying the land's produce as far as Gaza. At the end of the unit they assembled and encamped in the valley of Jezreel. The LORD promised Gideon he would strike the Midianites as one man.

Chapter 7

The Midianites, with the Amalekites and the children of the east, lie in the valley like locusts, with camels as countless as sand. Terrified by the trumpets, shouts, and torches, they flee, and the LORD sets their swords against one another. They are pursued as far as Beth Shittah, Abel Meholah, and the Jordan, and their princes Oreb and Zeeb are killed.

Chapter 8

Midian is subdued before the children of Israel and lifts up its head no more. Its remaining army of about fifteen thousand men, left after one hundred twenty thousand fell, is struck by Gideon while feeling secure, and its two kings are captured and killed.
