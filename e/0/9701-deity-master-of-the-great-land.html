Abstract written by a model

In Kojiki (Records of Ancient Matters), Volume I (The Age of the Gods), Deity Master-of-the-Great-Land begins as a bag-carrying attendant, obtains Princess of Yakami, assumes the role Master-of-the-Great-Land, takes spouses, begets children, helps make the land, and yields the Central Land of Reed-Plains in exchange for a great abode. In Kojiki (Records of Ancient Matters), Volume I (The Age of the Gods), Deity Great-Name-Possessor survives trials with help from the Forward-Princess and a mouse, escapes with divine treasures, pursues the eighty Deities, and begins to make the land. In Kojiki (Records of Ancient Matters), Volume I (The Age of the Gods), Spirit-of-the-Living-Land is the role the Great Deity commands Deity Great-Name-Possessor to assume, with the Forward-Princess as his consort.

Abstract written by a model

Deity Master-of-the-Great-Land is a deity also called Deity Great-Name-Possessor, Deity-of-the-Reed-Plains, Deity of Eight-Thousand-Spears, and Deity-Spirit-of-the-Living-Land. Begotten by Deity Heavenly-Brandishing-Prince-Lord and Young-Princess-of-the-Small-Country, he begins among eighty brethren as their bag-carrying attendant; the White Hare of Inaba foretells that he will obtain Princess of Yakami. After pursuing his half-brethren, he is commanded to assume the role Master-of-the-Great-Land. He seeks spouses, woos Princess of Nuna-kaha in the Land of Koshi, pledges by the cup with Her Augustness the Forward-Princess, and weds several deities and princesses, begetting children including Deity Aji-shiki-taka-hiko-ne, Her Augustness High-Princess, Deity Thing-Sign-Master, and Deity Bird-Growing-Ears. Dwelling at Miho, he seeks help to make the land and asks how Deity illuminating the sea should be laid to rest. As ruler of the Central Land of Reed-Plains, he receives Deity Ame-no-ho-hi, then is asked to yield the land. After consulting his sons, he consents in exchange for a great abode and respectful service to his children, and is established at a Heavenly august abode.

Sect.

The Deity Master-of-the-Great-Land was begotten by the Deity Heavenly-Brandishing-Prince-Lord and the Young-Princess-of-the-Small-Country. He is also called the Deity Great-Name-Possessor, the Deity-of-the-Reed-Plains, the Deity of Eight-Thousand-Spears, and the Deity-Spirit-of-the-Living-Land.

Sect.

The Deity Master-of-the-Great-Land is taken to Inaba by his eighty brethren as an attendant carrying their bag. The White Hare of Inaba later foretells that he will obtain the Princess of Yakami.

Sect.

Master-of-the-Great-Land is a role the Great Deity commands the Deity Great-Name-Possessor to assume after pursuing his half-brethren.

Sect.

He goes to the Land of Koshi to woo the Princess of Nuna-kaha after failing to find a spouse in the Land of the Eight islands. He sings outside her house and later cohabits augustly with her on the following night.

Sect.

The Deity-of-Eight-Thousand-Spears prepares to leave Idzumo for the Land of Yamato because his Chief Empress is jealous. He sings about his departure and pledges himself by the cup with Her Augustness the Forward-Princess.

Sect.

The Deity Master-of-the-Great-Land wedded several deities and princesses and begot children, beginning with Deity Aji-shiki-taka-hiko-ne and continuing through Deity Toho-tsu-yama-zaki-tarashi.

Sect.

The Deity Master-of-the-Great-Land dwelt at the august cape of Miho in Idzumo. He encountered the Little-Prince-the-Renowned-Deity and sought to learn his name.

Sect.

Deity Master-of-the-Great-Land laments that he cannot make the land alone and asks with what Deity he can make it. He asks how Deity illuminating the sea should be reverently laid to rest.

Sect.

Deity Master-of-the-Great-Land receives Deity Ame-no-ho-hi, who curries favour with him after being sent to subdue the Earthly Deities.

Sect.

Deity Master-of-the-Great-Land was asked to yield the Central Land of Reed-Plains to the august child of the Heavenly Deities. After consulting his sons and hearing their agreement, he consented to yield the land in exchange for the establishment of a great abode for himself and respectful service to his children.
