land animals (Chapter 11 dietary rules) in Leviticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where land animals (Chapter 11 dietary rules) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Chapter 11's dietary rules for land animals: only those with a parted, cloven hoof that chew the cud may be eaten. The camel, hyrax, hare and pig each meet only one test, so they are unclean, untouchable carcasses, as are paw-walkers. Israel must distinguish clean from unclean.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Land animals may be eaten if they part the hoof, are cloven-footed, and chew the cud. The camel, hyrax, and hare chew the cud but lack a parted hoof, and the pig has a split hoof but does not chew the cud, so all four are unclean; their meat may not be eaten and their carcasses may not be touched. Animals that go on paws are also unclean.

unclean animals in Deuteronomy

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where unclean animals first appears

Abstract written by a model

Unclean animals are a category of creatures that may not be eaten. Chapter 14 lists the camel, hare and rabbit (chew cud, hoof not parted) and the pig (split hoof, no cud), then finfless water creatures, named birds, and winged creeping things; the pig's carcass may not be touched.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

Unclean animals include the camel, hare, and rabbit, which chew the cud but do not part the hoof, and the pig, which has a split hoof but does not chew the cud. Water creatures without fins and scales, many named birds (eagle, vulture, raven, owl, stork, bat and others), and all winged creeping things are also unclean and may not be eaten. The pig's carcass may not be touched.
