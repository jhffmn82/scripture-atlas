Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the Canaanites inhabited the land Israel was entering, and the LORD promised to drive them out while warning Israel against covenants and idolatry. In Numbers, the Canaanites dwelled by the sea, along the Jordan, in the valley and mountain, and defeated the Israelites at Hormah. In The Book of Joshua, the Canaanites remained in the land, were subjected to forced labor, and possessed cities and iron chariots. In The Book of Judges, the Canaanites were struck down in several regions, remained among Israelite tribes, and were sometimes put to forced labor.

Canaanites in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · about 600 BC · the work’s page · read it where Canaanites first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Canaanites are a group who remained in the land despite Israelite tribes' efforts. In Gezer they were not driven out by Ephraim and became servants in forced labor. In Manasseh's allotment they held cities such as Beth Shean and Megiddo. Once Israel grew strong they were subjected to forced labor, not expelled. Valley Canaanites had iron chariots, yet Joshua said they would be driven out.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 16

The Canaanites who lived in Gezer were not driven out by the children of Ephraim. They continued to dwell in Ephraim's territory to the time of writing and became servants doing forced labor.

Chapter 17

The Canaanites remained in the cities Manasseh could not take, including Beth Shean, Ibleam, Dor, Endor, Taanach and Megiddo. When Israel grew strong they were put to forced labor rather than utterly driven out. The children of Joseph noted the valley Canaanites had iron chariots, and Joshua said they would be driven out regardless.
