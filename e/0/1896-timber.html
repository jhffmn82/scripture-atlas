Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, cedar and cypress timber is provided by Hiram, floated by raft to Solomon's specified place, and exchanged for wheat and oil. In The Second Book of Chronicles, timber of cedar, cypress, and algum is used for terraces of the LORD's and king's houses and for harps, and Solomon requests it from Huram for transport to Jerusalem.

Abstract written by a model

Solomon requests cedar from Lebanon, and Hiram agrees to provide cedar and cypress timber according to all Solomon's desire. It is to be brought down to the sea, made into rafts, floated to the place Solomon specifies, and broken up there for him to receive. Hiram gave the timber, and in exchange Solomon supplied wheat and oil for Hiram's household.

Abstract written by a model

Timber of cedar, cypress, and algum grows in Lebanon and is used for terraces of the LORD's and king's houses and for harps. Solomon requests it from Huram, offering food and drink; Huram agrees to cut it and raft it to Joppa for carrying to Jerusalem.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Solomon asks Huram for cedar, cypress, and algum trees from Lebanon, noting that Huram's servants know how to cut timber there, and offers wheat, barley, wine, and oil to the cutters. Huram agrees to cut as much wood as needed and bring it in rafts by sea to Joppa, from where Solomon's people will carry it up to Jerusalem.
