Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the Feast of Unleavened Bread is a seven-day observance commemorating the LORD bringing Israel's armies out of Egypt, requiring unleavened bread, holy convocations, and perpetual yearly observance. In Leviticus, the feast of unleavened bread begins on the fifteenth day of the first month and lasts seven days, with unleavened bread, holy convocations, and daily fire offerings. In Numbers, the Feast of Unleavened Bread begins on the fifteenth day of the first month and lasts seven days, with unleavened bread, holy convocations, and daily offerings. In Deuteronomy, the feast of unleavened bread is a seven-day observance commemorating the hasty departure from Egypt, with unleavened bread, no yeast within the borders, and a solemn assembly. In The Second Book of Chronicles, a very great assembly keeps the feast of unleavened bread in Jerusalem for seven days and then extends it for another seven days with gladness.

Feast of Unleavened Bread in Exodus

author unknown · about 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Feast of Unleavened Bread first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Feast of Unleavened Bread is a seven-day observance held in the month Abib, commemorating the LORD bringing Israel's armies out of Egypt. Across the records, it requires eating unleavened bread, with no yeast or leavened bread in houses or within Israel's borders; anyone eating leavened bread is cut off from Israel. The first and seventh days are holy convocations with no work except food preparation, and a feast to the LORD falls on the seventh day. It is kept forever, yearly in the land, explained to sons, and no one appears before God empty-handed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 12

The feast lasts seven days, with no yeast in the houses and holy convocations on the first and seventh days, with no work except preparing food. Anyone who eats leavened bread in that time is to be cut off from Israel, and the feast is to be kept forever because the LORD brought Israel's armies out on that day.

Chapter 13

Unleavened bread is to be eaten for seven days in the month Abib, with no leavened bread or yeast seen within Israel's borders and a feast to the LORD on the seventh day. It is to be kept yearly in the land and explained to sons as being because of what the LORD did when Israel left Egypt.

Chapter 23

The Israelites are to eat unleavened bread for seven days at the appointed time in the month Abib, because they came out of Egypt in it. No one is to appear before God empty-handed.

The Feast of Unleavened Bread begins on the fifteenth day of the first month and lasts seven days, during which unleavened bread is eaten.
