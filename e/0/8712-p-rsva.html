Abstract written by a model

In the Uttaradhyayana Sutra, Pârsva is a thoroughly enlightened, omniscient Gina, Arhat, and prophet of the Law whose teaching recognises four vows and permits under and upper garments. In the Kalpa Sutra, Pârsva is an Arhat and the people’s favourite who attains Kevala and final liberation.

Pârsva in Kalpa Sutra

Bhadrabâhu · the work’s page · read it where Pârsva first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pârsva is an Arhat and the people’s favourite. After living in Prânata Kalpa for twenty Sâgaropamas, he descends and is conceived, is born in Benares as Vâmâ’s son, and lives thirty years as a householder. He leaves home by tearing out his hair, renounces in Visâlâ, travels to Asramapada followed by gods, men, and Asuras, and approaches Asoka. After fasting without water, he attains Kevala under a Dhâtaki tree. He has eight ganas and ganadharas and large communities of Sramanas, nuns, and lay votaries. He lives something less than seventy years as a Kevalin, dies on mount Sammeta after a month’s fast, and obtains final liberation in Visâkhâ.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Life of Pârsva.

Pârsva was an Arhat and the people's favourite. He lived thirty years as a householder, eighty-three days in a state inferior to perfection, something less than seventy years as a Kevalin, and fully seventy years as a Sramana, living one hundred years in all.
