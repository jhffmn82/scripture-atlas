Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Samuel, Samuel served as prophet, judge, seer, and kingmaker of Israel. In Psalms, Samuel called on the LORD’s name, received his answer, and kept his testimonies and statute. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Samuel was a faithful prophet who established a kingdom, anointed princes, judged the people, and prophesied the king’s end.

Samuel in The First Book of Samuel

author unknown · about 930 to 721 BC · the work’s page · read it where Samuel first appears

Abstract written by a model

Samuel is a man whose life spans from birth to death and beyond, serving as prophet, judge, seer and kingmaker of Israel. Born to Hannah after the LORD remembered her, he was named because she asked him of the LORD, and was given to the LORD for all his life. As a child he served before Eli the priest at Shiloh in a linen ephod, grew in favor with the LORD and men, and heard the LORD call him, delivering a message of judgment against Eli's house. All Israel from Dan to Beersheba knew him as an established prophet whose words did not fall to the ground. He later exhorted Israel to put away foreign gods, gathered them at Mizpah, prayed and offered a lamb, and set up the Ebenezer stone. He judged Israel all his life on a yearly circuit of Bethel, Gilgal and Mizpah, returning to his home at Ramah, where he built an altar. When old, he made his sons Joel and Abijah judges, but the elders asked for a king. Displeased, he prayed, warned the people of a king's ways, and, told by the LORD to comply, anointed Saul, first as prince and then as king, and wrote the regulations of the kingdom in a book. In his farewell he stated he was old and gray-headed, had walked before Israel from youth, and had taken nothing from anyone, which the people confirmed; he promised to keep praying and instructing them. His relationship with Saul then broke down: he rebuked Saul at Gilgal for offering the sacrifice before he arrived, then for sparing Amalek, declaring that obedience is better than sacrifice and that the kingdom was torn from Saul. He cut Agag in pieces, went to Ramah, and did not see Saul again before Saul's death, though he mourned him. Told to stop mourning, he went to Bethlehem and anointed David among his brothers. David later came to him at Ramah and they lived at Naioth, where Samuel stood as head of the company of the prophets. He died and was buried at his house in Ramah, mourned by all Israel. Even so, the woman at Endor brought him up, appearing as an old man in a robe, and he told Saul the LORD had become his adversary, had given the kingdom to David, and that Saul and his sons would join him the next day.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

14 sections; each heading opens the text

1 Samuel / Chapter 1

Samuel was born to Hannah after the LORD remembered her, and she named him because she had asked him of the LORD. After he was weaned, he was brought young to the LORD's house in Shiloh and given to the LORD for as long as he lives.

Chapter 2

Samuel, still a child, serves the LORD before Eli the priest, clothed in a linen ephod. His mother brings him a little robe every year. He grows and increases in favor with both the LORD and men.

Chapter 3

Samuel, a child ministering to the LORD before Eli, hears the LORD call him three times and mistakes the voice for Eli's until Eli instructs him how to answer. He then receives the LORD's message of judgment against Eli's house, fears to tell Eli, but reports it all when Eli presses him. He grows, none of his words fall to the ground, and all Israel from Dan to Beersheba knows he is established as the LORD's prophet.

Chapter 7

Samuel exhorts Israel to put away foreign gods and serve the LORD only, gathers them at Mizpah, prays for them, and judges them there. He offers a suckling lamb as a burnt offering and cries to the LORD, who answers him, then sets up the Ebenezer stone. He judges Israel all his life, circuiting Bethel, Gilgal, and Mizpah, and returns to Ramah, where he builds an altar to the LORD.

Chapter 8

Samuel, now old, made his sons judges over Israel. He was displeased by the elders' request for a king and prayed to the LORD, then relayed the LORD's words and a warning about the king's ways to the people. When they refused to listen, he repeated their words to the LORD, was told to make them a king, and sent the men of Israel home.

Chapter 9

Samuel, the seer, was told by the LORD a day earlier that a man of Benjamin would come to be anointed prince over Israel, and he recognizes Saul when he sees him. He invites Saul to eat with him at the high place, tells him the donkeys are found, seats him in the best place, and has the reserved portion set before him. The next morning he sends Saul's servant ahead so he can give Saul God's message.

Chapter 10

Samuel pours oil on Saul's head, kisses him, and gives him signs and instructions, including to go down ahead of him to Gilgal and wait seven days. He gathers the people at Mizpah, rebukes them for rejecting God, presents Saul as the LORD's chosen, and writes the regulations of the kingdom in a book laid up before the LORD.

Chapter 11

Samuel is named alongside Saul in the summons to war. After the victory the people ask him to bring forward those who doubted Saul's reign, and he instead tells them to go to Gilgal to renew the kingdom there.

Chapter 12

Samuel reminds Israel that a king now walks before them, declares he is old and gray-headed, and has the people affirm he has taken nothing and oppressed no one. He recounts the LORD's saving acts, calls for thunder and rain as a sign, and, after the people's confession, tells them not to fear and promises to keep praying for them and to instruct them in the good and right way.

Chapter 13

Samuel had set the seven-day time for Saul's waiting at Gilgal but did not arrive until Saul had finished the burnt offering. He told Saul he had acted foolishly and not kept the LORD's commandment, and that his kingdom would not continue. He then left Gilgal for Gibeah of Benjamin.

Chapter 15

Samuel relays the LORD's command to Saul to destroy Amalek, then cries to the LORD all night after hearing that Saul has been rejected. He confronts Saul at Gilgal, declares that obedience is better than sacrifice, and announces that the kingdom is torn from Saul. He cuts Agag in pieces before the LORD, goes to Ramah, and never sees Saul again before Saul's death, though he mourns for him.

Chapter 16

Samuel is told to stop mourning for Saul and, fearing Saul will kill him, follows the LORD's instruction to take a heifer and say he has come to sacrifice. He goes to Bethlehem, sanctifies Jesse and his sons, wrongly assumes Eliab is the anointed one, and then anoints David on the LORD's word before returning to Ramah.

Chapter 19

David comes to Samuel at Ramah and tells him all that Saul has done, and the two go to live in Naioth. When Saul's messengers arrive, Samuel is seen standing as head over the company of the prophets who are prophesying.

Chapter 28

Samuel is noted as dead and buried in Ramah, mourned by all Israel. Brought up by the woman at Endor, appearing as an old man in a robe, he asks Saul why he has been disturbed, and tells him the LORD has become his adversary, has given the kingdom to David, and will deliver Israel to the Philistines, with Saul and his sons joining Samuel the next day.

Samuel in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · about 179 BC · the work’s page · read it where Samuel first appears

Abstract written by a model

Samuel is a prophet of the Lord, loved by him. He established a kingdom, anointed princes over the people, and judged the congregation by the Lord's law. His faithfulness proved him a prophet. He called on the Lord with a suckling lamb offering when enemies pressed. Before death he testified he had taken no one's goods, and no one accused him; after falling asleep he prophesied and showed the king his end.

Chapter 46

Samuel, the prophet of the Lord and loved by him, established a kingdom and anointed princes over the people. He judged the congregation by the law of the Lord, was proved a prophet by his faithfulness, and called on the Lord with a suckling lamb offering when enemies pressed him. Before his death he testified that he had taken no one's goods, not even a sandal, and no one accused him; even after falling asleep he prophesied and showed the king his end.
