Abstract written by a model

Philippians is a group of believers, the saints in Jesus Christ at Philippi, together with their overseers and servants. Their defining trait is partnership with Paul, the letter's author, in the Good News from the first day until now. They share in his grace, bonds, and defense of the Good News, and they were the only assembly to share with him in giving and receiving, sending support more than once to his need in Thessalonica. They have always obeyed him, and they have been granted both belief in Christ and suffering for him. The records unfold in four stages. First come greetings, thanks, and a call to stand firm and live worthily. Next comes an exhortation to humility, unity, and shining as lights among a crooked and perverse generation, with praise for their obedience and a request to welcome Epaphroditus. Then they are told to rejoice, beware of false teachers, and imitate Paul, since their citizenship is in heaven. The letter ends by urging steadfastness, prayer over anxiety, and praising their support of Paul.

Philippians in Paul’s Letter to the Philippians

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

The saints at Philippi, with their overseers and servants, are greeted and thanked for their partnership in the Good News from the first day until now. They are partakers with Paul of grace in his bonds and in the defense and confirmation of the Good News. They are urged to live worthily, stand firm in one spirit with one soul, not be frightened by adversaries, and are told they share the same conflict Paul has.

Chapter 2

The Philippian believers are exhorted to be like-minded, humble, and attentive to others' interests, and to work out their salvation with fear and trembling. They are told to avoid complaining and arguing so they may be blameless children of God shining as lights and holding up the word of life. They are praised for having always obeyed, and are asked to rejoice with Paul and to welcome Epaphroditus honorably.

Chapter 3

The brothers are told to rejoice in the Lord, to beware of the dogs, evil workers, and false circumcision, and to be imitators of Paul together. They are urged to walk by the same rule and be of the same mind, and are told their citizenship is in heaven, where they wait for a Savior.

Chapter 4

The Philippians are told to stand firm in the Lord, rejoice always, pray rather than be anxious, and practice what they learned from Paul. They are praised for reviving their thought for him, sharing in his affliction, and being the only assembly to share in giving and receiving with him, even sending to his need in Thessalonica.
