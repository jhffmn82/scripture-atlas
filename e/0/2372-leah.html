Abstract written by a model

Leah is Laban's elder daughter, weak-eyed and less loved than Rachel, given to Jacob as his wife in Rachel's place. Seeing she is hated, the LORD opens her womb, and she bears Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah, reading each birth in light of hoping for her husband's love. After she stops bearing, she gives her servant Zilpah to Jacob, then hires Jacob for a night with mandrakes, conceiving Issachar, Zebulun, and a daughter, Dinah. Finally, she joins Rachel in agreeing to leave Laban, who searches her tent for his gods without finding them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 29

Leah, Laban's elder daughter, has weak eyes and is given to Jacob in the evening in place of Rachel. The LORD sees she is hated and opens her womb, and she bears Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah, interpreting each birth in relation to the LORD and to her hope for her husband's love. After Judah she stops bearing.

Chapter 30

Leah, having stopped bearing, gives her servant Zilpah to Jacob and names Gad and Asher. She hires Jacob for a night with her son Reuben's mandrakes, conceives Issachar and Zebulun, and bears a daughter, Dinah.

Chapter 31

Leah, together with Rachel, is called by Jacob to the field and agrees to leave, saying their father has sold them and used up their money. Laban searches her tent for his gods but does not find them.
