Abstract written by a model

Gideon, also called Jerub-Baal or Jerubbaal, is a man of Israel, son of Joash the Abiezrite, from Ophrah in Manasseh. He describes his family as the poorest in Manasseh and himself as the least in his father's house. The LORD's angel calls him a mighty man of valor and commissions him to save Israel from Midian. After receiving a sign, he builds an altar named "The LORD is Peace" and destroys Baal's altar and the Asherah by night, earning the name Jerub-Baal. Filled with the LORD's Spirit, he gathers Abiezer and several tribes, asks for fleece signs, and reduces his army to three hundred men. He leads the trumpet-and-pitcher attack on the Midianite camp, receives the heads of Oreb and Zeeb, placates Ephraim, and pursues Zebah and Zalmunna. He punishes Succoth and Penuel for refusing bread and kills the two kings in revenge for his brothers killed at Tabor. He refuses to rule Israel, saying the LORD shall rule, yet makes an ephod from gold that becomes a snare to him and his house. He has seventy sons by many wives, plus Abimelech by a concubine in Shechem, and Jether is his firstborn. He dies at a good old age and is buried in Ophrah in his father's tomb. Jotham later notes that his house was repaid with the killing of his sons despite his deliverance of Shechem.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 6

Gideon, son of Joash the Abiezrite, was threshing wheat in a wine press to hide it from the Midianites when the LORD's angel called him a mighty man of valor and commissioned him to save Israel; he protested that his family was the poorest in Manasseh and that he was the least in his father's house. He asked for a sign, received it when fire consumed his offering, built an altar, and obeyed the LORD by night in destroying Baal's altar and the Asherah, after which he was named Jerub-Baal. Empowered by the LORD's Spirit, he blew a trumpet and gathered Abiezer, Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun and Naphtali, then twice asked God for a fleece sign, which God granted.

Chapter 7

Gideon, also called Jerubbaal, camps at the spring of Harod, reduces his army at the LORD's direction, and goes down to the Midianite camp with Purah his servant. After hearing the dream and its interpretation he worships, divides the three hundred men into three companies, and leads the trumpet-and-pitcher attack. He then summons the men of Ephraim to take the waters to the Jordan and receives the heads of Oreb and Zeeb.

Chapter 8

Gideon placates the men of Ephraim, pursues Zebah and Zalmunna with three hundred faint men, punishes Succoth and Penuel for refusing bread, and kills the two kings in revenge for his brothers killed at Tabor. He refuses to rule Israel, saying the LORD shall rule, but makes an ephod from collected gold that becomes a snare to him and his house. He has seventy sons by many wives and a son Abimelech by a concubine in Shechem, and dies in a good old age, buried in Ophrah.

Chapter 9

Jerubbaal is the father of seventy sons and of Abimelech. Jotham says he fought for Shechem, risked his life, and delivered them from Midian, yet his house was repaid with the killing of his sons.
