benevolent government

Also called: a benevolent rule, government that could not endure to witness the sufferings of men

Abstract written by a model

Benevolent government arises from concern for human suffering and is necessary to secure tranquil order in the kingdom. It lets benevolence overspread the kingdom and attracts people as water flows downward. Though a standard for princes, it may go unpractised; ministers enriching a prince without it are rejected by Confucius.

Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou) / Part I / Chapter 1

Benevolent government is necessary for the principles of Yao and Shun to secure the tranquil order of the kingdom. It arises from concern for human suffering and allows benevolence to overspread the kingdom.

Chapter 9

A benevolent rule attracts the people, who turn toward it as water flows downward and wild beasts fly to the wilderness.

Chapter 14

Benevolent government is presented as a standard that princes may fail to practise. Ministers who enrich a prince lacking it are rejected by Confucius.
