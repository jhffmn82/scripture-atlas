Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Solomon, virtue makes childlessness better than a large family, grants immortality through memory, is recognized before God and men, is imitated when present, longed for when gone, and marches crowned in contests for undefiled prizes. In Mencius, Book VII (Jin Xin), virtue is loved by able and virtuous monarchs, expected among well-provisioned people, and earnestly practiced from cock-crowing rather than pursuing gain.
