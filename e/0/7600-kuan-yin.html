Kuan Yin

person · 1 work · 9 quoted facts · 2 connections

Abstract written by a model

Kuan Yin is presented as a sage and, in some accounts, Lao Tzŭ’s disciple entrusted with publishing the Tao-Tê-Ching. He teaches that perfection comes from absolute purity rather than cunning, and that the perfect man gains security through unity, inner strength, harmonized virtue, and partnership with God. An enthusiastic follower of TAO, he builds a system on nothingness and ONE, advocating gentleness, humility, receptivity, non-aggression, and situational response. The text calls him a true Sage.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIX.

Kuan Yin teaches Lieh Tzŭ that the perfect man becomes secure through unity, inner strength, harmonized virtue, and partnership with God. He contrasts natural intelligence, which produces virtue, with artificial intelligence, which produces cunning.

CHAPTER XXXIII.

Kuan Yin became an enthusiastic follower of TAO and based his system on nothingness with ONE as its criterion. He taught gentleness, humility, receptivity, non-aggression, and responding to circumstances without adopting absolute positions; the text calls him a true Sage of old.
