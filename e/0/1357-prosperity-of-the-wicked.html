Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the prosperity of the wicked is envied by the psalmist, Asaph. In The Book of Jeremiah, the prosperity of the wicked is questioned by the speaker.

is questioned by the speaker [speaker asks why it happens] The speaker raises it as the central question: why the way of the wicked prospers and the treacherous are at ease. It is described as the LORD's own planting, with the wicked taking root, growing and bearing fruit, though only in word and not in heart. The speaker asks that they be pulled out for the day of slaughter, and the LORD's reply speaks of wheat sown yielding thorns and of fruits to be ashamed of.
