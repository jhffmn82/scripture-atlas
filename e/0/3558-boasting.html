Abstract written by a model

Boasting is a recurring theme in which Paul argues its proper object is the Lord and, if he must boast, it concerns weakness. In Chapter 10 he refuses to boast beyond his God-appointed limits or in others' labors, holding that the Lord's commendation matters. In Chapter 11 he boasts reluctantly, as foolishness, since many boast after the flesh, compares credentials and sufferings with rivals, and resolves to boast of weakness, citing the Damascus basket escape. In Chapter 12 he calls it unprofitable, says the Corinthians compelled him, and boasts only of weaknesses and the man caught up to Paradise.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10

Paul says he will not boast beyond proper limits or in other men's labors, but only within the boundaries God appointed to him. He quotes that whoever boasts should boast in the Lord. He adds that approval comes not to the one who commends himself but to the one whom the Lord commends.

Chapter 11

Paul boasts reluctantly and as foolishness, saying it is not according to the Lord, because many boast after the flesh. He compares his credentials and sufferings with those of his rivals, and then resolves to boast of the things that concern his weakness. He closes with the Damascus escape in a basket as an example.

Chapter 12

Paul calls boasting not profitable and says he has become foolish in it because the Corinthians compelled him. He will boast on behalf of the man caught up to Paradise and in his weaknesses but not on his own behalf, refraining so that no one thinks more of him than what they see or hear from him.
