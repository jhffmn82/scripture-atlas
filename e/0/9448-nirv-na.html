Abstract written by a model

In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I, Nirvâna is the final liberation reached through disciplined detachment and a good method of religious death. In Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Nirvâna and liberation is Mahâvîra’s path through restraint and virtues, culminating in a complete, unobstructed, infinite, supreme state. In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, Nirvâna is the chief object of the Law and a peaceful state reached through purity, restraint, and freedom from Karman. In Sutrakritanga, Book II, Nirvâna is a goal of the religious path associated with perfection, liberation, and final delivery. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, the highest Nirvâna is the place reached by the pure soul firmly standing in the Law. In Uttaradhyayana Sutra, Nirvâna is the great happiness and final deliverance reached through right knowledge, faith, conduct, austerities, and destruction of Karman.

Abstract written by a model

Nirvâna is the final liberation reached through disciplined detachment and one of three good methods of religious death, after enduring bodily dissolution. Mahâvîra proceeds undisturbed through hardship toward it and ends liberated, free from delusion.

A restrained ascetic reaches the end of life while unattached to external objects and choosing one of the described good methods of entering Nirvâna. The methods are presented as ways of attaining the highest law after bearing the pains involved in the dissolution of the body.

Nirvâna in Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Nirvâna first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nirvâna is the chief object of the Law and a state consisting in peace. It is reached through purity of heart, restraint, and freedom from Karman, especially by ceasing to injure living beings. It is also associated with abstaining from false speech, refusing what is not freely given, carefulness, and pursuing real good. Heretical doctrines and careless conduct do not lead to it. Mahâvîra, praised as Gñâtriputra, is presented as the best among those who taught Nirvâna.

Nirvâna in Uttaradhyayana Sutra

Hermann Jacobi · the work’s page · read it where Nirvâna first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nirvâna is the great happiness and final deliverance toward which the Law leads: freedom from pain, perfection, and an eternal safe, happy, quiet place free from old age, death, disease, and sorrow. It is difficult to approach, but great sages and self-subduing ascetics reach it through simplicity, right knowledge, faith, conduct, austerities, ending activity, and destroying Karman. Those who attain it end transmigration, the stream of existence, misery, and all sorrow.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Nineteenth Lecture.

Nirvâna is the great happiness toward which the Law leads. Mrigâputra reaches perfection and liberation, described as famous in the three worlds.

Twenty-First Lecture.

Nirvâna is the path entered by an ascetic who has ceased to act and practises simplicity. Samudrapâla reached exemption from transmigration after following this path.

Twenty-Third Lecture.

Nirvâna is freedom from pain, perfection, and a safe, happy, and quiet place. It is eternal and difficult to approach, and the sages who reach it are free from sorrow and have ended the stream of existence.

Twenty-Fifth Lecture.

A pious, self-subduing ascetic who reduces his flesh and blood and reaches Nirvâna is called a true Brâhmana.

Twenty-Eighth Lecture.

Final deliverance is the goal reached through right knowledge, faith, conduct, and austerities. Without deliverance there is no perfection.

Twenty-Ninth Lecture.

Perfection is obtained when the soul destroys Karman, ceases activity, and completes the required spiritual practices. The soul then ends all misery.
