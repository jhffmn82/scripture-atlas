Abstract written by a model

The kingdom is a political whole rooted in the State and shaped by the conduct of rulers, princes, people, and families. Walls, weapons, land, and wealth do not make it safe; righteousness, law, propriety, and learning matter more. Violations by superiors and inferiors bring calamity, while extreme oppression causes it to perish and lesser oppression weakens it. It is gained by gaining the people: a benevolent ruler or prince attracts submission, faces no opponent, and may give laws to the kingdom. The kingdom can be ruined if not improved and is portrayed as drowning, requiring rescue through right principles. Its transformation culminates when people submit with delight, Gu Sou finds delight in what is good, and fathers and sons are established in their respective duties.

Mencius, Book IV (Li Lou) / Part I / Chapter 1

A kingdom is not made safe merely by complete walls, abundant weapons, extended cultivable land, or accumulated stores and wealth. It faces calamity when superiors violate righteousness and inferiors violate penal laws, and it perishes when propriety is not observed and learned.

Chapter 2

A kingdom perishes when its ruler carries oppression of the people to the highest pitch. If the oppression stops short of that pitch, the kingdom is weakened.

Chapter 4

When a man's person is correct, the whole kingdom will turn to him with recognition and submission.

Chapter 5

The kingdom has the State as its root.

Chapter 6

The whole kingdom is affected by whoever any one State affects.

Chapter 7

The kingdom is governed by patterns of submission among princes. A prince who loves benevolence can have no opponent throughout the kingdom and may eventually give laws to it.

Chapter 9

The kingdom is gained by gaining the people. A ruler who loves benevolence would become sovereign because the other princes would drive the people toward him.

Chapter 13

The kingdom's fathers joined king Wen when Bo Yi and Tai Gong followed him, and the kingdom's sons would then have no reason to go to anyone else. Any prince who practiced the government of king Wen would within seven years be giving laws to the kingdom.

Chapter 17

The whole kingdom is described by Chun Yu Kun as drowning. Mencius says it must be rescued with right principles.

Chapter 28

The whole kingdom turned in great delight to submit to an individual, but Shun regarded this as insignificant. When Gu Sou found delight in what was good, the kingdom was transformed and fathers and sons were established in their respective duties.
