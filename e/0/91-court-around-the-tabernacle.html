Abstract written by a model

The court is the enclosed space around the tabernacle, one hundred cubits long, fifty wide and five high. It is enclosed by fine twined linen hangings hung on pillars. Most important is its layout: the south and north sides run one hundred cubits with twenty pillars and twenty bronze sockets each, the west side is fifty cubits with ten pillars and sockets, and the east side is fifty cubits with the gate in the middle and fifteen-cubit hangings on each side. The pillars stand in bronze sockets, with silver hooks, fillets and overlaid capitals, and bronze pins. The gate is a twenty-cubit screen of blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen on four pillars. Chapter 27 prescribes it; Chapter 38 recounts its construction.

Chapter 27

The court is to be one hundred cubits long, fifty wide, and five cubits high, enclosed by fine twined linen hangings on pillars set in bronze sockets. Its pillars have silver hooks and fillets, its pins are bronze, and its gate is a twenty-cubit screen of blue, purple, scarlet and fine twined linen.

Chapter 38

Enclosed by fine twined linen hangings: one hundred cubits on the south and north sides, fifty on the west and east, with twenty pillars and sockets per long side and ten on the west. Pillars stood on bronze sockets with silver hooks, fillets, and capital overlays, and the gate had a 20-cubit embroidered screen of blue, purple, scarlet, and linen on four pillars. The court's sockets and pins were made of bronze.
