Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Chronicles, Jeremiah speaks from the LORD's mouth, and his prophecy concerning Judah's desolation and Cyrus's rise is fulfilled. In The Second Book of the Maccabees, Jeremiah prays for the people and holy city, warns exiles, and hides the tabernacle, ark, and incense altar. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Jeremiah is presented as a prophet sanctified in the womb who foretold Jerusalem's destruction and was mistreated by Judah's kings. In The Book of Jeremiah, Jeremiah serves as the LORD's prophet, delivering messages and enduring persecution while ministering to Judah and its remnant. In The Book of Jeremiah, the speaker lamenting mourns the hurt of his people's daughter and questions why Gilead's balm or physician has not restored them.

Jeremiah in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Jeremiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jeremiah is a prophet of the LORD, son of Hilkiah, one of the priests of Anathoth in Benjamin, who receives the LORD's word during the reigns of Josiah, Jehoiakim, and Zedekiah. He objects that he is a child who cannot speak, but is touched on the mouth, given the LORD's words, and made a fortified city, iron pillar, and bronze wall against the whole land. He is told Judah will fight him but not prevail, and that people will not listen. The records center on a costly calling. He weeps day and night for his people and wishes to flee to a wilderness lodging, yet is forbidden to pray for them, marry, or enter houses of mourning or feasting. He is compared to a gentle lamb led to slaughter, with enemies among the men of Anathoth seeking his life. He is a man of strife cursed by all, sits alone, suffers mockery and reproach, and is watched by familiar friends for his fall. Still, he says the LORD's word burns in his bones and he cannot stay silent, and he calls the LORD his refuge. He also admits he once interceded for his opponents. His ministry is told through signs and messages: the linen belt, potter's house and container, two baskets of figs, the cup for the nations, and bonds and bars on his neck, which leads to conflict with Hananiah, who later dies as Jeremiah foretold. He sends a letter to the captives in Babylon. He is seized in the temple, put in the stocks by Pashhur, and acquitted with Ahikam's help. Under Zedekiah he is shut up in the court of the guard, buys a field at Anathoth, and predicts the Chaldeans will return and burn Jerusalem. He is thrown into a dungeon, rescued by Ebedmelech, and stays in the court until Jerusalem falls. He dictates his words to Baruch, whose scroll the king burns, and a second is written. After the fall, Nebuzaradan frees him at Ramah and commits him to Gedaliah at Mizpah. The remnant asks his prayer; he tells them to stay, but they accuse him of falsehood and take him to Egypt, where he keeps prophesying at Tahpanhes. He speaks to Baruch, and his final act is writing in a book all the evil to come on Babylon.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

34 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

Jeremiah, son of Hilkiah and one of the priests in Anathoth in Benjamin, receives the LORD's word during the reigns of Josiah, Jehoiakim, and Zedekiah. He objects that he is a child who cannot speak, but is touched on the mouth, given the LORD's words, and sent out. He sees an almond branch and a boiling cauldron, and is told to gird himself and speak without dismay.

Chapter 7

Jeremiah receives the LORD's word and is told to stand in the gate of the LORD's house and proclaim it to all Judah entering to worship. He is told not to pray or intercede for the people because the LORD will not hear, and is warned that the people will neither listen nor answer him when he speaks and calls to them. He is also told to tell them that truth has perished and to take up a lamentation.

Chapter 9

The unnamed speaker laments with tears for the slain of his people and wishes to leave them for a wilderness lodging, since they are adulterers and treacherous. He also asks who is wise enough to understand why the land has perished, and he relays the LORD's word, urging the women to teach lamentation. The name Jeremiah does not appear in this chapter.

Chapter 11

Jeremiah receives the word of the LORD, answers "Amen, LORD," and is told to proclaim the covenant in the cities of Judah and the streets of Jerusalem. He is forbidden to pray for the people, learns of a plot against his life, describes himself as a gentle lamb led to slaughter, and entrusts his cause to the LORD, expecting to see vengeance on his enemies.

Chapter 13

The narrator obeys the LORD's commands: he buys the belt, wears it, hides it by the Euphrates, and digs it up after many days to find it ruined. He is told to deliver the message about containers filled with wine and the coming drunkenness. He pleads with the people to hear and says his soul will weep in secret and his eye run down with tears because the LORD's flock has been taken captive.

Chapter 14

Jeremiah receives the LORD's word about the drought and is told not to pray for the people for their good. He protests that the prophets promise peace, and is told those prophets are liars. He is instructed to say a word of lament, with eyes running down with tears night and day over the broken people.

Chapter 15

Jeremiah is told not to expect the LORD to relent, and he laments that he is a man of strife and contention whom everyone curses though he has neither lent nor borrowed. He asks the LORD to remember and avenge him, recalls eating the LORD's words with joy, sitting alone out of indignation, and accuses the LORD of being like a failing brook. The LORD calls him to return, promising that he will be as his mouth and a bronze wall against opponents.

Chapter 16

The text does not name him, but the one addressed as 'you' receives the LORD's word forbidding him to take a wife, have children, enter houses of mourning, or sit in houses of feasting. He is told to answer the people's questions about why the evil has come, and the speaker in verses 19-20 calls the LORD his strength, stronghold and refuge in the day of affliction.

Chapter 17

The speaker asks the LORD to heal and save him, calling the LORD his praise and refuge in the day of evil. He says he has not hurried from shepherding after the LORD nor desired the woeful day, though people mock him by asking where the LORD's word is. He asks that his persecutors be disappointed and destroyed with double destruction, and he is then sent to stand in Jerusalem's gates to deliver the LORD's Sabbath message.

Chapter 18

Jeremiah is told by the LORD to go down to the potter's house, where he sees the potter remake a marred vessel, and he receives the LORD's word explaining it. He is to speak the LORD's warning to Judah and Jerusalem, and he becomes the target of a plot against him. He prays that the LORD listen to him, recalls that he once stood before the LORD to speak good for them, and asks that their children and men suffer and that their sin not be forgiven.

Chapter 19

Jeremiah is sent to buy a potter's container, take elders to the valley of the son of Hinnom, proclaim the LORD's words, and break the container before the men with him. He then returns from Topheth, stands in the court of the LORD's house, and repeats to all the people that the LORD will bring the pronounced evil on the city.

Chapter 20

Jeremiah was struck and put in the stocks by Pashhur after prophesying, and after his release he pronounced judgment on Pashhur in the LORD's name. He complains that he is a laughingstock and that the LORD's word has become a reproach, yet he cannot stay silent because the word burns in his bones. He trusts the LORD is with him against his enemies, but ends by cursing the day of his birth and asking why he was born to see sorrow and shame.

Chapter 21

Jeremiah is asked by Zedekiah, through Pashhur and Zephaniah, to inquire of the LORD about Nebuchadnezzar's war. He delivers the LORD's answer of judgment to Zedekiah, the people, and the House of David, rather than the hoped-for deliverance.

Chapter 24

Jeremiah is shown two baskets of figs before the LORD's temple and, when asked what he sees, answers that the good figs are very good and the bad figs are so bad they cannot be eaten. He then receives the LORD's word interpreting the vision.

Chapter 25

Jeremiah speaks to all Judah and Jerusalem in the fourth year of Jehoiakim, saying the LORD's word has come to him for twenty-three years and that he has spoken early and often without being heeded. He takes the cup from the LORD's hand and makes the nations drink it, then is told to prophesy the LORD's roaring judgment against them all.

Chapter 26

Jeremiah speaks the LORD's words of warning in the LORD's house and is seized by the priests, prophets, and people, who say he must die. He defends himself before the princes, urging repentance and warning of innocent blood, and is acquitted, with Ahikam son of Shaphan ensuring he is not given to the people.

Chapter 27

Jeremiah is told by the LORD to make bonds and bars, put them on his neck, and send word to the kings of Edom, Moab, Ammon, Tyre, and Sidon. He then speaks to Zedekiah, and to the priests and people, urging them to serve the king of Babylon and live, and to ignore prophets who say otherwise.

Chapter 28

Jeremiah, wearing a neck bar, responded to Hananiah with 'Amen' but cautioned that a prophet of peace is proved only when his word happens. After Hananiah broke the bar, Jeremiah went his way, then received the LORD's word and told Hananiah that iron bars replaced the wooden ones and that he would die that year. His words were fulfilled when Hananiah died in the seventh month.

Chapter 29

Jeremiah the prophet sends a letter from Jerusalem to the exiles in Babylon. Shemaiah's letter attacks him as a self-appointed prophet, and Zephaniah the priest reads that letter in his hearing. The LORD then gives Jeremiah a message of judgment against Shemaiah to send to the captives.

Chapter 32

Jeremiah is shut up in the court of the guard by Zedekiah for prophesying Jerusalem's fall. At the LORD's word he buys his cousin Hanamel's field at Anathoth, has the deed witnessed and entrusted to Baruch, and then prays, wondering at the command to buy land while the city is falling. The LORD answers him with judgment on the city and a promise of restoration.

Chapter 33

Jeremiah is still locked up in the court of the guard when the LORD's word comes to him a second time. He receives three further messages in this chapter, each introduced by the LORD's word coming to him.

Chapter 34

Jeremiah receives the word of the LORD and speaks all these words to Zedekiah king of Judah in Jerusalem while the Babylonian army fights against the city. He then receives further words from the LORD condemning the people for reversing the release of their servants.

Chapter 35

Jeremiah receives the LORD's word in the days of Jehoiakim and brings the Rechabites into the LORD's house, setting wine before them and telling them to drink. He then delivers the LORD's message of judgment to Judah and Jerusalem and announces the LORD's promise to the Rechabites.

Chapter 36

Jeremiah receives the LORD's command to put all his words on a scroll and dictates them to Baruch. Being restricted from the LORD's house, he sends Baruch to read it, and after the king burns the scroll he is sought for arrest but hidden by the LORD. He then dictates a second scroll with many similar words added.

Chapter 37

Jeremiah the prophet relays the LORD's word that Pharaoh's army will go home and the Chaldeans will return and burn Jerusalem. He is seized at Benjamin's gate by Irijah on a charge of defecting, which he denies, and is beaten and imprisoned for many days. He tells Zedekiah he will be delivered to the king of Babylon, asks not to be returned to Jonathan's house, and is placed in the court of the guard.

Chapter 38

Jeremiah proclaims that staying in Jerusalem means death while going out to the Chaldeans means life, and is thrown by the princes into a dungeon where he sinks in the mire. Ebedmelech rescues him, and he then tells Zedekiah privately that surrender will save the king, his house, and the city, while refusal will bring burning and capture. He misleads the princes at the king's instruction and stays in the court of the guard until Jerusalem is taken.

Chapter 39

Jeremiah was taken out of the court of the guard by Nebuzaradan and the other chief officers of the king of Babylon, on Nebuchadnezzar's orders that he not be harmed. He was committed to Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, and lived among the people. He had also received a word from the LORD, while shut up in the court of the guard, to carry to Ebedmelech.

Chapter 40

Jeremiah is freed from chains at Ramah by Nebuzaradan after having been bound among the captives bound for Babylon. He is given the choice to go or stay, receives food and a present, and goes to live with Gedaliah at Mizpah among the people left in the land.

Chapter 42

Jeremiah is asked by the people to pray to the LORD for them, and he promises to keep nothing back from his report. After ten days he receives the LORD's word and delivers it, telling the remnant to stay in the land and not go to Egypt. He charges them with dealing deceitfully against their own souls and declares that they will die by sword, famine, and pestilence in Egypt.

Chapter 43

Jeremiah finishes speaking all the LORD's words to the people and is accused by Azariah, Johanan, and the proud men of speaking falsely. He is taken into Egypt with the remnant and arrives at Tahpanhes. There the LORD's word comes to him, commanding him to hide great stones in the brickwork at the entry of Pharaoh's house and to announce Nebuchadnezzar's coming.

Chapter 44

Jeremiah receives and delivers the LORD's word to all the Jews in Egypt, rebuking their idolatry and warning of destruction. When the people and women reject him, he replies that the LORD brought the desolation of Judah because of their incense burning, and he proclaims the LORD's oath and the sign concerning Pharaoh Hophra.

Chapter 45

Jeremiah the prophet speaks the LORD's message to Baruch in the fourth year of Jehoiakim, king of Judah. Baruch writes Jeremiah's words in a book at his mouth.

Chapter 46

The speaker grieves that his heart is faint and that he is hurt, mourning and dismayed at the hurt of the daughter of his people. He asks whether there is no balm or physician in Gilead and why the people's health is not recovered.
