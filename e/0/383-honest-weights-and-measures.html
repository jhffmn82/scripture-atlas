Abstract written by a model

In Deuteronomy, it commands Israel to keep perfect and just weights and measures rather than diverse ones. In Proverbs, differing weights and measures and dishonest scales are called an abomination to the LORD. In The Book of Ezekiel, it is defined through just balances, ephah and bath, and their stated equivalences.

Abstract written by a model

Honest weights and measures: Israel is commanded to keep perfect and just ones, not diverse heavy and light, large and small. Obedience promises long days in the land; unrighteous dealing is an abomination to the LORD.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 25

Israel is forbidden to keep diverse weights in the bag or diverse measures in the house, one heavy and one light, one large and one small. It must keep perfect and just ones so its days may be long in the land, since all who act unrighteously are an abomination to the LORD.

just weights and measures in The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where just weights and measures first appears

Abstract written by a model

A set of standard weights and measures: the LORD requires just balances, ephah and bath. Both are one measure, a tenth of a homer; the shekel is twenty gerahs, and the mina sixty shekels.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 45

The LORD requires just balances, a just ephah, and a just bath. The ephah and bath are to be of one measure, each a tenth of a homer, the shekel is twenty gerahs, and the mina is twenty plus twenty-five plus fifteen shekels.
