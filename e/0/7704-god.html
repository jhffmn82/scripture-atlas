God

deity · 1 work · 33 quoted facts · 12 connections
Also called: Cause, Deity, GOD, God's, Supreme Ruler of Creation, divine, heaven, the Eternal Cause, the divine, virtue of God, will of God

Abstract written by a model

God is an infinite, self-existing principle and divine sovereignty: the ultimate, hidden, unknowable source, cause, refuge, and arbiter of life and death. Its unity obliterates distinctions, blending contraries without antagonism, while its TAO proceeds through inaction and compliance. God operates spontaneously, without self-manifestation, yet ceaselessly supports, shapes, and orders all things as the caster of the universe. It gives human beings form, grants talents and divine nourishment, governs the Sage’s birth and death, and is the source of man, natural intelligence, virtue, and perfection. The soul and man emanate from God; body, life, individuality, and posterity are delegated by God, and death reunites man with God. The true Sage looks to God, is guided by it, seeks harmony and subjective relation with all things, and may share divine virtue, passivity, energy, and union with God. God’s laws appear in nature and govern bodily and natural conditions, though God remains the hidden spring behind what is known. It assists constant people, regards the Son of Heaven and man as its children, punishes secret evil, destroys those who reject perfection, and explains calamity beyond human control. Finally, God preserves recurring reproduction and diverse forms through an equilibrium in which it holds the scales.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

16 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

GOD is the refuge of the true Sage, who places himself in subjective relation with all things. The soul is described as an emanation from GOD, passing to and from the earth through the portals of Life and Death.

Of old, the Emperor Yao said to Shun, "I would smite the Tsungs, and

God is described as the Infinite whose unity embraces and obliterates distinctions. In God, contraries are merged without antagonism.

CHAPTER IV.

A person who is inwardly straight is described as a servant of God and regards the Son of Heaven and himself as equally children of God.

CHAPTER V.

God gives man his form and grants the divine nourishment by which the truly wise live. The grace of God is said to have reached Yao and Shun.

CHAPTER VI.

God is described as a principle existing by its own intrinsicality and operating spontaneously without self-manifestation. Man emanates from God, and the divine finds expression in the human.

Nan Po Tzŭ K'uei

God is described as governing transformation and as the caster who shapes beings in the universe's smelting-pot. Human beings are said to return to God, and man is said to have always yielded before God.

CHAPTER XI.

God is associated with the TAO of God, whose way consists in inaction and compliance. The true Sage looks up to God but does not offer to aid.

CHAPTER XII.

God is associated with sovereignty that begins in virtue and is called divine. The true Sage may pass into the kingdom of God, and unconsciousness of objective existences and of God is described as combining the human and divine.

CHAPTER XIII.

GOD operates ceaselessly and is associated with the universe’s spontaneous production and order. The happiness of GOD consists in supporting and shaping all things without claiming cruelty, charity, age, or skill.

CHAPTER XV.

God determines the Sage's birth and the modification of existence that is death. The Sage shares the passivity of the Yin in repose and the energy of the Yang in action, while pure spirituality is nourished according to TAO and brought into relationship with God.

CHAPTER XIX.

God is the source of the natural intelligence, virtue, and perfection sought by the Sage. The text says that one whose physical frame and vitality are perfect is one with God, and that the perfect man brings his nature into partnership with God.

CHAPTER XX.

God is associated with the natural laws governing hunger, thirst, cold, and heat, which Confucius accepts as part of his allotted course. Confucius says that man is from God, that God is from God, and that death reunites man with God.

CHAPTER XXII.

God is presented as the source of delegated body, life, individuality, and posterity. The unit says that bodily movement, rest, taste, and the operations of life follow God's laws.

CHAPTER XXIII.

God assists those who are constant and makes them his chosen children. God punishes those who do evil in secret, and calamities that come despite proper self-cultivation are attributed to God rather than man. The perfect man seeks harmony with God and is not disturbed by praise or insult.

Kuan Chung being at the point of death, Duke Huan went to see him.

God is described as the ultimate end, manifested in the laws of nature and existing as a hidden spring. God is ultimately unknowable, though the known is reached from the unknowable.

CHAPTER XXVII.

God is associated with language that flows constantly over, as from a full goblet. All things are reproduced in diverse forms in a recurring equilibrium called the equilibrium of God, and God holds the scales.
