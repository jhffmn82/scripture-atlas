This is Paul's Second Letter to the Thessalonians, a three-chapter letter sent by Paul with Silvanus and Timothy to the assembly of the Thessalonians. It first encourages the community, then corrects a misunderstanding about the Lord's coming, and finally gives practical instructions about conduct. In Chapter 1, Paul thanks God for the Thessalonians' growing faith and love and praises their perseverance through persecution. He says God will repay those who afflict them and give relief to the afflicted when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven, while those who do not know God or obey the Good News face eternal destruction. He prays that God will count them worthy of their calling. Chapter 2 urges them not to be shaken by claims that the day of Christ has already come. That day will not arrive until the rebellion occurs and the man of sin, the son of destruction, is revealed, exalting himself as God and working through Satan with lying wonders. Those who refused the truth are deceived and judged. The writer thanks God for choosing them for salvation, tells them to stand firm in the traditions they were taught, and asks that they be comforted and established. In Chapter 3, Paul requests prayer for the word to spread and for deliverance from evil men. He commands the brothers to withdraw from those who walk in rebellion, points to his own example of working night and day, and repeats that anyone unwilling to work should not eat. He tells idle busybodies to work quietly, urges the brothers not to grow weary in doing right, and closes with a personal greeting and a blessing of peace and grace.

Paul’s Second Letter to the Thessalonians / Chapter 1

Paul, together with Silvanus and Timothy, greets the assembly of the Thessalonians and thanks God for their growing faith and abounding love. They boast about the Thessalonians' perseverance through persecutions and afflictions, which they present as a sign of God's righteous judgment and of being counted worthy of God's Kingdom. The letter says God will repay affliction to those who afflict them and give relief to the afflicted when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven with his angels in flaming fire. Those who don't know God or obey the Good News will pay the penalty of eternal destruction, while Jesus will be glorified in his saints. Paul and his companions pray that God will count the Thessalonians worthy of their calling and that the Lord Jesus' name will be glorified in them.

Chapter 2

The writer addresses the brothers about the coming of the Lord Jesus Christ and their gathering to him, urging them not to be shaken by claims that the day of Christ has already come. He says that day will not come unless the rebellion comes first and the man of sin, the son of destruction, is revealed. This figure exalts himself as God in the temple of God, is restrained for now, and acts by the working of Satan with lying wonders, deceiving those who refused the truth. God sends those people a powerful delusion so they will be judged. The writer gives thanks that God chose the brothers for salvation, tells them to stand firm in the traditions they were taught, and asks the Lord Jesus Christ and God the Father to comfort and establish them.

Chapter 3

In Chapter 3, Paul asks the brothers to pray that the word of the Lord spreads and that he and his companions are delivered from unreasonable and evil men, while expressing confidence that the Lord is faithful and will guard them. He commands the brothers in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ to withdraw from any brother who walks in rebellion and not after the tradition received from Paul and his companions. Paul points to his own example of working night and day so as not to burden anyone, and recalls the rule that anyone unwilling to work should not eat. He reports hearing of some who do not work but are busybodies, and commands them to work quietly and eat their own bread. He instructs the brothers not to grow weary in doing right, to note and avoid anyone who disobeys the letter yet admonish him as a brother, and closes with a greeting in his own hand and a blessing of peace and grace.
