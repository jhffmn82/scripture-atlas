King Hui of Liang

Abstract written by a model

King Hui of Liang is a king and prince who rules and administers Liang’s kingdom, seeking Mencius’s counsel on how to profit it. He is concerned with governing, says he exerts his mind fully during bad years by moving people and grain, and wonders why his population does not grow. Yet he has small virtue: while his pond holds large geese and deer and his kitchen and stables are supplied, his people suffer hunger. Mencius challenges his pleasure in possessions, his treatment of famine as the year’s fault, and the violence inflicted through government, likening it to killing. Hui is also fond of war and, after Jin’s defeats, his eldest son’s death, territorial loss, and disgrace to his departed predecessors, asks for a course to erase that shame once for all.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

king Hui of Liang asks Mencius whether he has counsels to profit the kingdom because Mencius has traveled a thousand li to see him.

Chapter 2

King Hui of Liang asks Mencius whether wise and good princes find pleasure in large geese and deer. He stands with Mencius by a pond while looking at these animals.

Chapter 3

King Hui of Liang says that he exerts his mind to the utmost in governing his kingdom, moving people and conveying grain during bad years. He asks why his people do not increase while the people of neighboring kingdoms do not decrease. Mencius criticizes him for allowing famine and blaming the year.

Chapter 4

King Hui of Liang asks Mencius to give him instruction. He agrees that killing with a stick is no different from killing with a sword, and that killing with a sword is no different from doing so through government. His kitchen and stables are well supplied while his people suffer hunger.

Chapter 5

King Hui of Liang tells Mencius about Jin's defeats, his eldest son's death, territorial loss, and disgrace. He wishes to wipe away the shame brought upon his departed predecessors and asks what course to pursue.
