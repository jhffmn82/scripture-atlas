Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the psalmist’s prayer is asked to rise before the LORD like incense and evening sacrifice and is declared always set against evil deeds. In Psalms, David’s prayer pleads for the LORD to hear his petitions and relieve him, requesting a quick answer, deliverance, guidance, revival, and the cutting off of his enemies. In The Gospel According to Matthew, prayer is taught as something done secretly, in an inner room with the door shut, without being seen or using vain repetitions. In The Gospel According to Luke, Jesus gives a prayer addressed to the Father asking for his name to be kept holy, his Kingdom to come, daily bread, forgiveness, and deliverance from the evil one, and urges persistence in asking, seeking, and knocking. In The Gospel According to Luke, Jesus teaches that people must always pray and not give up, contrasting the Pharisee’s self-righteous prayer with the tax collector’s humble plea for mercy. In The Letter from James, prayer is prescribed for the suffering and sick, with prayer of faith bringing healing and forgiveness and a righteous person’s insistent prayer being powerfully effective. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), prayer is worship and a means for believers to seek help with steadfastness and Allah’s companionship, performed facing the Inviolable Place of Worship and guarded especially as the midmost prayer. In Surah 13: The Thunder (Ar-Ra'd), prayer belongs truly to Allah, while prayer directed to others receives no response; the righteous are regular in prayer and promised reward.

and 2 more works

In Surah 21: The Prophets (Al-Anbiyaa), prayer is made to Allah in times of distress and need, and Allah hears and responds to the prayers of Noah, Job, Dhu'n-Nun, and Zachariah. In The Table-Talk of the Prophet Mohammad (Part 1), prayer is praised as a duty witnessed by angels, with congregational prayers receiving double the reward of prayers said at home; missed prayers are performed when remembered.
