householder's abode

place · 1 work · 13 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: abode of a householder, house, householder's lodging, lodging used by the householder, lodging-place which is used by the householder, the abode of a householder, the householder's abode

Abstract written by a model

A householder's abode is a lodging used by a householder, containing family, cattle, food, drink, and features such as doors, bathroom, privy, loopholes, fissures, and secured entrances. Monks or nuns must remain unseen while cows are milked and food is prepared or distributed, then enter or leave circumspectly for alms. They need permission, examination, and sweeping to pass a thorn-secured entrance. They should not use the lodging for postures, study, or rest while household people, animals, provisions, fire, or water are present, lest improper dependence or involvement arise.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Fourth Lesson.

A monk or nun should step aside and remain unseen when milking or food preparation and distribution are still taking place. After the cows are milked and the dinner is prepared and distributed, the abode may be entered or left circumspectly for alms.

Fifth Lesson.

A householder's abode may have walls, gates, bolts, holes, secured entrances, or other obstacles. A monk or nun may enter or leave through an entrance secured by a thorn bush only after obtaining the owner's permission and examining and sweeping it.

Second Lecture, Called Begging for a Couch. / First Lesson. .. Third Lesson.

A monk or nun should not use a householder's lodging for religious postures, study, or rest when it contains the householders' family, cattle, food, drink, fire, or water. Living there could lead to illness, dependence on householders, approval or dislike of their conduct, exposure to sexual talk, or other improper involvement.
