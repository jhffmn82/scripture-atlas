Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Manasseh was a king of Judah who did evil through idolatry, child sacrifice, sorcery, and bloodshed, leading Judah into sin; he was buried in the garden of Uzza and succeeded by his son Amon. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Manasseh was a king of Judah who first did great evil, repented in Babylon after being captured by Assyria, was restored, removed foreign gods, rebuilt the LORD's altar, and commanded Judah to serve the LORD.

Manasseh in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Manasseh first appears

Abstract written by a model

Manasseh was a king of Judah, son of Hezekiah and Hephzibah, who took the throne at twelve and reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem. He did evil in the LORD's sight: he rebuilt the high places Hezekiah had destroyed, built altars for Baal and the army of the sky, even in the LORD's house, made an Asherah, sacrificed his son in fire, practiced sorcery, and filled Jerusalem with innocent blood. He led Judah into sin with his idols, and the LORD would not pardon. He was buried in the garden of Uzza and succeeded by his son Amon.

Chapter 21

Manasseh became king of Judah at twelve and reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem, doing evil by rebuilding high places, raising altars for Baal and the army of the sky, making an Asherah, passing his son through the fire, practicing sorcery, and shedding much innocent blood. He seduced Judah to do more evil than the nations the LORD destroyed, and the LORD pronounced judgment because of him. He died, was buried in the garden of Uzza, and was succeeded by his son Amon.

Manasseh in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Manasseh first appears

Abstract written by a model

Manasseh was a king of Judah, son and successor of Hezekiah, who became king at twelve and reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem. He first did great evil: rebuilding the high places, worshiping the army of the sky, building altars in the LORD's house, setting up an idol, making Asheroth, practicing sorcery, sacrificing his children by fire, and seducing Judah into worse evil than the nations. Assyrian captains took him in chains to Babylon, where he repented and learned the LORD was God. Restored, he built an outer wall, placed captains in fortified cities, removed foreign gods, rebuilt the LORD's altar, and commanded Judah to serve the LORD. He was buried in his own house; his son Amon succeeded him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 33

Manasseh became king at twelve and reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem, doing great evil by rebuilding high places, building altars to Baals and the army of the sky, placing an idol in God's house, passing his children through fire, and practicing sorcery. Taken in chains to Babylon by Assyrian captains, he humbled himself and prayed, was restored to Jerusalem, fortified the city and Judah, removed the foreign gods and idol, restored the LORD's altar, and commanded Judah to serve the LORD. He slept with his fathers, was buried in his own house, and was succeeded by his son Amon.
