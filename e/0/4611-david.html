In The First Book of Samuel, David is Jesse's youngest son, a shepherd anointed by the LORD who kills Goliath, serves Saul, escapes his pursuit, spares him twice, and leads his men from Ziklag. In The Second Book of Samuel, David is the LORD's anointed king who rules Israel, defeats enemies, receives a promise of an everlasting house, sins with Bathsheba, and endures family strife before confessing and worshiping. In The First Book of Kings, David is the king of Israel and father of Solomon who arranges Solomon's succession, charges him to keep the LORD's law, and is remembered as the standard of wholehearted devotion. In 1 Chronicles, David is the seventh son of Jesse who becomes king of Israel, leads the nation, organizes worship, prepares for the temple, and dies after reigning forty years. In The Second Book of Chronicles, David is the king and man of God who wishes to build the LORD's house, dedicates treasures and weapons, and receives a promise of a lasting throne. In Psalms, David as ruler commits to praising the LORD, living blamelessly, favoring the faithful, and destroying the wicked. In Psalms: David, the speaker, is a worshipper with a steadfast heart toward God. In Psalms, David as psalmist cries out when distressed and surrounded by nations, takes refuge in the LORD, and vows to live, give thanks, and exalt God.

David in The First Book of Samuel

author unknown · about 930 to 721 BC · the work’s page · read it where David first appears

Abstract written by a model

David is a man introduced as Jesse's youngest son, a ruddy, handsome shepherd from Bethlehem, anointed among his brothers, after which the LORD's Spirit comes mightily on him. He is a skillful harp player and becomes Saul's armor bearer. Having killed a lion and a bear, he trusts the LORD and kills Goliath in the valley of Elah with a sling and stone, despite Eliab's anger and Saul's doubts. He then rises in Saul's service: he behaves wisely, becomes captain of a thousand, kills two hundred Philistines, and marries Michal, becoming Saul's son-in-law and highly esteemed. Jonathan says he will be king of Israel, and they make a covenant before the LORD. Saul's jealousy, sparked by the song that David has slain ten thousands, turns to pursuit. Saul's spear, messengers at his house, and daily searching force David to flee, aided by Michal, Samuel, Jonathan and Ahimelech. He gathers about four hundred men at Adullam, later six hundred, and habitually inquires of the LORD, saving Keilah from the Philistines yet leaving when told its men would betray him. David serves wherever Saul sends him, behaves wisely, and is set over the men of war, winning the favor of the people and Saul's servants. He kills two hundred Philistines, brings their foreskins, and marries Michal, and his name is highly esteemed.

David, hiding in the innermost parts of the cave, secretly cuts off the skirt of Saul's robe but is struck in his heart and restrains his men from harming Saul, since Saul is the LORD's anointed. He raids the Geshurites, Girzites, and Amalekites, leaving no man or woman alive, and deceives Achish by claiming he raided Judah's South and the South of the Jerahmeelites and Kenites. He kills all survivors so no one can report his deed.

Chapter 28

Chapter 29

David marches in the rear with Achish and his men, and is vouched for by Achish but rejected by the Philistine princes. Chapter 30

David in The Second Book of Samuel

author unknown · about 950 BC · the work’s page · read it where David first appears

Abstract written by a model

David is a king, chosen by the LORD from the sheep pen to be prince over Israel, son of Jesse, and called the LORD's servant, anointed, and sweet psalmist. The records follow him from Ziklag, where he mourns Saul and Jonathan and has the Amalekite messenger executed for killing the LORD's anointed. He then goes to Hebron, where the men of Judah anoint him king and he reigns seven years and six months. There he has wives including Ahinoam, Abigail and Eglah, and six sons, among them Amnon, Chileab and Absalom. He regains Michal through Abner's alliance, declares himself innocent of Abner's murder, and admits he is weak against Zeruiah's sons. He also punishes Ishbosheth's killers. The elders anoint him over all Israel, and he reigns forty years in all, thirty-three of them in Jerusalem. He takes Zion, makes it David's city, defeats the Philistines, and brings the ark in with dancing and sacrifice. The LORD promises him an everlasting house and throne, and he answers in humble prayer. He subdues Philistines, Moab, Syria and Edom, rules with justice and righteousness, and keeps an administration headed by Joab, Zadok, Benaiah and others. He shows kindness to Mephibosheth for Jonathan's sake and later spares him because of their oath. The middle of the records turns to failure and family strife. He takes Bathsheba, arranges Uriah's death, and confesses 'I have sinned' when Nathan confronts him. Solomon is born. Amnon's rape of Tamar and Absalom's revolt follow, and David flees Jerusalem, weeps, endures Shimei's curses, and crosses the Jordan to Mahanaim. He grieves deeply for Absalom, is rebuked by Joab, and returns, then puts his ten concubines in widowhood. Sheba's revolt is ended by Joab. Famine, the Gibeonites, and the burial of Saul's bones follow, and his men insist he stop going to battle. The close shows him delivered by the LORD in song, speaking last words about an everlasting covenant, pouring out water won at risk by his mighty men, and numbering the people against Joab's objection. He confesses sin, pleads that the LORD's hand fall on him and his father's house, buys Araunah's threshing floor, refuses sacrifices that cost him nothing, and builds an altar.

The Second Book of Samuel / Chapter 1

David, back in Ziklag after slaughtering the Amalekites, questions the man who brings news of Saul's death and then tears his clothes and, with his men, mourns and fasts until evening. He has the Amalekite killed for stretching out his hand against the LORD's anointed, and composes a lamentation for Saul and Jonathan, calling Jonathan his brother whose love surpassed the love of women.

Chapter 2

David inquired of the LORD and was told to go up to Hebron, where he settled with his two wives, Ahinoam and Abigail, and his men with their households. The men of Judah anointed him king over the house of Judah, and he reigned in Hebron seven years and six months. He sent messengers blessing the men of Jabesh Gilead for burying Saul and told them he would reward them.

Chapter 3

David grows stronger, has six sons born to him in Hebron, and accepts Abner's alliance on the condition that Michal be returned to him. After Joab kills Abner, David declares himself and his kingdom guiltless, curses Joab's house, mourns and fasts, and admits he is weak and that the sons of Zeruiah are too hard for him.

Chapter 4

In Hebron, David receives Ishbosheth's head from Rechab and Baanah and rejects their claim of vengeance, citing his earlier killing of the man who reported Saul's death in Ziklag. He orders the brothers killed and mutilated, and has Ishbosheth's head buried in Abner's grave.

Chapter 5

David is anointed king over all Israel at Hebron after making a covenant with the elders, having begun to reign at thirty and reigning forty years. He takes the stronghold of Zion, calls it David's city, builds there, and perceives that the LORD has established him. He also fathers more children in Jerusalem and defeats the Philistines twice by following the LORD's answers to his inquiries.

Chapter 6

David gathers thirty thousand chosen men and sets out to bring up God's ark, but becomes displeased and afraid after Uzzah's death and diverts the ark to Obed-Edom's house. After hearing of the blessing on Obed-Edom, he brings the ark into his city with joy, sacrifices, dancing, and offerings, then blesses the people and gives them bread, dates, and raisins. He answers Michal's rebuke by saying he celebrated before the LORD, who chose him over Saul's house, and that he will be even more undignified.

Chapter 7

David, now at rest in his house, observes that he lives in cedar while God's ark is in curtains. The LORD, through Nathan, recalls raising him from the sheep pen to be prince over Israel and promises him a great name, rest from enemies, and a house and throne that will last forever. David responds by sitting before the LORD and praying in humble gratitude.

Chapter 8

David struck and subdued the Philistines, Moab, Hadadezer, the Syrians of Damascus, and Edom, placing garrisons in Syria and Edom, and the LORD gave him victory wherever he went. He took gold shields and a great quantity of bronze, dedicated silver and gold from all subdued nations to the LORD, and earned a reputation after striking down eighteen thousand Syrians in the Valley of Salt. He reigned over all Israel, executing justice and righteousness for all his people, with Joab, Jehoshaphat, Zadok, Ahimelech, Seraiah, Benaiah, and his own sons serving in his administration.

Chapter 9

David asks whether anyone is left of Saul's house so that he may show kindness for Jonathan's sake. He summons Ziba, sends for Mephibosheth, promises to restore Saul's land to him and to have him eat at his table continually, and commands Ziba to farm the land for Mephibosheth.

Chapter 10

David sent servants to comfort Hanun out of kindness for Hanun's father, and after they were shamed he told them to wait at Jericho. He sent Joab against the Ammonites and Syrians, then personally led all Israel across the Jordan to Helam, where he killed seven hundred charioteers and forty thousand horsemen and struck down Shobach.

Chapter 11

David stays in Jerusalem while his army fights, sees Bathsheba, sends for her, and lies with her. After she conceives, he tries to get Uriah to go home, makes him drunk, and finally sends a letter ordering Uriah's death in battle. He then takes Bathsheba as his wife, and what he did displeases the LORD.

Chapter 12

David is angered by Nathan's parable and pronounces that the rich man deserves to die, only to be told he is that man. He confesses "I have sinned against the LORD," fasts and mourns for the sick child, and after its death worships and eats. He comforts Bathsheba, fathers Solomon, and leads the people to take Rabbah, where he receives the Ammonite king's crown and puts the people to forced labor.

Chapter 13

David, the king, sends Tamar to Amnon's house at Amnon's request, and is very angry when he hears of the rape, though the text records no action by him. He reluctantly lets Amnon go to Absalom's feast, then tears his garments on hearing a false report that all his sons are dead. He mourns Amnon daily, and in time longs to go out to Absalom, being comforted over Amnon's death.

Chapter 14

The king hears the woman's story, promises by the LORD's life that not a hair of her son will fall, and then recognizes that Joab is behind it. He orders Joab to bring Absalom back but says Absalom must not see his face. Two years later, after Joab brings Absalom's plea, he calls for Absalom and kisses him.

Chapter 15

David granted Absalom's request to go to Hebron, then on hearing that Israel's hearts followed Absalom, fled Jerusalem with his servants and household, leaving ten concubines to keep the house. He sent the ark back with Zadok, accepting whatever the LORD decides, wept barefoot with covered head on the Mount of Olives, prayed that Ahithophel's counsel be made foolish, and sent Hushai back as a spy.

Chapter 16

David receives provisions from Ziba and gives him all that belonged to Mephibosheth. At Bahurim he endures Shimei's curses and stones, restrains Abishai, and says the LORD may repay him good for the cursing. He arrives weary with his people and refreshes himself.

Chapter 17

David was warned through Hushai, the priests, Jonathan and Ahimaaz to pass over the water at once. He and all his people crossed the Jordan by morning, with none missing, and he came to Mahanaim, where Shobi, Machir and Barzillai supplied food and bedding for him and his people.

Chapter 18

David counted his people, appointed captains, and divided them into three divisions, but yielded to the people's plea that he not go out to battle and instead stood beside the gate. He commanded Joab, Abishai, and Ittai to deal gently with Absalom, and later, on learning from the Cushite that Absalom was dead, he went up to the room over the gate and wept.

Chapter 19

David covers his face and cries out for Absalom until Joab rebukes him, after which he sits in the gate and sends word to the elders of Judah to bring him back. He offers Amasa the captaincy in place of Joab, swears to spare Shimei, tells Mephibosheth and Ziba to divide the land, and takes Chimham with him after kissing and blessing Barzillai.

Chapter 20

David returns to Jerusalem and puts his ten concubines in custody, providing for them but not going in to them, so they live as widows until death. He tells Amasa to gather Judah's men within three days, and when Amasa delays he sends Abishai to pursue Sheba. Joab ultimately ends the revolt and returns to him, and a list of his officials follows.

Chapter 21

David seeks the LORD during the famine, learns it is because of Saul's killing of the Gibeonites, and agrees to give them whatever they ask. He spares Mephibosheth son of Jonathan because of their oath, hands seven of Saul's descendants to the Gibeonites, and later gathers the bones of Saul, Jonathan and those hanged for burial. In the Philistine wars he grows faint and is saved by Abishai, and his men swear he must no longer go to battle lest he quench the lamp of Israel.

Chapter 22

David speaks this song to the LORD after being delivered from all his enemies and from Saul. He recounts distress, rescue, and his own righteousness, and says the LORD trained him for war, made him head of the nations, and enabled him to destroy his enemies. He vows to give thanks among the nations and is named as the LORD's anointed, to whom loving kindness is shown forever along with his offspring.

Chapter 23

David speaks his last words, calling himself the anointed of the God of Jacob and the sweet psalmist of Israel, and affirms that God made an everlasting covenant with his house. He longs for water from the well of Bethlehem while the Philistines hold the town, and when three mighty men bring it, he refuses to drink it and pours it out to the LORD. He sets Benaiah over his guard.

Chapter 24

David, moved by the LORD, orders Joab to count the people and overrides Joab's objection. Afterward his heart strikes him, he confesses that he has sinned greatly, chooses to fall into the LORD's hand, and pleads that the LORD's hand be against him and his father's house instead of the people. He buys Araunah's threshing floor at full price, saying he will not offer burnt offerings that cost him nothing, builds an altar, and offers burnt and peace offerings.

David in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where David first appears

Abstract written by a model

David is a king of Israel, a servant of the LORD who kept his commandments, and father of Solomon. He reigned forty years: seven in Hebron and thirty-three in Jerusalem. The records open with him old and unable to keep warm, unaware that Adonijah has made himself king. Moved by Bathsheba and Nathan, he fulfills his oath and has Solomon anointed at Gihon. Near death he charges Solomon to keep the LORD's law, to deal with Joab and Shimei, and to show kindness to Barzillai's sons. He dies and is buried in David's city, Jerusalem. Afterward he is remembered mainly through Solomon: wars prevented him from building the LORD's house, though it was in his heart, and the LORD promised his son would build it. Solomon asks that David never lack a man on Israel's throne. David becomes the standard of a wholehearted heart against which Solomon is judged. For David's sake the kingdom's tearing is delayed and one tribe is left, giving him a lamp in Jerusalem. His conquests in Edom and Zobah form the background to later adversaries.

David is recalled as having had it in his heart to build a house for the LORD's name, and the LORD said he did well but that his son would build it instead. Chapter 11

David is the standard of a wholehearted heart against which Solomon is judged. For his sake the LORD delays the kingdom's tearing until Solomon's son's time and leaves one tribe so that David may have a lamp in Jerusalem. David in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · 449 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where David first appears

Abstract written by a model

David is a man, the seventh son of Jesse, with sisters Zeruiah and Abigail, and a member of the house of Judah. He becomes king of Israel, chosen and established by the LORD, and the records follow his rise, reign, and death. Early on he is a fugitive from Saul at Ziklag, where mighty men from many tribes gather to him. After Saul's death the LORD turns the kingdom to him. All Israel gathers at Hebron, where he covenants with the elders and is anointed, in line with the word given through Samuel. He reigns seven years and six months there, then thirty-three years in Jerusalem, taking the stronghold of Zion, which becomes David's city. He grows greater because the LORD of Armies is with him. Hiram sends cedar, masons and carpenters for his house. He becomes famous in all lands, and the nations fear him. His family is large. Six sons are born in Hebron, beginning with Amnon, Daniel, Absalom, Adonijah, Shephatiah and Ithream. In Jerusalem he takes more wives and fathers many more children, including Solomon, Nathan, Shobab, Shammua and others. He also has a daughter, Tamar, and sons by concubines. Religiously, he brings the ark toward Jerusalem. The attempt falters when Uzza is struck, and the ark rests at Obed-Edom's house for a time. He then prepares a tent, insists that Levites carry it, and sets it in the tent. He appoints Levites and singers to serve before it and organizes the priests, Levites and musicians into divisions. Nathan tells him he will not build the LORD a house, but the LORD promises him a great name and a house, and he responds in humble prayer. Militarily, he defeats the Philistines, taking Gath, as well as Moab, Hadadezer of Zobah and the Syrians. He puts garrisons in Damascus, dedicates the spoils to the LORD, and rules with justice and righteousness. He wears the Ammonite king's crown after Rabbah. He sins by ordering a census at Satan's prompting, confesses, and buys Ornan's threshing floor at full price, refusing a sacrifice that costs him nothing. There he builds an altar. In old age he makes Solomon king. He explains that he was barred from building the temple because he was a man of war who shed blood. He gives Solomon the plans, gathers materials, gives his personal treasure, and charges Solomon and the leaders to build. He dies at a good old age, full of days, riches and honor, after reigning forty years. Solomon succeeds him, and his acts are recorded in the histories of Samuel, Nathan and Gad.

He takes the stronghold of Zion, lives there, and grows greater because the LORD of Armies is with him. When three mighty men bring him water from the well of Bethlehem, he refuses to drink it and pours it out to the LORD, calling it the blood of men who risked their lives.

Chapter 12

David was a fugitive from Saul at Ziklag and in the stronghold, where mighty men from many tribes came to help him until his force was like God's army. He tested the Benjamin and Judah arrivals, welcomed them after Amasai's pledge, and was made king over all Israel at Hebron, where he feasted three days.

Chapter 13

David consulted Israel's leaders and proposed bringing the ark back, since it was not sought in Saul's days, and he led all Israel to Kiriath Jearim to fetch it. He played music before God, was displeased when the LORD struck Uzza, and named the place Perez Uzza. Afraid of God, he asked how he could bring the ark home and left it in Obed-Edom's house instead of taking it into David's city.

Chapter 14

David receives cedar, masons, and carpenters from Hiram king of Tyre to build his house, and he perceives that the LORD has established him as king over Israel. He takes more wives in Jerusalem and fathers more sons and daughters, including Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, and Solomon. He twice inquires of God before fighting the Philistines, obeys the instructions given, defeats them at Baal Perazim and from Gibeon to Gezer, and becomes famous in all lands.

Chapter 15

David prepared a place and a tent for God's ark and insisted that only the Levites carry it. He assembled Israel and the Levite leaders, told them to sanctify themselves, and had singers appointed. He went with the elders and captains to bring the ark up with joy, dressed in fine linen and a linen ephod, and was seen dancing and playing by Michal, who despised him.

Chapter 16

David has the ark set in the tent he pitched, offers burnt and peace offerings, blesses the people in the LORD's name, and distributes bread, meat, and a raisin cake to all Israel. He appoints Levites to minister before the ark, first ordains giving thanks to the LORD through Asaph and his brothers, and arranges ongoing service at the ark and at Gibeon. He then returns to bless his house.

Chapter 17

David observes that he lives in a cedar house while the ark of the LORD's covenant is in a tent, but is told through Nathan that he will not build the LORD a house. He is reminded that the LORD took him from the sheep pen to be prince over Israel and promised him a great name and a house. David responds by sitting before the LORD in humble prayer, praising him and asking that the promise be established forever.

Chapter 18

David defeats the Philistines, Moab, Hadadezer king of Zobah, and the Syrians of Damascus, taking Gath, tribute, chariots, gold shields, and bronze, and he puts garrisons in Syria. He dedicates the silver, gold, and vessels from Hamath and from many nations to the LORD, and he reigns over all Israel, executing justice and righteousness for all his people.

Chapter 19

David chose to show kindness to Hanun because Hanun's father had shown kindness to him, and sent messengers to comfort him. After the servants were humiliated, he told them to stay at Jericho until their beards grew, and sent Joab against the Ammonites and their hirelings. He later gathered all Israel, crossed the Jordan, defeated the Syrians, killing seven thousand charioteers and forty thousand footmen along with Shophach, and received the submission of Hadadezer's servants.

Chapter 20

David stayed at Jerusalem while Joab fought at Rabbah, then took the Ammonite king's crown, which weighed a talent of gold and held precious stones, and had it set on his own head. He brought out much plunder and had the people cut with saws, iron picks, and axes, doing so to all the Ammonite cities, before returning to Jerusalem. The giants born in Gath are said to have fallen by his hand and the hands of his servants.

Chapter 21

David, moved by Satan, orders a census of Israel over Joab's objection, then confesses that he has sinned greatly and acted foolishly. He chooses to fall into the LORD's hand rather than man's, pleads that the punishment fall on himself and his father's house instead of the people, and buys Ornan's threshing floor at full price, refusing to offer a sacrifice that costs him nothing. Abstract written by a model

David is a person presented throughout these records as the attributed author of many psalms and prayers, the son of Jesse, father of Absalom, servant and anointed king of the LORD, and shepherd of Israel. The records open with him fleeing his son Absalom, yet unafraid of tens of thousands against him, trusting the LORD as shield and sustainer. What matters most is the recurring pattern of his life: he is hunted, afflicted and surrounded by enemies, pursuers, betrayers and false witnesses, and he responds by taking refuge in the LORD, pleading for mercy and deliverance, and ending in confidence and vows of thanks. His distress is vivid: he is faint, with troubled bones, nightly tears, sorrow, mourning, and a desolate heart. He is poor and needy, imprisoned in a cave, and forgotten by those around him. He also confesses sin, including his great iniquity, his sin against God after Nathan came to him over Bathsheba, and his being born in iniquity. At the same time he claims integrity, avoids the wicked, loves the LORD's house, delights in the saints, and shows compassion by fasting for enemies when they were sick. The records tie particular songs to episodes: Cush the Benjamite, pretending insanity before Abimelech, Doeg's report to Saul, the Ziphites telling Saul where he hid, being seized by the Philistines in Gath, fleeing Saul in the cave, Saul's men watching his house, and the desert of Judah. Deliverance from Saul and all his enemies leads to his being made head of the nations, served by foreigners, and appointed firstborn, highest of the kings of the earth, who will call the LORD Father and rock of his salvation. Later records stress God's choice of him from the sheepfolds, the LORD's oath that his descendants will sit on his throne, and his own oath to find a place for the LORD. The final records return to prayer for rescue, with his hatred of the wicked, his awareness that he is fearfully and wonderfully made, his thirst for God, and his identity as the LORD's servant, rescued from the deadly sword and vowing a new song.
