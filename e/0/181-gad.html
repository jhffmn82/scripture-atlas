Abstract written by a model

In Numbers, Gad is a tribal group led by prince Eliasaph that requests and receives land east of the Jordan, promises to fight for Israel, and builds fortified cities and sheepfolds. In Deuteronomy, the Gadites receive Gilead and adjacent territory with the Reubenites, and their men of valor cross armed ahead of the other tribes. In The Book of Joshua, the Gadites receive their inheritance east of the Jordan, pledge to obey Joshua and aid their brothers, and build a witness altar by the Jordan. In 1 Chronicles, the Gadites live beside the Reubenites in Bashan, fight the Hagrites, are later exiled by Assyrian kings, and some join David as mighty men.

Gad in Numbers

author unknown · 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Gad first appears

Abstract written by a model

Gad is a tribal group led by prince Eliasaph, counted at 45,650 fighting men (also listed as 40,500). With Reuben and their great livestock, they request land east of the Jordan, promise to fight armed before Israel, receive it, and build fortified cities and sheepfolds including Dibon, Ataroth, Aroer and Jazer.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 32

The children of Gad, with Reuben, request the livestock land east of the Jordan and agree to fight armed before Israel until all have their inheritance. They receive the land with Reuben and build Dibon, Ataroth, Aroer, Atroth-shophan, Jazer, Jogbehah, Beth Nimrah, and Beth Haran as fortified cities and sheepfolds.

Gadites in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · about 600 BC · the work’s page · read it where Gadites first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Gadites are a tribe who received their inheritance east of the Jordan from Moses, shared in Gilead with Reuben and the half-tribe of Manasseh. Their territory included Jazer, the cities of Gilead, half the Ammonite land, Mahanaim, Succoth and other places, reaching to the sea of Chinnereth. In Joshua 1 they pledge to obey Joshua and send armed men across to help their brothers. In chapter 13 their land is described. In chapter 22 Joshua sends them home, and they build a witness altar by the Jordan, defended and vindicated by Phinehas.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Joshua / Chapter 1

The Gadites are addressed by Joshua with the Reubenites and the half-tribe of Manasseh and reminded that their mighty men must cross armed to help their brothers until the land is possessed. They answer with a pledge to obey Joshua in everything.

Chapter 13

The Gadites received land east of the Jordan from Moses according to their families. It included Jazer, the cities of Gilead, half the Ammonite land to Aroer near Rabbah, and places such as Mahanaim, Beth Haram, Beth Nimrah, Succoth and Zaphon, reaching to the sea of Chinnereth.

Chapter 22

The Gadites are sent home by Joshua and return to Gilead, where with Reuben and Manasseh they build a great altar by the Jordan. They defend it as a witness rather than a place of sacrifice, and are vindicated by Phinehas; with Reuben they name the altar "A Witness Between Us that the LORD is God."

Gadites in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · 449 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Gadites first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Gadites are a tribe who lived beside the Reubenites in Bashan as far as Salecah, with chiefs including Joel, Shapham, Janai, and Shaphat, recorded by genealogy in the days of Jotham and Jeroboam. They fought the Hagrites alongside Reuben and half of Manasseh, who were delivered into their hand, but were later carried into exile by Assyrian kings. Some Gadites joined David as mighty men of valor, lion-faced and gazelle-swift, serving as army captains and crossing the Jordan to rout valley dwellers.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

The Gadites lived beside the Reubenites in Bashan to Salecah, and their chiefs included Joel, Shapham, Janai, and Shaphat, along with kinsmen of their fathers' houses. They were listed by genealogy in the days of Jotham of Judah and Jeroboam of Israel. They joined the war against the Hagrites and were later carried into exile by the Assyrian kings.

Chapter 12

Some Gadites joined David in the wilderness stronghold as mighty men of valor trained for war, with faces like lions and swiftness like gazelles. Eleven are named in order, from Ezer the chief to Machbannai, as army captains, the least equal to a hundred and the greatest to a thousand. They crossed the overflowing Jordan in the first month and put valley dwellers to flight.
