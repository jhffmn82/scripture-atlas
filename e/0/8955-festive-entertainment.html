festive entertainment

event · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: an entertainment, entertainment, festival, festival entertainment, festivity, scene of entertainment, such an entertainment

Abstract written by a model

A festive entertainment is an assembly setting that entertains many people, sometimes feeding Sramanas, Brâhmanas, guests, paupers, and beggars on communal or religious occasions. It may chiefly serve meat, fish, or roasted slices and involve liquors. For monks and nuns, it is not a reason or destination for travel, especially beyond half a yogana; after learning its location, they should go the opposite way. They should not plan to attend when linked with a feast or entertainment, particularly with such food, though suitable route and assembly conditions may permit attendance.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Second Lesson.

A festive entertainment may involve feeding Sramanas, Brâhmanas, guests, paupers, and beggars on communal or religious occasions. The monk or nun should not go to it for the sake of the entertainment, particularly when it is more than half a yogana away, and should travel in the opposite direction after hearing where it is held.

Third Lesson.

A festive entertainment may involve various liquors and can lead a mendicant to illness, mixed company, drunkenness, sensual temptation, and sin. Monks and nuns should not resolve to go to a festival preceded or followed by a feast.

Fourth Lesson.

A monk or nun should not resolve to attend a festival preceded or followed by an entertainment when chiefly meat, fish, or roasted slices are served. Such an entertainment may be attended when the specified route and assembly conditions are suitable.
