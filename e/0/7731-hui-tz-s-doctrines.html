Hui Tzŭ was a man of many ideas. His works would fill five carts. But

Hui Tzŭ's doctrines were paradoxical, and his terms were used ambiguously. They included claims about infinite greatness and smallness, the relativity of heaven and earth, the nature of objects and perception, and numerous sophisms concerning animals, names, motion, measurement, and division.
