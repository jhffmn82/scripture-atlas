Abstract written by a model

In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, the believer’s body belongs to the Lord and should glorify God as a member of Christ and temple of the Holy Spirit. In Hymns of Guru Nanak, the body is an impure, temporary mixture that houses the soul but ultimately falls into dust or ashes.

the body in Hymns of Guru Nanak

Guru Nanak · the work’s page · read it where the body first appears

Abstract written by a model

The body is an impure, temporary mixture of wind, water, and fire, with nine gates and a tenth door and soul within the heart. It is called a carriage, palace, temple, and house of God, yet depends on breath, is burdened by worldly conduct, and finally falls into dust or ashes, leaving possessions behind.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Sri Rag .. Ashtapadi

The body is impure without the Word and is temporary, dependent on breath, and destined to return to dust. It cannot be preserved by adornment, ritual, wealth, or worldly pleasure.

Rag Gauri .. Gauri Chhant

The body is made of wind, water, and fire, has nine gates and a tenth door, and is ultimately reduced to ashes. It is described as temporary, burdened by worldly conduct, and unable to retain possessions after death.
