Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Luke, the chief priests conspire to put Jesus to death, arrange his arrest and trials, secure his crucifixion, and scoff at him. In The Gospel According to John, the chief priests shout for Jesus to be crucified, declare allegiance to Caesar, and ask Pilate to alter Jesus’ title.

chief priests in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where chief priests first appears

Abstract written by a model

The chief priests, with scribes and elders, are a group set on putting Jesus to death while fearing the people. They agree to pay Judas, come against Jesus with swords and clubs, and convene a council that declares no more witness is needed after he claims to be Son of God. They then accuse him before Herod and Pilate, secure his crucifixion, and scoff at him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 22

The chief priests, with the scribes, seek a way to kill Jesus because they fear the people, and they gladly agree to give Judas money. Chief priests, temple captains, and elders come against Jesus with swords and clubs, and Jesus tells them it is their hour and the power of darkness. Chief priests and scribes later gather with the elders in council, question Jesus, and declare they need no more witness after hearing him say he is the Son of God.

Chapter 23

The chief priests, with the scribes, vehemently accuse Jesus before Herod and press the charges before Pilate. Their voices, with the crowd's, prevail in securing Jesus' crucifixion, and the rulers scoff at him on the cross.
