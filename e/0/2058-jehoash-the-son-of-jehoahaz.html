Jehoash the son of Jehoahaz in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 560 to 539 BC · the work’s page · read it where Jehoash the son of Jehoahaz first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jehoash (also called Joash), son of Jehoahaz and grandson of Jehu, was king of Israel who reigned sixteen years in Samaria. He did evil in the LORD's sight, walking in the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat. He wept over the sick Elisha, but struck the ground only three times at Elisha's command, provoking Elisha's anger. He struck Benhadad three times and recovered Israel's cities. Challenged by Amaziah of Judah, he answered with a parable of a thistle and a cedar, then defeated Judah at Beth Shemesh, captured Amaziah, broke down part of Jerusalem's wall, and carried treasures and hostages to Samaria. He died, was buried in Samaria, and was succeeded by his son Jeroboam.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

Jehoash (also called Joash) began to reign over Israel in Samaria in the thirty-seventh year of Joash of Judah and reigned sixteen years, doing evil and walking in the sins of Jeroboam. He fought Amaziah king of Judah, wept over the sick Elisha, and struck the ground only three times at Elisha's command, drawing Elisha's anger. He struck Benhadad three times and recovered the cities of Israel, then slept with his fathers and was buried in Samaria while Jeroboam sat on his throne.

Chapter 14

Jehoash answered Amaziah's challenge with a parable of a thistle and a cedar, warning him to stay home. He defeated Judah at Beth Shemesh, took Amaziah, broke down part of Jerusalem's wall, carried off gold, silver, vessels and hostages to Samaria, and then died and was buried in Samaria, succeeded by his son Jeroboam.
