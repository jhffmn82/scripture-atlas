Abstract written by a model

In Proverbs, pride precedes destruction; everyone proud in heart is an abomination to the LORD and will not go unpunished, and an arrogant spirit comes before a fall. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, pride is hated by the Lord and by men, begins when a man's heart departs from his maker, has sin as its beginning, and causes whoever keeps it to pour out abomination; it was not created for men. In The Book of Jeremiah, pride is condemned by the LORD, who says he will ruin it in Judah and Jerusalem; the people are urged not to be proud and to give glory to the LORD, while the narrator weeps over their pride and the king and queen mother are told to humble themselves. In The Second Book of Nephi, pride brings destruction to the wicked, causes Gentiles to stumble, burns the proud who do wickedly, corrupts and lifts up churches, leads them to rob the poor and persecute the meek, and condemns the wise, learned, and rich who are puffed up. In The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi, pride is abominable to God; it causes some people to lift up their hearts, wear costly apparel, and persecute their brethren because they suppose they are better than others, and it can destroy their souls. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, pride prevents preparation to meet God when a person is not stripped of it; those puffed up in pride and setting their hearts on the vain things of the world must repent. In The Book of Helaman, pride begins with little dissensions, grows through riches and prosperity, enters professing church members’ hearts, causes persecution of many brethren, and remains among most people by the eighty-seventh year. In The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record, pride pollutes churches through self-exaltation and fine apparel and causes churches, leaders, teachers, and people to engage in envy, strife, malice, persecution, and other iniquities.
