Zaotar

person · 3 works · 7 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: The Zaotar, him who is the Zaotar, the Zaotar

Abstract written by a model

In Yasna, Zaotar is a priest, Ratu, and speaker in the ritual dialogue who brings or presides over the Baresman and Zaothra ritual, asks the Ratu to speak forth, and speaks forth as the Ahû to be revered and chosen. In Yasna, Zaotar is described as far advanced in good thoughts, words, and deeds, receives the Ratu’s blessing, and responds by wishing what is better than the good and rejecting what is worse than the evil. In Visparad, Zaotar is a priest and Ratu’s announcer who calls priests, community, chiefs, immortals, and Saoshyants, identifies himself, and recites Staota Yêsnya with praise.

Zaotar in Yasna

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Zaotar first appears

Abstract written by a model

Zaotar is a priest, Ratu, and speaker in the ritual dialogue. It brings or presides over the Baresman and Zaothra ritual, asks the Ratu to speak forth, and speaks forth as the Ahû to be revered and chosen. Its speech recurs in the concluding exchange and is also associated with a righteous, holy, and learned Ratu.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Yasna III.

The Zaotar brings or presides over the Baresman and Zaothra ritual and speaks in the concluding exchange. He asks the Ratu to speak forth and is then told to speak forth as the Ahû to be revered and chosen.

Yasna VIII.

The Zaotar is described as speaking forth to the Ratu as an Ahû to be revered and chosen. The Zaotar's speech is repeated in the concluding exchange.

Yasna XIV (Sp. XV).

The Zaotar speaks in the ritual dialogue and is described as speaking forth in relation to an Ahû and the Ratu. The Zaotar is also said to speak forth when the Ratu is righteous, holy, and learned.

Visparad III.

The Zaotar calls for priests, community members, chiefs of the Mazdayasnians, the Bounteous Immortals, and the Saoshyants. He identifies himself as the Zaotar and declares that he will recite the Staota Yêsnya with memorised intoning, chanting, and praise.
