Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, the psalmist’s prayer is asked to rise before the LORD like incense and evening sacrifice and is declared always set against evil deeds. In Psalms, David’s prayer pleads for the LORD to hear his petitions and relieve him, continuing with requests for a quick answer, deliverance, guidance, revival, and the cutting off of his enemies. In The Gospel According to Matthew, prayer should be done in secret in an inner room with the door shut, not to be seen, and should avoid vain repetitions because the Father knows needs beforehand. In The Gospel According to Luke, prayer is taught through Jesus’ response to a disciple, including requests for the Father’s name to be kept holy, his Kingdom to come, daily bread, forgiveness, and deliverance from the evil one, with persistence urged. In The Gospel According to Luke, prayer is taught as something people must always do without giving up, illustrated by the persistent widow and contrasted between the Pharisee’s self-righteous prayer and the tax collector’s humble plea for mercy. In The Letter from James, prayer is prescribed for the suffering and sick alongside the elders’ anointing, with the prayer of faith healing the sick and a righteous person’s insistent prayer being powerfully effective. In Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara), prayer is worship and a means for believers to seek help with steadfastness and Allah’s companionship, performed facing the Inviolable Place of Worship and guarded especially as the midmost prayer. In Surah 13: The Thunder (Ar-Ra'd), prayer belongs truly to Allah, while prayer directed to others receives no response, and the righteous are regular in prayer and promised reward.

and 4 more works

In Surah 21: The Prophets (Al-Anbiyaa), prayer is made to Allah in times of distress and need, and Allah hears and responds to the prayers of Noah, Job, Dhu’n-Nun, and Zachariah. In The Table-Talk of the Prophet Mohammad (Part 1), prayer is praised as a duty witnessed by angels, with congregational prayers receiving double the reward, missed prayers performed when remembered, and prayer connected with ablution, purity, and charity. In Yasna, prayer is offered by the praiser to Ahura Mazda with praises, seeking grace, protection, righteous lands, guidance, and help through supplication and stretched hands. In Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, prayer is an act of worship and ritual utterance offered with sacrifice, propitiation, and glorification to Ahura Mazda, holy beings, and figures including Asha-Vahista, Drvâspa, and Sraosha.

prayer in Yashts (Yasts) I-XII

James Darmesteter · the work’s page · read it where prayer first appears

Abstract written by a model

Prayer is an act of worship and ritual utterance offered with sacrifice, propitiation, and glorification to Ahura Mazda, holy beings, and figures including Asha-Vahista, Drvâspa, and Sraosha. It uses hymns, invocations, holy words, blessings, and adoration; it is blessed with strength and vigour, increases Asha-Vahista, and is associated with protection from fiends.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

II. Haptân Yast.

Prayer is offered with sacrifice, propitiation, and glorification to Ahura Mazda and the holy beings. The prayer is blessed together with strength and vigour.

III. Ardibehist Yast.

Prayer is used with hymns, invocations, holy words, sacrifice, blessings, and adoration to increase Asha-Vahista. Sacrifice and prayer are blessed along with the strength and vigour of Asha-Vahista, Airyaman, and Saoka.

IX. Gôs Yast.

Prayer accompanies sacrifice and glorification to Drvâspa, and the unit blesses the sacrifice and prayer of the powerful, holy Drvâspa.

XI. Srôsh Yast Hâdhôkht.

Prayer is offered with sacrifice, propitiation, and glorification to Sraosha and other holy beings. The strength and vigour of Sraosha's prayer are blessed, and prayer is associated with protection from fiends.
