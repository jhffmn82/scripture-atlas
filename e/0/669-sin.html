Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Chapter 21 portrays sin as a snake whose lion-like teeth slay souls and as a two-edged sword whose stroke cannot heal. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Chapter 27 pictures sin as a nail wedged between stones in buying and selling and as a lion lurking for evildoers, with many sinning for profit. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, sin must not be minimized or compounded, sinners face the Lord’s indignation, and unrighteous gains profit nothing in calamity. In The Book of Hosea, sin is the reason Israel has fallen, and Israel is to ask the LORD to forgive its sins so that he may heal its waywardness and turn away his anger. In The Gospel According to John, sin enslaves everyone who commits it; Jesus forgives the woman accused of adultery, tells her to sin no more, and says unbelievers will die in their sins. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, sin is a universal power that affects all people, entered through one man, reigns in death, enslaves as a master, and is overcome by abounding grace. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, sin dwells in the narrator’s flesh, produces coveting through the commandment, deceives and kills, and wars against the mind to hold the narrator captive. In The Letter to the Hebrews, sins are not taken away by animal blood or repeated sacrifices; Christ’s one offering deals with them permanently, while willful sin after receiving truth leaves no sacrifice for sins.

and 2 more works

In The Letter from James, lust draws away and entices, conceives and bears sin, and full-grown sin produces death; knowing good but not doing it is also sin. In 1 John, sin is lawlessness and all unrighteousness; denying it is self-deception, confessing it brings forgiveness and cleansing through Jesus Christ, and sin is distinguished as leading to death or not leading to death.

sin in Paul’s Letter to the Romans

author unknown · about AD 57 · the work’s page · read it where sin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sin is presented as a universal power affecting the world: all, Jews and Greeks alike, have sinned and fall short of God's glory, and the law brings knowledge of it. It entered through one man, bringing death to all; it existed before the law but is not charged without law. It reigns in death and enslaves as a master, paying wages of death. Grace abounds more, so believers, who died to sin, are not under its dominion.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

All, both Jews and Greeks, are under sin, and the text quotes scripture that no one is righteous, no one does good, and there is no fear of God before their eyes. All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and the law brings the knowledge of sin.

Chapter 5

Sin entered the world through one man, and all sinned, so death passed to all. Sin was in the world before the law but is not charged when there is no law, and the law came in so that the trespass might abound. Sin reigned in death, but grace abounded more where sin abounded.

Chapter 6

Sin is something believers died to and must not let reign in their mortal body or have their members as instruments of unrighteousness. It holds people in bondage as a master, and serving it leads to death. Its wages are death, and it will not have dominion over believers because they are under grace.

sin in 1 John

author unknown · undated · the work’s page · read it where sin first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sin is lawlessness and includes all unrighteousness. The letter first says denying sin is self-deception and makes God a liar, while confessing it brings God's forgiveness and cleansing through the blood of Jesus Christ. It then says whoever sins has not seen or known him, those born of God do not sin because his seed remains, and the Son was revealed to take sins away. Finally it distinguishes sin leading to death from sin not leading to death, for which one should ask and God gives life.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

1 John / Chapter 1

Saying we have no sin deceives ourselves, and saying we haven't sinned makes God a liar, with his word not in us. Sin is cleansed by the blood of Jesus Christ, and confessing sins results in God's forgiveness and cleansing from all unrighteousness.

Chapter 3

Sin is described as lawlessness, and whoever sins has not seen or known him. Those born of God do not commit sin because his seed remains in them, and the Son of God was revealed to take sins away.

Chapter 5

All unrighteousness is sin, and there is sin not leading to death and sin leading to death. For a brother's sin not leading to death, one should ask and God will give life. About sin leading to death, the writer does not say to make a request.
