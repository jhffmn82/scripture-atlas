Abstract written by a model

In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, Môksha is the truth and perfection known and sought by monks who abandon worldly bonds and follow the right path. In Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I, Liberation is the distant goal of religious practice and virtuous exertion, reached through annihilating Karman and escaping Samsâra. In Sutrakritanga, Book II, Liberation is an existent state, the shore of Samsâra, and the goal of the religious path. In Sutrakritanga, Book II, Môksha is the gain sought by a Sramana through the vow leading to Brahman.

Môksha in Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Môksha first appears

Abstract written by a model

Môksha is a truth known by a monk, called perfection and valued by those free from worldly bonds. Its knowledge helps distinguish household from monastic conduct, reject attachment, and follow the right path. It cannot be attained through harmful ritual practices. Some claim to realise it through Vinaya. Great sages ascend to it after abandoning pride and distinctions, and those who turn from worldly life reach its goal through pious acts.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Third Lecture, Called the Knowledge of Troubles / First Chapter. .. Fourth Chapter.

A monk who knows Môksha can answer those who confuse household and monastic conduct. Knowledge of Môksha supports the rejection of worldly attachment and the pursuit of the right path.

Seventh Lecture, Called Description of the Wicked.

Môksha is not attained through ablutions, abstention from acids and salt, or tending fire. The fool who treats such practices as a means to Môksha kills living beings.

Twelfth Lecture, Called the Creed.

It is described as an object that some claim to realise through Vinaya.

Thirteenth Lecture, Called the Real Truth.

Môksha is the place to which great sages ascend after leaving behind pride and distinctions such as Gôtra.

Fifteenth Lecture, Called the Yamakas.

Môksha is valued most by men who are free from bondage and do not desire life. Those who turn from worldly life reach its goal through pious acts.

final Liberation in Sutrakritanga (Sûtrakritâṅga), Book I

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where final Liberation first appears

Abstract written by a model

Liberation is the real, best, distant goal of religious practice and virtuous exertion. It is freedom from Samsâra, reached when Karman is annihilated and one does not return to the world. It is wrongly linked to bathing, water, fire, and other outward practices. A monk seeks it through meditation, religious practices, carefulness, right knowledge and conduct, renunciation of pride, deceit, sin, attachment, worldly vanities, and causes of worldly existence. It requires realizing singleness and solitude, and is not gained by a monk who still acts like a householder. It is finally obtained through the highest good, austerities, silence, and pure food.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Seventh Lecture, Called Description of the Wicked.

Liberation is wrongly associated with bathing, using water, tending fire, and other outward practices. A person who annihilates Karman and does not return to the world reaches the condition described as freedom from the Samsâra.

Eighth Lecture, Called on Exertion.

Liberation is the distant end toward which the virtuous exert themselves. A monk should wander, meditating and performing religious practices, until he obtains liberation.

Ninth Lecture, Called the Law.

A wise man brings about final Liberation by knowing and renouncing excessive pride, deceit, and worldly vanities.

Tenth Lecture, Called Carefulness.

Liberation is obtained by realizing that one is single and alone, practicing carefulness, and abandoning sin and worldly attachment. It is described as real and the best thing.

Eleventh Lecture, Called the Path.

Liberation is brought about by giving up excessive pride, deceit, and all causes of worldly existence. The wise man who abandons these obtains liberation.

Twelfth Lecture, Called the Creed.

It is reached through right knowledge and conduct.

Thirteenth Lecture, Called the Real Truth.

Final Liberation is not obtained by a person who becomes a monk but continues to act like a householder.

Fourteenth Lecture, Called the Nirgrantha.

Final Liberation is obtained by one who desires the highest good, practises austerities and silence, and lives on pure food.

Sixteenth Lecture, Called the Song.

Liberation is sought by the Nirgrantha.

Liberation in Sutrakritanga, Book II

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Liberation first appears

Abstract written by a model

Liberation is an existent state, shore of Samsâra, and goal of the religious path. A monk seeks, knows, and strives to gain it by renouncing actions, worldly occupations, and householder life. It cannot be reached through suffering, killing, evil deeds, or condemned religious and worldly activity.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

I say: here in the East, West, North, and South there are some men, viz. Âryas,

A monk searches the Law, knows the Law, and endeavours to gain Liberation. He renounces actions, worldly occupations, and the life of a householder in pursuit of this aim.

Second Lecture, Called on Activity.

Liberation is not reached through the conduct of those who cause suffering, kill, commit various evil deeds, or practise the condemned forms of religious and worldly activity.

Fifth Lecture, Called Freedom from Error.

Liberation exists.
