Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Joshua, the children of Joseph, also called the house of Joseph, receive a lot, complain that it is too small, and are promised the hill country and victory over the Canaanites. In The Book of Judges, the house of Joseph goes up against Bethel with the LORD's help, takes the city, and prevails over the Amorites, who become subject to forced labor. In The Book of Zechariah, the house of Joseph is promised salvation and return through the LORD's mercy.

children of Joseph in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · about 600 BC · the work’s page · read it where children of Joseph first appears

Abstract written by a model

The children of Joseph, also called the house of Joseph, are a group made up of two tribes, Manasseh and Ephraim, living in the north. They first receive a lot with a border from the Jordan at Jericho through the hill country to the sea. They then complain to Joshua that a single allotment is too small for a numerous people blessed by the LORD. Joshua affirms their great power and promises them the hill country and victory over the Canaanites.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 16

The lot came out for the children of Joseph, with a border running from the Jordan at Jericho through the hill country to Bethel, Luz, Ataroth, Beth Horon the lower and Gezer, ending at the sea. Manasseh and Ephraim, the children of Joseph, took their inheritance.

Chapter 17

The children of Joseph complained to Joshua that one lot and one part was too little for a numerous people blessed by the LORD. When Joshua told them to clear the forest, they said the hill country was not enough and the valley Canaanites had iron chariots. Joshua, addressing them as the house of Joseph (Ephraim and Manasseh), said they were numerous and powerful and would have the hill country and drive out the Canaanites.
