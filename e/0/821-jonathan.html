In The Second Book of Samuel, Jonathan is Saul’s son whom David mourns and whose memory inspires kindness to Mephibosheth; another Jonathan serves as David’s messenger.

Jonathan in The First Book of Samuel

author unknown · about 930 to 721 BC · the work’s page · read it where Jonathan first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jonathan is Saul's son and a warrior who leads one thousand men at Gibeah of Benjamin and strikes the Philistine garrison in Geba. He and Saul alone have a sword or spear on the day of battle. Acting in faith in the LORD's power to save, he secretly attacks another garrison with his young armor bearer, setting off a panic. Unaware of his father's oath, he eats honey, is chosen by lot, and Saul says he must die, but the people rescue him, crediting him with a great salvation in Israel and saying he worked with God. The story then turns to his bond with David. He loves David as his own soul, makes a covenant with him, and gives him his robe, sword, bow, and sash. He warns David of Saul's plans, defends him to Saul, and restores him to Saul's presence. After Saul rages and throws a spear at him, he accepts that David is doomed, grieves, carries out the arrow signal, and parts from David with an oath of peace. At the end he strengthens David's hand in God, affirms David will be king with Jonathan beside him, makes a covenant before the LORD, and returns home.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

Jonathan led one thousand men at Gibeah of Benjamin and struck the Philistine garrison in Geba. He later stayed in Geba with Saul and the people, and he and Saul alone had a sword or spear on the day of battle.

Chapter 14

Jonathan secretly goes with his armor bearer to attack the Philistine garrison, setting a sign and climbing up to kill the men there, which starts the panic. Unaware of his father's oath, he eats honey and is later picked by lot as the offender, and Saul says he must die. The people rescue him, saying he worked with God that day.

Chapter 18

Jonathan's soul is knit with David's, and he loves David as his own soul. He makes a covenant with David and gives him his robe, clothing, sword, bow, and sash.

Chapter 19

Jonathan, who greatly delights in David, warns him of Saul's plan and tells him to hide. He speaks well of David to Saul, argues that David has not sinned and that killing him would be shedding innocent blood, and brings David back into Saul's presence after Saul swears not to kill him.

Chapter 20

Jonathan first doubts that his father wants David dead, then promises to help, makes a covenant with David's house, and arranges the arrow signal. After Saul rages at him and throws a spear, Jonathan knows David is doomed, eats nothing that day in grief, carries out the signal, and parts from David with an oath of peace.

Chapter 23

Jonathan goes to David in the woods and strengthens his hand in God. He tells David that Saul will not find him, that David will be king over Israel with Jonathan next to him, and that Saul knows this too; the two make a covenant before the LORD and Jonathan returns home.

Jonathan in The Second Book of Samuel

author unknown · about 950 BC · the work’s page · read it where Jonathan first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jonathan is a son of Saul, described as lovely and pleasant, whom David calls brother and whose love surpassed the love of women. He owns a bow that never turned back from the blood of the slain. He is reported dead with Saul, and David mourns him in a lamentation. His memory drives David's kindness toward his lame son Mephibosheth. Later, a Jonathan stays at En Rogel with Ahimaaz as David's messenger, evades being seen, hides in a well at Bahurim, and urges David to cross the water quickly.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Second Book of Samuel / Chapter 1

Jonathan is reported dead along with his father Saul, and David and his men mourn him. In David's lamentation his bow is said not to have turned back, he and Saul were not divided in death, and David calls him his brother whose love was wonderful to him.

Chapter 9

Jonathan is the reason David seeks to show kindness, and is named as father of Mephibosheth. Ziba reports that he still has a son who is lame in his feet.

Chapter 17

Jonathan stayed with Ahimaaz by En Rogel, where a female servant reported to them, because they could not risk being seen entering the city. After a boy saw them and told Absalom, they fled to Bahurim and hid in a well, then came out and told King David to cross the water quickly.
