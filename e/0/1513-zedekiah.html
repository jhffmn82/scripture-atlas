Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Kings, Zedekiah is Judah’s king, originally named Mattaniah, who rebels against Babylon and is blinded, fettered, and carried to Babylon after Jerusalem falls. In The Book of Jeremiah, Zedekiah is the last king of Judah, whose disobedience and wavering resistance to Jeremiah culminate in Jerusalem’s fall, the killing of his sons, his blinding, and his captivity in Babylon. In The Book of Ezekiel, Zedekiah is Judah’s royal offspring who rebels against Babylon by seeking Egypt’s aid and is condemned to die in Babylon.

Zedekiah in The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Zedekiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Zedekiah, originally named Mattaniah, was the uncle of Jehoiachin and was installed as king of Judah by Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon, who changed his name. He began reigning at twenty-one and ruled eleven years in Jerusalem, doing evil like Jehoiakim and rebelling against Babylon. When the city was breached he fled by night, but the Chaldeans overtook him near Jericho. At Riblah his sons were killed before him, he was blinded, fettered, and carried to Babylon.

Chapter 24

Zedekiah, formerly named Mattaniah and uncle of Jehoiachin, was made king by the king of Babylon, who changed his name. He was twenty-one when he began to reign, reigned eleven years in Jerusalem, and his mother was Hamutal daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah. He did evil like Jehoiakim and rebelled against the king of Babylon.

Chapter 25

Zedekiah fled by night by the way of the Arabah when the city was breached, but the Chaldean army overtook him in the plains of Jericho and his army scattered. He was taken to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where his sons were killed before his eyes, his eyes were put out, and he was bound in fetters and carried to Babylon.

Zedekiah in The Book of Jeremiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Zedekiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Zedekiah is the last king of Judah in these records, a son of Josiah and son of Hamutal, who succeeded Coniah as king. He began to reign at twenty-one and reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. He did evil in the LORD's sight like Jehoiakim, and neither he, his servants, nor the people heeded the LORD's words spoken through Jeremiah. He rebelled against the king of Babylon. The records center on his wavering relationship with Jeremiah and the prophesied doom that follows from his disobedience. Early on, he sends Elasah to Babylon and sends men such as Pashhur and Zephaniah to ask Jeremiah to inquire of the LORD, hoping Nebuchadnezzar will withdraw. The answers are consistently grim: he is likened to the bad figs and given up with his princes, told to put his neck under Babylon's yoke and live, and warned he will be delivered to Babylon. He imprisons Jeremiah in the court of the guard for prophesying the city's fall, yet secretly consults him, moves him to better conditions with a daily loaf of bread, and orders Ebedmelech to rescue him after yielding to the princes, who he says he cannot oppose. He makes a covenant to free Hebrew servants, and a prophecy says he will die in peace with burnings like his fathers. When urged to surrender, he refuses out of fear of mockery by Jews who defected to the Chaldeans. The end comes with the fall of Jerusalem. He flees by night toward the Arabah but is overtaken in the plains of Jericho and brought to Nebuchadnezzar at Riblah. There his sons are killed before his eyes, his eyes are put out, and he is bound in fetters and carried to Babylon, imprisoned until his death. His story is one of a weak, fearful king whose resistance to the prophetic word ends in the loss of his sons, his sight, his freedom, and his kingdom.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

Zedekiah sends Pashhur and Zephaniah to ask Jeremiah to inquire of the LORD, hoping the LORD will deal with them according to his wondrous works so that Nebuchadnezzar withdraws. The answer is that he, his servants, and the surviving people will be delivered into Nebuchadnezzar's hand and struck with the sword without pity or mercy.

Chapter 24

Zedekiah, king of Judah, is likened to the bad figs. The LORD says he will give up Zedekiah and his princes to be tossed among all the kingdoms of the earth as a reproach, proverb, taunt, and curse.

Chapter 27

Zedekiah king of Judah is addressed by Jeremiah, who tells him to bring his people's necks under the yoke of the king of Babylon, serve him, and live. He is asked why he and his people should die by sword, famine, and pestilence, and is warned not to heed prophets who say they will not serve Babylon.

Chapter 32

Zedekiah, king of Judah, has shut Jeremiah in the court of the guard for prophesying that the city will fall. The prophecy states that he will not escape the Chaldeans but will be delivered to the king of Babylon, speak with him mouth to mouth, and be taken to Babylon until the LORD visits him.

Chapter 34

Zedekiah is told he will not escape Babylon's hand, will be taken, will see the king of Babylon face to face, and will go to Babylon, yet will die in peace with burnings made for him like his royal predecessors. He had made a covenant with the people of Jerusalem to proclaim liberty to Hebrew servants, and is later named among those to be given into the hands of enemies and the Babylonian army.

Chapter 37

Zedekiah, son of Josiah, becomes king in place of Coniah, and neither he nor his servants nor the people heed the LORD's words through Jeremiah. He sends men to ask Jeremiah to pray, later secretly asks him for a word from the LORD, and is told he will be delivered to the king of Babylon. He grants Jeremiah's plea by moving him to the court of the guard with a daily loaf of bread.

Chapter 38

Zedekiah tells the princes that Jeremiah is in their hand and that the king cannot oppose them, but later orders Ebedmelech to rescue Jeremiah. He secretly swears not to kill Jeremiah, hears the counsel to surrender, and refuses out of fear of the defected Jews. He then tells Jeremiah to conceal their conversation from the princes.

Chapter 39

Zedekiah king of Judah fled Jerusalem by night with his men of war through the gate between the two walls toward the Arabah, but was overtaken in the plains of Jericho and brought to Nebuchadnezzar at Riblah. There his sons were killed before his eyes, his eyes were put out, and he was bound in fetters to be taken to Babylon.

Chapter 52

Zedekiah began to reign at twenty-one, reigned eleven years in Jerusalem, did evil in the LORD's sight as Jehoiakim had, and rebelled against the king of Babylon. He fled the besieged city by night but was overtaken in the plains of Jericho, taken to Riblah, and judged; his sons were killed before his eyes, his eyes were put out, and he was bound in fetters and imprisoned in Babylon until his death.

A royal offspring of Judah taken by Babylon's king, bound by covenant and oath so the kingdom would stay low but stand. He rebelled by sending ambassadors to Egypt for horses and many people. The LORD declares he will not escape, will be snared, and will die in Babylon; his fugitives fall by the sword.

His fugitives will fall by the sword and the rest will be scattered to every wind.
