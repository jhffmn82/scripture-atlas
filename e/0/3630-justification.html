Abstract written by a model

Justification is presented as a status before God that cannot be obtained by works of the law; no flesh is justified that way. It is given freely by God's grace through redemption in Christ Jesus and received by faith apart from works. The account begins with this principle, then illustrates it with Abraham, whose belief was credited as righteousness while uncircumcised, with circumcision a seal. It extends to those who believe in him who raised Jesus. Believers are justified by faith and Christ's blood, gaining peace with God and assurance of salvation from wrath, as one act of righteousness brings life to all. Freed from sin, they become servants of righteousness, aiming at sanctification. Finally, Gentiles attained the righteousness of faith while Israel, pursuing it by works, stumbled.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

No flesh will be justified in God's sight by works of the law. Instead, people are justified freely by his grace through the redemption in Christ Jesus, and a man is justified by faith apart from works of the law.

Chapter 4

Righteousness is credited to Abraham because he believed God, and is counted by God apart from works. It was counted while he was in uncircumcision, and circumcision was a seal of it. It will also be accounted to those who believe in him who raised Jesus from the dead.

Chapter 5

Believers are justified by faith and by Christ's blood, which brings peace with God and assurance of being saved from wrath. The free gift followed many trespasses to justification, and through one act of righteousness all men were justified to life.

Chapter 6

Righteousness is what believers, freed from sin, became bondservants of, and it is the goal of obedience. They are told to present their members as instruments and servants of righteousness to God for sanctification. When they were servants of sin, they were free from righteousness.

Chapter 9

Gentiles who did not pursue righteousness attained the righteousness of faith, while Israel, following a law of righteousness, did not arrive at it. The reason given is that Israel did not seek it by faith but as if by works, and so stumbled over the stumbling stone, while those who believe in him will not be disappointed.
