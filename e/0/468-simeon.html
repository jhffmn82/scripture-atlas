Simeon

group · 3 works · 16 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Simeonites, children of Simeon, sons of Simeon, tribe of Simeon, tribe of the children of Simeon

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Joshua, the tribe of Simeon receives the second lot of land, located within Judah's territory, including thirteen cities with villages and four additional cities. In The Book of Judges, Simeon joins Judah in fighting the Canaanites of Zephath, which they destroy and rename Hormah. In 1 Chronicles, Simeon is a patriarchal figure and the name of his descendant clan, whose genealogy and settlement history are recorded.

tribe of Simeon in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where tribe of Simeon first appears

Abstract written by a model

The tribe of Simeon received the second lot of the land, with its inheritance located in the middle of Judah's territory because Judah's portion was too large. It gained thirteen cities with villages, including Beersheba, Moladah, Ziklag and Sharuhen, plus four more cities and surrounding villages to Baalath Beer.

Chapter 19

The second lot came out for the tribe of Simeon, whose inheritance lay in the middle of Judah's. It received thirteen cities with villages (Beersheba, Moladah, Ziklag, Sharuhen and others) plus four more (Ain, Rimmon, Ether, Ashan), and the villages around them as far as Baalath Beer, Ramah of the South.

Simeon in 1 Chronicles

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Simeon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Simeon is a patriarchal figure and the name of his descendant clan. His sons were Nemuel, Jamin, Jarib, Zerah, and Shaul. Descent is traced onward through Shallum, Mibsam, and Mishma, with Shimei's large family. The clan did not multiply like Judah, lived in Beersheba, Ziklag and other cities until David's reign, and kept its genealogy.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Simeon's sons are listed as Nemuel, Jamin, Jarib, Zerah, and Shaul, with descent traced through Shallum, Mibsam, Mishma, and onward. Shimei had sixteen sons and six daughters, but the family did not multiply like Judah. They lived in cities such as Beersheba, Moladah, and Ziklag until David's reign, and kept their genealogy.
