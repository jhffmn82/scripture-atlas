Simon

Abstract written by a model

Simon is a Jewish leader, son of Mattathias and brother of Judas and Jonathan, of the sons of Joarib. He is described as a man of counsel and father to his brothers, and he fought for Israel with his family since youth. The records follow him from soldier to ruler of his people and then to his death. Early on, Judas sends him with three thousand men to rescue the Jews of Galilee. He defeats the Gentiles, pursues them to the gate of Ptolemais, and brings the Galilean and Arbatta Jews to Judea. After Judas dies he helps bury him at Modin and flees with Jonathan to the wilderness of Tekoah. He joins an ambush of the children of Jambri, and at Bethbasi he burns Bacchides's siege engines and helps defeat him. He later supports Jonathan against Apollonius and at Azotus brings the army forward to defeat the phalanx. He takes Joppa and garrisons it, and builds Adida in the plain. When Jonathan falls, Simon encourages the fearful people and is accepted as leader in place of Judas and Jonathan. He pays Tryphon's ransom demand without result, buries Jonathan at Modin, and builds a tomb monument for his father, mother and four brothers. He fortifies Judea and secures peace and tax release from Demetrius, who confirms him as high priest and makes him a friend. He takes Gazara, Bethsura and the Jerusalem citadel, makes Joppa a harbor, and enlarges the borders. He glorifies the sanctuary, renews the alliance with Rome and Sparta, and sends Numenius to Rome with a gold shield. Contracts are dated by the first year of Simon. The people honor him as leader, governor and high priest, a position held until a faithful prophet arises, and his son John is made leader of all his forces. He answers Antiochus's demands by calling the lands his fathers' inheritance and offering one hundred talents for Joppa and Gazara. Now old, he sends his oldest sons Judas and John to fight in his place. On a visit to Jericho with his sons Mattathias and Judas in the 177th year, Ptolemy receives him deceitfully at Dok, and Simon is killed at a banquet.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Judas tells Simon to choose men and rescue the Jews of Galilee, and he goes with three thousand men. He fights many battles, defeats the Gentiles, pursues them to the gate of Ptolemais, kills about three thousand, and brings the Galilean and Arbatta Jews to Judea with gladness.

Chapter 9

Simon helped bury Judas at Modin and fled with Jonathan to the wilderness of Tekoah. He joined the ambush of the children of Jambri. Left in charge of Bethbasi, he sallied out, burned Bacchides's siege engines, and helped defeat him.

Chapter 10

Simon, Jonathan's brother, met him to help when Jonathan marched out against Apollonius. At Azotus he brought forward the army against the phalanx after the cavalry tired, and defeated them.

Chapter 12

Simon traveled as far as Ascalon and its nearby strongholds, then took Joppa because he heard its people planned to hand the stronghold to Demetrius' men, and he placed a garrison there. He also built Adida in the plain, fortifying it with gates and bars.

Chapter 13

Simon encourages the fearful people, recalls what his family has done for the laws and sanctuary, and is accepted as leader in place of Judas and Jonathan. He pays Tryphon's ransom demand without result, buries Jonathan at Modin, builds the tomb monument, fortifies Judea, and secures peace and tax release from King Demetrius. He takes Gazara and the Jerusalem citadel, is recorded as high priest in contracts, and makes his son John leader of all his forces.

Chapter 14

Simon brings peace and prosperity: he takes Joppa as a harbor, enlarges the borders, captures Gazara, Bethsura and the citadel, fortifies cities, and glorifies the sanctuary. He renews the alliance with Rome and Sparta, sends Numenius to Rome with a gold shield, and is honored by the people as leader, governor and high priest, accepting the office and its prerogatives of purple, gold and universal obedience.

Chapter 15

Simon, the high priest and governor, receives Antiochus's letter and the Roman letter written to him, and sends two thousand chosen men with silver, gold and arms to Antiochus, who refuses them. He impresses Athenobius with his wealth and attendance, and answers the demands by saying the lands are the fathers' inheritance, offering one hundred talents for Joppa and Gazara.

Chapter 16

Simon, now old, sent his two oldest sons Judas and John to fight in his and his brother's place, recalling the battles of Israel his family had fought since youth. While visiting the cities of the country, he went down to Jericho with his sons Mattathias and Judas in the 177th year, eleventh month (Sebat). Ptolemy received him deceitfully at Dok, and Simon was killed at a banquet.
