Boaz

Abstract written by a model

Boaz is a mighty man of wealth from Bethlehem, a relative of Naomi's husband and of the family of Elimelech, and a near kinsman of Naomi and Ruth. He owns a field and barley, and employs reapers, a servant over them, and maidens. In Chapter 2 he protects Ruth the Moabitess, commanding his young men not to touch her, welcoming her to stay in his field, praising her kindness to Naomi, and ordering extra grain left for her to glean. In Chapter 3 he is winnowing barley at night, wakes startled at midnight to find Ruth, blesses her as a worthy woman, and says a nearer kinsman exists. He swears to perform the kinsman's duty if the other will not, asks for secrecy, and gives her six measures of barley. In Chapter 4 he meets the nearer kinsman and ten elders at the gate. When the kinsman refuses to redeem Elimelech's land, Boaz takes the right of redemption, buys all that was Elimelech's, Chilion's and Mahlon's, and acquires Ruth as his wife. He marries her and is named in the genealogy as son of Salmon, descendant of Perez, and father of Obed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Boaz is introduced as a mighty man of wealth and a relative of Naomi's husband, of the family of Elimelech. He greets his reapers with a blessing, asks about Ruth, and tells her to stay in his field and drink from the vessels, saying he has commanded the young men not to touch her. He praises her kindness to Naomi, invites her to eat, and orders his men to let her glean among the sheaves and to leave extra grain for her.

Chapter 3

Boaz is described by Naomi as a kinsman who will be winnowing barley that night. After eating and drinking he lies down at the end of the heap of grain, wakes startled at midnight, and finds Ruth. He blesses her, praises her kindness and reputation as a worthy woman, and says a nearer kinsman exists. He swears by the LORD to do the kinsman's duty if the other will not, asks that her visit be kept secret, gives her six measures of barley, and goes into the city.

Chapter 4

Boaz sits at the gate, summons the near kinsman and ten elders, and offers the kinsman the chance to redeem Elimelech's land. When the kinsman refuses, Boaz takes the right of redemption, declares before the witnesses that he has bought all that was Elimelech's, Chilion's and Mahlon's and has purchased Ruth to be his wife. He marries Ruth, and he is named in the genealogy as the son of Salmon and father of Obed.
