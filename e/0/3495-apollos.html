Apollos

person · 2 works · 10 quoted facts · 1 connection

Abstract written by a model

In The Acts of the Apostles, Apollos is an eloquent Alexandrian Jew who teaches accurately about Jesus, receives fuller instruction from Priscilla and Aquila, helps believers in Achaia, and publicly refutes Jews by showing that Jesus is the Christ. In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, Apollos is a servant of God and fellow worker through whom the Corinthians believed, whom some claimed to follow, who watered what Paul planted, and who declined to visit Corinth for now but would come when able.

Apollos in The Acts of the Apostles

Abstract written by a model

Apollos is an eloquent Alexandrian Jew, mighty in the Scriptures, who comes to Ephesus teaching accurately about Jesus though knowing only John's baptism. Priscilla and Aquila instruct him more fully; encouraged by the brothers, he goes to Achaia, helps believers, and publicly refutes Jews, showing Jesus is the Christ.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

Apollos is a Jew from Alexandria, eloquent and mighty in the Scriptures, who comes to Ephesus. He teaches accurately about Jesus though he knows only John's baptism, and Priscilla and Aquila take him aside to teach him more fully. Encouraged by the brothers, who write to the disciples in Achaia, he goes there, helps believers, and publicly refutes the Jews from the Scriptures showing that Jesus is the Christ.

Apollos in Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians

author unknown · AD 53 to 54 · the work’s page · read it where Apollos first appears

Abstract written by a model

Apollos is a servant of God and fellow worker, called brother, through whom the Corinthians believed. Some Corinthians claimed to follow him. He watered what Paul planted, and he and Paul are the same, each rewarded for his own labor. Later, Paul urged him to visit Corinth; he declined for now but will come when able.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

Apollos is named as a figure some Corinthians claim to follow, and Paul calls him a servant through whom they believed. He watered what Paul planted, and he and Paul are the same, each receiving his own reward according to his own labor. He is listed among the things that belong to the believers.

Chapter 16

Paul says he strongly urged Apollos, called the brother, to come to the Corinthians with the brothers. It was not at all Apollos's desire to come now, but he will come when he has an opportunity.
