Abstract written by a model

Gao is a philosopher who compares man’s nature to qi-willow and water, calling it neither good nor bad. He treats benevolence as internal and righteousness as external; Mencius challenges him, arguing nature tends toward good. Gao also criticizes Xiao Pan as an ode of a little man.

Part I / Chapter 1 .. Chapter 16

Gao compares man's nature to the qi-willow and to water, arguing that it is indifferent to good and evil and that benevolence is internal while righteousness is external. Mencius challenges these claims by arguing that fashioning virtue from man's nature does not require violence and that man's nature tends toward good.
