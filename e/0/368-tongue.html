Abstract written by a model

In Proverbs, the tongue is portrayed as speech with power over death and life, whose fruit satisfies or brings strife and ruin. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, the tongue is portrayed as a deadly destructive force, and readers are urged to guard their mouths. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, speech and the tongue are presented as powers that can bring glory or dishonor, downfall or favor. In The Letter from James, the tongue is portrayed as a destructive, untamable force that can bless God and curse people.

tongue in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where tongue first appears

Abstract written by a model

The tongue is portrayed as a destructive force: a spark that burns or is quenched, its stroke breaks bones, and it kills more than the sword. Bound by iron and brass, it brings an evil death worse than Hades, falls on those forsaking the Lord, and the reader is urged to guard his mouth.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 28

The tongue is likened to a spark that burns when blown on and is quenched when spat upon, both coming from the mouth. Its stroke breaks bones, it has felled more people than the sword, and its yoke is iron and its bands brass. The reader is told to put a balance, weight, door, and bar on his mouth to guard his words.

speech and the tongue in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where speech and the tongue first appears

Abstract written by a model

Speech and the tongue are presented as a double-edged power: they hold both glory and dishonor and may cause a man's downfall. Chapter 5 urges consistency, patient answers or silence, and avoiding whispering, ambush, and double-tongued speech. Chapter 20 warns that much talk breeds hatred, a tongue's slip is worse than a fall on pavement, and wise speech wins love and favor.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Speech is described as holding both glory and dishonor, and a man's tongue may be his downfall. The reader is urged to keep speech consistent, to answer with patience or stay silent if he lacks understanding, and to avoid being a whisperer, lying in wait with the tongue, or having a double tongue.

Chapter 20

Much talk makes a person hated and abhorred, and an untimely reproof is faulty, though reproving is better than being angry. A slip with the tongue is said to be worse than a slip on a pavement. A man without grace is a tale out of season, and speech wisely used wins love and favor.

tongue in The Letter from James

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where tongue first appears

Abstract written by a model

The tongue is a little member that boasts great things, likened to a horse's bit, a ship's rudder, and a small fire spreading through a forest. It is a fire and world of iniquity that defiles the whole body, set aflame by Gehenna. No one can tame it; it is a restless evil full of deadly poison, both blessing God and cursing men.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

The tongue is described as a little member that boasts great things, likened to a horse's bit, a ship's rudder, and a small fire that spreads through a large forest. It is called a fire and a world of iniquity that defiles the whole body and is set on fire by Gehenna. Nobody can tame it, and it is a restless evil full of deadly poison, used both to bless God and to curse men.
