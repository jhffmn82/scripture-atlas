Moses' staff

Also called: A rod, Aaron’s rod, God’s rod, a snake, his rod, his staff, my staff, rod, rod (Aaron's rod), that in thy right hand, that which is in thy right hand, the rod, the rod that is in my hand, the rod which was turned to a serpent

Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, the rod is carried by Moses as God's rod, becomes a snake and back again, and as Aaron's rod becomes a serpent, swallows the magicians' rods, and turns the river's waters to blood. In Surah 7: The Heights (Al-A'raaf), the staff of Moses becomes a manifest serpent before Pharaoh and his people, swallows the wizards' lying display, and vindicates the Truth. In Surah 20: Taa-Haa, Moses' staff is used for leaning, tending sheep, and other purposes, then becomes a serpent when cast down and is taken up without fear. In Surah 26: The Poets (Ash-Shu'araa), Moses' staff becomes a manifest serpent, swallows what the wizards falsely display, and parts the sea when Moses strikes it.

rod (Aaron's rod) in Exodus

author unknown · about 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where rod (Aaron's rod) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The rod is a sign-working staff carried by Moses as God's rod. In Chapter 4 it becomes a snake when thrown down and a rod again when grasped by the tail, showing the LORD has appeared. In Chapter 7, as Aaron's rod, it becomes a serpent before Pharaoh, swallows the magicians' rods, and strikes the river, turning the waters to blood.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

The rod in Moses' hand becomes a snake when thrown down and a rod again when he takes it by the tail, as a sign that the LORD has appeared to him. The LORD tells him to take it to do the signs, and Moses takes it as God's rod on his journey to Egypt.

Chapter 7

Aaron's rod becomes a serpent when cast down before Pharaoh and swallows the magicians' rods. It is the rod Moses is told to take to the river, and Aaron lifts it to strike the waters, turning them to blood.
