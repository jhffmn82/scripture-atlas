Abstract written by a model

In The Acts of the Apostles, Timothy is a disciple from Lystra whom Paul circumcises and takes as a companion. In Paul’s First Letter to the Corinthians, Timothy is Paul's faithful child who does the Lord's work and is sent to the Corinthians. In Paul’s Letter to the Philippians, Timothy is Paul's co-sender and like-minded servant who genuinely cares for the Philippians. In 1 Thessalonians, Timothy is the authors' brother and God's servant sent to establish and comfort the Thessalonians in their faith. In 1 Timothy, Timothy is stationed at Ephesus and is charged by Paul to teach, lead, guard the faith, and pursue godliness. In 2 Timothy, Timothy is Paul's beloved child and the addressee charged to endure hardship, preach the word, fulfill his ministry, and come to Paul soon.

Timothy in 1 Timothy

Paul · the work’s page · read it where Timothy first appears

Abstract written by a model

Timothy is a young man, addressed as Paul's true child in faith and called a man of God, who is stationed at Ephesus. Paul charges him to command certain men not to teach a different doctrine, in keeping with earlier prophecies, so that he may wage the good warfare with faith and a good conscience. He has a gift given by prophecy with the laying on of the elders' hands, is nourished in the words of the faith, and suffers frequent infirmities. He is urged not to let anyone despise his youth but to be an example to believers in word, conduct, love, spirit, faith and purity, to devote himself to reading, exhortation and teaching, and to instruct the brothers. Finally he is told to flee the love of money, pursue righteousness and godliness, fight the good fight, take hold of eternal life, keep the commandment blameless, charge the rich, guard what was committed to him, and turn from false knowledge and false teachers.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

1 Timothy / Chapter 1

Timothy is addressed as Paul's true child in faith and is told to stay at Ephesus to command certain men not to teach a different doctrine. Paul commits this instruction to him according to earlier prophecies, so that he may wage the good warfare, holding faith and a good conscience.

Chapter 4

The addressee is told to instruct the brothers, refuse profane and old wives' fables, exercise himself toward godliness, and let no one despise his youth. He is to devote himself to reading, exhortation, and teaching until the writer comes, not neglect his gift given by prophecy with the laying on of the elders' hands, and keep watch over himself. By continuing in these things he will save himself and those who hear him.

Chapter 6

Timothy is addressed as a man of God and told to flee the love of money and follow righteousness, godliness, faith, love, perseverance, and gentleness. He is commanded to fight the good fight of faith, take hold of eternal life, keep the commandment blameless, charge the rich, and guard what was committed to him. He is also told to withdraw from false teachers and turn away from empty chatter and falsely called knowledge.

Timothy in 2 Timothy

Paul · the work’s page · read it where Timothy first appears

Abstract written by a model

Timothy is a beloved child of Paul, the addressee of a four-chapter letter (2 Timothy). He is remembered for sincere faith that first lived in his grandmother Lois and mother Eunice, and he has known the holy Scriptures from infancy. He holds God's gift, received through the laying on of Paul's hands, and is entrusted to guard a good thing through the Holy Spirit. Chapter 1 urges him to stir up his gift, not be ashamed, and endure hardship. Chapter 2 tells him to be strengthened in grace, commit teaching to faithful men who can teach others, serve as a good soldier, and handle the Word of Truth properly while pursuing righteousness, faith, love, and peace. Chapter 3 tells him to turn from corrupt men and remain in what he has learned. Chapter 4 solemnly charges him to preach the word, do an evangelist's work, and fulfill his ministry, then asks him to come to Paul soon, bringing Mark, the cloak, and the books, and to beware of Alexander.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

2 Timothy / Chapter 1

Timothy is addressed as Paul's beloved child, remembered for his tears and for sincere faith that lived first in his grandmother Lois and mother Eunice. He is urged to stir up God's gift, not be ashamed of the testimony or of Paul, endure hardship for the Good News, hold sound words, and guard the good thing committed to him through the Holy Spirit.

Chapter 2

The addressee is told to be strengthened in grace, to commit what he has heard to faithful men who can teach others, and to endure hardship as a good soldier. He is also told to remind others not to argue about words, to present himself approved as a workman who handles the Word of Truth properly, to shun empty chatter and youthful lusts, and to pursue righteousness, faith, love, and peace.

Chapter 3

The addressee is told to turn away from the corrupt men and is commended as one who followed the speaker's teaching, conduct, and endurance. He is urged to remain in what he has learned and been assured of, knowing from whom he learned it, and is reminded that he has known the holy Scriptures from infancy.

Chapter 4

Timothy is solemnly charged to preach the word urgently, reprove, rebuke and exhort with patience, stay sober, endure hardship, do the work of an evangelist and fulfill his ministry. He is asked to come to Paul soon, before winter, bringing Mark, the cloak from Carpus at Troas and the books, and to beware of Alexander the coppersmith.
