Menelaus

person · 1 work · 11 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: the breaker of the law, the wicked wretch

Abstract written by a model

Menelaus, brother of Simon, is a corrupt high priest who rises by outbidding Jason by three hundred talents of silver. He rules as a cruel tyrant: he withholds the promised money, steals temple vessels, has Onias killed after being reproved, and escapes accusation by bribing Ptolemy. He leaves his brother Lysimachus as deputy. When Jason assaults the city, he takes refuge in the citadel, then guides Antiochus into the temple, proving a traitor to the laws and his country. Left as a governor, he is worse than the others. Later, joining the king's expedition and hoping to be set over the government, he encourages Antiochus hypocritically. Lysias blames him for all the evils, and the king has him taken to Beroea and pushed into a tower of ashes, dying without a grave, justly for sinning against the holy altar.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4

Menelaus, Simon's brother, was sent by Jason with money to the king, and instead secured the high priesthood by outbidding Jason by three hundred talents of silver. He returned with the passion of a cruel tyrant, did not pay the promised money, stole temple vessels, and had Andronicus kill Onias after Onias reproved him. Accused after the death of Lysimachus, he bribed Ptolemy son of Dorymenes, was acquitted, and remained in office, growing in wickedness.

Chapter 5

Menelaus took refuge in the citadel when Jason's assault nearly took the city. He guided Antiochus into the temple, proving himself a traitor to the laws and his country. He was also left as one of the governors and is described as worse than all the rest, exalting himself against his fellow citizens.

Chapter 13

Menelaus joined the king's expedition and with great hypocrisy encouraged Antiochus, hoping to be set over the government. Lysias blamed him for all the evils, and the king had him taken to Beroea and pushed into a tower of ashes, so that he died without a grave, justly, since he had sinned against the altar whose fire and ashes were holy.
