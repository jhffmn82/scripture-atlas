Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Kings, Abijam reigns as king of Judah, wars with Jeroboam, receives a son for David's sake, dies, and is succeeded by Asa. In The Second Book of Chronicles, Abijah, son of Rehoboam, reigns over Judah, prevails against Jeroboam, captures several cities, fathers many children, and is buried in David's city.

Abijam in The First Book of Kings

author unknown · about 549 BC · the work’s page · read it where Abijam first appears

Abstract written by a model

Abijam was king of Judah who reigned three years in Jerusalem, son of Maacah. His heart was not perfect with the LORD, unlike David, as he walked in his father's sins. He warred with Jeroboam, yet for David's sake received a son. He died, was buried in David's city, and Asa succeeded him.

Chapter 15

Abijam began to reign over Judah in Jeroboam's eighteenth year and reigned three years in Jerusalem; his mother was Maacah the daughter of Abishalom. He walked in his father's sins and his heart was not perfect with the LORD, yet for David's sake the LORD gave him a lamp in Jerusalem, a son to follow him. He was at war with Jeroboam, slept with his fathers, was buried in David's city, and was succeeded by his son Asa.

Abijah in The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Abijah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Abijah, a son of Rehoboam, is appointed chief and prince among his brothers because Rehoboam intends him to be king. He later reigns over Judah from Jerusalem for three years. Warring with Jeroboam, he relies on the LORD, and Judah prevails; he captures Bethel, Jeshanah, and Ephron with their villages. He grows mighty, takes fourteen wives, and fathers twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters. His remaining acts are recorded in the prophet Iddo's commentary, and he is buried in David's city.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Abijah is a son of Rehoboam and Maacah. Rehoboam appoints him chief, the prince among his brothers, because he intends to make him king.

Chapter 13

Abijah began to reign over Judah in the eighteenth year of Jeroboam and reigned three years in Jerusalem; his mother was Micaiah daughter of Uriel of Gibeah. He addressed Jeroboam and Israel from Mount Zemaraim, then led Judah to victory, pursued Jeroboam, and took Bethel, Jeshanah, and Ephron with their villages. He grew mighty, took fourteen wives, and fathered twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters; his other acts are written in the commentary of the prophet Iddo.
