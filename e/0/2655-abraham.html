Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, Abraham journeys from Ur to Canaan, receives God's covenant and promises, fathers Isaac and other sons, obeys God in offering Isaac, and dies buried with Sarah. In 1 Chronicles, Abraham, also called Abram, descends from Shem through Terah and fathers Isaac, Ishmael, and later sons. In The Book of Nehemiah, Abraham, formerly Abram, is chosen by the LORD, brought from Ur, renamed, found faithful, and given a covenant concerning his offspring's land. In Psalms: is_a servant of the LORD Abraham is called the LORD's servant, and his offspring are urged to remember the LORD's works. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Abraham is a great father of nations who keeps God's law, bears the covenant, and proves faithful under testing. In The Gospel According to Matthew: father_of Isaac Abraham stands at the head of the genealogy, becoming the father of Isaac. In The Gospel According to Luke, Abraham holds Lazarus at his bosom, explains the gulf between the dead, and addresses the tormented rich man as father. In The Gospel According to John: Abraham is a deceased figure whom the Jews claim as their father.

Abraham in Genesis

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Abraham first appears

Abstract written by a model

Abraham, first called Abram, is a man described as a Hebrew, originating from Ur of the Chaldees, son of Terah, brother of Nahor and Haran, and uncle of Lot. He is married to Sarai, later called Sarah. The records follow him from his origins to his death at 175, buried with Sarah in the cave of Machpelah. What matters most is his relationship with the LORD: he obeys, worships, builds altars, receives promises of land, countless offspring and a covenant, and is credited with righteousness for believing. He is called a prophet, a servant of the LORD, and a prince of God, and his obedience is later named as the reason for promises to Isaac. The story begins with his journey from Ur toward Canaan, stopping at Haran. At seventy-five he leaves Haran with Sarai and Lot, building altars through Canaan. In famine he goes to Egypt and, fearing for his life, has Sarai say she is his sister; he gains wealth, is rebuked and sent away. Returning very rich, he separates from Lot, offering first choice of land, and settles by the oaks of Mamre in Hebron. With 318 trained men and his allies Mamre, Eshcol and Aner, he rescues Lot, gives Melchizedek a tenth, and refuses the king of Sodom's goods. Childless, with Eliezer as presumptive heir, he is promised an heir of his own body. At eighty-six Ishmael is born to Hagar. At ninety-nine God renames him Abraham, promises he will be father of nations, and he circumcises his household. He shows hospitality to three visitors, pleads for Sodom, and sees its smoke; God remembers him and rescues Lot. In Gerar he repeats the sister claim to Abimelech, and God heals Abimelech's household through his prayer. At one hundred Isaac is born; he sends Hagar and Ishmael away, makes a covenant with Abimelech over a well at Beersheba, and calls on the Everlasting God. He obeys the command to offer Isaac, is stopped, sacrifices a ram, and names the place The LORD Will Provide. After mourning Sarah he buys Ephron's field and cave as a burial place, then sends his servant to find Isaac a wife from his relatives. He takes Keturah, fathers six more sons, gives all he has to Isaac, and dies full of years.

Chapter 12

Abram obeys the LORD's command, leaving Haran at age seventy-five with Sarai, Lot, and his possessions, and travels through Canaan, building altars and calling on the LORD's name. During the famine he goes to Egypt, where out of fear he asks Sarai to say she is his sister, and he gains livestock and servants because of her. After Pharaoh learns the truth, Abram is rebuked and escorted away with his wife and all that he has.

Chapter 13

Abram returns from Egypt very rich in livestock, silver, and gold, and goes back to the altar he had made earlier between Bethel and Ai, where he calls on the LORD's name. He resolves the strife with Lot by offering him first choice of land and taking the opposite direction, then lives in Canaan. After the LORD promises him the land and countless offspring, he settles by the oaks of Mamre in Hebron and builds an altar there.

Chapter 14

Abram, called the Hebrew, lived by the oaks of Mamre and was allied with Mamre, Eshcol, and Aner. On hearing that his relative Lot was captured, he led 318 trained men born in his house, pursued the captors to Dan and Hobah, struck them by night, and recovered Lot, the goods, the women, and the people. He was blessed by Melchizedek, gave him a tenth of all, and swore by the LORD, God Most High, to take nothing from the king of Sodom, though he allowed his allies and the young men their portion.

Chapter 15

Abram is told not to be afraid, complains that he is childless with Eliezer of Damascus as his heir, and is promised an heir from his own body. He believes the LORD, which is credited to him as righteousness, then asks how he will know he will inherit the land. He prepares and divides the animals, drives away birds of prey, falls into a deep sleep with terror and darkness, and is told he will go to his fathers in peace and be buried at a good old age.

Chapter 16

Abram listens to Sarai and goes in to Hagar, who conceives. When Sarai complains, he tells her Hagar is in her hand to treat as she sees fit, and he names the son Ishmael when Hagar bears him at age eighty-six.

Chapter 17

Abraham, formerly Abram, is ninety-nine when God appears to him, renames him, and promises he will be father of a multitude of nations. He falls on his face, laughs and doubts that a child can be born to a hundred-year-old man, and asks that Ishmael might live before God. He obeys the command on the same day by circumcising himself, Ishmael, and all the males of his house.

Chapter 18

Abraham welcomes the three men with eager hospitality, hurrying to have bread, cakes, and a calf prepared and standing by them as they eat. He walks with them on their way, and the LORD reveals that he has chosen Abraham to become a great nation and to command his household to keep the way of the LORD. Abraham then stays before the LORD and pleads for Sodom.

Chapter 19

Abraham goes up early in the morning to the place where he had stood before the LORD and looks toward Sodom and Gomorrah. He sees smoke rising like the smoke of a furnace. God remembers Abraham and, for that reason, sends Lot out of the overthrow.

Chapter 20

Abraham settles as a foreigner in Gerar and tells people Sarah is his sister, fearing he will be killed for his wife. He explains to Abimelech that she is his father's daughter and that he had asked her to say this everywhere they went. He receives livestock, servants, silver, and Sarah back, and God, who calls him a prophet, heals Abimelech's household through his prayer.

Chapter 21

Abraham names his son Isaac, circumcises him at eight days, and is one hundred years old at his birth. Though grieved, he obeys God's word, sending Hagar and the child away with bread and water, and later complains to Abimelech about a seized well, makes a covenant with him, plants a tamarisk tree at Beersheba, and calls on the LORD, the Everlasting God.

Chapter 22

Abraham answers God's call with "Here I am," rises early, and carries out the command, traveling to the appointed place, building the altar, binding Isaac, and taking the knife to kill him. Stopped by the LORD's angel, he sacrifices a ram instead, names the place "The LORD Will Provide," and receives an oath of blessing for having obeyed. He then returns to his young men, lives at Beersheba, and is told of the children born to his brother Nahor.

Chapter 23

Abraham mourned Sarah, then described himself to the children of Heth as a stranger and foreigner and asked for a burying-place. He bowed to the people of the land, insisted on paying full price rather than accept a gift, weighed out four hundred shekels of silver to Ephron, and buried Sarah in the cave.

Chapter 24

Abraham, old and blessed by the LORD in all things, makes his servant swear to find Isaac a wife from his own relatives and not from the Canaanites, and forbids him to take Isaac back to the old land. He assures the servant that the LORD will send his angel ahead and that the servant will be free of the oath if the woman is unwilling.

Chapter 25

Abraham took Keturah as another wife and fathered Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah by her. He gave all he had to Isaac, sent the sons of his concubines eastward with gifts, and died at 175 years old, full of years.

Chapter 26

Abraham is named as the one whose obedience to the LORD's voice, requirements, commandments, statutes, and laws is the reason for the promises to Isaac. His servants had dug wells that the Philistines stopped up after his death, and Isaac restores them under their original names.
