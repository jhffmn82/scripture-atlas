justice and righteousness

topic · 3 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: justice, righteousness, the fruit of righteousness

Abstract written by a model

The Book of Job presents Bildad as asserting that God does not pervert justice or righteousness. The Book of Jeremiah presents Josiah as having practiced justice and righteousness and judged the cause of the poor and needy. The Book of Amos commands Israel to establish justice in the courts and let righteousness roll like a mighty stream.

Abstract written by a model

Bildad asks rhetorically whether God perverts justice or the Almighty perverts righteousness, implying that he does not. He presents them as the basis for his claims that sin brings destruction, that the blameless are not cast away, and that evildoers are not upheld.
