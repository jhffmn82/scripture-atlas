Abstract written by a model

In Exodus, Moses leads Israel out of Egypt, mediates with the LORD, receives and delivers the covenant, intercedes for the people, and directs the construction of the tabernacle. In Leviticus, Moses receives and relays the LORD’s laws, officiates Aaron’s ordination, directs offerings and priestly duties, and communicates judgments and commands to Israel. In Numbers, Moses serves as the LORD’s intermediary and Israel’s wilderness leader, relaying commands, interceding, confronting rebellions, commissioning Joshua, and overseeing laws and land arrangements. In Deuteronomy, Moses leads Israel, declares and teaches the law, recounts the wilderness journey, commissions Joshua, blesses the tribes, and dies after viewing the promised land. In The Book of Joshua, Moses is remembered as the deceased servant of the LORD whose commands, victories, and land distributions guide Joshua’s conquest and Israel’s inheritance. In Psalms, Moses is portrayed as God’s servant and chosen mediator who prayed for the people, performed wonders with Aaron, interceded against wrath, and faltered at Meribah. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, Moses is portrayed as a merciful and faithful man who was glorified by God, taught Israel the law, and consecrated Aaron. In The Gospel According to Matthew, Moses appears as the lawgiver who stated the levirate marriage law and spoke with transfigured Jesus on the mountain.

and 3 more works

In The Acts of the Apostles, Moses is described as an Egyptian-raised leader who fled to Midian and was later sent by God as ruler and deliverer of Israel. In 2 Corinthians, Moses is described as wearing a veil over his shining face so the Israelites would not see the fading of its glory. In The Letter to the Hebrews, Moses is presented as a faithful servant who by faith rejected royal privilege, chose God’s people, left Egypt, kept the Passover, and led the people out.

Abstract written by a model

Moses is a Hebrew born to Amram and Jochebed of the house of Levi. Hidden as an infant and set in a basket by the river, he is drawn out of the water by Pharaoh's daughter, who adopts him and names him. As an adult he kills an Egyptian striking a Hebrew and flees to Midian, where he helps Reuel's daughters, marries Zipporah, and has sons Gershom and Eliezer. He keeps Jethro's flock, his father-in-law's, until the LORD speaks from the burning bush at Horeb and sends him to Pharaoh and the elders of Israel. Moses objects that he is slow of speech and has uncircumcised lips, so the LORD appoints his brother Aaron as spokesman. Carrying God's rod, he returns toward Egypt; the LORD nearly kills him until Zipporah circumcises their son. At eighty he is made as God to Pharaoh and, with Aaron, delivers the LORD's demands. He prays to remove the frogs, flies, locusts, hail and thunder, performs the wonders, and is regarded as very great in Egypt. He instructs the elders in the Passover, takes Joseph's bones, and leads Israel out. He stretches his hand over the sea to divide it, and the people believe in the LORD and in him. In the wilderness he sings the victory song, sweetens the water at Marah, relays the manna rules, strikes the rock (naming Massah and Meribah), and holds up his hand so Israel prevails over Amalek. He builds an altar, and on Jethro's advice appoints rulers to share judging. At Sinai he is the mediator and sole approacher of the LORD. The frightened people ask him to speak for them. He sanctifies the people, writes the book of the covenant, seals it with blood, and stays forty days and nights on the mountain, where he receives instructions for the tabernacle, the priesthood, and the Sabbath. When the people make the calf, he intercedes, breaks the tablets, destroys the calf, and has the sons of Levi kill; he offers to be blotted out for the people. He speaks with the LORD face to face in the Tent of Meeting and asks to see his glory. He returns with new tablets and a shining face, which he veils. He directs Bezalel and Oholiab in the work, blesses the people for finishing it as commanded, and sets up the tabernacle exactly as told. The story ends with the LORD's glory filling it, so that Moses cannot enter the Tent of Meeting.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

31 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Moses is born to a Levite couple, hidden for three months, set in a basket by the river, and drawn out by Pharaoh's daughter, who adopts him and names him. Grown, he kills an Egyptian who was striking a Hebrew and hides the body, then flees to Midian when Pharaoh seeks to kill him. In Midian he helps Reuel's daughters at a well, marries Zipporah, and has a son named Gershom, saying he has lived as a foreigner in a foreign land.

Chapter 3

Moses keeps Jethro's flock, goes to Horeb, and turns aside to see the burning bush. He hides his face when God speaks, then asks 'Who am I?' and what name to tell the Israelites. He is sent to Pharaoh and to the elders of Israel to bring the people out of Egypt.

Chapter 4

Moses doubts that the people will believe him and objects that he is slow of speech, asking the LORD to send someone else. After the LORD's anger and the appointment of Aaron, he gets Jethro's blessing, takes his wife and sons and God's rod, and returns toward Egypt. The LORD meets him at a lodging place and wants to kill him, but relents after Zipporah circumcises their son; Moses then tells Aaron all the LORD's words and gathers the elders.

Chapter 5

Moses, with Aaron, delivers the LORD's message to Pharaoh asking that Israel be let go to hold a feast, and is refused. The officers of Israel accuse him of making them a stench in Pharaoh's eyes. Moses returns to the LORD and complains that the LORD has brought trouble on the people and has not rescued them at all.

Chapter 6

Moses relays the LORD's promises to the children of Israel, but they do not listen. When told to speak to Pharaoh, he objects twice that Israel has not listened to him and that he has uncircumcised lips. The genealogy identifies him as a son of Amram and Jochebed, and as one of the Moses and Aaron the LORD told to bring Israel out of Egypt.

Chapter 7

Moses is told he is made as God to Pharaoh, with Aaron as his prophet, and he obeys the LORD's commands. He is eighty years old when he speaks to Pharaoh and is sent to meet Pharaoh at the river's bank to demand that the people be let go.

Chapter 8

Moses delivers the LORD's demands to Pharaoh and lets Pharaoh set the time for the frogs to be removed. He prays and the frogs die and later the flies depart, and he rejects sacrificing in Egypt, insisting on a three days' journey into the wilderness. He warns Pharaoh not to deal deceitfully again.

Chapter 9

Moses carries the LORD's warnings to Pharaoh, sprinkles furnace ashes toward the sky to produce the boils, and stretches out his rod toward the heavens when the hail comes. When Pharaoh pleads, Moses leaves the city, spreads his hands to the LORD and the thunder and hail stop. He tells Pharaoh that he does not yet fear the LORD God.

Chapter 10

Moses goes in to Pharaoh with Aaron and warns of locusts, stretches out his rod and later his hand at the LORD's command, and prays to remove the locusts. He refuses Pharaoh's offers to leave the livestock behind, insisting that not a hoof be left. He agrees never to see Pharaoh's face again after being threatened with death.

Chapter 11

Moses receives the LORD's instruction to have the people ask their neighbors for silver and gold jewels, and is described as very great in Egypt in the sight of Pharaoh's servants and the people. He delivers the LORD's warning of the death of the firstborn to Pharaoh and leaves him in hot anger. He and Aaron performed all the wonders before Pharaoh.

Chapter 12

Moses, with Aaron, receives the LORD's instructions for the Passover and relays them to the elders of Israel, telling them to kill the Passover and apply the blood with hyssop. Pharaoh summons him and Aaron by night and tells them to leave Egypt with the Israelites.

Chapter 13

Moses receives the LORD's command to sanctify the firstborn and relays it to the people along with the instructions about unleavened bread, teaching their sons, and redeeming firstborn. He takes the bones of Joseph with him, fulfilling the oath Joseph had made the children of Israel swear.

Chapter 14

Moses is told by the LORD where Israel should encamp and answers the people's fearful complaints by telling them not to be afraid and to stand still and see the LORD's salvation. At the LORD's command he stretches out his hand over the sea twice, first to divide it and then to make the waters return. At the end the people believe in the LORD and in his servant Moses.

Chapter 15

Moses sings the victory song to the LORD with the children of Israel. He leads Israel from the Red Sea into the wilderness, cries to the LORD when the people murmur at Marah, throws the tree into the water to make it sweet, and delivers the statute, ordinance, and the LORD's promise of healing.

Chapter 16

Moses tells the people that their murmurings are against the LORD, not against himself and Aaron, and relays the LORD's instructions about gathering an omer per person and keeping nothing until morning. He becomes angry when some leave manna overnight, explains the Sabbath rule when the rulers report the double portion, and commands Aaron to lay up a pot of manna.

Chapter 17

Moses is quarreled with by the people over water, asks the LORD what to do, and strikes the rock with his rod before the elders as commanded. He names the place Massah and Meribah, and during Amalek's attack he stands on the hill with God's rod, with Israel prevailing whenever his hand is raised. He is helped by Aaron and Hur, is told to write the event in a book, builds an altar called "The LORD our Banner," and declares the LORD's sworn war with Amalek.

Chapter 18

Moses meets Jethro with a bow and a kiss, and tells him all the LORD did to Pharaoh and the Egyptians and for Israel on the way. He sits alone judging the people from morning to evening until Jethro rebukes him, then he listens, chooses able men as rulers over the people, and lets Jethro depart.

Chapter 19

Moses goes up to God, receives the LORD's words for Israel, and reports them to the elders and then the people. He carries the people's answer back to the LORD, sanctifies the people, instructs them to be ready by the third day and abstain from sexual relations, and leads them out to meet God. He speaks and is answered by God, climbs to the top of Mount Sinai when called, objects that the bounds are already set, and is sent down again to warn the people, returning later with Aaron.

Chapter 20

Moses is asked by the frightened people to speak with God on their behalf, and he reassures them that God has come to test them so that his fear will keep them from sin. He draws near to the thick darkness where God is and receives the LORD's instructions to relay to the children of Israel.

Chapter 24

Moses relays the LORD's words to the people, writes them down, builds an altar with twelve pillars, reads the book of the covenant aloud, and sprinkles the people with blood. He goes up the mountain with Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and the elders, then goes higher with Joshua, his servant, and enters the cloud. He remains on the mountain forty days and forty nights.

Chapter 25

Moses is told to speak to the children of Israel about taking an offering for the LORD. He is the one to whom the LORD will speak from above the mercy seat, and he is reminded to see that everything is made after the pattern shown to him on the mountain.

Chapter 29

Moses is not named in this unit, but the LORD addresses him as "you" throughout. He is told to carry out the consecration of Aaron and his sons: washing, clothing, and anointing them, performing the sacrifices, and applying blood and oil. He is also told to cleanse and anoint the altar and to offer the daily lambs.

Chapter 30

Moses receives the LORD's instructions in several speeches: to make the incense altar, collect the atonement money, make the basin, compound the anointing oil, and prepare the incense. He is told to anoint the Tent of Meeting, its furnishings, and Aaron and his sons, and to speak to the children of Israel about the oil.

Chapter 31

Moses receives the LORD's word naming Bezalel and Oholiab as craftsmen for the work. He is told to speak to the children of Israel about keeping the Sabbath, and at the end of the LORD's speech on Mount Sinai he is given the two stone tablets of the covenant.

Chapter 32

Moses is delayed on the mountain, intercedes for the people when the LORD threatens to consume them, and wins the LORD's reprieve. He descends, breaks the tablets in anger, destroys the calf, rebukes Aaron, calls for those on the LORD's side, and commands the sons of Levi to kill. He then returns to the LORD, offers to be blotted out of the book if the people are not forgiven, and is told to lead the people onward.

Chapter 33

Moses pitches the Tent of Meeting outside the camp and enters it to speak with the LORD, who speaks with him face to face as with a friend. He asks to know whom the LORD will send and to be shown his way, insists that the LORD's presence must go with them or they should not go up, and asks to see the LORD's glory. The LORD grants the first requests, and Moses is to be placed in a cleft of the rock and covered by the LORD's hand as he passes.

Chapter 34

Moses chisels two stone tablets, goes up Mount Sinai alone at the LORD's command, bows and worships, and intercedes for the stiff-necked people. After forty days and nights without food or water he writes the covenant words and comes down with a shining face, unaware of it, then veils his face except when speaking with the LORD.

Chapter 35

Moses assembles the congregation and delivers the LORD's commands about the Sabbath, the offering, and the items to be made. He later announces that the LORD has called Bezalel and filled him and Oholiab with skill for the work.

Chapter 36

Moses calls Bezalel, Oholiab, and the wise-hearted men to the work and gives them the offerings the Israelites brought. When the workers report there is more than enough, he commands a proclamation throughout the camp that no one make anything further for the offering, so the people are restrained from bringing.

Chapter 39

Moses is the one to whom the LORD gave the commands that the workers follow. The children of Israel bring the finished tabernacle and its furnishings to him, he sees that the work was done as commanded, and he blesses them.

Chapter 40

Moses receives the LORD's instructions and carries out each one exactly, raising the tabernacle, placing the ark and furnishings, offering sacrifices, and setting up the court. When the LORD's glory fills the tabernacle, he is unable to enter the Tent of Meeting.

Moses in Leviticus

author unknown · 539 to 331 BC · the work’s page · read it where Moses first appears

Abstract written by a model

Moses is the human mediator through whom the LORD's laws and commands reach Aaron, his sons, and the children of Israel in Leviticus. Across the records he is called from the Tent of Meeting, receives the LORD's words repeatedly, and relays them: offering rules, laws on fat, blood and peace offerings, dietary laws, discharge laws, conduct and forbidden relations, holiness for priests, and the appointed feasts. The records close by noting that the commandments were given to him on Mount Sinai. Most important is his role as a faithful, commanded agent who acts as the LORD commanded him. He is also a relative of Aaron, whom he addresses as leader of the priests and calls his brother. The account unfolds in stages. In the early chapters (1, 6, 7) he is mainly a recipient and relayer of offering instructions. In Chapter 8 he becomes an active officiant at the ordination: he assembles the congregation, washes and clothes Aaron and his sons, anoints the tabernacle, altar and Aaron, performs the offerings, takes the breast of the ram of consecration as his portion, and instructs them to remain at the door for seven days. In Chapter 9, on the eighth day, he commands the offerings, tells the people the LORD's glory will appear, directs Aaron to make atonement, goes with Aaron into the Tent of Meeting, and blesses the people. In Chapter 10 he interprets the deaths as fulfillment of the LORD's word, has Mishael and Elzaphan carry the bodies out of the camp, instructs Aaron, Eleazar and Ithamar not to mourn openly or leave the Tent, and directs the eating of the offering portions. He feels anger at Eleazar and Ithamar over the burned sin offering goat but is pleased by Aaron's reply. Later chapters (11, 15-19, 21-23, 27) return him to the intermediary role, with Chapter 16 telling Aaron not to enter the Most Holy Place at just any time, and Chapter 23 closing with Moses declaring the feasts. In Chapter 24 the people bring a blasphemer to him; he waits for the LORD's will, then speaks the command, and the people carry out the stoning.

Moses in Numbers

author unknown · 1439 to 1399 BC · the work’s page · read it where Moses first appears

Abstract written by a model

Moses is a person: the servant of the LORD and leader of the children of Israel through the wilderness, from Sinai to the plains of Moab. He is brother of Aaron and Miriam, son of Amram and Jochebed, married to a Cushite woman, and has Joshua as his servant. The records describe him as humble beyond all people on earth and faithful in the LORD's house, one with whom the LORD speaks mouth to mouth. His defining role is intermediary: he repeatedly receives the LORD's commands and relays them to Israel, Aaron, tribal heads, and others, and he carries them out. The account opens with the census at Sinai, the arrangement of the camp, the counting and assignment of the Levites, redemption of the firstborn, and rules on uncleanness, jealousy, Nazirite vows, and Passover. He sets up and anoints the tabernacle, gives wagons and oxen to the Levites, and hears the LORD from above the mercy seat. On the journey he invites Hobab to join them, prays when the ark moves and rests, and, burdened by the people, asks to be killed. He gathers seventy elders and wishes all the LORD's people were prophets. He intercedes for Miriam. He sends the twelve spies, and intercedes when the people murmur. He faces challenges, notably Korah's rebellion, and answers them with the censer test, and Aaron's budding rod confirms Aaron's priesthood. A turning point comes at the waters of Meribah in the wilderness of Zin, where he strikes the rock instead of speaking to it and is told he will not bring the assembly into the promised land. He still deals with Edom, Aaron's death, and the bronze serpent. Late in the account he is told to hold the census with Eleazar, to take the daughters' inheritance case to the LORD, and to go up Mount Abarim to see the land before being gathered to his people. He asks for a successor and commissions Joshua. He relays laws on feasts and vows, commissions the war on Midian, rebukes Reuben and Gad, and gives land east of the Jordan to Gad, Reuben, half of Manasseh, and Gilead to Machir. He records Israel's journeys, relays the borders and land division by lot, and rules that Zelophehad's daughters must marry within their father's tribe.

Moses in Deuteronomy

author unknown · 624 BC · the work’s page · read it where Moses first appears

Abstract written by a model

Moses is a man of God and servant of the LORD, brother of Aaron, who serves as Israel's leader, lawgiver, and mediator at the end of the wilderness years. The records open in the fortieth year beyond the Jordan, where he begins declaring the law, recounts events from Horeb to Kadesh, and recalls appointing heads of tribes as judges because he could not bear the people alone. He recounts defeating Sihon and Og and distributing the eastern land to Reubenites, Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh. A central tension runs throughout: the LORD was angry with him for the people's sakes and, by decree and oath, barred him from crossing the Jordan, and he begged in vain to enter. He is told to view the land from Pisgah and commission Joshua. Later it is stated that he trespassed at Meribah of Kadesh by not upholding the LORD's holiness. Moses teaches the statutes and ordinances as commanded, warns against adding to or subtracting from them, and sets apart three cities of refuge. He recalls standing between the LORD and the people at Horeb, receiving two stone tablets, fasting forty days and nights without bread or water, breaking the tablets over the calf, and interceding for Israel and Aaron. He then cuts second tablets and makes an ark of acacia wood at the LORD's command. With the elders he commands the setting up of inscribed stones and an altar on Ebal, and assigns tribes to Gerizim and Ebal for blessing and curse. At age 120 he tells Israel he cannot cross, encourages Joshua, writes the law, orders its reading every seven years, and is told he will die and Israel will rebel. He writes and teaches a song, recites it with Joshua to all Israel, and charges them to teach the law to their children. He blesses each tribe, climbs Nebo to Pisgah, sees the whole land, and dies in Moab with undimmed eyes and undiminished strength. He is remembered as an unequaled prophet, known by the LORD face to face, who performed signs and wonders in Egypt.

Moses, aged 120, tells Israel he cannot go over the Jordan, encourages the people and Joshua, writes the law, and commands its public reading every seven years. Chapter 34

Moses climbs Mount Nebo to the top of Pisgah, is shown the whole land by the LORD, and is told he will not go over into it. He dies in the land of Moab at age 120, with his eye not dim and his strength not gone, and is remembered as the unmatched prophet whom the LORD knew face to face, in signs, wonders, and mighty deeds done before Pharaoh and all Israel.

Moses is presented in the Book of Joshua as the deceased servant of the LORD and man of God, whose authority and commands continue to shape Israel's conquest and land division. Chapter 1 opens with his death: the LORD promises to be with Joshua as with Moses, his law and command about land beyond the Jordan are invoked, and the eastern tribes pledge to heed Joshua as they heeded Moses. Chapter 12 recalls Moses striking Sihon and Og with the Israelites and giving their east-of-Jordan land to Reuben, Gad and the half-tribe of Manasseh. Chapter 13 adds that he gave these Transjordan inheritances in the plains of Moab, drove out Og's remnant of the Rephaim, struck Sihon with the chiefs of Midian, and gave Levi no inheritance. Chapter 14 credits him as the channel for the lot-based division, recalls the eastern inheritances, and notes he sent Caleb to spy out the land and swore the land Caleb walked would be his and his children's forever.

Moses in The Letter to the Hebrews

author unknown · about AD 64 to 69 · the work’s page · read it where Moses first appears

Abstract written by a model

Moses is a figure presented as faithful in all God's house as a servant, though shown to have less glory than Jesus. Hidden as a child by his parents, who did not fear the king's command, he grew up and refused to be called Pharaoh's daughter's son. He chose ill treatment with God's people, counted the reproach of Christ greater than Egypt's treasures, left Egypt by faith, kept the Passover, and led the people out, many of whom rebelled.

Chapter 3

Moses was faithful in all God's house as a servant, as a testimony of things to be spoken afterward. He is shown to have less glory than Jesus, and he led the people who came out of Egypt, many of whom rebelled.

Chapter 11

Moses was hidden for three months by his parents, who saw he was a beautiful child and did not fear the king's commandment. When grown, he refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter, chose ill treatment with God's people over the pleasures of sin, and counted the reproach of Christ greater riches than Egypt's treasures. By faith he left Egypt without fearing the king's wrath and kept the Passover and the sprinkling of the blood.
