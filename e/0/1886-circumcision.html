Abstract written by a model

In Genesis, circumcision is commanded by God as the token of the covenant, and Abraham, Ishmael, and all the men of the household are circumcised. In Genesis, circumcision is made by Jacob’s sons a condition for intermarriage, and the city’s males undergo it before being killed while sore. In The Book of Joshua, circumcision is restored among Israel’s sons born in the wilderness after the LORD commands Joshua to circumcise them with flint knives. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, circumcision’s value depends on obeying the law, with true circumcision being of the heart in spirit rather than outward letter. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, circumcision is presented as a sign and seal of the righteousness of faith Abraham had while uncircumcised. In Paul’s Letter to the Galatians, circumcision is treated as worthless in Christ Jesus and as making Christ profit nothing while obligating a man to the whole law.

Abstract written by a model

God commands that every male be circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin as a token of the covenant, at eight days old, including those born in the house or bought from foreigners. An uncircumcised male is to be cut off from his people for breaking the covenant. Abraham, aged ninety-nine, and Ishmael, aged thirteen, are circumcised on the same day along with all the men of the house.

circumcision in The Book of Joshua

author unknown · about 600 BC · the work’s page · read it where circumcision first appears

Abstract written by a model

Circumcision was neglected for children born in the wilderness on the journey. In Chapter 5 the LORD commands Joshua to circumcise Israel's sons again with flint knives; he does so, the people rest in camp until healed, and the reproach of Egypt is rolled away.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 5

The LORD commands that the sons of Israel be circumcised a second time with flint knives, and Joshua carries it out. It was needed because those born in the wilderness along the way had not been circumcised, and the people stayed in camp until healed. It is linked to the LORD's rolling away the reproach of Egypt.

circumcision in Paul’s Letter to the Romans

author unknown · about AD 57 · the work’s page · read it where circumcision first appears

Abstract written by a model

Circumcision is a sign whose value depends on obeying the law: it profits doers but becomes uncircumcision for transgressors. The uncircumcised who keep ordinances are counted as circumcised, and true circumcision is of the heart, in spirit, not outward letter.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2

Circumcision profits if one is a doer of the law, but for a transgressor it becomes uncircumcision. An uncircumcised person who keeps the law's ordinances will have his uncircumcision accounted as circumcision. True circumcision is declared to be of the heart, in the spirit and not the letter, rather than outward in the flesh.

circumcision in Paul’s Letter to the Galatians

author unknown · about AD 48 · the work’s page · read it where circumcision first appears

Abstract written by a model

Circumcision is treated as worthless in Christ Jesus. In Chapter 5, receiving it makes Christ profit nothing and obligates a man to the whole law, and Paul's refusal to preach it explains the cross's offense. In Chapter 6, others press it for self-serving reasons, though even the circumcised do not keep the law.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5

Circumcision is presented as something that, if received, makes Christ profit nothing and makes a man a debtor to do the whole law. In Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision amounts to anything, and Paul says his not preaching it explains why the stumbling block of the cross has not been removed in his case.

Chapter 6

Circumcision is presented as something being pressed on the recipients by others for self-serving reasons. The text declares that in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision is anything, and that even those who receive it do not keep the law.
