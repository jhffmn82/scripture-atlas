Abstract written by a model

The disciples are the believers gathered as an assembly in Jerusalem. At Pentecost they are united, filled with the Holy Spirit, and speak in other languages; mockers call them drunk, yet about three thousand are baptized and join them. After Stephen's burial, great persecution under Saul scatters them across Judea and Samaria, and they preach wherever they go. Later Herod oppresses them, killing James and arresting Peter, while the assembly prays constantly and is amazed when Peter arrives freed.

Chapter 2

The believers are together with one accord in one place when tongues like fire rest on each of them and they are all filled with the Holy Spirit and speak in other languages. Some onlookers mock them as full of new wine, but they are later joined by about three thousand who are baptized.

Chapter 8

A great persecution arose against the assembly in Jerusalem on the day of Stephen's burial. Saul entered houses and imprisoned men and women, and the believers were scattered throughout Judea and Samaria, which led them to preach the word wherever they went.

Chapter 12

The assembly is oppressed by Herod, who kills James and arrests Peter. While Peter is kept in prison, the assembly makes constant prayer to God for him. Many are gathered praying at Mary's house when Peter arrives, and they doubt Rhoda's report, saying it must be his angel, until they open the gate and are amazed to see him.
