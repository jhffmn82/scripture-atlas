Abstract written by a model

In Psalms, God's judgment of his people summons heaven and earth as witnesses, indicts Israel's sacrifices and the wicked's hypocrisy, and promises salvation to those who offer thanksgiving. In Psalms, vengeance is seen and celebrated by the righteous as proof of their reward and that God judges the earth. In Psalms, God's judgment on enemies is pronounced from heaven to save the afflicted, break weapons, silence the earth, and restrain survivors of his wrath. In Ecclesiastes or, The Preacher, divine judgment covers every work and hidden thing, whether good or evil. In The Wisdom of Solomon, divine judgment justly punishes the idol maker and deed, and the sinners' penalty overtakes the unrighteous who swore falsely. In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, divine judgment renders to everyone according to deeds and plans, bringing vengeance on the unrighteous and mercy to God's people. In Paul’s Letter to the Romans, God's judgment is truthful and righteous, bringing wrath on evildoers while rewarding well-doing and judging secrets through Jesus Christ. In Revelation, God's judgment arrives as the hour of judgment, Babylon's fall, the torment of the beast's worshippers, and the harvest and treading of God's wrath.

divine judgment in The Wisdom of Solomon

author unknown · about 99 BC · the work’s page · read it where divine judgment first appears

Abstract written by a model

Divine judgment is just: it punishes the idol maker together with the deed, announced as a visitation among the nation's idols, and ends by declaring that the sinners' just penalty, not the power of what they swore by, overtakes the unrighteous who swore falsely.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14

Judgment is announced as punishment of the deed together with the one who committed it, and as a visitation among the idols of the nation. The chapter closes by saying that the just penalty for sinners, not the power of the things sworn by, always overtakes the transgression of the unrighteous, who swore falsely and thought evil of God.
