Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Matthew, Pontius Pilate questions Jesus, releases Barabbas, hands Jesus over to be crucified, grants his body to Joseph, and orders the tomb guarded. In The Good News According to Mark, Pilate questions Jesus, frees Barabbas, hands Jesus over to be crucified, confirms his death, and grants his body to Joseph. In The Gospel According to Luke, Pilate questions Jesus, sends him to Herod, yields to the crowd, delivers Jesus, and grants Joseph the body. In The Gospel According to John, Pilate judges Jesus, questions him, delivers him to be crucified, writes the cross title, and permits Joseph to take the body.

Pontius Pilate in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Pontius Pilate first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pontius Pilate is a governor with a wife and a custom of releasing one prisoner at the feast. He questions Jesus, heeds his wife's warning and the crowd, washes his hands, releases Barabbas, hands Jesus over to be crucified, then grants his body to Joseph and orders the tomb guarded.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 27

Pilate, the governor, questions Jesus and marvels at his silence, knowing the leaders delivered him up out of envy. After his wife's warning and the crowd's insistence, he washes his hands, declares himself innocent of the blood of this righteous person, releases Barabbas, and has Jesus flogged and handed over to be crucified. He later gives Jesus' body to Joseph and tells the priests and Pharisees to secure the tomb with a guard.

Abstract written by a model

Pilate is a governor who questions Jesus, marvels at his silence, and perceives the chief priests' envy. Wishing to please the multitude and following the feast custom of releasing a prisoner, he frees Barabbas and hands Jesus over after flogging to be crucified. Pilate in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Pilate first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pilate, governor of Judea, questions Jesus and three times finds no basis for a charge, sends him to Herod, becomes Herod's friend, then yields to the crowd, releases Barabbas, delivers Jesus, and grants Joseph the body.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 23

Pilate questions Jesus, three times declares he finds no basis for a charge or capital crime, and sends him to Herod. Despite offering to chastise and release him, he yields to the loud voices, releases Barabbas, and delivers Jesus to the crowd's will. He also becomes friends with Herod that day and grants Joseph's request for the body.

Pilate in The Gospel According to John

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Pilate first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pilate is a judge who claims power to release or crucify and says he is not a Jew. When the Jews will not enter the Praetorium, he tells them to judge Jesus by their own law, then questions Jesus about being king and repeatedly finds no basis for a charge. He offers release under the Passover custom, but the crowd chooses Barabbas. He flogs Jesus, grows more afraid at the Son of God claim, yet, pressed about Caesar's friendship, delivers Jesus to be crucified. He writes the cross title, refuses to change it, and permits Joseph to take the body.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

Pilate goes out to the Jews who will not enter the Praetorium, tells them to judge Jesus by their own law, and learns they cannot put anyone to death. He questions Jesus about being king, declares he finds no basis for a charge, and offers to release Jesus under the Passover custom, but the crowd chooses Barabbas.

Chapter 19

Pilate flogs Jesus, brings him out, and repeatedly states he finds no basis for a charge. He becomes more afraid on hearing that Jesus claimed to be the Son of God, questions Jesus, and seeks to release him. Pressed by the charge that releasing him makes him no friend of Caesar, he sits on the judgment seat and delivers Jesus to be crucified. He writes the title for the cross and refuses to change it, saying "What I have written, I have written." He gives Joseph permission to take the body.
