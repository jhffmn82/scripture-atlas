Abstract written by a model

The kingdom is a realm of scholars, traders, travellers, husbandmen, and people won by the ruler’s policies. Its strength rests not on sharp arms but on many assisting the ruler, making it obedient to the prince. A large kingdom may lead princes through force, while virtue may make a prince sovereign. In peace it should follow clear principles and legal sanctions; commiserating government makes rule easy, and a ruler without enemies is called the minister of Heaven.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

A large kingdom is required to become the leader of the princes through force. It is not required for a prince to become the sovereign of the kingdom through virtue.

Chapter 4

A kingdom should be governed according to clearly understood principles and legal sanctions during periods of leisure and freedom from external troubles. If governed rightly, even great kingdoms will be constrained to stand in awe.

Chapter 5

The kingdom is the realm whose scholars, traders, travellers, husbandmen, and people may be won by the ruler's policies. A ruler with no enemy in the kingdom is called the minister of Heaven.

Chapter 6

The kingdom can be ruled as easily as making anything go round in the palm when commiserating government is practiced with a commiserating mind.

Chapter 1

A kingdom is overawed not by the sharpness and strength of arms. When many assist the ruler, the whole kingdom becomes obedient to the prince.
