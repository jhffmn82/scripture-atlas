Abstract written by a model

In The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus, almsgiving and charity is presented as a commanded duty toward the poor, mourners, sick, and dead. In The Gospel According to Matthew, charitable giving is taught as merciful deeds to be done secretly rather than before men, with reward from the Father.

almsgiving and charity in The Wisdom of Jesus the Son of Sirach, or Ecclesiasticus

author unknown · about 179 BC · the work’s page · read it where almsgiving and charity first appears

Abstract written by a model

Almsgiving and charity is a commanded duty: alms must not be neglected, and stretching a hand to the poor completes one's blessing. The teaching widens to not avoiding weepers, mourning with mourners, visiting the sick to gain love, and giving gifts with grace, even toward the dead.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

The reader is told not to neglect giving alms and to stretch out a hand to the poor so that his blessing may be complete. Charity extends to not avoiding those who weep, mourning with mourners, and visiting the sick, by which love is gained. A gift is said to have grace in the sight of every living person, and grace is not to be withheld from the dead.
