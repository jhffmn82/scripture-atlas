Abstract written by a model

In The Gospel According to Matthew, John son of Zebedee leaves his father and nets to follow Jesus when called. In The Gospel According to Matthew, John the Baptizer is a prophetic forerunner who preaches repentance, baptizes Jesus, rebukes Herod, and is beheaded in prison. In The Good News According to Mark, John refers both to the wilderness baptizer who prepares the Lord's way and to John son of Zebedee, one of Jesus' disciples. In The Good News According to Mark, John the Baptizer rebukes Herod, is imprisoned and beheaded, and has his corpse buried by his disciples. In The Gospel According to Luke, John is a prophet who preaches repentance, baptizes, rebukes Herod, teaches his disciples, and announces Jesus. In The Gospel According to John, John witnesses to Jesus as the Lamb of God, baptizes in water, and calls himself the bridegroom's friend. In The Acts of the Apostles, John is an apostle who accompanies Peter to the temple, is arrested with him, and appears before the council. In Surah 3: The Family of Imraan (Aal-i-Imraan), John is announced to Zachariah as a chaste prophet who confirms a word from Allah.

and 1 more work

In Surah 19: Mary (Maryam), John is given wisdom, compassion, purity, and devotion, and is dutiful toward his parents.

John the Baptizer in The Gospel According to Matthew

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where John the Baptizer first appears

Abstract written by a model

John the Baptizer is a prophet and forerunner of Jesus, foretold by Isaiah as the voice crying in the wilderness. He preaches repentance in the wilderness of Judea, wears camel's hair with a leather belt, eats locusts and wild honey, and baptizes in water, rebuking the Pharisees and Sadducees. He announces a mightier one coming and baptizes Jesus after first objecting. Later, imprisoned by Herod for telling him it was unlawful to have Herodias, his brother's wife, he hears of Christ's works and sends disciples to ask whether Jesus is the one. Jesus affirms him as more than a prophet, the messenger preparing the way, greater than anyone born of women, and the Elijah who is to come, though some dismissed him as having a demon for neither eating nor drinking. The multitude regards him as a prophet. He is beheaded in prison; his disciples bury him and tell Jesus. His baptism later traps the leaders, who did not believe him though tax collectors and prostitutes did.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3

John comes preaching in the wilderness of Judea, calling for repentance, and is identified as the one Isaiah spoke of. He wears camel's hair clothing with a leather belt, eats locusts and wild honey, baptizes people in the Jordan, and rebukes the Pharisees and Sadducees. He announces a mightier one coming after him, then baptizes Jesus after first objecting.

Chapter 11

John, in prison, hears of Christ's works and sends two disciples to ask whether Jesus is the one who comes. Jesus calls him a prophet and more, the messenger foretold who prepares the way, and says none born of women is greater, though the least in the Kingdom of Heaven is greater. Jesus also says John is Elijah who is to come, and that people dismissed him as having a demon because he came neither eating nor drinking.

Chapter 14

John had been arrested, bound, and imprisoned by Herod after telling him it was not lawful to have Herodias, his brother Philip's wife. The multitude counted him as a prophet. He is beheaded in prison, and his disciples bury his body and tell Jesus.

Chapter 21

John's baptism is the subject of Jesus' counter-question, which traps the leaders since all hold John as a prophet. Jesus says John came in the way of righteousness; the tax collectors and prostitutes believed him, but the leaders did not, even after seeing this.

John in The Good News According to Mark

author unknown · about AD 70 · the work’s page · read it where John first appears

Abstract written by a model

The records bring together two figures named John, which the text itself distinguishes. The first is a wilderness baptizer who wears camel's hair and a leather belt, eats locusts and wild honey, and preaches repentance in the Jordan. He is a messenger sent to prepare the Lord's way, announces a mightier one who will baptize in the Holy Spirit, baptizes Jesus, is taken into custody, and is regarded by all the people as a prophet. The second is John, son of Zebedee and brother of James, nicknamed Boanerges. Jesus calls him from mending nets, and he asks with James for seats of glory.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Good News According to Mark / Chapter 1

John is clothed in camel's hair with a leather belt, eats locusts and wild honey, and baptizes in the Jordan while preaching repentance. He announces that one mightier than he is coming who will baptize in the Holy Spirit, baptizes Jesus, and is later taken into custody. A separate John, brother of James and son of Zebedee, is called by Jesus while mending nets and follows him.

Chapter 10

John, with James, sons of Zebedee, asks Jesus for the seats at his right and left in his glory, and says they are able to drink his cup. Jesus says the seats are not his to give, and the other ten become indignant at the two.

Chapter 11

John's baptism becomes the subject of Jesus' counter-question to the leaders, asking whether it was from heaven or from men. The leaders will not answer because they fear either response, noting that all the people held John to really be a prophet.

John in The Gospel According to Luke

author unknown · AD 80 to 90 · the work’s page · read it where John first appears

Abstract written by a model

John is a prophet of the Most High, son of Zacharias, foretold by Gabriel as great before the Lord, abstaining from wine and strong drink, filled with the Holy Spirit from the womb, and going before God in the spirit and power of Elijah. He leaps in Elizabeth's womb, is born and named on the eighth day, and lives in the desert until appearing publicly to Israel. In the wilderness around the Jordan he preaches a baptism of repentance for remission of sins, baptizes with water, rebukes crowds, tax collectors, and soldiers, and announces one mightier who will baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire. After reproving Herod, he is imprisoned. He has disciples, whom he teaches to pray, and sends two to ask Jesus whether he is the coming one. Jesus calls him a prophet and more, the greatest born of women, though some said he had a demon for his ascetic habits. All the people regarded him as a prophet.

John in The Gospel According to John

author unknown · AD 90 to 100 · the work’s page · read it where John first appears

Abstract written by a model

John is a man sent from God as a witness to testify about the light, though he is not the light himself. He denies being the Christ, Elijah, or the prophet, and calls himself the voice crying in the wilderness. He baptizes in water, first at Bethany beyond the Jordan and later at Enon near Salim. He testifies that Jesus is the Lamb of God and Son of God, and two of his disciples follow Jesus. When his disciples complain that everyone is going to Jesus, John calls himself the bridegroom's friend and declares, 'He must increase, but I must decrease.'

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

The Gospel According to John / Chapter 1

John is a man sent from God as a witness to testify about the light, though he himself was not the light. He denies being the Christ, Elijah, or the prophet, calls himself the voice crying in the wilderness, and baptizes in water at Bethany beyond the Jordan. He testifies that Jesus is the Lamb of God and the Son of God after seeing the Spirit descend on him, and two of his disciples follow Jesus as a result.

Chapter 3

John is baptizing in Enon near Salim, where there is much water, and has not yet been thrown into prison. When his disciples complain that Jesus is baptizing and everyone is going to him, John answers that a man can receive only what is given from heaven. He says he is not the Christ but was sent before him, likens himself to the bridegroom's friend who rejoices, and declares 'He must increase, but I must decrease.'
