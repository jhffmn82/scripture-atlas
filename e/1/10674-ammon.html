Ammon

person · 1 work · 13 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: Ammon and his brethren, he, him, himself, one Ammon, the man

Abstract written by a model

Ammon is a strong and mighty man from the land of Zarahemla who leads sixteen men to seek their brethren in the land of Nephi. With Amaleki, Helem, and Hem, he is captured, imprisoned for two days, then released and welcomed by king Limhi after identifying himself. He recounts Zarahemla’s history and explains king Benjamin’s teachings to Limhi’s people. He explains that their king can translate ancient records through a God-given gift, though he himself cannot translate languages. Ammon rejoices that the engravings can be interpreted, mourns Abinadi and the losses among their people, and refuses to baptize because he considers himself unworthy. Finally, he consults on escape from bondage and leads Limhi’s people and his brethren toward Zarahemla.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 7

Ammon led sixteen men from the land of Zarahemla to inquire about their brethren. He and Amaleki, Helem, and Hem were captured, imprisoned, and brought before Limhi; after identifying himself, he was released and welcomed.

Chapter 8

Ammon rehearses the history of Limhi’s brethren and explains king Benjamin’s words to Limhi’s people. He says that the king of the people in the land of Zarahemla can translate ancient records because he is a seer.

Chapter 21

Ammon and his brethren come from Zarahemla and are initially mistaken for the priests of Noah, so they are bound and imprisoned. Ammon rejoices at learning that king Mosiah can interpret the engravings, mourns the deaths and departures of their people, and declines to baptize Limhi’s people because he considers himself an unworthy servant.

Chapter 22

Ammon consults with king Limhi and the people of Limhi about how they should deliver themselves out of bondage. He leads the people of Limhi and his brethren toward the land of Zarahemla.
