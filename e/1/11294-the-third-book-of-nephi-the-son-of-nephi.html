Abstract written by a model

This document summarizes the first thirty chapters of Third Nephi. Nephi, son of Helaman, leaves Zarahemla and entrusts the records to another Nephi. Signs of the Lord’s birth, a night without darkness and a new star, preserve believers and lead many to repentance, though the Gadianton robbers grow powerful. As people forget the signs and become wicked, the Nephites and converted Lamanites unite under Lachoneus and Gidgiddoni, gather at Zarahemla, repent, fortify themselves, and defeat the robbers led by Giddianhi and Zemnarihah. Peace follows, but pride, inequality, persecution, and secret combinations destroy the government; Nephi nevertheless preaches, performs miracles, and converts some people. In the thirty-fourth year, catastrophic storms, earthquakes, fire, burial, sinking cities, and three days of darkness destroy many wicked people. Jesus Christ speaks to the survivors, calls them to repent, declares that he fulfilled the law of Moses, and teaches sacrifice through a broken heart and contrite spirit. He then appears at the temple in Bountiful, shows the multitude his wounds, gives authority to baptize, and teaches repentance, faith, baptism, humility, mercy, reconciliation, love, prayer, fasting, forgiveness, trust in God, righteous judgment, and obedience. Jesus ministers further by healing the afflicted, blessing children, instituting bread and wine in remembrance of his body and blood, granting the Holy Ghost, praying for unity, and teaching scripture, covenant, gathering, Zion, New Jerusalem, and warnings to the Gentiles. He commands the people to search Isaiah, records Samuel the Lamanite’s prophecy, and gives words concerning the Lord’s refining judgment, tithes, the day of the Lord, and Elijah. Mormon explains that only part of Christ’s teachings is recorded. The disciples establish the church in Christ’s name, baptize and teach, and three are permitted to remain and minister without death. The book closes by warning Gentiles to repent, receive baptism and the Holy Ghost, accept revelation and miracles, and be numbered with the house of Israel.

The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman / Chapter 1

Nephi (son of Helaman) departs from Zarahemla after entrusting the records to Nephi (son of Nephi), who keeps them. The prophecies of the prophets are fulfilled when the sign of the Lord's birth appears, including a night without darkness and a new star, and the people learn that the Lord will come into the world. Believers are preserved from a planned destruction, while many unbelievers are astonished, fear because of their iniquity and unbelief, and recognize that the Son of God must shortly appear. The people are baptized unto repentance and have peace, but the Gadianton robbers grow stronger as dissenters and some Lamanites join them.

Chapter 2

The people increasingly forget the signs and wonders, harden their hearts, and fall into wickedness under Satan’s influence. The Nephites and converted Lamanites unite against the numerous Gadianton robbers, whose violence threatens the land with destruction. The converted Lamanites are numbered among the Nephites, their curse is taken from them, and they are called Nephites. The war continues through the fifteenth year, with the outcome worsened by the wickedness, contentions, and dissensions of the people of Nephi.

Chapter 3

Giddianhi, the governor of the secret society of Gadianton, sends Lachoneus a letter demanding that the Nephites surrender their lands, possessions, cities, and government or face destruction. Lachoneus rejects the threat, calls the people to repentance and prayer, and gathers them with their families, animals, and possessions into the land of Zarahemla. The Nephites build strong fortifications and prepare armies under Gidgiddoni, who advises them to wait for the robbers to attack rather than invade the robbers’ lands. The people repent, pray for deliverance, and make weapons and armor in preparation for battle.

Chapter 4

The robbers, led first by Giddianhi and later by Zemnarihah, sought to take the deserted Nephite lands and force the Nephites to yield. Giddianhi’s armies attacked in the nineteenth year but were defeated and pursued by the Nephites, and Giddianhi was slain. In the twenty and first year, Zemnarihah led a siege, but the Nephites cut off the robbers’ retreat, captured many, and slew the remainder. The Nephites rejoiced and credited their deliverance to the Lord God, repentance, and humility.

Chapter 5

The Nephites come to believe the prophets, recognize that Christ had come, forsake their sins, and serve God. They capture the robbers, preach the word of God to them, free those who repent and covenant not to murder, and punish those who refuse. Mormon explains that he is making a true record from earlier accounts and from what he has seen, and he testifies of Jesus Christ and the promises concerning the house of Jacob. The text teaches that the seed of Joseph and the seed of Jacob will be gathered from the earth and restored to knowledge of their covenant in the Lord’s due time.

Chapter 6

The Nephites returned to their lands with their families, possessions, provisions, and precious things, while lands were granted to repentant robbers who remained Lamanites, establishing peace and prosperity. Gidgiddoni, judge Lachoneus, and other leaders oversaw the peace, construction, roads, and laws, but pride, riches, learning differences, and persecution later caused inequality and the church to break up. Satan led the people into iniquity, and men sent from heaven preached about the sins of the people, the redemption the Lord would make, the resurrection of Christ, and Christ’s death and sufferings. Lawyers, judges, and high priests opposed these witnesses, secretly killed some prophets, and formed a covenant to defeat justice, destroy the governor, and establish a king.

Chapter 7

The people of Nephi destroyed the government of the land by murdering the chief judge and dividing into tribes with separate leaders and laws. A secret combination led by Jacob established a wicked band and fled to the northernmost part of the land. Nephi preached repentance and remission of sins through faith on the Lord Jesus Christ, performed miracles, cast out devils, and raised his brother from the dead. Some people were converted, baptized with water, and healed through the power and Spirit of God.

Chapter 8

In the thirty-fourth year, a great storm, tempest, thunder, lightning, and earthquake caused widespread destruction and changed the face of the land. Zarahemla was set on fire, Moroni sank into the sea, and Moronihah was covered by the earth and became a mountain. After about three hours of destruction, thick darkness covered the land for three days, preventing any light or fire. The people mourned, howled, and wept, and some cried that repentance would have spared their brethren, mothers, daughters, and children.

Chapter 9

This unit concerns the inhabitants of the earth, the destruction of many cities and people because of wickedness, and a call for those spared to repent and return to Jesus Christ. Jesus Christ declares that he created the heavens and the earth, fulfills the law of Moses, and offers eternal life, mercy, redemption, and salvation from sin. He teaches that sacrifices and burnt offerings are ended and that a broken heart and a contrite spirit are required. He also teaches that those who come to him as little children may enter the kingdom of God, and that believers may receive baptism with fire and the Holy Ghost.

Chapter 10

The people of the land hear a voice calling the house of Israel to repent and return, and warning that their dwellings will become desolate if they do not. After three days of darkness, trembling, destruction, mourning, and silence, the land is restored and the spared people turn their mourning into joy and praise to the Lord Jesus Christ, their Redeemer. The more righteous people are spared because they received the prophets and did not shed the blood of the saints. In the thirty-fourth year, Christ manifests himself to the people of Nephi who were spared and to the Lamanites who had been spared, showing them his body and ministering to them.

Chapter 11

A great multitude of the people of Nephi gathers around the temple in the land Bountiful and hears the Father testify of his Beloved Son. Jesus Christ appears, declares that he is the light and life of the world, and lets the multitude feel the wounds from his crucifixion. He gives Nephi and others power to baptize, specifies the baptismal words and method, and declares his doctrine of repentance, belief, baptism, and becoming as a little child. He teaches that contention is of the devil and that those who build on his doctrine build on his rock and can inherit the kingdom of God.

Chapter 12

Jesus teaches Nephi, the twelve, and the multitude about baptism, repentance, humility, righteousness, mercy, purity, peacemaking, and enduring persecution. He appoints twelve to minister and baptize with water, while he baptizes believers with fire and the Holy Ghost. Jesus explains that he fulfills the law and commandments and teaches reconciliation, purity of heart, truthful communication, nonresistance, generosity, love for enemies, and perfection. He tells the people to let their light shine, remain the salt of the earth, and keep his commandments to enter the kingdom of heaven.

Chapter 13

Jesus teaches about almsgiving, prayer, fasting, forgiveness, treasures, light, and serving God rather than Mammon. He instructs that the heavenly Father sees acts done in secret and rewards them openly, and that forgiveness from the Father depends on forgiving others. Jesus tells the twelve to seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness and not to worry about food, drink, or clothing. He teaches that the heavenly Father knows their needs and will provide for them, while each day has sufficient trouble of its own.

Chapter 14

Jesus teaches the multitude not to judge hypocritically, to ask, seek, and knock, and to treat others as they would want to be treated. He contrasts the strait gate and narrow way leading to life with the broad way leading to destruction, and warns against false prophets by their fruits. Jesus teaches that not everyone who calls him Lord will enter the kingdom of heaven, but those who do the will of his Father. He compares those who hear and do his sayings to a wise man whose house stands on rock, and those who hear but do not do them to a foolish man whose house falls on sand.

Chapter 15

Jesus teaches the multitude that the law given through Moses is fulfilled in him, while prophecies and the covenant concerning things to come are not destroyed. He commands the twelve disciples, who are a light to a remnant of the house of Joseph, to keep his commandments and endure to the end. Jesus explains that the Father has other sheep among separated tribes who will hear his voice and become one fold with one shepherd. He also teaches that the Gentiles will be converted through the preaching of the twelve but will not hear his voice directly except through the Holy Ghost.

Chapter 16

Jesus says that he has other sheep in lands outside Jerusalem who have not yet heard his voice, and that he will go to them so there may be one fold and one shepherd. He commands that his sayings be written so the people at Jerusalem and other tribes may gain knowledge through the Holy Ghost, and so the Gentiles may bring the remnant of the house of Israel to knowledge of him. The Father will gather the house of Israel from the earth and fulfill his covenant, while the Gentiles are blessed for belief but warned that rejecting the fulness of the gospel will cause it to be taken from them. The Father commands that this land be given to this people as an inheritance, and Isaiah’s words concerning Jerusalem and Zion will be fulfilled.

Chapter 17

Jesus tells the multitude to return to their homes, ponder his words, ask the Father for understanding, and prepare for the next day. He heals all the sick and afflicted people brought to him, and the multitude worships him. Jesus prays to the Father for the multitude and blesses their little children, while angels descend and minister to the children. The multitude witnesses these events and bears record of them.

Chapter 18

Jesus teaches the disciples and the multitude how to administer and receive bread and wine in remembrance of his body and blood. He commands them to pray, meet together, welcome and minister to others, keep his commandments, and avoid temptation and disputation. Jesus gives the disciples power to give the Holy Ghost, then departs from the multitude and ascends into heaven.

Chapter 19

After Jesus ascends into heaven, the multitude gathers again, and the twelve disciples teach them, pray, and baptize those Jesus had chosen. The Holy Ghost falls upon the baptized disciples with fire, angels minister to them, and Jesus comes to minister unto them. Jesus prays to the Father for the disciples and for those who will believe on their words, asking that they may receive the Holy Ghost, be purified, and be one with him and the Father. The disciples continue praying to Jesus, and Jesus says that he has never seen such great faith among all the Jews.

Chapter 20

Jesus Christ gives the multitude and the disciples bread and wine, and the multitude is filled with the Spirit and gives glory to him. He teaches that the words of Isaiah will be fulfilled, the covenant of the Father with the house of Israel will be fulfilled, and the remnants of Israel will be gathered and brought to the knowledge of the Lord their God. He declares that the Gentiles will be blessed through the Holy Ghost but will face judgment if they harden their hearts after receiving the fulness of the gospel. He prophesies the establishment of the house of Israel in a New Jerusalem, the redemption and reinhabiting of Jerusalem, and the fulfillment of the covenant made with the Father’s people.

Chapter 21

Jesus Christ declares that the Father will gather the dispersed house of Israel, establish Zion, and fulfill the covenant through works brought forth from the Gentiles. The Gentiles will be tested by whether they repent, accept Jesus Christ’s words, and are numbered among the house of Israel. The remnant of Jacob will receive power, assist in gathering Israel, and build the New Jerusalem, where the power of heaven will come down. The Father’s work and gospel will extend among the dispersed people and all nations, while those who refuse to repent will be cut off.

Chapter 22

The Lord speaks to a barren and forsaken woman, calling her his wife and promising to gather her with great mercies and everlasting kindness. He promises that her children will increase, inherit the Gentiles, and be taught of the Lord with great peace. The Lord will establish the woman in righteousness, protect her from oppression and terror, and cause those who gather against her to fall. He declares that no weapon formed against the Lord’s servants will prosper and that their righteousness is of him.

Chapter 23

Jesus commands the people to search the words of Isaiah diligently because they concern the house of Israel and the Gentiles. He teaches that those who hearken to his words, repent, and are baptized shall be saved. Jesus explains that Samuel the Lamanite prophesied that many saints would arise from the dead and minister to many, and he commands that this event be written in the record. He also commands the people to teach the things he has expounded from the scriptures.

Chapter 24

The Father gives words to Malachi concerning a messenger who will prepare the way and the messenger of the covenant who will come to the temple. The Lord of Hosts will refine, judge, and witness against those who commit wrongdoing, while purifying the sons of Levi. The Lord calls the people to return by bringing tithes and offerings, promising protection and a blessing. Those who fear the Lord will be remembered as his jewels and spared, and the righteous will be distinguished from the wicked.

Chapter 25

The day of the Lord will burn the proud and those who do wickedly, leaving them neither root nor branch. Those who fear the Lord’s name will receive healing from the Son of Righteousness and will tread down the wicked. The law of Moses, given in Horeb for all Israel, is to be remembered. Elijah the prophet will be sent before the great and dreadful day of the Lord to turn the hearts of fathers and children toward one another.

Chapter 26

Jesus expounded the scriptures and taught the multitude about events from the beginning until the last day, including judgment and the resurrections. Mormon wrote that only a small part of Jesus’s teachings could be included, and that greater things would be made manifest if the people believed what had first been given to them. Jesus taught and ministered to the multitude and their children, healed many, raised a man from the dead, and ascended into heaven. The disciples baptized and taught the people, who were filled with the Holy Ghost, taught and ministered to one another, dealt justly, and did as Jesus commanded.

Chapter 27

The disciples of Jesus pray, fast, preach, baptize, and ask Jesus what to call the church. Jesus teaches that the church must be called in his name and built upon his gospel, and he explains that repentance, baptism, endurance, and faithfulness are required for salvation. He commands the disciples to write what they see and hear, says that people will be judged by their works, and promises that requests made to the Father in his name will be answered. Jesus contrasts the strait gate and narrow way leading to life with the broad way leading to death, and warns about the fourth generation being led away.

Chapter 28

Jesus asks his disciples what they desire after he returns to the Father. Most desire to end their ministry after reaching the age of man and come to Jesus in his kingdom, while three disciples desire to remain until they bring the souls of men to him. Jesus promises the three disciples that they will not taste death, and they are changed so they can minister among the people without pain or sorrow except for the sins of the world. Mormon records that the three disciples minister among the Gentiles, Jews, scattered tribes of Israel, and all nations, while the people of Nephi are converted and united to the church of Christ.

Chapter 29

The unit concerns the Lord’s covenant with the children of Israel and its fulfillment when these sayings come to the Gentiles. It teaches that the words of the Lord spoken by the holy prophets will be fulfilled and that the Lord remembers his covenant with the house of Israel. It warns against spurning the doings of the Lord, denying Jesus Christ, rejecting revelation and spiritual gifts, or denying miracles. It also says that the Lord will execute judgment and fulfill his covenant with the house of Israel.

Chapter 30

Jesus Christ, the Son of the living God, commands that the Gentiles be addressed. The Gentiles are told to turn from their wicked ways and repent of their evil doings and abominations. They are instructed to come unto Jesus Christ and be baptized in his name. Through baptism, they may receive a remission of their sins, be filled with the Holy Ghost, and be numbered with the people who are of the house of Israel.
