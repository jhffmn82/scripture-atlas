Moroni

Abstract written by a model

In The Words of Mormon, Moroni is Mormon’s son, expected to witness the destruction of the Nephites and write about them and Christ. In The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record, Moroni is Mormon’s son who survives the destruction of his people, finishes Mormon’s record, and hides the records. In The Book of Ether, Moroni is the record keeper and writer who possesses Moroni’s record and recounts the destruction of the Jaredites. In The Book of Moroni, Moroni is a Nephite prophet and son of Mormon who preserves the records, writes for the Lamanites, and anticipates the resurrection. In The Book of Moroni, the writer is a living man who preserves sacred records for his son, prays for him, and trusts in Christ amid war.

Moroni in The Book of Ether

Ether · the work’s page · read it where Moroni first appears

Abstract written by a model

Moroni is a person, record keeper, and writer who possesses the record of Moroni and writes part of an account from the tower down to the destruction of the Jaredites. Drawing on the twenty and four plates and writing commanded words according to memory, he recounts ancient inhabitants of the north country, sealed things, faith, and the brother of Jared. He withholds detailed oaths and combinations while warning that secret combinations bring destruction and urging the Gentiles to repent. Though he acknowledges weakness in writing, he testifies that he has seen and spoken with Jesus Christ, records teachings on weakness, grace, faith, hope, and charity, prays for the Gentiles, and commends people to seek Jesus. He concludes by finishing the record of the people’s destruction.

The Book of Ether / Chapter 1

Moroni gives an account of the ancient inhabitants who were destroyed in the north country. He takes his account from the twenty and four plates.

Chapter 5

Moroni writes the commanded words, recounts the sealed things, and says he has authority that will be known when he is seen and stands before God at the last day.

Chapter 8

Moroni wrote that he would not describe the oaths and combinations because they were known among all people and among the Lamanites. He warned that these combinations cause destruction and urged the Gentiles to repent.

Moroni explains the nature and power of faith, recounts revelations and promises concerning the brother of Jared, and records the Lord’s teachings about weakness, grace, faith, hope, and charity. He prays for the Gentiles, is comforted by the Lord, testifies that he has seen and spoken with Jesus, and commends people to seek Him.

Chapter 13

Moroni proceeds to finish his record concerning the destruction of the people about whom he has been writing.

Moroni in The Book of Moroni

Moroni · the work’s page · read it where Moroni first appears

Abstract written by a model

Moroni is a Nephite man, prophet, and son of Mormon who remains alive after abridging the account of the people of Jared. Refusing to deny Christ, he conceals himself from the Lamanites and wanders to preserve his life while writing for their future benefit. Called by the Lord Jesus Christ to the ministry, he receives Mormon’s letters, is urged to correct the baptism of little children, labor diligently, stay faithful, and possibly receive sacred records. He writes to the Lamanites, exhorts them to remember the Lord’s mercy, and seals the records. Expecting resurrection, he anticipates resting in paradise until spirit and body reunite, then meeting the Lamanites before the bar of God.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1

Moroni has finished abridging the account of the people of Jared but continues to live and write. He refuses to deny Christ, hides from the Lamanites, wanders for safety, and writes additional things for his brethren, the Lamanites.

Chapter 8

Moroni receives Mormon’s epistle soon after being called to the ministry. Mormon asks him to labor diligently to remove the error of baptizing little children and to pray for the Nephites.

Chapter 9

The son receives a letter from the writer describing the failed battle, the sufferings and wickedness of the people, and the danger of destruction. The writer urges him to labor diligently, remain faithful in Christ, and not be weighed down by the things written. The writer recommends him to God and hopes to see him again and deliver sacred records to him.

Chapter 10

Moroni writes to the Lamanites, seals up the records, exhorts readers to remember the Lord’s mercy, and declares that God will show that what he has written is true. He says he will soon go to rest in the paradise of God until his spirit and body reunite and he is brought forth triumphant to meet others before the bar of the great Jehovah.

writer (the speaker) in The Book of Moroni

Moroni · the work’s page · read it where writer (the speaker) first appears

Abstract written by a model

A living man writes amid grievous war with the Lamanites. Unable to enforce commands among his increasingly perverse people, he continues laboring and fears their destruction, preserves sacred records for his son, prays for him, and trusts in Christ.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 9

The writer says that he is still alive but is writing about grievous matters. He has had a sore battle with the Lamanites, cannot any longer enforce his commands because his people have become strong in perversion, and fears for their destruction. He continues laboring with them, prays for his son, and trusts in Christ.
