Abstract written by a model

In The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry, Nephi serves as record maker, prophet, ruler, teacher, keeper of sacred records, and leader of his family, obtaining the plates of brass and guiding his people to the promised land. In The Second Book of Nephi, Nephi serves as speaker, writer, ruler, teacher, king or protector, and prophet, leading, teaching, governing, and protecting his people while recording scripture and prophesying. In The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi, Nephi serves as king and great protector of the people of Nephi, commands Jacob, consecrates priests and teachers, appoints a ruler, and dies. In The Book of Mosiah, Nephi obeys divine commands, leads a wilderness journey, and takes engraved records.

Nephi in The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry

Abstract written by a model

Nephi is a person, son of Lehi, brother of Laman, Lemuel, and Sam, record maker, prophet, ruler, teacher, and keeper of sacred records. Taught in his father’s learning, he writes with his own hand, abridges his father’s record, makes plates, and bears witness that his record is true. Though exceedingly young, he is large in stature, seeks the mysteries and things of God with lowliness of heart, gains great knowledge of God’s goodness and mysteries, and faithfully keeps the Lord’s commandments without murmuring. He believes his father after receiving a manifestation by the Holy Spirit, teaches Sam, and receives promises for faith and obedience. He leads his brothers to Jerusalem for the plates of brass, persists despite opposition, receives strength, enters the city under the Spirit’s direction, kills Laban at the Spirit’s command, takes Laban’s sword, garments, and armor, obtains the plates, and brings Zoram into the wilderness. He later returns for Ishmael’s family, confronts rebellion among his brethren, and records Lehi’s dream. Seeking to know what his father saw, he receives a vision of the Lamb of God’s ministry and suffering, the future of his seed and his brethren’s seed, nations, kingdoms, Gentiles, the great and abominable church, and the book of the Lamb; he is forbidden to write all he saw and heard. He teaches, preaches diligently, explains scripture and prophecy, and likens scripture for his people’s profit and learning. When his steel bow breaks, he makes a wooden bow, follows the compass, and obtains food. In Bountiful he is instructed to build a ship, makes it as shown by the Lord, guides it to the promised land, and prays during a storm after his brethren bind him. He then makes plates of ore, records sacred things, and teaches his people and brethren from the plates of brass about the Lord, scattering and gathering, destruction of the wicked, and preservation and salvation of the righteous.

The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry

Nephi returns with his brethren to Jerusalem for the record of the Jews. He later confounds his brethren, builds a ship, and writes the record.

Chapter 1

Nephi was born of goodly parents and was taught in the learning of his father. He records his proceedings, abridges his father's record, and declares that the record is true. He will show that the tender mercies of the Lord are over those chosen because of their faith.

Chapter 2

Nephi is exceedingly young and large in stature, and he desires to know the mysteries of God. He cries unto the Lord, receives a softened heart and a manifestation by the Holy Spirit, believes his father, teaches Sam, and receives promises for his faith and obedience.

Chapter 3

Nephi returns from speaking with the Lord and agrees to go to Jerusalem to obtain the records on the plates of brass. He persuades his brothers to continue, gathers his family's gold, silver, and precious things, and insists that they fulfill the Lord's commandment. After Laman and Lemuel smite him with a rod, Nephi hears the angel promise that Laban will be delivered into their hands.

Chapter 4

Nephi urges his brethren to trust in the Lord and return to Jerusalem. He enters the city, kills Laban at the Spirit’s command, takes Laban’s garments and sword, obtains the plates of brass with Zoram’s help, and returns to his brethren before departing into the wilderness.

Chapter 5 .. Chapter 8

Nephi returns with his brothers to Jerusalem to bring Ishmael and his family into the wilderness. He speaks to Laman and Lemuel during their rebellion, prays for deliverance, and records Lehi’s dream.

Chapter 9 .. Chapter 12

Nephi records his father’s teachings and desires to know the things his father saw. He receives a vision by the power of the Holy Ghost, sees the ministry and suffering of the Lamb of God, and is shown the future of his seed and the seed of his brethren.

Chapter 13 .. Chapter 14

Nephi sees many nations and kingdoms, the Gentiles, the great and abominable church, the book of the Lamb of God, and events concerning the house of Israel. He hears that John will write the remainder of the things shown, and he ends his record of this vision while bearing record that what he has written is true.

Chapter 15 .. Chapter 16

Nephi returns to his father's tent, explains his father's teachings and dream to his brethren, and exhorts them to keep the commandments of the Lord. After his steel bow breaks, he makes a wooden bow, follows the ball's directions, obtains food, and returns with joy to his family.

Chapter 17 .. Chapter 18

Nephi travels with his family through the wilderness and reaches the land of Bountiful. The Lord commands him to construct a ship, and he makes tools, builds the ship according to the Lord’s instructions, guides it to the promised land, and prays for the storm to cease after his brethren bind him.

Chapter 19

Nephi makes plates of ore by commandment, records sacred things and prophecies, and teaches his people from the plates of brass. He seeks to persuade the house of Israel to remember the Lord their Redeemer and reads Isaiah to his brethren.

Chapter 22

Nephi explains the prophecies on the plates of brass to his brethren. He teaches about the scattering and gathering of Israel, the destruction of the wicked, and the preservation and salvation of the righteous.

Nephi in The Second Book of Nephi

Abstract written by a model

Nephi is a brother of Laman, Lemuel, and Sam; a speaker, writer, ruler, teacher, king or protector, and prophet among the people of Nephi. From the family’s departure from Jerusalem, he keeps the Lord’s commandments, seeks God’s glory and his brethren’s eternal welfare, and acts through the power of God. Lehi describes him as helping bring the family through the wilderness and directs his brethren to hearken to him so they will not perish. Nephi records scriptures, the things of his soul, and writings on plates. He delights in scriptures, Isaiah’s words, the Lord’s covenants, grace, justice, power, mercy, and plainness. In sorrow over sin, temptations, afflictions, and enemies, he prays, trusts the Lord forever rather than the arm of flesh, and is supported through afflictions. When his brethren become angry, he flees into the wilderness with believers. He leads, teaches, governs, and protects his people; builds a temple, makes swords, consecrates priests and teachers, and keeps records. He possesses the plates of brass, the ball or compass prepared for his father, and the sword of Laban. He writes Isaiah and teaches plainly about Christ, repentance, baptism, receiving the Holy Ghost, enduring to the end, remission of sins, and obedience to God’s commandments. He prophesies concerning Jews, Gentiles, his seed, Israel, and the Lord’s future work. Near the end, he mourns over unbelief and wickedness, says he is stronger in speaking than writing, continues praying for his people, and writes as commanded, with faith in Christ and charity for his people, the Jews, and the Gentiles.

The Second Book of Nephi / Chapter 1

Nephi has taught his brethren and is described by Lehi as having kept the commandments, brought the family through the wilderness, and sought the glory of God and their eternal welfare. Lehi commands his brethren to cease rebelling against him and says that blessings will rest upon Nephi if they will not hearken to him.

Chapter 3

Joseph is instructed to hearken unto the words of his brother, Nephi.

Chapter 4

Nephi speaks about Joseph’s prophecies and records scriptures and the things of his soul. He describes his sins, temptations, afflictions, visions, prayers, trust in the Lord, and desire to be delivered from sin and enemies.

Chapter 5

Nephi cries unto the Lord because of his brethren’s anger and flees into the wilderness with those who believe the warnings and revelations of God. He leads, teaches, and governs the people of Nephi, builds a temple, makes swords, teaches industry, consecrates Jacob and Joseph as priests and teachers, and keeps records on plates.

Chapter 6

Nephi has consecrated Jacob and is regarded by the people of Nephi as a king or protector. Nephi desired Jacob to speak the words of Isaiah to the people.

Chapter 11

Nephi writes more of Isaiah’s words because he delights in them. He will liken Isaiah’s words to his people, send them to all his children, and use the words of Isaiah and Jacob as witnesses that his own words are true.

Chapter 25

Nephi explains Isaiah’s words and gives his own plain prophecy concerning the Jews, Jesus Christ, the restoration of Israel, and the preservation of his writings. He teaches his people to believe in Christ, be reconciled to God, and look to Christ for remission of sins.

Chapter 30

Nephi warns his beloved brethren not to suppose that they are more righteous than the Gentiles, because all who do not keep the commandments of God will perish. He prophesies concerning the Jews, the Gentiles, the remnant of his seed, and the Lord God’s future work.

Chapter 31

Nephi makes an end of his prophesying and speaks plainly concerning the doctrine of Christ. He teaches that people must enter by repentance and baptism by water, receive the Holy Ghost, and endure to the end.

Chapter 32

Nephi teaches his beloved brethren to receive the Holy Ghost, feast upon the words of Christ, ask, knock, and pray always. He cannot say more because the Spirit stoppeth his utterance, and he is left to mourn because of men's unbelief, wickedness, ignorance, and stiffneckedness.

Chapter 33

Nephi says he cannot write all that was taught among his people and that he is not mighty in writing as he is in speaking. He writes for the benefit of his people, prays for them, teaches about Christ, and says he must obey what the Lord commanded him to write.
