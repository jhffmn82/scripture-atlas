Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Nephi, Jesus Christ is the Holy Messiah and Son of God who offers himself as a sacrifice, redeems souls, rises from the dead, intercedes, manifests himself to believers, and calls people to endure. In The Second Book of Nephi, Christ is the Redeemer and Son who comes among the Jews, fulfills righteousness, teaches the way to salvation, gives the Holy Ghost, and gathers Israel. In The Second Book of Nephi, the Messiah is awaited to come in the fulness of his time, bringing light and freedom, destroying enemies, recovering believers, and making salvation possible. In The Book of Jacob, Christ is the Only Begotten Son whose coming, death, atonement, resurrection, kingdom, and doctrine are proclaimed by prophets and affirmed by Jacob. (The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi) In The Book of Mosiah, Messiah is the prophesied one who comes among humanity in human form, is oppressed and afflicted, and brings the resurrection of the dead. In The Book of Mosiah, Christ is the Son of God and Eternal Father who takes upon himself the sins of humanity, satisfies justice, rises from the dead, intercedes, judges the world, and makes salvation possible. In The Book of Mosiah, the Son of God is Jesus Christ, believed to have created heaven, earth, and all things and to come among humanity. In The Book of Alma, the Son of God is the Father’s Son who suffers and dies to atone for sins, rises from the dead, causes resurrection, and judges all people. (The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma)

Abstract written by a model

Jesus Christ is the Holy Messiah, the Only Begotten of the Father, the Son of God, the Eternal God, and the Holy One of Israel. Full of grace and truth, he offers himself as a sacrifice for sin for those with broken hearts and contrite spirits. He lays down his life in the flesh, takes it up by the Spirit’s power, rises first to bring about the resurrection of the dead, redeems souls from hell, and intercedes for all the children of men. Though rejected, scourged, and crucified, he becomes the source of salvation, reconciliation, remission of sins, strength, song, comfort, and healing for the righteous. He manifests himself in the flesh to the house of Israel and to Nephi’s people, gives law, and manifests himself through the Holy Ghost to those who believe. He works miracles among the children of men according to faith, recovers covenant people a second time in power and great glory, preserves believers, and will show at the last day that Nephi’s words are his. His message calls people to believe in him, be reconciled unto him, and endure to the end.

Jesus Christ in The Book of Helaman

The Book of Mormon · the work’s page · read it where Jesus Christ first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jesus Christ is the Son of God, the Father of heaven and earth, the Creator of all things, the Redeemer, and the only way people can be saved. Testified of by Moses and the fathers, he is represented by the serpent lifted up in the wilderness and is expected to come into the world, though some dispute that such a Christ would come. His coming marks the period to which the records extend. He will suffer and be slain for his people; his death brings about his resurrection, making resurrection and salvation possible. Through atoning blood, repentance, belief in his name, faith, and a contrite spirit, he redeems the world and the children of men from sins and spiritual death, gives eternal life, opens the gate of heaven, and brings peace. He is also the object of faith for Lamanites who refuse violence against their enemies.

Jesus in The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman

Mormon · the work’s page · read it where Jesus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jesus is the Redeemer, Lord, God, beloved Son of the Father, and the light and life of the world. He takes upon himself and is slain for the sins of the world, drinking the bitter cup given by the Father and suffering the Father’s will. Prophets testify that he will come, and after his ascension he manifests himself to the spared people. He appears to a great multitude, identifies himself, gives Nephi and the twelve authority to baptize, promises baptism with fire and the Holy Ghost, and teaches his doctrine. He fulfills the law of Moses in himself, gives commandments, requires repentance, a broken heart, love for enemies, obedience, endurance, and perfection. He teaches prayer, fasting, worship, judgment, seeking the kingdom of God, and hearing and doing his sayings. He heals the sick, afflicted, lame, blind, and deaf, raises a man from the dead, blesses and ministers to children, reveals greater things to them, and shows compassion and mercy. He institutes bread and wine, gives chosen disciples power to give the Holy Ghost, ascends to heaven, and later returns to minister, pray, and teach. He expounds and commands the writing and searching of scriptures, teaches of covenants, gathering, judgments, his future glory, and his judgment of the world. He establishes his gospel and church among the disciples, promises eternal life to those who endure, and commands that the Gentiles repent, come unto him, and be baptized in his name.

Jesus Christ in The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman

Abstract written by a model

Jesus Christ is the Son of God, Redeemer, creator of the heavens and earth, and the light and life of the world. He is with and in the Father from the beginning, existed before the world, and is one with the Father; he is Alpha and Omega. Prophets, including Moses and Samuel, testify of him, and people reckon time from his coming after foretold signs. Men from heaven testify of his death, sufferings, and resurrection. He comes to his own, calls people to repent and come unto him, saves the world from sin, brings redemption, offers eternal life, and extends mercy to all who will come. Those who receive and believe on his name become sons of God. He baptizes the broken heart and contrite spirit with fire and the Holy Ghost. He ministers among people, is praised by the spared people, and teaches prayer. Bread and wine represent his body and blood in the sacrament. He has other sheep beyond this land and Jerusalem, whom he will gather into one fold, and he gathers the house of Israel from the four quarters of the earth. He teaches the fulfillment of the Father’s covenant, the gathering of Israel, redemption of Jerusalem, and matters concerning Gentiles and the gospel. Mercy, justice, and holiness are in him, and his works and miracles should not be denied.
