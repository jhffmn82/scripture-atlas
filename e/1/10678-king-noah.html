king Noah

person · 1 work · 42 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: Noah, king, the king, thou, thy, thy father

Abstract written by a model

King Noah, son of Zeniff, reigns in Zeniff’s stead but rejects his father’s ways. He does not keep the commandments of God, follows his own desires, takes many wives and concubines, and leads his people into sin. He taxes them heavily to support himself, his household, and proud new priests, while removing the priests consecrated by his father. He builds elegant buildings, a spacious palace, temple work, and high towers; plants vineyards, makes abundant wine, lives riotously, and values riches. He sends guards against the Lamanites and armies against them. When Abinadi speaks against his rule, Noah grows angry, commands that Abinadi be seized and slain, imprisons him, and directs his priests to question, remove, and kill him. Though he briefly fears Abinadi’s words and God’s judgments, his priests stir him back to anger, and Abinadi is delivered to death. Noah also accuses Alma of rebellion and sends an army after Alma and the people of the Lord. His rule oppresses Alma’s people through bondage under Noah and his priests. During a Lamanite attack, Noah flees, leads his people into the wilderness, orders the men to abandon their wives and children, and is eventually captured by his people and killed by fire.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Noah begins to reign in Zeniff's stead but does not walk in his father's ways. He taxes the people, supports himself, his wives and concubines, and his priests, builds extensively, lives riotously, and hardens his heart against the word of the Lord. After hearing Abinadi's words, Noah becomes angry and commands that Abinadi be brought to him so that he may slay him.

Chapter 12

King Noah is warned that his life will be afflicted because of his iniquities unless he repents. He causes Abinadi to be imprisoned and orders the priests to question him.

Chapter 13

King Noah orders his priests to remove and slay Abinadi, calling him mad. Abinadi teaches him that salvation does not come by the law of Moses alone and condemns the iniquity taught under his rule.

Chapter 17

King Noah commands that Abinadi be taken and put to death, then imprisons and questions him. He briefly considers releasing Abinadi because he fears his words and the judgments of God, but the priests stir him to anger and he delivers Abinadi to be slain.

Chapter 18

King Noah’s servants searched for Alma and the people who believed him. The king accused Alma of stirring up the people to rebellion and sent his army to destroy them.

Chapter 19

King Noah flees from Gideon by climbing a tower when Gideon is about to slay him. He leads his people into the wilderness during the Lamanite attack, commands the men to leave their wives and children, and is later captured by his people and killed by fire.

Chapter 21

Noah is identified as the king whose priests caused the people to commit many sins and iniquities against God. His priests are associated with the destruction involving the Lamanites.

Chapter 23

King Noah and his priests oppress Alma’s people, hold them in bondage, and bring them into iniquity. His armies pursue Alma’s people but cannot overtake them.
