pleasures

Also called: fivefold human pleasures, objects of the senses, pleasant objects of the senses, pleasure, sensual pleasures, sensual things

Abstract written by a model

Pleasures are portrayed as beautiful or pleasant in appearance but painful in effect: like poisonous Kimpâka fruit and glue. Attachment to them causes passions, misery, soiling, and wandering in Samsâra. Mrigâputra gives them up after reflecting on continuous suffering; Râgîmatî rejects Rathanêmi’s urging to enjoy them. The enlightened turn away, and one seeking liberation avoids fixing thoughts on pleasant sensory objects or turning the mind toward unpleasant ones.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Nineteenth Lecture.

Pleasures are compared to poisonous or Kimpâka fruit whose consequences are painful. Mrigâputra gives them up after reflecting on their connection with continuous suffering.

Twenty-Second Lecture.

Rathanêmi urges Râgîmatî to enjoy pleasures before entering the path of the Ginas. Râgîmatî rejects this, and the unit teaches that the enlightened turn from pleasures.

Twenty-Fifth Lecture.

Pleasure is compared to glue because those who are attached to it become soiled and must wander in Samsâra. Those who are not given to pleasure are not soiled by it, like a dry clod of clay that does not stick to a wall.

Thirty-Second Lecture.

Pleasures produce misery and attachment, even though they may appear beautiful or pleasant. A person who seeks liberation should not fix his thoughts on pleasant sensory objects or turn his mind toward unpleasant ones.
