The Book of Moroni

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This document is Moroni’s continuing record after completing an abridgment of the people of Jared. Hiding from the Lamanites and refusing to deny Christ amid warfare and killings, Moroni preserves teachings intended to benefit future readers, including the Lamanites. He records Christ’s instructions to twelve disciples to pray and confer the Holy Ghost by laying on hands, then describes ordaining priests and teachers to preach repentance and remission of sins through Jesus Christ. He gives church practices for administering bread and wine in remembrance of Christ’s body and blood, and for baptism, membership, repentance, forgiveness, fasting, prayer, and meetings led by the Holy Ghost. Mormon’s teachings follow: people should distinguish good from evil, exercise faith in Jesus Christ, hope through his atonement and resurrection, and seek charity, the pure love of Christ. In an epistle to Moroni, Mormon teaches that little children are alive in Christ and need neither repentance nor baptism; he condemns their baptism and warns of Nephite rejection of God, the Holy Ghost, and prophetic teachings. Another letter recounts military defeat, deaths, and grave cruelty by both the Lamanites and their own people, urging Moroni to remain faithful as destruction threatens. Finally, Moroni seals the record, calls the Lamanites and all people to remember God’s mercy, seek truth through sincere prayer and the Holy Ghost, cultivate faith, hope, and charity, come unto Christ, receive grace, deny ungodliness, and become holy through Christ’s blood.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 1

Moroni says he has finished abridging the account of the people of Jared but continues to live and write. He hides his identity from the Lamanites because they may kill him, and he wanders for safety. The Lamanites fight fiercely among themselves and kill Nephites who will not deny Christ. Moroni refuses to deny Christ and writes additional things that may someday benefit the Lamanites.

Chapter 2

Christ gives his twelve chosen disciples instructions about prayer and giving the Holy Ghost. He tells them to call on the Father in his name in mighty prayer and says they will have power to give the Holy Ghost by laying hands on people. The multitude does not hear these words, but the disciples do, and the Holy Ghost falls on as many as they lay their hands upon.

Chapter 3

The disciples, who were called the elders of the church, ordained priests and teachers. After praying to the Father in the name of Jesus Christ, they laid their hands upon those being ordained. Priests and teachers were ordained to preach repentance and remission of sins through Jesus Christ by the endurance of faith on his name to the end. They were ordained according to the gifts and callings of God unto men by the power of the Holy Ghost.

Chapter 4

Elders and priests administer the flesh and blood of Christ to the church according to the commandments of Christ. The elder or priest kneels with the church and prays to God, the Eternal Father, in the name of Jesus Christ. The prayer asks that the bread be blessed and sanctified so participants remember the body of Jesus Christ, take upon themselves his name, keep his commandments, and have his Spirit with them.

Chapter 5

The unit describes the manner of administering the wine. The participants take the cup and ask God, the Eternal Father, in the name of Jesus Christ, to bless and sanctify the wine. The wine is intended to be consumed in remembrance of the blood of Jesus Christ, which was shed for them. Those who drink are to witness that they always remember Jesus Christ and may have his Spirit with them.

Chapter 6

This unit concerns baptism and the practices governing membership in the church of Christ. Elders, priests, teachers, and others are baptized only after bringing forth worthy fruit, showing a broken heart and contrite spirit, witnessing true repentance, and taking upon themselves the name of Christ. Members are numbered, remembered, nourished, and instructed to meet often to fast, pray, speak about their souls, and partake of bread and wine in remembrance of the Lord Jesus. The church removes names for unrepented iniquity but forgives those who repent and seek forgiveness with real intent, and its meetings follow the workings and power of the Holy Ghost.

Chapter 7

Mormon teaches the church about judging good and evil, faith in Jesus Christ, hope through Christ’s atonement and resurrection, and charity as the pure love of Christ. He explains that all good things come from God through Christ, while evil comes from the devil. He teaches that faith enables miracles, angelic ministry, salvation, and becoming the sons of God. He urges the people to pray for charity and to remain meek, lowly, faithful, hopeful, and charitable.

Chapter 8

Mormon writes an epistle to his son Moroni after Moroni’s calling to the ministry. He teaches that little children are alive in Christ, cannot commit sin or repent, and do not need baptism. Mormon condemns baptizing little children as a denial of the mercies and power of Jesus Christ, and teaches that baptism and repentance are for those accountable and capable of committing sin. He also warns that the Nephites are in danger because they reject God, the Holy Ghost, and prophetic teachings.

Chapter 9

The writer tells his son that he is alive but describes a failed battle with the Lamanites, the deaths of important men, and the loss of many choice men. He reports that both the Lamanites and their own people have committed extreme cruelty against prisoners, women, and children, and that their people have become without order, mercy, principle, and feeling. The writer fears that the Lord’s Spirit has ceased striving with them and that the people will be destroyed unless they repent and return to God. He urges his son to remain faithful in Christ and recommends him to God, trusting in Christ’s mercy and eternal life.

Chapter 10

Moroni writes to the Lamanites and seals up the records more than four hundred and twenty years after the sign of the coming of Christ. He exhorts readers to remember the Lord’s mercy, ask God in the name of Christ with sincere faith, and learn truth by the power of the Holy Ghost. He teaches that gifts of God come through the Spirit of Christ and that faith, hope, and charity are necessary for salvation in the kingdom of God. He calls all people to come unto Christ, receive grace, deny ungodliness, and become holy through the blood of Christ, and he bids farewell until his spirit and body reunite.
