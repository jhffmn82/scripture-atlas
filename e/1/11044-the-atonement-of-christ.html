the Atonement of Christ in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where the Atonement of Christ first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Atonement of Christ is the infinite and eternal sacrifice of the Only Begotten Son, made through the Son of God’s suffering, death, and shed blood for the sins and iniquities of the world. It is required for redemption because without it all mankind would unavoidably perish; only an infinite atonement can suffice. Through faith and repentance, it atones for sins and offers mercy. It also brings about the plan of mercy, appeases the demands of justice, breaks the bands of death, swallows up death’s sting in hopes of glory, and brings to pass the resurrection of the dead. Prepared from the foundation of the world, it is taught as the means of redemption, though Korihor denied that any atonement could be made. Alma’s remembrance that Jesus Christ would come to atone led him to cry for mercy.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 13

The atonement of the Only Begotten Son is connected with the holy calling prepared from the foundation of the world.

Chapter 21

Aaron teaches that redemption comes through the atonement of Christ's blood.

Chapter 22

The sufferings and death of Christ are taught to atone for sins through faith and repentance. They also break the bands of death and cause the sting of death to be swallowed up in the hopes of glory.

Chapter 24

The blood of the Son of our great God is described as being shed for the atonement of the people's sins. The people fear that staining their swords again would prevent them from being washed bright through that blood.

Chapter 30

Korihor taught that there could be no atonement made for the sins of men. He instead taught that every man fared according to the management of the creature.

Chapter 33

The Son of God shall suffer and die to atone for the sins of his people.

Chapter 34

An atonement is necessary because all mankind would otherwise unavoidably perish. Only an infinite atonement can suffice for the sins of the world.

Chapter 36

Alma remembered that Jesus Christ, a Son of God, would come to atone for the sins of the world. This thought led Alma to cry to Jesus Christ for mercy.

Chapter 42

The atonement is made by God himself for the sins of the world. It brings about the plan of mercy, appeases the demands of justice, and brings to pass the resurrection of the dead.
