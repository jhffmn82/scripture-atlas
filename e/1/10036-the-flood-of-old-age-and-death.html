Abstract written by a model

Old age and death endanger the world, consume it, and carry away living beings. Making Samsâra a wilderness of danger, their flood drives Mrigâputra toward renunciation and the Law, portrayed as an unflooded island, refuge, and shelter.

Nineteenth Lecture.

Old age and death are portrayed as consuming the world and making Samsâra a wilderness of danger. Their threat motivates Mrigâputra to save himself through renunciation and the Law.

Twenty-Third Lecture.

The flood of old age and death carries away living beings. The Law is described as an island, firm ground, refuge, and excellent shelter that is not inundated by this flood.
