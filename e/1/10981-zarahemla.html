Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Omni, the land of Zarahemla is where Mosiah’s people arrive, unite with the people of Zarahemla, and establish Mosiah as king. In The Book of Mosiah, the land of Zarahemla sends Ammon to seek people who had left, receives Alma and his people, and becomes the setting for the reign of the judges. In The Book of Mosiah, Zarahemla is a land where peoples unite, live peacefully under a just king, and establish churches. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, Zarahemla is a city and land central to the church, government, war, and migration. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, the land of Zarahemla is a Nephite land and center of departure, arrival, preaching, settlement, military supply, and conflict. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, the city of Zarahemla is a Nephite city threatened and seized by enemies, then recovered by Moroni and Pahoran. In The Book of Helaman, Zarahemla is a great city containing Nephi’s tower and garden, where preaching and baptizing occur despite widespread wickedness. In The Book of Helaman, the land of Zarahemla contains Zarahemla as its capital, is invaded and regained, and later holds a wicked people mourned by Nephi.

and 3 more works

In The Book of Helaman, the great city of Zarahemla is the land’s strongest hold and capital, captured by Lamanites and later retaken by Nephites. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, Zarahemla is a great city and land appointed for Nephites to gather and defend, later destroyed by fire in judgment. In The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record, Zarahemla is the land to which Mormon is carried as a child, where war begins in its borders by the waters of Sidon.

land of Zarahemla in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where land of Zarahemla first appears

Abstract written by a model

The land of Zarahemla is a Nephite land containing the city of Zarahemla and, within its borders, the land of Ammonihah. The river Sidon runs by it, and a narrow wilderness separates it from the land of Nephi; it is the origin of a journey to Nephi. It is repeatedly a center of departure, arrival, preaching, settlement, military supply, and conflict. The sons of Mosiah and Ammon’s brethren depart from it, while the people of Anti-Nephi-Lehi seek it and are conducted there before settling in Jershon. Korihor preaches there and is brought before Alma and the chief judge. It receives captured Amalickiahites, sends inhabitants to the east wilderness, and supplies Helaman’s army. Lamanites and Amlicites attack near it; later Lamanites threaten and seize its lands. Pachus and dissenters take it, but Moroni and Pahoran recover it, restore peace, and leave defenders.
