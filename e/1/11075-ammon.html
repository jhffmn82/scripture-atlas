Ammon

person · 2 works · 42 quoted facts · 32 connections
Also called: Ammon, the son of Mosiah, I, a man, me, my, one of them, servant, the speaker, this Nephite, this man, thy brother Ammon, thy servant

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Mosiah, Ammon travels through the lands under king Mosiah, confesses his sins, explains the prophecies and scriptures, and helps bring many to the knowledge of the truth. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, Ammon serves as a missionary and preacher among the Lamanites, defends king Lamoni’s flocks, teaches the people about God and Christ, frees his imprisoned brethren, and leads the Anti-Nephi-Lehies to Jershon. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, Ammon serves as a high priest and Alma’s fellow laborer among the Zoramites.

Ammon in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Ammon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ammon is a Nephite man, son of Mosiah and brother of Aaron, Omner, Himni, and Alma. Called by the Holy Spirit, he becomes a missionary and preacher among the Lamanites, receiving knowledge and power through faith and desire in God. He journeys to Ishmael, chooses to serve king Lamoni, and proves his great strength while defending the king’s flocks: he gathers scattered flocks, kills attackers, and cuts off the arms of others. Through the Spirit of God he discerns Lamoni’s thoughts, teaches Lamoni, the queen, servants, and the people about God, the scriptures, redemption, and Christ, and continues teaching them righteousness and the commandments. He knows Lamoni is not dead, prays and gives thanks, and is overcome with joy. Directed by the Lord, he goes to Middoni to free his imprisoned brethren, protects Lamoni from his father, defeats an attack, and helps secure both his brethren’s release and Lamoni’s rule. Protected so he can preach freely, he later counsels with Lamoni and Anti-Nephi-Lehi as the Lamanites prepare against them. With compassion for the people, he asks that they flee to the Nephites, seeks the Lord’s direction, leads them toward Zarahemla, meets Alma, and helps bring them to Jershon. Ammon sees himself as weak personally but as an instrument in God’s hands. His central joy is God’s power, the repentance of many thousands, the success and safety of his brethren, and the Lord’s mercy and answers to prayer.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

12 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 17

Ammon goes to the land of Ishmael, chooses to be king Lamoni’s servant, and watches the king’s flocks. When the flocks are scattered, he gathers them, kills some attackers with his sling, cuts off the arms of those who strike at him, and brings the severed arms as testimony before the king.

Chapter 18

Ammon defends king Lamoni’s servants and flocks, killing seven attackers with a sling and sword and cutting off the arms of others. He perceives Lamoni’s thoughts and teaches Lamoni and his servants about God, the scriptures, the plan of redemption, and the coming of Christ.

Chapter 19

Ammon goes to the queen and then sees king Lamoni, knowing that he is not dead. He teaches the queen, prays and gives thanks to God, is overcome with joy and falls to the earth, and later teaches the people.

Chapter 20

Ammon is told by the Lord to go to the land of Middoni to deliver his brethren from prison. He protects Lamoni from his father, defeats the king’s attack, and obtains the release of his brethren and the continuation of Lamoni’s rule.

Chapter 21

Ammon helps deliver Aaron and the others from prison with Lamoni. He returns with Lamoni to Ishmael, preaches to Lamoni's people, teaches righteousness, and exhorts them daily to keep the commandments of God.

Chapter 22

Ammon continually teaches the people of Lamoni. He remains in the land of Ishmael because the Spirit of the Lord called him there, while Aaron and his brethren go to the land of Nephi.

Chapter 23

Ammon was protected by the king of the Lamanites from being bound, imprisoned, assaulted, expelled, scourged, or stoned so that he could preach the word of God freely.

Chapter 24

Ammon and his brethren see the preparations of the Lamanites and come to the land of Midian. They then go to the land of Ishmael to hold a council with Lamoni and Anti-Nephi-Lehi.

Chapter 26

Ammon rejoices in God for enabling him and his brethren to help bring many Lamanites to repentance. He rejects boasting in his own strength and instead boasts in God, declaring that he is weak and that God’s power enables him to do all things.

Chapter 27

Ammon asked the king to let the people flee to the Nephites and inquired of the Lord on their behalf. He led them toward the land of Zarahemla, met Alma, and helped bring the people to the land of Jershon.

Chapter 28

Ammon is included in the account of the journeyings, sufferings, sorrows, afflictions, joy, and safety of the brethren in the land of Jershon.

Chapter 29

The speaker wishes he were an angel so he could proclaim repentance to every people, but recognizes that he should be content with his allotted work. He glories in what the Lord has commanded, rejoices in the repentance and success of others, and remembers the Lord’s mercy toward him.
