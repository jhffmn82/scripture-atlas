Abstract written by a model

Helaman is a son of Alma, brother of Moroni, keeper of sacred records, preacher, church leader, and military leader. In his youth, Alma teaches him to remember the captivity of their fathers, trust God, keep God’s commandments, and counsel with the Lord. Alma entrusts him with sacred records, plates, interpreters, and the duty to preserve a record of the people, while warning that transgression would lead to being delivered to Satan. Helaman believes in Jesus Christ, promises to keep Alma’s commandments, declares God’s word, and reestablishes the church throughout the land with his brethren. He persuades the people of Ammon to keep their oath not to take up weapons, then leads their two thousand young men, later two thousand and sixty, in defense of the Nephites. He directs campaigns and stratagems against the Lamanites, commands Nephite forces, records victories and losses, and attributes the preservation of the young men to their faith and God’s miraculous power. He gains lost lands with exceeding success but endures hunger, thirst, fatigue, and other afflictions while awaiting support. After receiving six thousand men and provisions, he preserves his part of the land, returns to preaching, and helps establish the church again. He receives sacred things from Shiblon, sends engravings throughout the land except restricted parts, and dies at the end of the thirty-fifth year.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

11 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 36

Helaman is Alma’s son. Alma teaches him to remember the captivity of their fathers, trust in God, and keep the commandments of God.

Chapter 37

Helaman is commanded to preserve the sacred records, plates, twenty-four plates, and interpreters, and to keep a record of the people. He is told to keep the commandments of God, counsel with the Lord, preach repentance and faith in Jesus Christ, and teach the people to reject sin and iniquity.

Chapter 45

Helaman affirms his belief in the records and in Jesus Christ and promises to keep Alma’s commandments. He later goes among the people to declare the word and, with his brethren, reestablishes the church throughout the land.

Chapter 53

Helaman and his brethren persuade the people of Ammon not to take up weapons and break their oath. He leads the two thousand young men who covenant to defend the Nephites and marches with them toward the southern borders by the west sea.

Chapter 56

Helaman leads two thousand young men in defense of their country, joins them to the army of Antipus, and directs a stratagem against the Lamanites. He returns with the young men to the battle, helps surround and defeat the Lamanites, finds that none of the two thousand has fallen, and returns toward Judea.

Chapter 57

Helaman receives and rejects Ammoron's proposal to exchange prisoners for Antiparah, commands the Nephite forces around Cumeni, and records their victories and losses. He attributes the preservation of the two thousand and sixty to their faith and the miraculous power of God.

Chapter 58

Helaman led the Nephite effort to capture the city of Manti and sent an embassy to the governor requesting provisions and strength. He directed the stratagem using Gid and Teomner, reported the Nephites' victories and difficulties to Moroni, and closed his epistle.

Chapter 59

Helaman has had exceeding success in obtaining the lands that were lost. Moroni requests that men be gathered to strengthen Helaman’s armies so they can maintain those lands.

Chapter 60

Helaman and his men suffer hunger, thirst, fatigue, and other afflictions. Moroni asks that food and men be sent to Helaman so he can support the parts of the country he has regained.

Chapter 62

Helaman received an army of six thousand men and provisions to assist him in preserving his part of the land. He returned to the place of his inheritance, resumed preaching the word of God, helped reestablish the church of God, and died in the thirty and fifth year of the reign of the judges.

Chapter 63

Helaman received the sacred things from Shiblon. The engravings in his possession were written and sent throughout the land, except for the parts Alma had commanded not to go forth.
