Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Mosiah, Mosiah becomes king, receives the sacred records and relics, grants Alma authority over the church, establishes judges, brings peace, and reigns until his death. In The Book of Mosiah, king Mosiah rules and serves as a seer, revelator, and prophet, establishes churches, grants Alma authority, and entrusts the records and interpreters to Alma the younger. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, Mosiah is the last king, establishes accepted laws, dies upright, and leaves no successor.

Mosiah in The Book of Mosiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Mosiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mosiah, son of king Benjamin, becomes king and ruler over the people and receives from his father the records, plates of Nephi, sword of Laban, and ball or director. He proclaims his kingship and calls the people to gather. As king of Zarahemla, he receives Alma and Alma’s people with joy, grants his people liberty, and gives Alma authority over the church, including judgment of accused members. As unbelief and dissensions increase, he asks whether the people want a king and warns them in writing about unrighteous kings. He establishes judges, brings peace throughout the land, reigns until his death at sixty-three, and is succeeded as ruler.

The Book of Mosiah / Chapter 1

Mosiah is taught by king Benjamin and is brought before him to receive charge concerning the kingdom and its sacred objects. He is commanded to proclaim throughout the land that the people should gather, and he does as his father commanded.

Chapter 24

King Mosiah receives Alma and his people with joy when they arrive in the land of Zarahemla.

Chapter 26

Mosiah reigns when the unbelieving people increase through dissensions among the brethren. He has given Alma authority over the church and declines to judge the accused, delivering them into Alma’s hands.

Chapter 29

Mosiah asks the people whether they want a king and sends them a written message explaining the dangers of unrighteous kings. He remains king for the rest of his days, establishes judges, and dies in the thirty and third year of his reign at sixty and three years old.

king Mosiah in The Book of Mosiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where king Mosiah first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mosiah is a ruler and king over the people, consecrated by king Benjamin and beginning his reign in his father’s stead at thirty. He observes the Lord’s judgments and statutes, keeps the commandments, appoints priests to teach, and tills the earth so he will not burden his people. After three years of peace, he authorizes sixteen strong men led by Ammon to inquire about people who had gone to dwell in the land of Lehi-Nephi. Mosiah is also a seer, revelator, and prophet with a gift from God to interpret engravings and translate records by means of interpreters. He gathers the people and has Zeniff’s records and Alma’s account read to them, then grants Alma authority to establish churches and ordain priests and teachers throughout Zarahemla. He prohibits persecution of church members and commands equality, humility, and labor for support. When his sons seek to preach among the Lamanites, he inquires of the Lord and grants their request. With none of his sons accepting the kingdom, he gives the records and interpreters to Alma the younger, commanding him to preserve them and keep a record of the people.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

Mosiah is consecrated by king Benjamin as ruler and king, receives charges concerning the kingdom, and begins to reign in his father's stead at thirty years of age. He walks in the ways of the Lord, keeps God's commandments, appoints priests to teach the people, and tills the earth so he will not become burdensome to them.

Chapter 7

King Mosiah had continual peace for three years and was troubled by questions about the people who had gone to the land of Lehi-Nephi. He permitted sixteen strong men, led by Ammon, to go up and inquire concerning their brethren.

Chapter 8

The king of the people in the land of Zarahemla is the man commanded to use the interpreters and possesses a high gift from God. He is a seer, revelator, and prophet who can translate ancient records.

Chapter 21

King Mosiah has a gift from God that allows him to interpret engravings.

Chapter 25

King Mosiah gathered the people, caused the records of Zeniff and the account of Alma and his brethren to be read, spoke to the people, and granted Alma power to establish churches and ordain priests and teachers.

Chapter 27

King Mosiah consulted with his priests and sent a proclamation that unbelievers should not persecute members of the church of God. He also commanded equality, humility, and labor for support throughout the churches.

Chapter 28

King Mosiah asks the Lord whether he should let his sons preach among the Lamanites, and the Lord tells him to let them go. He translates the plates of gold with the interpreters, then gives the records and interpreters to Alma the younger and commands him to preserve them and keep a record of the people.
