Abstract written by a model

A people belonging to king Benjamin, dwelling in Zarahemla and later gathered at the temple throughout the land. Though exceedingly multiplied and too numerous to number, they assemble with their families, offer sacrifices and burnt offerings according to the law of Moses, and hear king Benjamin’s teachings about serving one another and keeping God’s commandments. They collectively believe his words, receive truth through the Spirit of the Lord Omnipotent, undergo a mighty change of heart, and experience great joy. Willingly they covenant with God to do his will and keep his commandments, take upon themselves the name of Christ, and become children of Christ. They are taught to retain that name in their hearts, remain steadfast and immovable in good works, and thereby receive salvation, eternal life, and heaven through Christ rather than torment or God’s wrath. Except for little children, they enter this covenant, return to their houses by families, and have no contention for three years. They have continual peace for the remainder of king Benjamin’s days.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Mosiah / Chapter 1

The people who belonged to king Benjamin have continual peace for the remainder of his days. They are part of the people whom Mosiah is commanded to gather.

Chapter 2

The people gather throughout the land at the temple with their families, offer sacrifices, and listen to king Benjamin's words. They are taught that they are eternally indebted to God, should serve one another, and must keep the commandments.

Chapter 5

King Benjamin’s people believe his words, know their truth through the Spirit of the Lord Omnipotent, and rejoice with exceedingly great joy. They covenant to do God’s will, obey his commandments, take upon themselves the name of Christ, and remain steadfast in good works.

Chapter 6

The people enter into a covenant with God to keep his commandments and take upon themselves the name of Christ, except for little children. They return to their own houses according to their families, and there is no contention among them for three years.
