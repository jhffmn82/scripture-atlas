Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Nephi, Jacob is Lehi’s first-born in the wilderness, is consecrated and ordained, teaches Nephi’s people, explains Isaiah, teaches of Christ and God’s promises, and testifies that he has seen Christ. In The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi, Jacob is consecrated as a priest and teacher, called as a prophet, teaches God’s word, writes spiritual teachings, rebukes sin, teaches Christ and the atonement, and calls the people to repent. In The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi, Jacob testifies of Christ and the atonement, confounds Sherem, prays for God’s will, and gives the plates to Enos.

Jacob in The Second Book of Nephi

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Jacob first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jacob is Lehi’s first-born in the wilderness and Nephi’s brother. He suffers affliction and sorrow in childhood because of his brethren, knows God’s greatness, beholds Christ in youth, and is redeemed through his Redeemer’s righteousness. Consecrated by Nephi and ordained after God’s holy order, he serves God throughout his days. He speaks to Nephi’s people and beloved brethren, anxiously teaching for their souls’ welfare. He reads and explains Isaiah concerning the house of Israel, teaches of Christ, Jews, Gentiles, God’s promises, and reconciliation to God’s will, and witnesses that he has seen Christ. He receives revelation, word, and mercy from the Lord; the house of Jacob will be joined by strangers and his people brought to their place.

Jacob will be shown mercy, and the house of Jacob will be joined by strangers. Jacob’s people will be brought to their place and will rule over those who oppressed them.

Jacob in The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi

Jacob · the work’s page · read it where Jacob first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jacob, Nephi’s brother, is consecrated by Nephi as a priest and teacher and is called as a prophet to declare and diligently teach God’s word to the people of Nephi. Nephi commands him to write precious spiritual teachings on the small plates and preserve them for Christ’s sake and the people. With Joseph, Jacob bears responsibility for teaching, fearing sin if he fails to do so. After Nephi’s death, he speaks in the temple, rebuking crimes, pride, riches, and whoredoms. He comforts the pure in heart, urging faith, prayer, and attention to God’s pleasing word, while calling the people to repent and avoid reviling the Lamanites. Though engraving is difficult, he writes so later children and brethren may know their fathers. He teaches Christ, the atonement, resurrection, revelation, and the sure foundation; worships the Father in Christ’s name; and receives power through grace. He prophesies, calls his brethren to cleave unto God, warns against hardened hearts and rejection of words concerning Christ, and bids farewell before the bar of God.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi / Chapter 1

Jacob receives commandments from Nephi to write precious spiritual teachings on the small plates and preserve them for his seed. He and Joseph are consecrated priests and teachers, and Jacob teaches the people of Nephi, urging them to come unto Christ and fulfill God’s commandments.

Chapter 2

Jacob speaks to the people of Nephi in the temple after Nephi's death. He declares the word of God, admonishes the people for their crimes, and testifies concerning their pride, riches, and whoredoms.

Chapter 3

Jacob speaks to those who are pure in heart and urges them to look unto God, pray with exceeding faith, and receive the pleasing word of God. He warns the people of Nephi to repent, remember their own filthiness, and avoid reviling the Lamanites.

Chapter 4

Jacob says he has ministered to his people and writes on plates so their children and brethren may gain knowledge of their fathers. He teaches about Christ, the atonement, resurrection, revelations, and the sure foundation, and he is led by the Spirit to prophesy concerning the Jews.

Chapter 6

The speaker prophesies concerning the house of Israel and urges my brethren to repent, cleave unto God, and not harden their hearts. The speaker warns of judgment for rejecting the words concerning Christ and bids farewell until meeting before the bar of God.

Jacob, a prophet among the Nephi people and father of Enos, has faith in Christ through angels, God’s voice, revelation, and the Holy Ghost. He testifies of Christ and the necessary atonement, confounds Sherem, prays for God’s will, and gives the plates to Enos.
