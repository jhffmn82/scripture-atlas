Alma's people in The Book of Mosiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Alma's people first appears

Abstract written by a model

Alma’s people, covenant believers led by Alma, have great faith and patience amid bondage, afflictions, persecution, tasks, task-masters, and guards. Warned of the king’s army, they depart with tents and families, endure strengthened burdens, travel through the wilderness, give thanks, and arrive in Zarahemla.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 18

The people of the Lord were warned of the coming of the king’s army. They took their tents and families and departed into the wilderness.

Chapter 24

Alma’s people suffer persecution, tasks, task-masters, and guards under Amulon. They pour out their hearts to God, bear their burdens with ease after God strengthens them, depart into the wilderness, give thanks, and arrive in the land of Zarahemla.

Alma's brethren in The Book of Mosiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where Alma's brethren first appears

Abstract written by a model

Alma's brethren are the people who fled with Alma into the wilderness after forming a church of God. Taught and led by Alma, they settle in the land of Helam, prosper with flocks and grain, and build its city. Captured by the Lamanites and placed under guards, they are ultimately delivered from bondage by the power of God and become free in liberty.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 21

The people who went with Alma flee into the wilderness with him after forming a church of God. Ammon and his brethren mourn their departure, and Limhi’s people desire to become like them.

Chapter 23

The people of Alma flee from king Noah, settle in a beautiful land, and build the land and city of Helam. They prosper, are captured by the Lamanites, and are placed under guards.

Chapter 25

Alma's brethren were delivered from the hands of the Lamanites and from bondage, and their account and afflictions were read to the people.
