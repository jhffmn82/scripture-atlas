The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record

Abstract written by a model

These chapters present Mormon’s record of the Nephites’ final decline, destruction, and continuing witness of Jesus Christ. As a youth, Mormon is instructed by Ammaron to observe events and later retrieve the plates of Nephi at hill Shim. Though Mormon is visited by the Lord and tastes the goodness of Jesus, he is forbidden to preach because widespread wickedness, unbelief, and hardened hearts dominate the land. He becomes a military leader as the Nephites repeatedly fight the Lamanites, lose cities and lands, make temporary treaties, and persist in sorrow without true repentance. After the Nephites vow vengeance, Mormon refuses to lead them, warning that all people will stand before the judgment-seat of Christ. War intensifies around Desolation and Teancum. The Lamanites defeat the Nephites, take prisoners, and sacrifice Nephite women and children to idol gods. Mormon retrieves the hidden records, later returns to command the Nephite armies, and records their destruction as the Lamanites overwhelm them. He explains that the record is intended for the remnant, the Gentiles, Jews, and the house of Israel, warning against unbelief, idolatry, and wickedness while affirming God’s covenants and the prayers of the righteous. At Cumorah, nearly all remaining Nephites are killed. Mormon hides the sacred records, gives a few plates to Moroni, and mourns the dead, teaching that all will become immortal and face Christ in judgment. He addresses the remnant of the house of Israel, urging repentance, peace, belief in Jesus Christ, and baptism. Moroni completes Mormon’s record, hides it in the earth, and prophesies its future coming forth amid unbelief, pride, and exploitation. The final message affirms that God is unchanging and performs miracles, calls disciples to preach, promises salvation to believers, acknowledges the record’s imperfections, and prays for restoration and fulfillment of God’s covenant with the house of Israel.

The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record / Chapter 1

Mormon records what he has seen and heard in the Book of Mormon record. Ammaron instructs Mormon to remember what he observes, retrieve the plates of Nephi at the hill Shim when he is twenty-four, and engrave his observations on them. The Nephites and Lamanites fight near Zarahemla, followed by about four years of peace, but wickedness and unbelief spread throughout the land. Mormon is visited by the Lord at age fifteen and tastes the goodness of Jesus, but he is forbidden to preach to the people.

Chapter 2

Mormon leads the Nephites in renewed wars against the Lamanites as the Nephites are repeatedly driven from their lands and cities. The land is filled with robbers, Lamanites, blood, carnage, wickedness, and abominations, while the Nephites’ sorrow does not result in true repentance. Mormon records the people’s wickedness on the plates of Nephi and mourns their spiritual decline. After further battles, the Nephites and their enemies make a treaty dividing the lands of inheritance between them.

Chapter 3

The Lamanites resumed battle against the Nephites after ten years, but the Nephites defeated them in two successive years. The Lord called the Nephites to repent, be baptized, and rebuild his church, but they hardened their hearts. Mormon refused to continue leading them because of their wickedness, after the Nephites vowed vengeance against their enemies. Mormon testified that all people will stand before the judgment-seat of Christ and urged them to believe the gospel of Jesus Christ and repent.

Chapter 4

The Nephites and Lamanites engage in repeated battles over the land Desolation, city Desolation, and city Teancum. The Lamanites repeatedly defeat the Nephites, take prisoners, and sacrifice Nephite women and children to idol gods, while the Nephites persist in wickedness and sometimes boast of their strength. Mormon observes the destruction and retrieves the records that Ammaron had hidden at hill Shim. The unit describes unprecedented wickedness, bloodshed, and hardened hearts among the people.

Chapter 5

Mormon returns to assist the Nephites and commands their armies, but the Lamanites eventually overwhelm and destroy the Nephites despite their resistance. Mormon writes an abridgment of the bloodshed and explains that hidden things will be revealed to the remnant of the people and to the Gentiles. The record concerns the scattering and future restoration of the house of Israel, the belief of the Jews and their seed in Jesus, and warnings to the Gentiles to repent. The chapter teaches that unbelief, idolatry, and wickedness bring destruction, while God remembers covenants and the prayers of the righteous.

Chapter 6

Mormon finishes his record concerning the destruction of the Nephites and leads the remaining people to the land of Cumorah for a final battle against the Lamanites. The Lamanites destroy nearly all the Nephites, leaving Mormon, Moroni, twenty and four survivors, and a few others who escaped or deserted. Mormon hides the sacred records in the hill Cumorah, gives a few plates to Moroni, and mourns the fallen people. He teaches that the dead will become immortal and stand before the judgment-seat of Christ, and that repentance before the destruction could have prevented their fall.

Chapter 7

The speaker addresses the remnant of this people, identified as a remnant of the house of Israel and the seed of Jacob. They are told to repent, lay down their weapons of war, learn of their fathers, believe in Jesus Christ, and be baptized. Jesus Christ is described as the Son of God who was slain, rose again, overcame the grave, and brings about the resurrection of the dead and the redemption of the world. Those who believe in Christ and are baptized with water, fire, and the Holy Ghost will fare well in the day of judgment.

Chapter 8

Moroni finishes the record of Mormon after the Nephites are destroyed and hides the records in the earth. He describes the Lamanites, the land’s continual war, murder, and bloodshed, and the disappearance of the disciples of Jesus. Moroni prophesies that the record will come forth by the power of God amid widespread unbelief, wickedness, pride, pollution, and churches built for gain. He warns that the Lord will remember his covenant, fulfill his promises, and bring vengeance upon those who oppress the saints and the needy.

Chapter 9

This unit concerns unbelievers, those who deny revelations, and all people who will stand before Jesus Christ for judgment. It teaches that God is unchanging and remains a God of miracles, while miracles cease among people because of unbelief and departure from the right way. Jesus Christ’s disciples are commanded to preach the gospel, and believers are promised signs, salvation, and confirmation of Christ’s words. The record also explains its language, imperfections, purpose, and prayers for the restoration of the brethren and the fulfillment of God’s covenant with the house of Israel.
