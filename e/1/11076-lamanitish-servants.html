Lamanitish servants

group · 1 work · 7 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: his servants, king Lamoni’s servants, my brethren, other servants, servants, servants of Lamoni, servants of the king, the king and his servants, the king’s servants, they, those men, those men who had stood at the waters of Sebus, thy servants

Abstract written by a model

Lamanitish servants serve king Lamoni as flock watchers. With Ammon, they drive the flocks to Sebus, fear death when they scatter, and follow his instructions to recover them. They prepare horses for the king’s journey and testify to Lamoni of Ammon’s defense, preservation of their lives, strength, and the power of God. After falling to the earth when the Lord is called upon, their hearts change and they no longer desire evil.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 17

The Lamanitish servants work with Ammon to drive Lamoni’s flocks to the water of Sebus. They fear the king will slay them after the flocks are scattered, but they follow Ammon’s instructions and help encircle and recover the flocks.

Chapter 18

King Lamoni’s servants testify about Ammon’s defense of the flocks and their own lives. They say that Ammon cannot be slain by the king’s enemies and that his strength and expertness make him a friend to the king.

Chapter 19

The servants testify to the king about Ammon's power, fall to the earth after calling on the name of the Lord, and later declare that their hearts had been changed and that they no longer desired to do evil.
