Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Omni, the land of Nephi is the place from which Mosiah is warned to flee and to which most returning travelers are slain before reaching the land of Zarahemla. In The Book of Mosiah, the land of Nephi is the land where Zeniff’s people settle and prosper, where Limhi’s people dwell under Lamanite oppression, and where Ammon and others go to preach. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, the land of Nephi is a place nearly surrounded by water, containing the city of Nephi and serving as a destination for preaching, journeys, and military movements.

land of Nephi in The Book of Mosiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where land of Nephi first appears

Abstract written by a model

The land of Nephi is a land containing the city of Nephi and associated with the fathers’ first inheritance. Zeniff’s people journey there, settle it, inherit it for twenty-two years, prosper, and have continual peace, though the Lamanites attack them; Zeniff’s people drive the attackers out and return to the land. Later, Limhi’s people dwell there under Lamanite oppression, and captured people are carried back there before returning after learning their wives and children were not slain. Searchers return there after failing to find Zarahemla and after finding a destroyed, peopled land. Ammon and three brethren go down there, meet the king’s guard, and are imprisoned. The Lamanites and Amulon seek it, and the sons of Mosiah seek permission to go there to preach to the Lamanites.

land of Nephi in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where land of Nephi first appears

Abstract written by a model

The land of Nephi is a place nearly surrounded by water with Zarahemla, lying south of Nephite lands and running from the east sea to the west. It borders Zarahemla, contains wilderness where Lamanites live, and includes the city of Nephi, its chief city and site of the king’s palace. Owned by the Lamanites as possessions, it hosts a feast and is a destination for journeys, preaching, and military movements. Aaron, Ammon, and their brethren go there to teach in houses, streets, hills, temples, and synagogues, laboring and bringing forth much fruit amid journeyings and sufferings. After war, Lamanites return there, while Anti-Nephi-Lehi departs. Amalickiah travels there, gains command, and marches to its chief city; Moroni later names it Ammoron’s possession and demands his armies return there.
