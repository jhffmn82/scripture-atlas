Abstract written by a model

Monks are houseless, well-disciplined practitioners whose creed centers on compassion, forbearance, chastity, abstaining from sin, and control of their senses. They walk carefully, speak blamelessly and concisely, avoid faults in begging, and inspect and handle their outfit, almsbowl, mouth-cloth, broom, and cloth with care. They practise Samitis, Guptis, tenfold correct behaviour, study, meditation, confession, Kâyôtsarga, and prescribed duties through day and night. They endure pleasant and unpleasant conditions, praise and blame, dangers, and bodily and mental difficulties without fear or improper replies. Having abandoned household life, property, egoism, passions, attachments, and Âsravas, they follow discipline, the Law, renunciation, control, and austerities. Through conduct and discipline they destroy Karman, obtain perfection, attain final liberation, and reach eternal beatitude.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Twenty-First Lecture.

Monks should endure pleasant and unpleasant conditions, avoid improper replies, remain indifferent to praise and blame, and bear dangers caused by gods, men, or animals without fear. They should control their senses, practise compassion and chastity, and use beds that are not prepared or strewn for their sake.

Twenty-Fourth Lecture.

The well-disciplined monk walks purely with regard to cause, time, road, and effort, and he avoids the eight faults of speech. He carefully practices the Samitis and Guptis in walking, begging, handling articles, disposing of waste, and controlling mind, speech, and body.

Twenty-Sixth Lecture.

Monks must practise the tenfold correct behaviour and fulfil prescribed duties throughout the day and night. They study, meditate, beg when permitted, inspect their possessions, confess transgressions, and perform Kâyôtsarga.

Twenty-Ninth Lecture.

Houseless monks abandon household life, practise discipline and the Law, and endure bodily and mental difficulties. Through conduct, renunciation, control, and austerities, they can destroy Karman and attain final liberation.

Thirty-Fifth Lecture.

The Houseless Monk follows the road shown by the wise ones, giving up house life and renouncing attachments. He lives without property, egoism, passions, and Âsravas, and reaches eternal beatitude.
