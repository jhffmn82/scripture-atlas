Abstract written by a model

The Book of Helaman recounts Nephite political contention, war, spiritual decline, prophetic warnings, and the conversion and growing righteousness of many Lamanites before the coming of Christ. It begins after Pahoran’s death, when disputes over the judgment-seat lead to Pahoran’s murder by Kishkumen, a Lamanite invasion under Coriantumr, and Nephite recovery under Moronihah and Lehi. Helaman becomes chief judge, escapes Kishkumen’s plot through his servant, and confronts the rise of Gadianton’s secret band. Amid migrations, building, prosperity, and church growth, pride, persecution, secret combinations, and warfare repeatedly weaken the Nephites. After Helaman dies, his son Nephi succeeds him. Nephi and his brother Lehi leave political office and preach repentance among Nephites and Lamanites. Their imprisonment is followed by divine manifestations that lead thousands of Lamanites to repent, receive baptism and the Holy Spirit, see angels, abandon weapons and hatred, and yield lands to the Nephites. As the Lamanites become firm in faith, many Nephites become proud, wealthy, corrupt, and controlled by Gadianton robbers. Nephi condemns corrupt judges and warns that the Nephites will be destroyed unless they repent. He reveals the murders of Seezoram and Seantum’s guilt, receives power from God to declare judgments, and calls for famine after the people reject him. Famine brings humility and repentance; Nephi then prays for rain, peace returns, and the robbers are eliminated. Yet prosperity again produces forgetfulness, pride, dissent, and renewed robber bands. The record emphasizes that prosperity often leads people to forget the Lord, while repentance brings mercy. Samuel, a Lamanite, warns Zarahemla from its walls of destruction, famine, pestilence, and lost treasures unless the people repent and believe in Jesus Christ. He prophesies Christ’s birth, death, resurrection, and accompanying signs, including a new star, darkness, earthquakes, and opened graves. Though believers seek Nephi and are baptized, many reject Samuel, harden their hearts despite signs and miracles, and increasingly trust their own strength and wisdom.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

17 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Helaman

The Book of Helaman concerns the Nephites, their wars, contentions, dissensions, and the prophecies of many holy prophets before the coming of Christ. It records the conversion of many of the Lamanites and the righteousness of the Lamanites. It also records the wickedness and abominations of the Nephites down to the coming of Christ.

Chapter 1

After Pahoran’s death, Pahoran, Paanchi, and Pacumeni contend over the judgment-seat, causing divisions among the people of Nephi. Pahoran is appointed chief judge, but Paanchi is condemned to death and Kishkumen murders Pahoran; Pacumeni then becomes chief judge before being killed by Coriantumr. Coriantumr leads a Lamanite invasion, captures the city of Zarahemla, and advances through the land, but Moronihah and Lehi surround the Lamanites, kill Coriantumr, and force their surrender. Moronihah retakes Zarahemla and permits the captured Lamanites to leave the land in peace.

Chapter 2

Helaman, the son of Helaman, was appointed by the voice of the people to fill the judgment-seat after contention arose over who should hold it. Kishkumen, supported by his band and led by Gadianton, planned to murder Helaman as part of their secret work to murder, rob, and gain power. Helaman's servant learned of the plan through disguise, led Kishkumen toward the judgment-seat, stabbed him to death, and reported what happened to Helaman. Gadianton's band fled into the wilderness, and Gadianton was later identified as causing the overthrow and near-destruction of the people of Nephi.

Chapter 3

The people of Nephi experience contention, dissensions, wars, migration, and settlement throughout the land northward, followed by periods of peace, prosperity, and joy. The people build cities, temples, synagogues, and sanctuaries, while many Lamanites and people of Ammon also enter the land northward. The church of God grows through baptism unto repentance, but pride, persecution, and secret combinations arise among the people. Helaman governs righteously, dies in the fifty-third year of the reign of the judges, and is succeeded by his son Nephi.

Chapter 4

The Nephites suffered bloodshed, lost most of their lands to the Lamanites and dissenters from the Nephites, and later regained half their possessions under Moronihah after repenting. Their wickedness included pride, oppression, violence, disbelief, and violations of the laws of Mosiah and commandments of God, causing the Spirit of the Lord to withdraw and the Lord to cease preserving them by miraculous and matchless power. Nephi and Lehi preached and prophesied to the people of the Nephites, while Moronihah led the armies of Moronihah in defending and recovering Nephite lands. The Nephites recognized that their weakness, slaughter, loss, and fear resulted from their iniquity and failure to cleave unto the Lord their God.

Chapter 5

Nephi gives up the judgment-seat to Cezoram and, with Lehi, preaches the word of God after remembering Helaman’s teachings about commandments, repentance, Jesus Christ, and the rock of the Redeemer. Nephi and Lehi preach among the Nephites and Lamanites, leading many dissenters and thousands of Lamanites to confess their sins and receive baptism unto repentance. After Nephi and Lehi are imprisoned, God manifests power through fire, earthquakes, darkness, and a voice, and the Lamanites repent, receive the Holy Spirit of God, and see angels of God. About three hundred people testify of these things, and many Lamanites lay down their weapons of war, hatred, and the traditions of their fathers, yielding their lands to the Nephites.

Chapter 6

The Lamanites become largely righteous through firmness in the faith, while many Nephites grow hardened, wicked, and attached to riches. The people of the church rejoice in the Lamanites’ conversion, and Lamanites preach faith and repentance among the Nephites. Peace and prosperity end when Gadianton’s band spreads secret murders, robberies, oaths, and covenants among the Nephites, leading them to control the government and oppress the poor and humble. The Spirit of the Lord withdraws from the Nephites but is poured out upon the Lamanites, who destroy the robbers among them.

Chapter 7

Nephi, the son of Helaman, returned to the land of Zarahemla after the people in the land northward rejected his preaching and prophecies. He mourned the wickedness of the Nephites, including the Gadianton robbers’ control of judgment-seats and government. Nephi called the people to repentance, warning that their cities, lands, and strength would be taken away if they did not repent. He testified that the Lord God had revealed these things to him and that the Nephites would be destroyed unless they repented.

Chapter 8

Nephi condemns the judges and their corrupt law, secret works of darkness, murders, fornication, and wickedness, while some of the people defend him as a prophet. He teaches that Moses, Abraham, and the holy prophets testified of the Messiah, the coming of Christ, and redemption. Nephi warns that the judgments will come upon the people unless they repent, and reveals that their judge has been murdered by his brother, who seeks the judgment-seat. The judges and the murderer belong to the secret band of Gadianton, whose author is Gadianton and the evil one.

Chapter 9

Nephi’s prophecy that the chief judge would be dead is confirmed when five men find Seezoram murdered at the judgment-seat. The people mistake the five men for the murderers and imprison them, but the judges later release them after hearing their account. Nephi directs the people to question Seantum, Seezoram’s brother, and his guilt is exposed when he confesses to the murder. Some Nephites believe Nephi is a prophet, while others call him a god because he knows hidden things.

Chapter 10

Nephi is left alone after a division among the people and ponders the wickedness of the people of the Nephites. God tells Nephi that he has served faithfully and grants him power over the people, including power connected with sealing, loosening, and declaring judgments. Nephi declares that the people must repent or be smitten unto destruction, but they harden their hearts, revile him, and try to imprison him. The Spirit conveys Nephi away, and afterward contentions arise until the people begin slaying one another with the sword.

Chapter 11

The people of Nephi suffer wars, famine, and destruction caused by the secret band of robbers and their own wickedness. Nephi prays for famine and later prays for rain after the people repent, humble themselves, and eliminate the band of Gadianton. The Lord ends the famine, and the people prosper, rejoice, and enjoy peace as the church spreads. Later, dissenters and Lamanite descendants form a powerful band of robbers, bringing destruction and fear, while the people again grow forgetful, proud, and wicked.

Chapter 12

The unit concerns the children of men and their tendency to forget the Lord their God during prosperity, becoming proud, wicked, and slow to do good. The Lord their God blesses and prospers the people, chastens them with afflictions, and commands the earth, waters, mountains, treasures, and people according to his word. Repentance and hearkening to the Lord are declared so that men might be saved, while those who do evil face endless misery and everlasting damnation. In the great and last day, some will be cast off from the presence of the Lord, while those who have done good shall have everlasting life.

Chapter 13

Samuel, a Lamanite, returns to Zarahemla at the Lord’s command and warns the Nephites to repent and have faith on the Lord Jesus Christ. He prophesies that the sword of justice, destruction, famine, pestilence, and the curse of the land will come upon the Nephites because of their wickedness, pride, iniquity, and rejection of the prophets. The Lord declares that the righteous preserve Zarahemla and that repentance can spare those who turn to him. Samuel also teaches that riches and hidden treasures will become slippery and be lost when the people set their hearts upon them.

Chapter 14

Samuel, the Lamanite, prophesies of the coming, birth, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ and gives signs that will accompany these events. He calls the people to repent and teaches that believers may receive everlasting life through the name and merits of Jesus Christ. He describes darkness, a new star, thunderings and lightnings, earthquakes, tempests, broken rocks, changed landscapes, opened graves, and other signs. He teaches that people are free to choose good or evil, life or death, and that the unrepentant bring condemnation and a second spiritual death upon themselves.

Chapter 15

The Lord warns the people of Nephi that unless they repent, they will be utterly destroyed despite the signs and wonders and mighty works shown to them. The Lamanites receive salvation through the preaching of the Nephites, and many follow the path of duty, keep the commandments, and strive to bring others to knowledge of the truth. Those Lamanites who gain knowledge of the truth, believe the holy scriptures, and repent experience a change of heart, become firm and steadfast in faith, and bury their weapons of war. The Lord promises mercy and the restoration of the Lamanites to knowledge of the truth, while declaring that the people of Nephi will be destroyed if they remain in unbelief and do not repent.

Chapter 16

Samuel, the Lamanite, preached from the walls, and those who believed his words sought Nephi, confessed their sins, and were baptized. Unbelievers attacked Samuel with stones and arrows, but the Spirit of the Lord protected him, and he fled to his own country. During the ninetieth year of the reign of the judges, signs, wonders, miracles, and angels appeared as the words of the prophets began to be fulfilled, but most of the people hardened their hearts. Satan spread rumors and contentions, and the people increasingly depended on their own strength and wisdom.
