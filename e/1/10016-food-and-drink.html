Abstract written by a model

destroys living beings [beings living in water, corn, or in earth and wood] Food and drink may contain or destroy beings living in water, corn, earth, and wood. A monk should neither cook food and drink nor cause another to cook them.

  • Sramana does not cook food and drink (nor cause another to cook; out of compassion for living beings)
    • “The same holds good with the cooking of food and drink, or with one's causing them to be cooked. Out of compassion for living beings one should not cook nor cause another to cook. (10)”Uttaradhyayana Sutra · Thirty-Fifth Lecture.
  • Sramana eats food and drink (for sustenance of life, not for pleasant taste)
    • “A great sage should not eat for the sake of the pleasant taste (of the food) but for the sustenance of life, being not dainty nor eager for good fare, restraining his tongue, and being without cupidity. (17)”Uttaradhyayana Sutra · Thirty-Fifth Lecture.
  • Sramana rejects food and drink (when the time of death arrives)
    • “Rejecting food when the time of his death arrives, and leaving the human body, he becomes his own master, and is liberated from misery. (20)”Uttaradhyayana Sutra · Thirty-Fifth Lecture.
