king Benjamin

person · 3 works · 37 quoted facts · 14 connections
Also called: Benjamin, his son, king, my father Benjamin, this king Benjamin, your king

Abstract written by a model

In The Book of Omni, Benjamin is Mosiah’s son who reigns in his stead and receives the plates from Amaleki. In The Words of Mormon, king Benjamin receives and preserves the plates, reigns righteously, fights the Lamanites with the sword of Laban, and establishes peace with prophetic help. In The Book of Mosiah, king Benjamin is a king, teacher, servant of God, father, and dying covenant teacher who prepares Mosiah to rule, teaches the people, records their covenant, consecrates Mosiah as king, and dies.

king Benjamin in The Book of Mosiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where king Benjamin first appears

Abstract written by a model

King Benjamin is a king, teacher, servant of God, and father of Mosiah, Helorum, and Helaman. During continual peace, he teaches his sons their fathers’ language, prophecies, records, commandments, and the Lord’s dealings. Old and subject to infirmities, he prepares Mosiah to rule by giving him charge over the kingdom and its records, including the sword of Laban and the ball or director. He gathers his people and, serving them with all his might, mind, and strength, teaches service, obedience, repentance, accountability, God’s goodness and power, salvation through the atonement, faith, care for the poor, and steadfast good works. He says he can no longer be their teacher or king and seeks to be blameless before God concerning them. After learning that the people believe his words, he accepts their covenant, teaches them to become children of Christ and take upon themselves the name of Christ, and records those who covenant with God. He consecrates Mosiah as ruler and king, appoints priests to teach the people, dismisses the multitude, lives three more years, and dies. His last words are also taught to the people of king Limhi through Ammon.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

The Book of Mosiah / Chapter 1

King Benjamin has continual peace for the remainder of his days and teaches his sons concerning their fathers, the records, the commandments of God, and the Lord’s dealings. He grows old, gives Mosiah charge concerning the kingdom and its records, and plans to proclaim Mosiah as king and ruler.

Chapter 2

King Benjamin gathers the people so he can speak to them, teaches them about service, obedience, repentance, and accountability before God, and announces that he can no longer be their teacher or king. He declares that Mosiah is a king and ruler over the people and seeks to be blameless before God concerning them.

Chapter 4

King Benjamin teaches the people about the goodness and power of God, salvation through the atonement, repentance, faith, and obedience. He commands them to remember their dependence on God, care for the poor, and continue in faith concerning the coming of the Lord.

Chapter 5

King Benjamin sends among his people to learn whether they believe his words. He accepts their covenant as righteous and teaches them about becoming children of Christ, taking upon themselves the name of Christ, and remaining steadfast in good works.

Chapter 6

King Benjamin finishes speaking to the people, takes the names of those who entered into a covenant with God, consecrates Mosiah as ruler and king, gives him charges concerning the kingdom, appoints priests, and dismisses the multitude. He lives three years and then dies.
