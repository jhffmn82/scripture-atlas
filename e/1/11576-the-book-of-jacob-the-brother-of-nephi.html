The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

The Book of Jacob records Jacob’s stewardship of the small plates, which Nephi commanded be used to preserve sacred teaching. Jacob and his brother Joseph are consecrated priests and teachers among the people of Nephi. After Nephi dies and appoints a successor, the people continue calling their kings Nephi. Under the second king, many become proud, seek riches, and practice wickedness. Jacob speaks in the temple, condemning costly apparel, persecution of the poor, misuse of wealth, whoredoms, and plural wives. He teaches that they must have one wife and warns that their conduct has wounded wives and children. He urges the pure in heart to pray in faith and trust God, while warning the unrepentant that their land will be cursed and the Lamanites will scourge them. He says the Lamanites are more righteous concerning marriage and forbids reviling them and committing sin. Jacob explains that the plates preserve knowledge of Christ for later generations. He testifies that Christ’s atonement, resurrection, and glory were known through prophets and fathers before Christ’s coming. He warns against rejecting revelation and prophesies that the Jews will reject the sure foundation, though the stone will become the head of the corner. He includes Zenos’s allegory of the olive-tree: the Lord of the vineyard labors to preserve, nourish, prune, and graft the trees until good fruit is restored; corrupt branches are cast away, and eventually the vineyard is burned. Jacob applies this to the house of Israel, urging repentance, faithfulness to prophets and Christ, entrance through the strait gate, and perseverance toward eternal life. Finally, Sherem denies Christ and challenges Jacob. Jacob confounds him through scripture and the Lord’s power. Sherem seeks a sign, is struck down, confesses that the devil deceived him, and dies. The people return to peace and the love of God, search the scriptures, defend themselves from the Lamanites, and Jacob gives the plates to Enos.

The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi / Chapter 1

This unit concerns Jacob’s record of Nephi’s commandments about the small plates and the spiritual teachings to be preserved on them. Jacob and Joseph serve as consecrated priests and teachers, laboring to persuade the people of Nephi to come unto Christ and obey God. Nephi dies after appointing a successor, and the people continue naming their kings after him. Under the second king, the people begin to grow hard in their hearts, pursue wealth, and practice wickedness, including desiring many wives and concubines.

Chapter 2

Jacob speaks to the people of Nephi in the temple after Nephi's death, declaring the word of God and warning them about their sins. He condemns their pride, costly apparel, persecution of poorer brethren, and misuse of riches. He also declares that whoredoms and plural wives are abominable, and that the people must have one wife and keep the commandments. Jacob says their wickedness has wounded their wives and children and brought condemnation upon them.

Chapter 3

Jacob speaks to the people of Nephi, urging the pure in heart to look to God, pray with faith, and receive comfort and justice. He warns the filthy that unless they repent, the land of their inheritance will be cursed, the Lamanites will scourge them, and the righteous will be led away. Jacob teaches that the Lamanites are more righteous in observing the commandment concerning marriage and warns the people of Nephi against reviling them, fornication, lasciviousness, and every kind of sin. He records that these plates are called the plates of Jacob and were made by the hand of Nephi.

Chapter 4

Jacob explains that he and his people engrave their words on plates so their children and brethren may learn about their fathers and their knowledge of Christ. He teaches that Christ, his atonement, resurrection, and glory were known and hoped for by Jacob, the holy prophets, and their fathers before Christ’s coming. Jacob warns against despising revelations or counseling God, and he prophesies that the Jews will reject the sure foundation but that the stone will become the head of the corner.

Chapter 5

The Lord of the vineyard tends a tame olive-tree and grafts branches from a wild olive-tree into it to preserve its roots and fruit. The natural branches are planted in different parts of the vineyard, but over time the trees bring forth both good and evil fruit, and the vineyard becomes corrupt. The Lord of the vineyard and his servants prune, nourish, graft, and labor with their might until the natural fruit is restored and the bad branches are cast away. At the end, the Lord of the vineyard says that the vineyard will eventually be burned when the season and the end come.

Chapter 6

The speaker prophesies that Zenos’s words concerning the house of Israel, likened to a tame olive-tree, will come to pass. The Lord’s servants will again labor in the vineyard to nourish and prune it, after which the end will soon come, with the diligent blessed and the cast out cursed. The speaker urges my brethren to repent, cleave unto God, and not harden their hearts, warning that rejecting the prophets, Christ, and the good word of Christ brings shame before God and condemnation. The speaker calls them to enter the strait gate and continue in the way which is narrow until they obtain eternal life.

Chapter 7

Sherem preaches among the people of Nephi that there is no Christ and seeks to overthrow the doctrine of Christ, but Jacob confounds him through the power of the Lord and testimony of the scriptures. Sherem asks for a sign, is struck by the power of the Lord, and later confesses before the people that he was deceived by the devil and that Christ, the power of the Holy Ghost, and the ministering of angels are real. After Sherem dies, the people witness these events, are overcome by the power of God, and peace and the love of God are restored among them. The people of Nephi search the scriptures, defend themselves against the Lamanites, and Jacob gives the plates to Enos as he concludes his record.
