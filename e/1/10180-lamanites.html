Abstract written by a model

In The Second Book of Nephi, the Lamanites reject the Lord’s words, become separated and cursed, and are made a scourge to Nephi’s seed. In The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi, the Lamanites seek to destroy the Nephites but observe marriage commandments and are promised future blessings. In The Book of Enos, the Lamanites remain wild, ferocious enemies, and Enos prays for their eventual salvation through the Nephite records. In The Book of Jarom, the Lamanites are numerous, murderous, and repeatedly attack the Nephites without prospering. In The Book of Omni, the Lamanites are enemies of the Nephites who are defeated and driven from Zarahemla. In The Words of Mormon, the Lamanites attack king Benjamin’s people but are defeated and driven from their lands. In The Book of Mosiah, the Lamanites fight and oppress the Nephites, though some show compassion and later become the people preached to by Mosiah’s sons. In The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, the Lamanites hold captured cities and lands, retreat into Mulek, and are surrounded, defeated, and taken prisoner.

In The Book of Helaman, the Lamanites conquer and invade Nephite lands, then many convert, establish the church, and live righteously. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, the Lamanites experience decline and conversion, with some joining the Nephites against the robbers. In The Third Book of Nephi, the Son of Nephi, Who Was the Son of Helaman, the Lamanites who had been spared receive Christ’s manifestations, blessings, and ministry. In The Fourth Book of Nephi, Who Is the Son of Nephi, One of the Disciples of Jesus Christ, the Lamanites are converted and live in peace before some become proud, wicked, and reject the gospel. In The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record, the Lamanites war against, conquer, and ultimately destroy the Nephites before continuing their own wars. In The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record, the armies of the Lamanites attack and drive back the Nephites, but are defeated before continuing the conflict that leaves nearly all Nephites dead. In The Book of Moroni, the Lamanites wage fierce wars, imprison and abuse Nephite women and children, and receive Moroni’s exhortation to come unto Christ.

The Lamanites are a people and brethren of the people of Nephi, named by Jacob for those who seek to destroy the people of Nephi rather than by former family names. Abstract written by a model

The Lamanites are a people descended from Laman, Lemuel, the sons of Ishmael, and Ishmaelitish women. They are distinguished by shorn heads, skins girded about their loins, armor, and weapons including bows, arrows, stones, and slings; their dark skins are associated with a mark set upon their fathers. They bear a curse tied to transgression and rebellion, are cut off from the presence of the Lord, and inherit traditions that foster hatred, sin, violence, and opposition to the Nephites, though promises of salvation and future belief remain open to them. They inhabit the land of Nephi and surrounding wilderness, including areas divided from Nephite lands by a narrow wilderness. They repeatedly war against, kill, rob, plunder, capture, and take prisoners from the Nephites, while also suffering defeats, scattering, captivity, and loss of cities. Their armies are numerous and at times led by Zerahemnah, Amalickiah, Ammoron, and Zoramite chief captains. They capture and lose cities including Manti, Zeezrom, Cumeni, Antiparah, and Nephihah, attempt encirclements and poisoning, fortify cities, and eventually flee back toward the land of Nephi after reverses. A central development is conversion. Through Ammon, his brethren, records, preaching, and miracles, many Lamanites receive knowledge of God, repent, are baptized, become righteous, lay down or bury weapons, join the people of Anti-Nephi-Lehi or Ammon, and covenant for peace. Some refuse conversion or are stirred to anger by Amalekites, Amulonites, Zoramites, and dissenters, renewing war. Thus their history unfolds through inherited hostility and continual conflict, alongside repeated opportunities for repentance, faith, peace, and preservation for future generations.

The Lamanites bury their dead and the dead Nephites, then labor under Moroni’s orders to build a ditch, breastwork, and wall around the land Bountiful. Their prisoners are guarded within the fortification they build.

Chapter 54

The Lamanites had taken many women and children, and they held prisoners whom Moroni sought to obtain through an exchange. Ammoron identified himself as their king and said that they would continue the war against the Nephites.

Chapter 55

The Lamanites guarding the Nephites at city Gid drink the wine offered by Laman and become drunk and fall into a deep sleep. They surrender their weapons when surrounded, later attempt encirclements and the use of wine against the Nephites, and continue fortifying city Morianton.

Chapter 56

The Lamanites possess Manti, Zeezrom, Cumeni, and Antiparah after shedding the blood of many Nephite men. Their strongest army pursues Helaman’s force, is surrounded, and surrenders its weapons of war and its members as prisoners of war.

Chapter 57

The Lamanites abandon Antiparah, defend Cumeni, attempt repeated night attacks, and later receive reinforcements. They lose Cumeni and are driven back to Manti after suffering defeat.

Chapter 58

The Lamanites were more numerous, received strength and provisions, and repeatedly attempted to destroy the Nephites. Their army pursued the retreating Nephites into the wilderness, lost Manti when Gid and Teomner attacked its guards, and later fled toward the land of Nephi after finding the Nephites prepared.

Chapter 59

The Lamanites attack the people of Nephihah and begin killing them with an exceedingly great slaughter. Their armies are strengthened by people who had fled from Manti and the surrounding land, and they take the city of Nephihah.

Chapter 60

The Lamanites come upon the people, take possession of their lands, murder their people, and carry women and children away captive. Their hatred is connected to the tradition of their fathers and has been increased by dissenters.

Chapter 61

The Lamanites are allied with the king appointed by the rebels. They are expected by that alliance to conquer the remainder of the land and place the rebel king over the people.

Chapter 62

The Lamanites were defeated in several encounters, lost the city of Nephihah, and fled into the land of Moroni before being driven from the land. Many prisoners entered a covenant not to fight the Nephites and joined the people of Ammon.

Chapter 63

The Lamanites were stirred up by dissenters and came down with a numerous army against Moronihah’s army. They were beaten, driven back to their own lands, and suffered great loss.

Lamanites in The Book of Helaman

The Book of Mormon · the work’s page · read it where Lamanites first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Lamanites are a people, including some formerly identified with Nephites and people of Ammon, who at times become mixed with Nephites and are called Lamanites. They are often numerous, possess extensive weapons and armor, and conquer the land southward and Zarahemla after dissenters stir them to anger. Under Coriantumr they invade, capture Zarahemla and other cities, then surrender after being surrounded and leave in peace. Their story also centers on conversion: after Nephi and Lehi preach, many repent, eight thousand are baptized, abandon weapons, hatred, and inherited traditions, and yield lands to the Nephites. For the most part they become more righteous than the Nephites, firm in faith, establish the church of God, preach faith and repentance, keep commandments, and live in peace and free intercourse with the Nephites while gaining riches. They experience famine and attacks, and some descendants join robbers. Though some harden their hearts and depend on their own strength and wisdom, many receive salvation, strive to bring others to truth, and are promised mercy, prolonged days, future restoration to truth, and a place among the sheep of their Redeemer.

Chapter 1

The Lamanites gather an innumerable army under Coriantumr and invade the center of the Nephite land. They capture Zarahemla and many cities, but after Coriantumr is killed they surrender to the Nephites and are allowed to leave in peace.

Chapter 3

The Lamanites include people of Ammon who go into the land northward. Some Nephites are eventually mixed with the Lamanites and become called Lamanites.

Chapter 4

The Lamanites were stirred up to anger by dissenters from the Nephites and obtained possession of the land of Zarahemla, the land southward, and other Nephite lands. They were more numerous than the Nephites and had strength equal to them man for man.

Chapter 5

The Lamanites hear Nephi and Lehi preach, and eight thousand in Zarahemla and the surrounding area are baptized unto repentance. Lamanites imprison Nephi and Lehi, later repent, receive spiritual manifestations, and many lay down their weapons, hatred, and traditions before yielding lands to the Nephites.

Chapter 6

The Lamanites become, for the most part, a righteous people whose righteousness exceeds that of the Nephites because of their firmness and steadiness in the faith. They preach faith and repentance, travel freely among the Nephites, grow rich, and destroy Gadianton’s band from among them.

Chapter 11

The Lamanites suffer the famine and destruction along with the Nephites. Some Lamanite descendants join with dissenters and become part of a great band of robbers.

Chapter 15

The Lamanites receive salvation through the preaching of the Nephites, and the more part of them walk circumspectly before God and keep his commandments. They are promised mercy and restoration to knowledge of the truth despite future afflictions, scattering, and being hunted.

Chapter 16

The Lamanites were among the people affected by hardening hearts and dependence on their own strength and wisdom.

Abstract written by a model

The Lamanites are a people made up of Lamanites, Lemuelites, and Ishmaelites, one of two parties and enemies of the Nephites. They have their own lands, exceed the Nephites in number, and include Gadianton robbers among them. They fight near Zarahemla, attack with great power, drive the Nephites northward, and gain the land southward by treaty. Though defeated at city Desolation with many slain, they repeatedly invade, capture cities and prisoners, sacrifice Nephite women and children, and gain continuing power. They ultimately overcome the Nephites, burn and destroy their towns, villages, cities, and remaining inhabitants. After allowing the gathering at Cumorah, their armies kill nearly all of Mormon's people, hunt the survivors until none remain, and are also at war among themselves.
