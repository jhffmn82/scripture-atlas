Moroni

person · 1 work · 78 quoted facts · 43 connections
Also called: I, Moroni’s, beloved brother, chief captain, chief captain over the armies of the Nephites, chief captain over the army, he, me, men of Moroni, mine, my, my beloved brother, Moroni, myself, your chief captain

Abstract written by a model

Moroni is a Nephite chief captain and military commander, appointed over the armies at age twenty-five. He leads the Nephites through wars against the Lamanites and internal dissenters, combining stratagem, intelligence, fortified defenses, and continual preparation. Although strong, mighty, and effective in battle, he does not delight in bloodshed or murder; his central commitments are the liberty, safety, religion, and welfare of his people, faith in Christ, fear of God, and obedience to covenants. He raises and plants the title of liberty, leads action against Amalickiah and the king-men, and seeks peace by requiring enemies to surrender weapons and covenant not to return to war. He builds forts, earthworks, walls, strongholds, guarded borders, and fortified cities, appoints leaders including Lehi and Teancum, drives back Lamanite forces, and captures places such as Mulek, Gid, and Nephihah. Wounded in battle and burdened by losses, he still protects prisoners, women, and children, and seeks to preserve his people rather than destroy enemies. After receiving reports from Helaman and disputing with Pahoran over neglected armies, he joins Pahoran under the standard of liberty, restores peace in Zarahemla, defeats remaining enemies, fortifies the land, gives command to his son Moronihah, and dies at the end of the thirty-sixth year of the reign of the judges.

Moroni in The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

Narrative, section by section written by a model

17 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 43

Moroni, appointed chief captain at twenty-five years of age, commands the Nephite armies and their wars. He uses spies, Alma’s revelation, concealed forces, and stratagem to defeat and surround the Lamanites.

Chapter 44

Moroni tells Zerahemnah that the Nephites do not desire to be men of blood or bring anyone into bondage. He demands the surrender of the Lamanites’ weapons and an oath not to return to war, commands the Nephites to attack when Zerahemnah refuses, and stops the killing when Zerahemnah agrees to a covenant of peace.

Chapter 46

Moroni, chief commander of the Nephite armies, becomes angry when he hears of the dissensions. He creates and proclaims the title of liberty, gathers those who want to maintain their liberty, marches against Amalickiah, captures many of his followers, and establishes the standard of liberty among the Nephites.

Chapter 48

Moroni prepares the Nephites to be faithful to God, strengthens their armies, and builds forts, earthworks, and stone walls. He is described as mighty, understanding, opposed to bloodshed, devoted to liberty, and committed to the welfare and safety of his people.

Chapter 49

Moroni stationed an army by the borders of Ammonihah, raised defensive earthworks, and fortified cities throughout the land. He appointed Lehi as chief captain over Noah, and his preparations enabled the Nephites to defeat the Lamanites.

Chapter 50

Moroni directs preparations for war and defense, builds strongholds around the Nephite cities, drives the Lamanites from the east wilderness, establishes armies and cities, and sends Teancum against Morianton. He protects the liberty and peace of the Nephites.

Chapter 51

Moroni becomes exceedingly angry at the king-men's refusal to defend their country and leads action against them. He also prepares for war against the Lamanites.

Chapter 52

Moroni strengthens Teancum’s army, orders the retention of prisoners, and directs the fortification of land Bountiful and its narrow pass. He captures the city of Mulek through a stratagem, fights Jacob’s forces, is wounded, and accepts the surrender of many Lamanites.

Chapter 53

Moroni places men to guard Lamanite prisoners while they bury the dead and labor. He takes command of the city of Mulek with Lehi, gives the city to Lehi, and has the prisoners build fortifications around the land Bountiful. He prepares his men for war, protects women and children from famine and affliction, and does not attempt another battle that year.

Chapter 54

Moroni rejoiced at Ammoron's request to exchange prisoners because he desired the provisions used to support the Lamanite prisoners and wanted his own people to strengthen his army. He sent an epistle to Ammoron, warned him of God's wrath, and offered to exchange prisoners only if a man, his wife, and his children were given for one prisoner.

Chapter 55

Moroni refuses to exchange prisoners unless Ammoron withdraws his purpose, and he plans to seek death among the Lamanites until they sue for peace. He uses Laman’s deception to arm the Nephite prisoners and capture city Gid, but he does not kill the drunken Lamanites because he does not delight in murder or bloodshed.

Chapter 56

Moroni receives an epistle from Helaman concerning the affairs of the people and the warfare in Helaman’s part of the land.

Chapter 59

Moroni rejoices at Helaman’s success, informs his people so they may rejoice, and sends an epistle to Pahoran requesting men to strengthen Helaman’s armies. He plans to recover the remaining possessions and cities taken by the Lamanites, but retains his force elsewhere and fails to prevent the loss of Nephihah. He becomes exceedingly sorrowful, doubts because of the wickedness of the people, and is angry with the government.

Chapter 60

Moroni writes to Pahoran and condemns the government for neglecting the armies, withholding provisions, and allowing thousands to fall. He demands repentance and support for the armies, and threatens to come to Zarahemla with the sword if the government does not act.

Chapter 61

Moroni receives Pahoran’s epistle, in which Pahoran says he does not seek power beyond retaining the judgment-seat and asks Moroni to come speedily with a few men. Moroni is told to strengthen Lehi and Teancum in the Lord.

Chapter 62

Moroni took a small number of men, gathered more under the standard of liberty, and joined Pahoran in defeating Pachus and restoring Pahoran to the judgment-seat. He captured the city of Nephihah without losing a Nephite, pursued the Lamanites with Lehi and Teancum, mourned Teancum's death, defeated the Lamanites, fortified the land, and later gave command of his armies to his son Moronihah.

Chapter 63

Moroni died at the end of the thirty-sixth year of the reign of the judges.
