interpreters

Also called: them, those two stones

Abstract written by a model

The interpreters are a gift from God: two stones fastened in bow rims, prepared to interpret languages and translate ancient records. Only a commanded seer may look in them; their possessor is called a seer. Preserved by the Lord to reveal iniquities, abominations, and mysteries, they are used by king Mosiah on plates of gold and later given to Alma the younger.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

The interpreters are instruments through which ancient records can be translated. Ammon says no one may look in them unless commanded, and Limhi believes they were prepared to unfold mysteries to the children of men.

Chapter 28

The interpreters are two stones fastened into the two rims of a bow and are used by king Mosiah to translate the records on the plates of gold. They are preserved by the Lord and later given by king Mosiah to Alma the younger.
