Lamoni

person · 1 work · 28 quoted facts · 13 connections
Also called: husband, king, king Lamoni, king, Lamoni, the king

Abstract written by a model

Lamoni is a king over the land and people of Ishmael, a descendant of Ishmael, son of another king, husband and father, and owner of the king’s flocks. He first employs Ammon to watch his flocks, then is pleased with him, frees him from bands, and offers him a daughter in marriage. After learning of Ammon’s faithfulness and power, Lamoni believes Ammon is the Great Spirit, accepts his teaching about God and redemption, prays for mercy, and collapses as if dead. After two days and nights he awakens, says he has seen his Redeemer, and is filled with joy. He teaches his people the words he heard from Ammon. Later he travels with Ammon toward Middoni, refuses his father’s command to slay Ammon, helps free Aaron and his brethren, retains his kingdom, builds synagogues, and grants his people liberty to worship the Lord.

Lamoni is in one work on this site, The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 17

King Lamoni rules the land of Ishmael and is a descendant of Ishmael. He is pleased with Ammon, frees him, offers him one of his daughters to wife, and then appoints him among the servants who watch his flocks.

Chapter 18

King Lamoni learns from his servants about Ammon’s faithfulness and power and believes that Ammon is the Great Spirit. He asks Ammon about his power, believes his teachings about God and the plan of redemption, prays for mercy, and falls to the earth as if dead.

Chapter 19

King Lamoni lies on his bed for two days and two nights, then rises according to Ammon's words. He declares that he has seen his Redeemer, is overcome with joy, and later rebukes and teaches his people.

Chapter 20

Lamoni desires to take Ammon to the land of Nephi to show him to his father, but instead travels with Ammon to the land of Middoni. He refuses his father’s command to slay Ammon and to return to the land of Ishmael, and he retains his kingdom.

Chapter 21

Lamoni helps deliver Aaron and his brethren from prison and returns with Ammon to Ishmael. He does not allow Ammon to serve him, builds synagogues, teaches his people, and grants them liberty to worship God.

Chapter 22

Lamoni is the son of the king over the land except the land of Ishmael. His people are continually taught by Ammon.
