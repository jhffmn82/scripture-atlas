Abstract written by a model

Ammonihah is a city and land of the people of Nephi, named for its first possessor. Alma preaches there, is expelled, returns, and later teaches with Amulek; Amulek also warns its people that their sins, rejection of the righteous, and failure to repent could bring famine, pestilence, the sword, and destruction. Its chief judge visits the prison with teachers and lawyers, while people are cast out and Amulek leaves his possessions. The remaining people stay hard-hearted, and the Lamanites invade and destroy Ammonihah, leaving the land desolate for years. Later the city is partly rebuilt, fortified with a high earth ridge and restricted entrance, and successfully defended by the Nephite army against a Lamanite attack.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

Ammonihah is a city named after the man who first possessed the land. Alma preaches there, is expelled, returns by another way, and later preaches there with Amulek.

Chapter 9

Ammonihah is the city whose people hear Alma and Amulek preach. Its destruction is presented as a possible consequence if its people refuse to repent.

Chapter 10

The land of Ammonihah is where Amulek preaches to the people. It contains people whose sins and rejection of the righteous place them under warning of famine, pestilence, the sword, and destruction.

Chapter 14

The chief judge over the land of Ammonihah comes to the prison on the twelfth day of the tenth month in the tenth year of the reign of the judges, accompanied by teachers and lawyers.

Chapter 15

The land of Ammonihah was the place from which people were cast out and where Amulek left his gold, silver, and precious things. Its remaining people continued in hard-heartedness and did not repent.

Chapter 16

Ammonihah was invaded and destroyed by the Lamanites. Its land remained desolate for many years because of the dead bodies and their scent.

Chapter 49

The city of Ammonihah had been partly rebuilt after its earlier destruction and was defended by a high ridge of earth and a restricted entrance. The Lamanites expected it to be an easy target but were driven back with great slaughter.
