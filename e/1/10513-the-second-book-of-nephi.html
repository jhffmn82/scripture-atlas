The Second Book of Nephi

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

These records summarize The Second Book of Nephi, in which Lehi’s final teachings, Nephi’s leadership, Jacob’s sermons, and extensive prophetic writings describe God’s covenants, Christ’s redemption, Israel’s scattering and gathering, and the path to eternal life. Lehi recalls the Lord’s mercy in bringing his family from Jerusalem, warns that obedience brings liberty and prosperity while sin brings captivity, blesses his children, and urges Laman, Lemuel, their families, Zoram, and others to follow Nephi. He teaches that opposition, agency, the Fall, and the Holy Messiah’s atonement make joy, redemption, resurrection, and choice possible. He also recounts prophecies concerning Joseph’s descendants, records, seers, restoration, and the house of Israel. After Lehi dies, conflict intensifies: Nephi’s brothers seek his life, so Nephi leads his family and followers away, establishes the people of Nephi, builds a temple, appoints Jacob and Joseph as priests and teachers, keeps records, and faces wars with the Lamanites. Jacob teaches from Isaiah about Israel’s scattering, the Messiah’s suffering and return, judgment, restoration, and the need to repent and come unto Christ. Isaiah’s words then portray Judah and Jerusalem’s wickedness, desolation, judgment, future purification, a righteous ruler, gathering, deliverance, and joy; they also foretell judgments on Assyria and Babylon and the eventual recovery of Israel. Nephi explains that these prophecies point to Christ, whom he and Jacob have seen, and teaches that the law of Moses typifies him. Nephi further prophesies Christ’s ministry, death, resurrection, appearance to his people, the destruction and later fall of his own people, the rise of false churches and secret combinations, the coming forth of a sealed book and written testimonies, a marvelous work, and the gathering of Israel. He warns against pride, priestcraft, false doctrine, and rejecting further revelation, while affirming that God speaks to many nations and offers salvation freely. The book closes with Nephi’s plain doctrine of Christ: repent, be baptized, receive the Holy Ghost, press forward in faith, feast on Christ’s words, pray, endure to the end, and be reconciled to Christ before final judgment.

The Second Book of Nephi / Chapter 1

Lehi teaches his sons about the Lord’s mercy in bringing them from Jerusalem to the land of promise and warns that obedience will bring liberty and prosperity, while iniquity will bring judgments, scattering, and captivity. He urges Laman, Lemuel, Sam, the sons of Ishmael, and Zoram to obey Nephi, cease rebelling, and unite in righteousness. Lehi testifies that the Lord has redeemed him and that he will soon die, and he gives blessings and promises concerning the seed of Zoram and Nephi.

Chapter 2

Lehi teaches Jacob and his sons that God created all things and that opposition, law, and choice are necessary to fulfill God's eternal purposes. He explains that Adam and Eve's transgression brought the fall of man, mortality, probation, and knowledge of good and evil, while Adam's fall made it possible for men to exist and have joy. The Holy Messiah provides redemption and salvation through his merits, mercy, grace, sacrifice, intercession, and resurrection. Men are free to choose liberty and eternal life through the great Mediator or captivity and eternal death through the devil.

Chapter 3

Lehi blesses Joseph, his last-born son, and teaches that Joseph's seed will not be destroyed if they keep the commandments of the Holy One of Israel. Joseph prophesied that the Lord would raise up a choice seer from his loins to bring forth the word of the Lord, write records, and help convince his descendants of the Lord's word. The writings of Joseph's descendants and Judah's descendants will grow together to confound false doctrines, lay down contentions, establish peace, and bring the descendants to knowledge of their fathers and the covenants of the Lord. The unit also describes the Messiah, Moses, a spokesman, one mighty among Joseph's descendants, and the future restoration of the house of Israel.

Chapter 4

Nephi speaks about Joseph’s prophecies concerning his seed, Nephi’s future generations, and the promises recorded on the plates of brass. Lehi blesses the children of Laman, Lemuel, Ishmael’s household, and Sam, teaching that obedience to the commandments brings prosperity while disobedience leads to being cut off from the Lord God’s presence. After Lehi dies, Laman, Lemuel, and the sons of Ishmael become angry with Nephi because of the Lord’s admonitions. Nephi records his delight in the scriptures and describes his struggle with temptations, sins, and afflictions while trusting in and praying to the Lord.

Chapter 5

Nephi flees into the wilderness with his family, Zoram and his family, Sam and his family, Jacob, Joseph, his sisters, and others after the anger of his brethren leads them to seek his life. They establish the place Nephi, call themselves the people of Nephi, follow the commandments of the Lord, and prosper by farming, raising animals, building, and working with materials. Nephi builds a temple, teaches and leads his people, and consecrates Jacob and Joseph as priests and teachers, while the Lamanites are separated from the presence of the Lord and become a scourge to Nephi’s people. Nephi keeps records, makes new plates at the Lord God’s command, and states that forty years have passed with wars and contentions with their brethren.

Chapter 6

Jacob speaks to the people of Nephi for the welfare of their souls and reads the words of Isaiah concerning the house of Israel. The Lord God will lift His hand to the Gentiles, and the house of Israel will be scattered, afflicted, and later gathered when they come to know their Redeemer. The Messiah will manifest Himself to the house of Israel, be scourged and crucified, and later return in power and glory to recover those who believe in Him. The Lord God will deliver His covenant people and contend with those who oppose them.

Chapter 7

The Lord addresses the house of Israel, asking whether He has put it away and explaining that its iniquities and transgressions caused its separation. The Lord declares His power to dry up the sea, darken the heavens, and deliver, while the speaker describes receiving instruction, suffering abuse, and trusting that the Lord God will help and justify him. The Lord's servant challenges his adversaries and says those who condemn him will grow old like a garment and be eaten by moths. The Lord warns those who kindle their own fire and walk by its sparks that they will lie down in sorrow.

Chapter 8

The Lord calls those who follow righteousness to remember Abraham and Sarah, promises comfort and restoration to Zion, and declares that his righteousness and salvation will endure forever. He tells his people not to fear men or the oppressor, whose power will pass away, and promises that the redeemed will return to Zion with everlasting joy. The Lord says he has put his words in his people's mouth, identifies them as his people, and removes the cup of trembling from Jerusalem. Jerusalem is told to awake, rise, put on beautiful garments, and be freed from captivity.

Chapter 9

The unit concerns the covenants of the Lord with the house of Israel, the restoration of the Jews, and the promises made to their children. It teaches that the Holy One of Israel provides resurrection and an infinite atonement to overcome the fall, death, hell, and the grave. All people will be judged according to God's holy judgment, with the righteous inheriting the kingdom of God and those who remain in sin suffering everlasting punishment. The text calls the children of men to repent, be baptized, believe in the Holy One of Israel, endure to the end, and come unto him.

Chapter 10

Jacob speaks to his beloved brethren about Christ, the Jews, the Gentiles, and the promises made to the house of Israel. He teaches that Christ will come among the Jews and be crucified, after which the Jews will suffer destruction and dispersion until they believe in Christ and are restored to their lands. God will bless the Gentiles, make the land a land of liberty, and fortify it against other nations. Jacob urges his brethren to remember God, choose eternal life, reconcile themselves to God's will, and rely on grace, the resurrection, and the atonement to enter the eternal kingdom of God.

Chapter 11

Nephi writes that Jacob has spoken many things to Nephi’s people, but only some were recorded. Nephi writes more of Isaiah’s words because he delights in them and wants to liken them to his people and send them to all his children. Nephi and Jacob have both seen Christ, and their words serve as witnesses that Nephi’s words are true. Nephi teaches that the law of Moses and all things given by God from the beginning typify Christ, and that without Christ all men must perish.

Chapter 12

Isaiah sees a word concerning Judah and Jerusalem about the last days, when the mountain of the Lord’s house will be established above the hills and all nations will flow to it. Many people will go to the mountain of the Lord’s house, where they will be taught his ways, and the law will go forth from Zion and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem. The Lord will judge and rebuke the nations, bringing an end to war, while the house of Jacob is called to walk in his light but is forsaken for its wickedness and idolatry. The day of the Lord will humble the proud, abolish idols, and cause people to hide from the fear of the Lord and the glory of his majesty.

Chapter 13

The Lord of Hosts takes away Jerusalem’s and Judah’s support, leadership, and resources, and gives children as rulers so the people are oppressed and led into error. Jerusalem is ruined and Judah has fallen because their tongues and deeds are against the Lord. The Lord judges the ancients and princes for consuming the vineyard and harming the poor, while declaring that the righteous will prosper and the wicked will perish. The daughters of Zion are humbled by the removal of their ornaments, and Jerusalem becomes desolate as its men fall by the sword.

Chapter 14

Seven women ask one man to let them be called by his name while providing their own bread and apparel. The branch of the Lord will be beautiful and glorious, and the fruit of the earth will be excellent to the escaped of Israel. Those left in Zion and Jerusalem will be called holy after the Lord washes away filth and purges blood. The Lord will protect mount Zion and its assemblies with cloud, smoke, and flaming fire, and provide a tabernacle for shade and refuge.

Chapter 15

The Lord of Hosts compares the house of Israel and the men of Judah to a carefully prepared vineyard that produces wild grapes instead of good fruit. Jerusalem and Judah are called to judge between the Lord of Hosts and his vineyard, which will be exposed to desolation because it yields oppression instead of judgment and a cry instead of righteousness. The unit condemns drunkenness, greed, moral confusion, pride, injustice, and rejection of the law and word of the Holy One of Israel. The Lord of Hosts brings judgment through captivity, destruction, and nations summoned from far away, leaving darkness and sorrow over the land.

Chapter 16

The speaker sees the Lord of Hosts sitting upon a high and lifted-up throne, while seraphim proclaim his holiness and the temple fills with smoke. The speaker confesses that he is a man of unclean lips among a people of unclean lips, and a seraph purges his sin with a live coal. The Lord sends the speaker to tell the people, who will hear and see without understanding or being converted. The land will become desolate and its people will be removed, but a tenth will return, and the holy seed will remain as its substance.

Chapter 17

The unit concerns Ahaz, king of Judah, whose kingdom is threatened by Rezin, king of Syria, and Pekah the son of Remaliah, king of Israel. The Lord sends Isaiah to reassure Ahaz that the counsel against Judah will not succeed and invites Ahaz to ask for a sign. The Lord gives the sign that a virgin will conceive, bear a son, and call his name Immanuel. The unit also describes the coming devastation brought by the king of Assyria and the resulting desolation of the land.

Chapter 18

The Lord instructs the narrator to write concerning Maher-shalal-hash-baz and to name the prophetess’s son Maher-shalal-hash-baz. The Lord warns that the king of Assyria will overwhelm this people, pass through Judah, and reach to the neck of the land. The people are told to fear and sanctify the Lord of Hosts rather than follow their own confederacies or seek familiar spirits and wizards. The chapter describes darkness, hunger, anguish, and trouble for those who reject the law and testimony.

Chapter 19

The Lord of Hosts promises light and joy to people in darkness and announces that a child will be born to rule on David’s throne with everlasting government and peace. The Lord sends his word to Jacob and Israel, but Israel responds with pride and refuses to turn to him. The Lord allows adversaries to attack Israel and declares judgment on its leaders and people for wickedness, hypocrisy, and folly. His anger remains, and his hand is still stretched out against them.

Chapter 20

The Lord God of Hosts condemns those who make unrighteous decrees and declares that the Assyrian will be used against a hypocritical nation and the people of his wrath. The Assyrian intends to destroy many nations and boasts of conquering Calno, Carchemish, Hamath, Arpad, Samaria, and Damascus, but the Lord will punish his proud heart after completing his work upon Mount Zion and Jerusalem. The Lord will burn the Assyrian's glory, while a remnant of Israel and the house of Jacob will return and rely upon the Holy One of Israel. The Lord promises that the Assyrian's burden and yoke will be removed, and that the Lord of Hosts will humble the high and haughty.

Chapter 21

The unit concerns a rod and branch from Jesse upon whom the Spirit of the Lord rests, giving wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, and fear of the Lord. He judges with righteousness and equity, and under his reign animals dwell peacefully while the earth is full of the knowledge of the Lord. The Lord sets his hand to recover the remnant of his people, gathers Israel and Judah, and sets up an ensign for the nations. A highway is made for the remnant from Assyria, like Israel’s exodus from Egypt.

Chapter 22

The speaker praises the Lord because divine anger has turned away and comfort has come. God is declared to be salvation, strength, and song, and people draw water with joy from the wells of salvation. The people are told to praise the Lord, call on his name, declare his doings, and sing because he has done excellent things. The inhabitant of Zion is told to cry out and shout because the Holy One of Israel is in its midst.

Chapter 23

The unit concerns Babylon and the coming day of the Lord, when the Lord of Hosts gathers armies from distant nations against it. The whole land will be laid desolate, the wicked will be punished, and Babylon will become uninhabited like Sodom and Gomorrah. The Medes will attack Babylon without regard for silver or gold. The Lord of Hosts will shake the heavens and the earth, while a man will become more precious than fine gold.

Chapter 24

The Lord will have mercy on Jacob, choose Israel, return them to their lands of promise, and give them rest from sorrow, fear, and bondage. The king of Babylon, identified as Lucifer, is brought down from heaven to hell after exalting himself above the Most High and oppressing nations. Babylon will be swept with destruction, while the Lord will overthrow the Assyrian and establish safety for the poor of his people in Zion. The Lord’s purpose over the whole earth and all nations will stand.

Chapter 25

Nephi explains that Isaiah’s words are difficult for many because they do not know the manner of prophesying among the Jews, but they are plain to those filled with the spirit of prophecy. He prophesies that the Jews will be destroyed, restored to Jerusalem, reject and crucify Jesus Christ, and later be scattered until they believe in Christ. He teaches that Jesus Christ is the only name by which people can be saved, that the Lord will restore his people through a marvelous work and a wonder, and that Nephi’s writings will be preserved to judge and convince future generations. Nephi’s people believe in Christ, keep the law of Moses until it is fulfilled, and teach their children to seek remission of sins through Christ.

Chapter 26

Nephi teaches that after Christ rises from the dead, he will show himself to his people, and his words will be their law. He prophesies of wars, Christ’s birth, death, and resurrection, the destruction of the wicked, and the preservation of the righteous who heed the prophets and look to Christ. He describes the last days, including the fall of Nephi’s people, the Gentiles’ pride and churches, secret combinations, and the emergence of written words from those destroyed. He also teaches that God offers salvation and goodness freely to all, forbids priestcraft and iniquity, and commands charity.

Chapter 27

The unit concerns the Lord of Hosts, the Lord God, and God bringing forth a sealed book and its words to the children of men in the last days. The book contains a revelation from God, while its sealed words are withheld until the Lord’s due time, and its unsealed words are delivered by a man who receives the book and reads them. The nations, Gentiles, and Jews face visitation and destruction for iniquity and abominations, while Zion, the house of Jacob, the meek, and the poor are promised deliverance and understanding. The learned reject the book because of worldly motives, but God performs a marvelous work and a wonder through the man who reads the words and through witnesses.

Chapter 28

The Lord God warns the children of men about churches built up without Him, false doctrines, pride, wickedness, and denial of the Holy Ghost and the power of God. The devil deceives people through anger, carnal security, claims that there is no hell, and teachings that people have enough revelation. The Lord God calls for repentance, promises mercy, and teaches that He gives wisdom and revelation line upon line to those who heed His counsel. The great and abominable church and the kingdom of the devil will fall, while the humble followers of Christ may be led into error but remain distinct from the wicked.

Chapter 29

The Lord God declares that a marvelous work will recover the house of Israel and fulfill covenants and promises made to Nephi and his father. Gentiles are rebuked for rejecting additional scripture and for mistreating the Jews, from whom the Bible came. The Lord God teaches that he speaks to many nations, including the Jews, Nephites, lost tribes of Israel, and all nations, and that their written testimonies will be shared. The house of Israel will be gathered to the lands of its possessions, and the Lord God's word will be gathered into one.

Chapter 30

Nephi teaches that Gentiles who repent become covenant people, while Jews who do not repent are cast off, and he prophesies concerning both groups. The book will come forth among the Gentiles, and believers will carry its words to the remnant of Nephi’s seed, who will learn of their ancestry and Jesus Christ. The Lord God will restore his people, judge the wicked, and bring peace and righteousness to the earth. All things will be revealed to the children of men, and Satan will have power over their hearts no more for a long time.

Chapter 31

Nephi concludes his prophesying and teaches his beloved brethren plainly about the doctrine of Christ. He explains that Jesus Christ was baptized to fulfill all righteousness, and that people must repent, be baptized by water, receive the Holy Ghost, and endure to the end. He teaches that the way to eternal life requires pressing forward with faith in Christ, feasting upon the word of Christ, and relying on the merits of him who is mighty to save.

Chapter 32

Nephi teaches his beloved brethren what they should do after entering in by the way. They should receive the Holy Ghost, feast upon the words of Christ, ask, knock, and pray always. The Holy Ghost reveals what they should do, while the words of Christ tell them all things necessary. Nephi says this is the doctrine of Christ and mourns the unbelief, wickedness, ignorance, and stiffneckedness of men.

Chapter 33

Nephi says he cannot write everything taught among his people and that his written words, though weak, have great worth because they teach about Jesus Christ and persuade people to do good, believe in him, and endure to the end. He prays continually for his people and knows that God will consecrate his prayers for their gain. Nephi teaches that Jews, Gentiles, and all people must be reconciled to Christ, enter the narrow gate, and continue in the strait path until the end of the day of probation. He warns that Christ will confirm the words at the final judgment and that those who reject the goodness of God and these words will be condemned.
