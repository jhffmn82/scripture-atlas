misery

Also called: agonies, distress, pain, pains, sorrows, suffering

Abstract written by a model

Misery is an affliction of mankind, characterizing Samsâra through birth, old age, disease, and death, especially hell. It arises from birth and death, attachment, aversion, desire, and sensory pleasures. It ceases with ignorance and delusion; dispassion, the Law, and the road of the wise lead to freedom and liberation.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Nineteenth Lecture.

Suffering occurs in birth, old age, disease, death, Samsâra, and especially hell. Mrigâputra recalls experiencing bodily and mental suffering countless times and seeks freedom from it through the Law.

Thirty-Second Lecture.

Misery arises from birth and death and from attachment, aversion, desire, and sensory pleasures. It ceases when delusion ceases, and the dispassionate eventually become free from all misery.

Thirty-Fifth Lecture.

Following the road shown by the wise ones leads a monk to the end of all misery. At death, a monk who rejects food and leaves the human body is liberated from misery.
