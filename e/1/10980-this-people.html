this people in The Book of Mosiah

author unknown · the work’s page · read it where this people first appears

Abstract written by a model

The people under king Noah labor and are taxed to support Noah, his household, priests, and widespread iniquity. Deceived into idolatry, they delight in violence, boast after victory over the Lamanites, and reject Abinadi’s prophecy. They harden their hearts, refuse repentance, grow angry, seize Abinadi, and bring him before Noah, though they cannot harm him while the Spirit is upon him. Abinadi’s true words stand as testimony against their iniquities and foretell bondage, affliction, and destruction. Later, they pursue Alma’s people but cannot overtake them, and are linked to Alma’s people’s oppression and bondage.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 11

Noah's people are taxed to support Noah, his household, and his priests, and they labor exceedingly to support iniquity. They become idolatrous, participate in wickedness and violence, rejoice in victory over the Lamanites, and harden their hearts against Abinadi's words.

Chapter 12

This people have hardened their hearts against the Lord's words and have not repented of their evil doings. They become angry with Abinadi, seize him, and bring him before king Noah, and they face prophesied bondage, affliction, and destruction unless they repent.

Chapter 13

The people of king Noah cannot lay their hands on Abinadi because the Spirit of the Lord is upon him. They are cut to their hearts, filled with wonder and amazement, and angered because he tells them the truth concerning their iniquities.

Chapter 17

This people is the subject of Abinadi’s testimony concerning iniquity. Abinadi says the words spoken against this people are true and will stand as a testimony against them.

Chapter 23

The people of king Noah pursue Alma’s people but cannot overtake them. They are associated with the oppression and bondage suffered by Alma’s people.
