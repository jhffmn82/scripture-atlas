Abstract written by a model

A group that begins with Jared, his brother, and about twenty-two souls is taught humility, crosses the great waters, praises the Lord, and settles the promised land. They multiply, grow strong, cultivate the earth, and seek a king. Under rulers such as Orihah and Shule they prosper and become exceedingly numerous, though rebellions lead them into wickedness, idolatries, and mockery of prophets. Repeated repentance brings renewed prosperity. Secret combinations under Akish corrupt them and lead to devastating war, nearly destroying them. Rebuilt under Shez, they spread across the land, endure rebellion and war, and prosper under Morianton through trade, mining, crafts, agriculture, animal husbandry, and weapons-making. In later periods they reject, persecute, and kill prophets, harden their hearts, and suffer wars, famine, pestilence, destruction, captivity, dearth, and poisonous serpents; some eventually repent and cry unto the Lord.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6

The people prepare food and animals, travel across the great waters, praise the Lord, and land in the promised land. They till the earth, multiply, wax strong, and request that one of the sons be anointed king over them.

Chapter 7

The people become exceedingly numerous and are drawn away by Corihor and Noah during their rebellions. They revile and mock the prophets, then repent of their iniquities and idolatries and begin to prosper again.

Chapter 9

The people become corrupted by the secret society, and nearly all of them are destroyed in the war between Akish and his sons. Later, the people reject and persecute the prophets, suffer dearth and poisonous serpents, and eventually repent and cry unto the Lord.

Chapter 10

The people were rebuilt under Shez and later spread across the land. They rebelled against Riplakish, fought in wars, prospered under Morianton, and became exceedingly industrious, engaging in trade, mining, crafts, agriculture, animal husbandry, and warfare.

Chapter 11

The people rejected and killed or sought to destroy the prophets, hardened their hearts, and would not hearken to prophetic warnings. They suffered wars, famines, pestilences, destruction, and captivity, and some began to repent of their iniquity.
