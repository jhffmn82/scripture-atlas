In The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry, the remnant of the house of Israel is scattered by nations, judged and smitten, later receives further records, and may include Gentiles who hearken to the Lamb of God. In The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry: Nephi's people possess promised land, endure wars and destructions, and receive Nephi’s instructive records about the Lord and prophets. In The Second Book of Nephi, the people of Nephi follow the Lord, prosper, labor, and live happily under priests and teachers, but later face wars, destruction, and unbelief. In The Second Book of Nephi: The Nephites are a people who write God’s words, possess records from the Jews and lost tribes of Israel, and share their words with those peoples. In The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi, the Nephites are people friendly to Nephi, including Jacobites, Josephites, Zoramites, and others who are not Lamanites. In The Book of Jacob, the Brother of Nephi, the people of Nephi grow proud and sinful, are warned to repent, and later have peace, fortify themselves, and conquer their enemies. In The Book of Enos, the Nephites seek to restore the Lamanites to the true faith, till the land, raise crops and animals, and are preserved by plain preaching and prophesying. In The Book of Jarom, the Nephites become strong, multiply, spread across the land, grow rich, and withstand and drive away the Lamanites.

Abstract written by a model

The people of Nephi are a people in the land of Zarahemla, ruled by judges and maintaining a free government. They accept Mosiah’s laws, enforce them as far as possible, and use the voice of the people to reject Amlici as king and later settle disputes over the law and Pahoran. They experience both prosperity and peace and persecution, conflict, invasion, and losses caused by the Lamanites. Some become proud, contentious, unequal, and persecuting, while others aid the poor and needy. They establish the church of God more fully, many are baptized in Sidon, and the church becomes generally established after an invasion and years of peace. Highly favored by the Lord, they receive light, knowledge, deliverance, prosperity, the Spirit, prophecy, revelation, and spiritual gifts. Moroni strengthens and teaches them to defend themselves without offense; they rejoice in deliverance and Helaman’s success, giving thanks, fasting, praying, and worshipping. Yet Alma warns that despite their blessings, rebellion could bring destruction; after Jesus Christ manifests himself to them, they dwindle in unbelief and ultimately become extinct through wars, pestilences, famines, and bloodshed.

Nephites in The Book of Helaman

The Book of Mormon · the work’s page · read it where Nephites first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Nephites are a people of Nephi, governed by laws and governments established by the voice of the people. Their account centers on wars, contentions, dissensions, prosperity, repentance, corruption, and approaching destruction. Early, they contend over succession to the judgment-seat, are attacked by the Lamanites, suffer heavy losses, and then surround their attackers, receive their surrender, and recapture Zarahemla. They later face further contention, dissensions caused by pride, wars, migration, prosperity, persecution, scattering, and mixing with the Lamanites. Because of iniquity they lose nearly all the land southward and are driven into Bountiful; after repentance prompted by preaching, they regain half their possessions but cannot recover more. Their laws of Mosiah are corrupted as those choosing evil become more numerous, and the more part become hardened, impenitent, and wicked. Many join Gadianton’s bands, seek riches and gain, oppress the poor, practice murder, plunder, stealing, and false witness, and allow robbers and murderers to manage the government. Though blessed with fields, flocks, herds, wealth, safety, and peace when they cleave unto the Lord, they become proud, set their hearts on riches, reject the word of God and prophets, depend on their own strength and wisdom, and disbelieve prophecy and revelation. They are repeatedly warned that their cities, lands, and people will be destroyed unless they repent.

The Book of Helaman

The Nephites are the subject of an account concerning their wars, contentions, and dissensions. They are also described as wicked and abominable down to the coming of Christ.

Chapter 1

The Nephites experience serious contention over the judgment-seat and fail to keep sufficient guards in Zarahemla. They suffer heavy losses during Coriantumr’s invasion but later surround the Lamanites, receive their surrender, and retake Zarahemla.

Chapter 2

The Nephites had peace established again with the Lamanites by Moronihah. Among them, contention arose over who should fill the judgment-seat.

Chapter 3

The people of Nephi experience contention, dissensions, wars, migration, prosperity, peace, pride, and persecution. They keep many records, and many are eventually scattered, slain, or mixed with the Lamanites.

Chapter 4

The Nephites suffered bloodshed and were driven by the Lamanites into the land of Bountiful, losing almost all their lands. After repenting, they regained half of their possessions, but they could obtain no more land because the Lamanites were too numerous.

Chapter 5

The Nephites have laws and governments established by the voice of the people, but their laws have become corrupted because those choosing evil are more numerous. Nephi and Lehi preach among them, and repentant Lamanites yield lands to them.

Chapter 6

Many Nephites become hardened, impenitent, and grossly wicked. They join Gadianton’s bands, commit murders and other wickedness, set their hearts upon riches, gain control of the government, oppress the poor and humble, and dwindle in unbelief.

Chapter 7

The Nephites had become greatly wicked, setting their hearts on riches and permitting murder, plunder, theft, false witness, and other iniquity. They were warned that their cities and lands would be taken from them and that they would be destroyed unless they repented.

Chapter 8

The people are stirred to anger against Nephi, but some defend him and testify that his warnings will come to pass unless they repent. Their murders, fornication, and wickedness bring them toward destruction.

Chapter 12

The people are blessed and prospered by the Lord in their fields, flocks, herds, wealth, safety, and peace. During ease and great prosperity, they harden their hearts, forget the Lord, and trample under their feet the Holy One.

Chapter 13

The Nephites remain in great wickedness while the Lamanites observe the commandments of God. They reject Samuel, cast out and persecute prophets, set their hearts upon riches, and face destruction unless they repent.

Chapter 14

The people become angry with Samuel because he is a Lamanite and speaks hard words against them, and they seek to destroy him and cast him out. They are told to hear his words, know God’s judgments, and learn the conditions of repentance.

Chapter 16

The Nephites were among the people who began to harden their hearts and depend on their own strength and wisdom.

people of Nephi in The Book of Helaman

Abstract written by a model

The people of Nephi are a chosen people of the Lord, governed under judges and divided over the claims of Pahoran, Paanchi, and Pacumeni; by their voice they appoint Pahoran and later Pacumeni. At the judgment-seat they mistake five men for murderers, mourn and fast at Seezoram’s burial, and respond differently to Nephi, with some believing he is a prophet and others calling him a god. Most become proud, wicked, and hardened, practicing secret works of darkness, murderings, plunderings, and all manner of iniquities; they reject, revile, and seek to imprison Nephi. Famine and destruction lead them to remember the Lord, repent, humble themselves, and plead for relief. They then prosper, spread from sea west to sea east, and enjoy great peace, but renewed wickedness and robbers threaten them. They are warned to repent or be destroyed.

The Book of Mormon, Mormon's Own Record / Chapter 1

The Nephites consist of the Nephites, Jacobites, Josephites, and Zoramites. They gather more than thirty thousand men, defeat the Lamanites in several battles, and slay many of them.

Chapter 2

The Nephites fight the Lamanites but are repeatedly frightened, driven from cities and lands, and hunted northward. They briefly defeat larger Lamanite armies, yet remain weakened by wickedness and later divide their inherited lands with the Lamanites and robbers of Gadianton.

Chapter 3

The Nephites prepared their lands and arms, gathered at the land Desolation, and fortified against the Lamanites. They defeated the Lamanites twice, then boasted in their strength and swore to avenge their slain brethren.

Chapter 4

The Nephites fight repeated battles against the Lamanites but are repeatedly driven back, slaughtered, and swept off. They persist in wickedness, boast of their strength after victories, and their women and children are taken prisoner and sacrificed.

Chapter 5

The Nephites give Mormon command of their armies and maintain several cities against the Lamanites. In the three hundred and eightieth year they are overwhelmed, and those who cannot escape are destroyed.

Chapter 6

The Nephites gather with their wives and children in the land of Cumorah for their last struggle against the Lamanites. Nearly all are slain, while twenty and four survive with Mormon and a few others escape or desert.

Chapter 8

The Nephites who escaped the battle at Cumorah were hunted by the Lamanites until they were destroyed. Moroni describes their fall and destruction as great and marvelous.
