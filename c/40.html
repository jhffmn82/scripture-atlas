How these timelines were made

Which events. For each religion, every event the reader found in the religion's works, ranked by the number of chapters that tell it, then the number of works, then the number of facts about it; the top 40 told in 2 or more chapters (when fewer than 10 reach 2 chapters, the top 10), and with them every event tied with the last one in on all three counts, so no tie at the cut is broken by name. Code counted these from the text; no model chose them. The reader found the events, and each event’s page holds the verses it was read from.

Their order. Each event sits where the text first tells it: the works in canon order, then the chapter. Events a source dates are also shown on a time axis by that date.

Dates. No date comes from the text or from a model. For each event a model searched English Wikipedia, the Jewish Encyclopedia (1906), the Catholic Encyclopedia (1913, New Advent) and Wikidata for a page that dates it, and proposed the page and the date. Code then fetched the page and kept the date only when a sentence on that page names the event, by a word of the event’s own name (the page’s title or the person alone does not count: a sentence giving Mahavira’s birth year does not date his descent from heaven), and states the date; that sentence is quoted, and the date is shown as it words it. A second model then read two or three of the event’s own passages, verbatim from the religion’s works, beside the sentence, and the date was kept only when it judged the sentence to describe that same event as those passages tell it (so Jesus’ baptism does not date the Book of Mormon’s baptisms); its answer is kept with the date. A date stands on the time axis only at a year its own words state, so a count such as “in his 18th year” or “the 2666th year after creation” dates nothing here. Britannica was not used: its pages refuse automated fetches, so nothing from it could be checked. A date is the source’s, in its own words; a traditional date is given as the source gives it. An event no page dates this way stays undated.
