Hymns of Guru Nanak

Guru Nanak · 22 sections
Hymns of Guru Nanak, translated by Max Arthur Macauliffe, The Sikh Religion, vol. 1 (1909), public domain, via sacred-texts.com. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://sacred-texts.com/skh/tsr1/

Abstract written by a model

This document is a sequence of devotional teachings attributed repeatedly to Nanak, arranged across many Rags, Wars, Ashtapadis, Chhants, and related units. Its central concern is the human path to God: God alone is true, eternal, creating, sustaining, judging, forgiving, and present in all creatures; the Guru, the Word, and remembrance of God’s Name enable people to know and unite with Him. From the opening Sri Rag onward, the text contrasts happiness, honour, purity, truth, virtue, love, and deliverance with pride, mammon, attachment, greed, counterfeit conduct, and worldly wealth. Human bodies, youth, beauty, status, possessions, and family ties are transient; death, destiny, judgment, suffering, and transmigration confront all. The true Guru’s instruction, Sat Sangat, obedience to God’s will, truthful conduct, humility, contentment, mercy, service, good deeds, and devotion to the Name lead toward God’s court, peace, and salvation. A recurring figure is the longing woman or bride, separated from her Beloved and dishonoured until she seeks Him through love, virtue, the Name, and divine favour. Her fulfillment represents union with God. Nanak also presents himself as God’s servant and minstrel, receiving the true Name, praising the Indescribable, and acknowledging his own foolishness and dependence on his Master. Throughout, the units reject empty outward religion: ritual washing, alms, pilgrimage, austerity, religious dress, ceremonies, learning, yogic practices, external observances, caste, status, and displays of piety are futile when they lack God’s Name, the Word, and the Guru’s guidance. Particular criticism is directed at hypocrisy among Jogis, Qazis, Brahmans, Musalmans, Pandits, gurus, pirs, and idol worshippers; Bihagre ki War questions worship of stones that themselves sink. The document closes in Prabhati by reaffirming the same conclusion: religious acts without knowledge of God are futile, while those imbued with the Word, guided by the true Guru, and devoted to the Name overcome desire, pride, false wisdom, defilement, strife, and separation from the True One.

Hymns of Guru Nanak front matter

Sri Rag .. Ashtapadi body

Sri Rag and Ashtapadi concern God, the Guru, God's name, truth, virtue, love, and the human condition. They teach that man gains happiness, honour, purity, and deliverance through the Guru, the Word, and devotion to God's name, while pride, mammon, counterfeit conduct, and attachment to worldly wealth lead to suffering and ruin. God's court is reached by those who are true, virtuous, and imbued with God's name. Nanak repeatedly declares that God alone is true, eternal, and the source of all gifts, while death overtakes all worldly bodies and possessions.

Ghar III / Sri Rag ki War body

Ghar III explains that Sat Sangat is known by the mention of the one God and that Duhagins are women separated from their husbands who forget their Spouse and wander unhonoured. It teaches that those pleased with God's will remove superstition from their minds. It identifies Nanak, the true Guru, by his association with every one. Sri Rag ki War presents faith, resignation, patience, divine judgment, and the unity of all creatures under God as central teachings.

Majh ki War body

This unit concerns the Guru, Nanak, God, and the spiritual condition of human life in the world. It describes human aging, death, worldly impermanence, pride, sin, greed, and the folly of relying on external observances. It teaches that obedience to God's orders, meditation on God's Name, and the Guru's instruction lead toward truth, contentment, and God's court. Nanak, serving as a minstrel, sings the Word and receives the robe of true praise and the ambrosial food of the true Name.

Rag Gauri .. Gauri Chhant body

The unit teaches that God, the Guru, the Name, and the Word deliver human beings from ignorance, pride, worldly desire, and death. It describes the soul and body, the body’s transience, and the soul’s immortality, while urging fear of God, truthful conduct, contentment, and meditation. Gauri Ashtapadi contrasts empty religious display and worldly power with devotion and Guru’s instruction. Gauri Chhant presents the bride’s longing for the Bridegroom and her fulfillment when united with Him through the Name and divine favor.

Rag Asa .. Asa Ashtapadi body

Rag Asa teaches that God alone creates, sustains, judges, forgives, and grants truth, while caste, worldly status, ritual, wealth, and family attachment cannot secure spiritual benefit. It concerns man’s need to remember God’s Name, follow the Guru and the Guru’s instruction, abandon worldly love and mammon, and cultivate truth, devotion, mercy, contentment, and humility. Asa Ashtapadi continues that the Word and the true Name free people from sin, fear, transmigration, and death, while attachment, pride, greed, and false religious display bind them. Nanak repeatedly declares that God’s favour, the Guru’s guidance, and love of the Name lead to salvation and union with God.

Asa Chhant body

The unit concerns devotion to God, especially the young woman who seeks her Beloved through love, virtue, the true Word, and God's name. It teaches that God's favour brings happiness, spiritual companionship, and the ambrosial fruit, while sin, mammon, and worldly desire bring bitterness, anxiety, punishment, and death. Nanak urges the soul to remember God, seek the true Guru, and recognize divine knowledge. The unit also warns that without God's name and the true Word, beings wander in superstition and suffer separation.

Rag Gujari .. Gujari Ashtapadi body

Rag Gujari teaches that true worship consists in meditating on the Name, cleansing the heart, performing good acts, and recognizing God as near and sustaining. It contrasts the benefits of devotion with the emptiness of ritual, arguing that the Name is necessary for purity, knowledge, peace, and deliverance. Gujari Ashtapadi presents prayers for love of the pure Name, describes worldly suffering and worldly love, and praises God's service under the guidance of the true Guru. It teaches that repeating God's name, receiving the Word, and seeking the true Guru's protection free people from pride, doubt, transmigration, avarice, and covetousness.

Bihagre ki War body

Bihagre ki War describes Hindus as having forgotten God and going in the wrong direction. It says they worship according to Narad’s instruction and characterizes them as blind, dumb, and ignorant fools. The unit says they take stones and worship them, questioning how a stone that sinks can carry them across.

Rag Wadhans .. Wadhans ki War body

Rag Wadhans concerns a fervent woman who longs for God, grieves separation from the Beloved, and is willing to sacrifice herself in service. Wadhans Chhant teaches that truth, God's word, and God's name are superior to ritual washing, alms, and other good works, while human life and happiness come according to God's will and past acts. Wadhans Alahanian says that God created and governs the world, is present in every heart, and is known through the Guru's instruction and meditation on the Name. Wadhans ki War declares that ritual is worthless when it causes a person to forget the Beloved, while true love preserves honour with God.

Rag Sorath .. Sorath Ashtapadi body

Rag Sorath teaches that death, destiny, and separation come to all creatures, while God alone is beyond death and destiny. It urges humanity to praise God, repeat God's Name and Word, serve the Guru and True Guru, abandon mammon, and cultivate virtue. Sorath Ashtapadi describes the Bairagi as one who renounces desire, remains absorbed in God's love, and treasures the Word at home. It also teaches that Pandit learning and religious ceremonies are worthless without meditation, the Guru's instruction, and the Name.

Rag Dhanasari .. Dhanasari Chhant body

Rag Dhanasari concerns devotion to God, reliance on God's name, human mortality, destiny, moral conduct, and the true religious life. It teaches that Nanak serves God, that people receive the fruits of their own actions, and that deceit leads toward death. It contrasts genuine spiritual conduct with the failings of the Qazi, Brahman, and Jogi, and describes the virtues of one who recognizes and serves the One. Dhanasari Ashtapadi emphasizes love and service to God, while Dhanasari Chhant describes a woman seeking honour by pleasing her Spouse.

Rag Suhi .. Suhi ki War body

This unit presents Rag Suhi, Suhi Ashtapadi, Suhi Chhant, and Suhi ki War as teachings on devotion, divine knowledge, moral conduct, and union with God. It describes man as wandering through attachment, passion, falsehood, and transmigration unless he serves God, follows the Guru, and embraces the Name and the Word. Nanak teaches that God tests deeds, protects those devoted to Him, pardons the loving, and grants liberation. The unit also contrasts genuine spiritual understanding with empty ritual, including bathing, reading the Veds, and outward religious display.

Rag Bilawal body

Rag Bilawal is a devotional hymn in which Nanak praises God as the source of all things and the object of complete devotion. Nanak asks God for understanding to sing His praises and admits that he cannot describe the Indescribable. He presents himself as a foolish, blind, strange dog who survives by barking for his belly, yet still bears his Master's name. He describes his body as a Qalandar's dress, his heart as a temple, and his soul as pierced by the Compassionate One.

Lunar Days body

The unit concerns repeating the Name of the Lord of the world under the Guru's instruction. It teaches that attachment to mammon leads people to Death's gate, suffering, and transmigration, while no one is emancipated without the Guru's instruction. On the tenth day of the lunar month, people are told to repeat the Name as offerings and ablutions and to immerse themselves in the attributes and knowledge of the True One. When doubts and fears depart, the true heart is free of impurity, and fixing the mind on the True One brings happiness.

Ramkali .. Ramkali ki War I body

This unit concerns Ramkali, Ramkali ki War I, and teachings about God, the Guru, the Name, good works, truth, and salvation. It contrasts genuine devotion and meditation with hypocrisy, pride, worldly love, mammon, and sin among Jogis, Qazis, Brahmans, Musalmans, and others. It teaches that all people face death, judgment, and possible transmigration, while those who follow the Guru's instruction and remember the Name may obtain divine knowledge, peace, and salvation. Nanak describes the True Guru's instruction as the way to recognize God's will and live truthfully.

Rag Maru Ashtapadi .. Maru ki War I body

The unit concerns Rag Maru Ashtapadi, Maru Solhe, and Maru ki War I, describing God, the Guru, the True Name, and the way to emancipation. It condemns hypocritical religious practices, pride, avarice, lust, slander, and attachment to the world, while praising devotion, humility, truthful conduct, and service to the Guru. It teaches that the Guru's instruction and God's love save people from transmigration and lead them to God's court. Nanak presents the Guru, divine knowledge, true merchandise, and remembrance of God's Name as the means of crossing the world's terrible ocean.

Bhairo body

The unit concerns Bhairo's teaching that rituals, austerities, learning, pilgrimage, and religious dress are useless without God's name. It states that salvation is obtained through God's name and the Guru's instruction. It describes God as present in all creatures and says that Nanak drank God's elixir after stirring it. Man is portrayed as entangled in transmigration unless he repeats God's name.

Rag Basant body

Rag Basant praises the great month in which spring begins and urges remembrance of the Sustainer of the earth. It teaches that pride should be subdued and that meditation on God and excellent virtues bring joy and spiritual wealth. The passage describes good acts, God's name, religion, divine knowledge, attainment of God, and dispelling mental pride through a tree metaphor. Nanak teaches that the pious who remain absorbed in God shall be perennial and never wither.

Sarang ki War body

Sarang ki War teaches that impurity is not found in songs, knowledge, natural elements, or ablution, but in turning away from God. Nanak describes inner virtues and practices, including divine knowledge, continence, contentment, justice, meditation, mercy, forgiveness, truth, and good deeds. The unit criticizes idol worship, false wisdom, dishonest religious conduct, and gurus or pirs who beg, while praising those who earn honestly and give to others. It also teaches that God's will determines events and that God's name gives sight to the blind.

Rag Malar .. Malar ki War body

Rag Malar teaches that people should renounce covetousness, pride, slander, lust, wrath, and attachment to worldly distinctions, and should seek God through the Guru's instruction, God's name, and association with the saints. Malar Ashtapadi describes longing for the Beloved through images of the chakwi, lotus, chatrik, fish, and separated woman, teaching that God is found within through the Guru's favour. Malar ki War contrasts worldly bondage, transient wealth, beauty, nobility, and youth with liberation through recognizing the True One and the Word. Nanak praises God's name as the cooling remedy for the world's fire and blesses the means by which the True Name is written and divine knowledge is received.

Rag Prabhati .. Prabhati Ashtapadi body

Rag Prabhati concerns Jogis, Sanyasis, Brahmans, the Word, the Guru, and devotion to God. It teaches that religious acts without knowledge of God are futile, while those tinctured by the Word become perfect Bairagis and are united with the True One. Prabhati Ashtapadi rejects yogic breath practices, ritual purification, austerities, and ceremonies when they are performed without the true Guru or repetition of the Name. It presents worldly desire, pride, false wisdom, and evil speech as sources of defilement, strife, and separation from God.
