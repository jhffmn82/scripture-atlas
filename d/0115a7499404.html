Abstract written by a model

This record summarizes the First Book of Nephi, centered on Lehi’s family, Sariah and their sons Laman, Lemuel, Sam, and Nephi, and Nephi’s account of their departure from Jerusalem, journey, and teachings. Lehi receives visions, warns the Jews of Jerusalem’s destruction, and is threatened for prophesying. Commanded by the Lord, he leaves his possessions and leads his family into the wilderness. Nephi is repeatedly portrayed as faithful, while Laman and Lemuel often murmur and rebel. The family returns to Jerusalem twice: first, Nephi and his brothers obtain the plates of brass from Laban after Nephi kills Laban by command and brings Zoram with them; then they bring Ishmael’s family so the sons may take wives. Lehi examines the plates, teaches and prophesies, and recounts a symbolic dream of a tree, iron rod, river, darkness, and great building. Nephi receives related visions of the love of God, the Messiah’s life and death, the apostles, his descendants, Gentiles, Israel’s scattering and gathering, and future records restoring plain and precious parts of the gospel. In the wilderness, the family is guided according to faith, faces hunger and rebellion, and is sustained after Nephi makes a new bow and finds food. At Bountiful, Nephi builds a ship despite his brethren’s opposition. During the sea journey, they bind him, the compass stops, and a storm arises; when freed, Nephi prays and the family reaches the promised land, where they find abundance. Nephi then explains his record-making and teaches from the plates of brass, including writings concerning the God of Israel’s rejection, crucifixion, judgments, salvation, and Israel’s future restoration. The closing chapters address Jacob and Israel: condemnation for treachery, promises of redemption from Babylon, comfort and gathering for scattered Israel, salvation reaching the Gentiles, destruction for those opposing Zion, and preservation and final salvation for those who obey God’s commandments and endure to the end.

The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry body

The First Book of Nephi, His Reign and Ministry concerns Lehi, his wife Sariah, and their four sons: Laman, Lemuel, Sam, and Nephi. The Lord warns Lehi to leave Jerusalem because he prophesies about the people’s iniquity and they seek to destroy his life. Lehi’s family travels into the wilderness, returns to Jerusalem for the record of the Jews, takes the daughters of Ishmael as wives, and later reaches the large waters. Nephi builds a ship after his brethren rebel, and the family crosses the large waters to the promised land.

Chapter 1 body

Nephi introduces himself, his family, his record, and the language and knowledge used to make it. Lehi prays for his people, receives visions of God, angels, a book, and the destruction of Jerusalem, and then prophesies to the Jews. The Jews mock and oppose Lehi, becoming angry and seeking his life. Nephi declares that the tender mercies of the Lord are over those chosen because of their faith, to make them mighty unto deliverance.

Chapter 2 body

The Lord warns Lehi in a dream that people seek to take his life and commands him to take his family into the wilderness. Lehi leaves his house, inheritance, gold, silver, and precious things, taking only his family, provisions, and tents, and travels to a valley near the Red Sea. The Lord speaks through Lehi and Nephi to Laman, Lemuel, and Sam, while Laman and Lemuel murmur and refuse to hearken. The Lord promises Nephi prosperity, a land of promise, and authority over his brethren if he keeps the commandments, while warning of a curse and a scourge for rebellion.

Chapter 3 body

Nephi returns to his father, who tells him that the Lord commanded Nephi and his brothers to return to Jerusalem to obtain the records on the plates of brass from Laban. Nephi agrees to go, and he and his brothers first send Laman to request the records, then offer Laban their gold, silver, and precious things, but Laban rejects them and attempts to seize their property. After Nephi and his brothers flee into the wilderness, Laman and Lemuel mistreat Nephi and their other younger brothers until an angel of the Lord commands them to return to Jerusalem and promises that the Lord will deliver Laban into their hands. Laman and Lemuel continue to question how Laban can be delivered, since he is powerful.

Chapter 4 body

Nephi persuades his brethren to return to Jerusalem, enters the city by night, and finds Laban drunk outside his house. Led by the Spirit and commanded by the Lord, Nephi kills Laban and takes the plates of brass, which contain the law needed for his people to keep the commandments of the Lord. Nephi disguises himself in Laban’s garments and persuades Zoram to retrieve the plates and leave Jerusalem with him and his brethren. Zoram swears an oath to remain with them, and they all depart into the wilderness to Nephi’s father.

Chapter 5 .. Chapter 8 body

Lehi’s family returns to the wilderness with the plates of brass, and Lehi and Sariah rejoice after learning that the Lord protected Nephi and his brothers. Lehi searches the plates, learns of his genealogy and their contents, and prophesies concerning his seed and the records. The Lord commands Nephi and his brothers to bring Ishmael and his family from Jerusalem, and after a rebellion they return safely. Lehi then recounts a dream in which a tree, its fruit, a river, an iron rod, a path, darkness, and a great building represent dangers and choices affecting his family and their seed.

Chapter 9 .. Chapter 12 body

Nephi records Lehi’s teachings about the Jews, the Messiah, the gospel, the Gentiles, and the scattering and gathering of the house of Israel. Nephi receives a vision in which an angel shows him the tree of life, the virgin and the Son of God, the Messiah’s baptism, ministry, crucifixion, resurrection, and the work of the twelve apostles of the Lamb. He also sees the land of promise, the destruction of many cities, the appearance of the Lamb of God, and the ministry of twelve disciples among his seed. The angel explains that the tree of life represents the love of God, the rod of iron represents the word of God, the mists of darkness represent the temptations of the devil, and the large and spacious building represents vain imaginations and pride.

Chapter 13 .. Chapter 14 body

Nephi sees the nations and kingdoms of the Gentiles, the formation and works of the great and abominable church, and the scattering and suffering of the seed of his brethren. He learns that the book of the Lamb of God came from the Jews, that plain and precious parts of the gospel and covenants were taken away, and that further records will restore them. The Gentiles may be numbered among the house of Israel if they hearken to the Lamb of God and repent, while the church of the Lamb and the covenant people are armed with righteousness against the great abominable church. Nephi is shown that John, one of the twelve apostles of the Lamb, will write the remainder of the vision concerning the end of the world.

Chapter 15 .. Chapter 16 body

Nephi explains to his brethren the meanings of his father's dream, including the tree of life, the rod of iron, the river of water, the separation of the wicked and righteous, and the future restoration and grafting in of the remnant of Israel. The Lord commands Nephi's father to lead the family into the wilderness, where the ball guides their journey and provides written directions according to their faith and diligence. After Nephi's bow breaks, the family suffers famine and murmurs against the Lord, but Nephi makes a new bow, obtains food, and the family is blessed. Ishmael dies at Nahom, after which the daughters of Ishmael and others murmur and seek to return to Jerusalem, but the Lord chastens them and provides food again.

Chapter 17 .. Chapter 18 body

Nephi and his family travel through the wilderness to the land of Bountiful, where the Lord commands Nephi to construct a ship. Nephi makes tools, builds the ship according to the Lord’s instructions, and rebukes his brethren when they murmur against him. During the voyage, Laman, Lemuel, the sons of Ishmael, and their wives bind Nephi, causing the compass to stop and a great storm to arise. After Nephi is loosed, he prays, the storm ceases, and the family arrives in the promised land, where they plant seeds and find abundant crops, animals, and ore.

Chapter 19 body

Nephi explains that the Lord commanded him to make plates of ore and record his father’s record, their journey in the wilderness, and prophecies. He teaches that the God of Israel will be rejected, scourged, crucified, and buried, while signs and judgments will come upon the house of Israel and Jerusalem. Nephi reads from the plates of brass, including the writings of Isaiah, to teach his people about the Lord and give them hope. The unit also describes the future remembrance of covenants, gathering of Israel, and salvation for all nations, kindreds, tongues, and peoples.

Chapter 20 body

The Lord addresses the house of Jacob, who are called Israel and come forth from the waters of Judah or baptism, but who swear by the Lord without truth or righteousness. The Lord explains that he declared former and hidden things because the house of Jacob was obstinate and treacherous, and he refined it in the furnace of affliction for his own name’s sake. The Lord promises to fulfill his word against Babylon and the Chaldeans, and calls Jacob to leave Babylon and declare that the Lord has redeemed his servant Jacob. The Lord says that peace would have been like a river if Jacob had hearkened to his commandments, but there is no peace to the wicked.

Chapter 21 body

The Lord addresses the house of Israel, including those broken off and scattered because of the wickedness of the pastors of the people. He describes his servant as a light to the Gentiles and as a means of salvation unto the ends of the earth, and promises to restore Israel, comfort the afflicted, and gather its people from far places. Zion is assured that the Lord has not forgotten her, will protect her children, and will cause kings and queens to support her. The Lord declares that he will deliver the captives, save the children of Israel, and make all flesh know that he is their Savior and Redeemer.

Chapter 22 body

Nephi explains to his brethren that the plates of brass concern the temporal and spiritual scattering and gathering of the house of Israel. He teaches that the Lord God will raise up a nation among the Gentiles, perform a marvelous work, reveal his covenants and gospel, and gather Israel. The wicked, the great and abominable church, and all who fight against Zion will be brought down, while the righteous will be preserved. Nephi concludes that the writings testify that people must obey the commandments of God and endure to the end to be saved at the last day.
