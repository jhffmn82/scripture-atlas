Abstract written by a model

This document is the Book of Malachi, four chapters of prophetic speech in which the LORD of Armies addresses Israel through Malachi, rebuking the people and especially the priests for unfaithfulness while promising purification, judgment, and blessing. In chapter 1 the LORD affirms his love for Israel by contrasting Jacob with Esau, whose land Edom he has made desolate and will keep from rebuilding. He then accuses the priests of despising his name by offering polluted bread and blind, lame, or sick animals, gifts they would never give their governor, and he declares that his name is great among the nations and curses the deceiver who sacrifices a defective animal. Chapter 2 warns the priests of a curse, contrasting them with Levi, who revered the LORD, taught true law, and turned many from iniquity, whereas they have caused many to stumble and corrupted the covenant. It then charges Judah with treachery, including marrying the daughter of a foreign god, with faithlessness to the wife of one's youth and with divorce, and with wearying the LORD by calling evildoers good. Chapter 3 announces a messenger who will prepare the way before the Lord's sudden coming to his temple, like a refiner's fire and launderers' soap, purifying the sons of Levi and bringing swift judgment on sorcerers, adulterers, perjurers, and oppressors of workers, widows, orphans, and foreigners. Because the LORD does not change, Jacob's sons are not consumed; he calls them to return, accuses them of robbing him in tithes and offerings, and invites them to bring the whole tithe and test him for overflowing blessing. Against the complaint that serving God is vain, those who feared the LORD are recorded in a book of memory and will be spared as his own possession. Chapter 4 describes the coming day burning like a furnace over the proud and wicked, while for those who fear his name the sun of righteousness rises with healing. The people are told to remember the law of Moses, and the LORD promises to send Elijah before the great and terrible day to turn the hearts of fathers and children, lest he strike the earth with a curse.

The Book of Malachi / Chapter 1 body

In Malachi chapter 1, the LORD tells Israel through Malachi that he has loved them, and when they ask how, he points to his love for Jacob over his brother Esau, whose mountains he made a desolation. Edom says it will rebuild, but the LORD of Armies says he will throw down what they build, and they will be called 'The Wicked Land.' The LORD then rebukes the priests for despising his name by offering polluted bread and blind, lame, and sick animals on his altar, which they would not dare present to their governor. He declares that he has no pleasure in them and will not accept their offerings, while his name is great among the nations, with pure offerings made everywhere. The chapter closes with a curse on the deceiver who vows a good male from his flock but sacrifices a defective one to the LORD, the great King.

Chapter 2 body

In Chapter 2, the LORD of Armies addresses the priests, warning that he will send a curse on them and rebuke their offspring if they do not take his commandment to heart and honor his name. He contrasts them with Levi, with whom his covenant of life and peace stood, who revered him, taught true law, and turned many from iniquity. The priests have turned from the path, caused many to stumble in the law, and corrupted the covenant of Levi, so they have been made contemptible before the people. The text then turns to Judah and Israel, who deal treacherously against one another, profane the covenant by marrying the daughter of a foreign god, and face being cut off. The LORD also rejects offerings covered with tears because of faithlessness to the wife of one's youth, condemns hating and divorcing, and charges the people with wearying him by claiming evildoers are good in his sight.

Chapter 3 body

In this chapter the LORD of Armies announces that he will send a messenger to prepare the way before him, followed by the sudden coming of the Lord to his temple. His coming is likened to a refiner's fire and launderers' soap, purifying the sons of Levi so that the offerings of Judah and Jerusalem will again please him, and he promises swift judgment on sorcerers, adulterers, perjurers, and those who oppress workers, widows, orphans, and foreigners. Because the LORD does not change, the sons of Jacob are not consumed, and he calls them to return to him, charging that the nation robs him in tithes and offerings and is cursed. He invites them to bring the whole tithe into the storehouse and test him, promising overflowing blessing, protection of their crops, and that all nations will call them blessed. The people's harsh words that serving God is vain and that the proud and wicked prosper are answered by the account of those who feared the LORD, whose names are written in a book of memory and who will be spared as his own possession.

Chapter 4 body

The LORD of Armies announces that the coming day will burn like a furnace, consuming the proud and all who work wickedness so that nothing remains of them, neither root nor branch. For those who fear his name, the sun of righteousness will rise with healing in its wings, and they will leap like calves of the stall and tread down the wicked, who will become ashes under their feet. The LORD calls the people to remember the law of Moses his servant, given at Horeb for all Israel as statutes and ordinances. He promises to send Elijah the prophet before the great and terrible day of the LORD comes. Elijah will turn the hearts of fathers to children and children to fathers, so that the LORD does not come and strike the earth with a curse.
