Vendidad (Vendîdâd)

author unknown · 15 sections
Vendidad (Vendîdâd), translated by James Darmesteter and L. H. Mills, Sacred Books of the East vols. 4, 23 and 31 (1880-1887), public domain. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://sacred-texts.com/zor/

Abstract written by a model

These records summarize Fargards IX–XXII, a sequence of teachings in which Zarathustra repeatedly asks Ahura Mazda how to protect and restore the world against death, defilement, disease, demons, and wrongdoing. The account begins with the nine-night Barashnûm cleansing for contamination by the dead: prepared ground, holes, sprinkling, washing, recitations, seclusion, rewards for competent cleansers, and penalties for ignorant practice. Further sections prescribe sacred words, ritual spaces, and cleansing chants to repel the Drug Nasu, Aêshma, Angra Mainyu, and other harmful beings from people, homes, fire, water, earth, animals, and creation. It then regulates mourning, cleansing a bereaved household, and the treatment of dogs, especially water dogs. Killing or mistreating such animals brings major penalties; killing a water dog requires extensive restitution and atonement, with holiness or wickedness as the final consequence. Other laws identify grave, unatoned sins involving false teaching, harm to dogs or pregnant beings, sexual acts, miscarriage, and failure to support pregnant women and bitches. Menstruation, bodily emissions, hair, nails, and personal ritual observance are also governed by rules of distance, disposal, seclusion, cleansing, and punishment. Later teachings define an Âthravan and describe how Sraosha, Fire, Parôdars, sacred clothing, discipline, and atonement oppose Death and the Drug. Angra Mainyu’s attempt to destroy Zarathustra fails when Zarathustra recites the Ahuna-Vairya and refuses to abandon the good law. Ahura Mazda also explains the righteous soul’s crossing of the Kinvad bridge, guidance by the well-shapen maid, and arrival at Garô-nmânem, while the Daêvas retreat to hell. The sequence concludes with healing. Thrita is named the first healer, receiving remedies and opposing sickness, fever, death, and pain. Waters, heavenly lights, healing spells, Airyaman, Mãthra Spenta, and Nairyô-sangha are invoked to bring health, fertility, purification, and protection. Finally, after Angra Mainyu creates diseases, Airyaman arrives with animals and nine twigs, makes nine furrows, and drives away diseases, deaths, demons, and afflictions.

Vendidad (Vendîdâd) front matter

Fargard IX. body

Fargard IX concerns the nine-night Barashnûm, a ritual for cleansing a person defiled by the dead. Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda how the cleansing should be performed, and Ahura Mazda describes the prepared ground, holes, furrows, sprinkling, washing, recitations, and periods of seclusion. The unit also sets rewards for those who cleanse others, penalties for those who perform the rites without knowing them, and the consequences for the material world. It teaches that the Drug Nasu is driven away by proper cleansing and that sweetness, health, and growth return only after an Ashemaogha is killed and Sraosha is offered sacrifice for three days and three nights.

Fargard X. body

Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda how to fight the Drug that rushes from the dead upon the living and defiles them. Ahura Mazda instructs him to recite specified words in the Gâthas twice, thrice, or four times, followed by fiend-smiting and most-healing words that drive away various fiends and defilements. He commands Zarathustra to dig nine holes in a dry, sparsely wooded part of the ground and teaches that purity is the greatest good next to life. Purity can be won by cleansing oneself with good thoughts, words, and deeds according to the law of Mazda.

Fargard XI. body

Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda how to cleanse the house, fire, water, earth, cow, tree, faithful people, celestial bodies, boundless light, and all good things made by Mazda. Ahura Mazda answers that cleansing is performed by chanting cleansing words, including repeated Ahuna-Vairyas, Mazdâ ad môi, and Airyama-ishyô. The words drive away Aêshma, the Nasu, defilements, several named demons, the Pairika, the demon of uncleanness, and Angra Mainyu. The unit teaches that the law of holiness and the prescribed chants protect and purify the material world and its inhabitants.

Fargard XII. body

Fargard XII. concerns the periods of mourning required after the death of relatives and the cleansing of the house afterward. Ahura Mazda states that the required stays range from thirty or sixty days for parents and children to six months or a year for the master or mistress of the house, with shorter periods for more distant relatives. The house is cleansed by washing bodies and clothes, chanting the Gâthas, offering sacrifice and baresma, and bringing libations to the good waters before the waters, fire, and Amesha-Spentas may enter. Ahura Mazda also teaches that a dead stranger who did not profess the true faith no longer directly or indirectly defiles creation, unlike the living Ashemaogha.

Fargard XIII. body

This unit concerns Ahura Mazda’s teachings about dogs, their protection of people and property, and penalties for harming or neglecting them. It identifies the dog Vanghâpara as a good creature of the good spirit and the daêva Zairimyangura as an evil creature of the evil spirit. It classifies shepherd’s dogs, house dogs, Vohunazga dogs, young dogs, mad dogs, and water dogs, describing their duties, treatment, and punishments connected with them. It also teaches that killing a water dog causes drought until the murderer is killed and a sacrifice is offered.

Fargard XIV. body

Fargard XIV. records Zarathustra’s question to Ahura Mazda about the penalty for killing a water dog. Ahura Mazda prescribes extensive atonements, including stripes, offerings, killing harmful creatures, repairing or providing implements and buildings, giving land and livestock, and performing other acts for godly men. The atonement includes a rill of running water, a piece of arable land, a house with ox-stalls, and a virgin maid. Whoever completes the penalty enters the world of the holy ones, while whoever does not falls into the world of the wicked.

Fargard XV. body

Fargard XV concerns five unconfessed and unatoned sins that make a committer a Peshôtanu, including teaching a wrong creed, harming dogs, harming a pregnant bitch, intercourse with a menstruating woman, and intercourse with a pregnant woman when mischief follows. It also concerns damsels who conceive, induced miscarriage, and the duties of men and others to support pregnant females and their offspring. Ahura Mazda teaches that the faithful must support pregnant women and bitches, with failure causing the penalty for wilful murder. The unit further describes breeding a bitch with three dogs and the penalty for striking her after she has milk and bears a young one.

Fargard XVI. body

This unit concerns the treatment and seclusion of a woman who has an issue of blood in the house of a worshipper of Mazda. Ahura Mazda commands that she be kept away from the fire, water, consecrated bundles of baresma, and faithful, and that food be brought to her from three paces away in vessels of common metal. The woman remains in the place of infirmity while the issue continues, and she is washed with gômêz and water. It also sets penalties for suppressing the issue, repeatedly touching her, and having sexual intercourse with her.

Fargard XVII. body

Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda which deed most increases the baleful strength of the Daêvas. Ahura Mazda says that dropping combed hair, shaved hair, or pared nails into a hole or crack without observing the lawful rites produces Daêvas and lice in the earth. He prescribes distances, holes, furrows, spoken words, and repeated recitation of the Ahuna-Vairya for disposing of hair and nails, warning that undedicated nails become weapons for the Mâzainya Daêvas.

Fargard XVIII. body

Ahura Mazda teaches Zarathustra who may be called an Âthravan, what increases the unseen power of Death, and how Sraosha, Fire, and Parôdars oppose harmful forces. The unit describes the Drug’s four males and the acts that cause or counteract her offspring, including neglect of wealth-sharing, improper urination, nocturnal emission, and going without the sacred girdle and sacred shirt after fifteen. Ahura Mazda also describes Gahi’s harm and prescribes severe atonement for intercourse with a woman who has an issue of blood. Those who fulfill the required atonement enter the world of the holy ones, while those who do not fall into the world of the wicked.

Fargard XIX. body

Angra Mainyu sends the Drug to destroy Zarathustra, but Zarathustra repels it by chanting the Ahuna-Vairya and refuses to renounce the good law of the worshippers of Mazda. Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda how to cleanse Vohu-manô and people from defilement, and Ahura Mazda instructs him to invoke sacred beings and perform prescribed rites. Ahura Mazda also describes the soul’s journey after death: the soul of the righteous crosses the Kinvad bridge, is guided by the well-shapen maid, and reaches Garô-nmânem. The Daêvas flee from Zarathustra and retreat into the depths of hell.

Fargard XX. body

The unit concerns Thrita, whom Ahura Mazda identifies as the first healer among the strong men of yore. Thrita drove back sickness and death and turned away the point of the poniard and the fire of fever from mortal bodies. He obtained a source of remedies, while Ahura Mazda brought down healing plants growing around Gaokerena. The text invokes health upon mortal bodies and commands sickness, death, pain, fever, disease, the Drug, and other harmful beings to depart.

Fargard XXI. body

Fargard XXI concerns waters, light, healing, fertility, and the driving away of sickness, death, and hostile beings. It calls on clouds, the sun, moon, and stars to rise and bring waters, light, growth, health, and healing. It describes holy spells that purify children and women and make women rich in children and milk. It invokes Airyaman and other sacred formulas against diseases, deaths, Yâtus, Pairikas, Gainis, and the fiendish Drug.

Fargard XXII. body

Ahura Mazda tells Spitama Zarathustra that Angra Mainyu created many diseases through witchcraft after looking at the mansion made by Ahura Mazda. Ahura Mazda asks Mãthra Spenta and then Airyaman to heal him, offering sacrifices and a holy blessing-spell in return. Ahura Mazda sends Nairyô-sangha to deliver the request to Airyaman. Airyaman comes with animals and nine twigs, draws nine furrows, and drives away diseases, deaths, demons, and other afflictions.
