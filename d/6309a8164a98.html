Abstract written by a model

The Master teaches that virtue, proper conduct, and the right way should guide personal life, government, relationships, and responses to hardship. He explains that the superior man follows righteousness, values virtue over comfort or gain, and remains committed to it in haste and danger. The text also describes duties of a son toward parents and warns against excessive remonstrance in service and friendship. Tsang identifies the Master's doctrine of an all-pervading unity as being true to one's nature and benevolently exercising it toward others.
