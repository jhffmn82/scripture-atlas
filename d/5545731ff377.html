Abstract written by a model

This is the book of Haggai, a short prophetic text set in the second year of King Darius, in which the LORD of Armies speaks through the prophet Haggai to Zerubbabel the governor of Judah, Joshua the high priest, and the remnant of the people. In chapter 1, the LORD rebukes them for living in paneled houses while his house lies waste, ties their poor harvests to that neglect, and commands them to build. The leaders and people obey, the LORD affirms he is with them, and work begins on the twenty-fourth day of the sixth month. Chapter 2 gives three further messages. The people are told to be strong because the house's latter glory will exceed the former. Haggai uses the priests' answers on holiness and uncleanness to show the people's unclean state, and the LORD promises blessing from that day. Finally, he promises to shake the heavens and earth, overthrow kingdoms, and make Zerubbabel his signet ring.

The Book of Haggai front matter

Chapter 1 body

In the second year of King Darius, the LORD of Armies sends a message through the prophet Haggai to Zerubbabel the governor of Judah and Joshua the high priest. The people claim it is not yet time to build the LORD's house, but the LORD asks why they dwell in paneled houses while his house lies waste. He tells them to consider their ways, pointing out that their labor yields little because of drought and loss, which he attributes to their neglect of his house. He commands them to bring wood and build, promising to take pleasure in it and be glorified. Zerubbabel, Joshua, and the remnant obey, the LORD affirms 'I am with you,' stirs their spirits, and they begin work on the house on the twenty-fourth day of the sixth month.

Chapter 2 body

In Haggai chapter 2, the LORD's word comes through Haggai the prophet on three occasions in the seventh and ninth months of Darius's second year. First he tells Zerubbabel, Joshua the high priest, and the remnant to be strong and work on the seemingly humble house because the LORD is with them, promising to shake the heavens, earth, and nations, fill the house with glory, and make its latter glory greater than the former. Then Haggai asks the priests about holiness and uncleanness, using their answers to show that the people and their offerings are unclean, and recalls how blight, mildew, and hail reduced their harvests before the temple's foundation was laid. The LORD promises to bless them from that day. Finally the LORD tells Zerubbabel that he will shake the heavens and earth, overthrow the kingdoms and their chariots, and make Zerubbabel his chosen signet ring.
