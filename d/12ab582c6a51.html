This document is a letter from Paul and Timothy to the saints and faithful brothers at Colossae, presented in four chapters. It centers on Christ's supremacy and what that means for how the Colossians live. Chapter 1 opens with thanksgiving for their faith, love, and hope, learned from Epaphras, and a prayer that they be filled with the knowledge of God's will and bear fruit. It portrays the Son as the image of the invisible God, firstborn of all creation, creator of all things, head of the assembly, and reconciler through the blood of his cross. Paul rejoices in his sufferings and proclaims the once-hidden mystery, Christ in you, the hope of glory. Chapter 2 describes Paul's struggle for the Colossians, the Laodiceans, and others who have not seen him, so they may know Christ, in whom all wisdom is hidden. He warns against philosophy, empty deceit, and human tradition, stating that the fullness of the Deity dwells bodily in Christ, who forgave trespasses, nailed the handwriting of ordinances to the cross, and triumphed over principalities and powers. Hence no one should judge them over food, feast days, Sabbaths, or angel worship, and rules like 'Don't handle, nor taste, nor touch' are questioned. Chapter 3 turns practical: having been raised with Christ, readers should set their minds on things above, put to death vices, put off the old man and put on the new, and clothe themselves in compassion, kindness, humility, forgiveness, and love. It gives instructions to wives, husbands, children, fathers, and servants. Chapter 4 closes with directions to masters, a call to persevere in prayer, and wise speech toward outsiders. Tychicus and Onesimus are sent to report and comfort, greetings come from Aristarchus, Mark, Jesus called Justus, Epaphras, Luke, and Demas, the Laodiceans are to be greeted and letters exchanged, Archippus is told to fulfill his ministry, and Paul signs in his own hand, asking them to remember his chains.

Paul’s Letter to the Colossians front matter

Chapter 1 body

Paul and Timothy greet the saints and faithful brothers at Colossae and give thanks for their faith in Christ Jesus, their love for all the saints, and their hope, which they learned from Epaphras. Paul prays that they be filled with the knowledge of God's will, walk worthily, bear fruit, and be strengthened with joy. He describes how God delivered believers from the power of darkness into the Kingdom of his Son, in whom there is redemption and forgiveness of sins. The Son is portrayed as the image of the invisible God, firstborn of all creation, through whom and for whom all things were created, head of the assembly, and the one through whom all things are reconciled through the blood of his cross. Paul then says the Colossians, once alienated, are now reconciled if they continue in the faith, and he rejoices in his sufferings as a servant of the assembly, proclaiming the once-hidden mystery, Christ in you, the hope of glory.

Chapter 2 body

In this chapter the author says he struggles for the Colossians, for those at Laodicea, and for others who have not seen him, so that their hearts may be comforted and they may know the mystery of God and of Christ, in whom all treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden. He urges the recipients to keep walking in Christ as they received him, and warns them not to be robbed by philosophy, vain deceit, and the tradition of men. He explains that in Christ the fullness of the Deity dwells bodily, that they were circumcised, buried, and raised with him, and that God made them alive and forgave their trespasses. The handwriting in ordinances against them was wiped out and nailed to the cross, and Christ stripped the principalities and powers and triumphed over them. On this basis the author tells them not to let anyone judge them over food, drink, feast days, new moons, or Sabbaths, nor rob them through self-abasement and angel worship, and he questions why those who died with Christ submit to rules such as 'Don't handle, nor taste, nor touch'.

Chapter 3 body

Chapter 3 addresses readers who have been raised together with Christ, urging them to seek and set their minds on things above, since they died and their life is hidden with Christ in God. It commands them to put to death earthly vices such as sexual immorality, anger, malice, slander, and lying, because God's wrath comes on the disobedient. It describes putting off the old man and putting on the new man, who is renewed after the image of his Creator, where Christ is all and in all. The readers are told to put on compassion, kindness, humility, forgiveness, and love, to let the peace of God rule and the word of Christ dwell richly, and to do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus. The chapter ends with instructions to wives, husbands, children, fathers, and servants, noting that the wrongdoer will be repaid and there is no partiality.

Chapter 4 body

Chapter 4 closes the letter with final instructions and greetings. Paul tells masters to treat servants justly, urges the recipients to persevere in prayer, including for his own opportunity to speak the mystery of Christ, and to walk wisely toward outsiders with gracious speech. He says Tychicus and Onesimus are being sent to report on his affairs and to comfort the recipients' hearts. Aristarchus, Mark, Jesus called Justus, Epaphras, Luke and Demas send greetings, and Paul asks that the Laodiceans be greeted and that letters be exchanged between the two assemblies. He tells Archippus to fulfill his ministry and signs the letter with his own hand, asking them to remember his chains.
