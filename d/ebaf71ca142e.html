Revelation

author unknown · 22 sections
Revelation, from the World English Bible, Catholic Edition, dedicated to the public domain by eBible.org. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://ebible.org/eng-web-c/

Abstract written by a model

This document is the book of Revelation, twenty-two chapters presented as a vision given by God to Jesus Christ and shown through his angel to John, who writes it for seven assemblies in Asia. The central figures are Jesus, the Lamb who was slain and the Living one holding the keys of Death and Hades; God on the throne; and John, the narrator. Opposing them are the dragon (the old serpent, the devil and Satan), the beast from the sea, the second beast or false prophet, and the great prostitute called Babylon. Chapter 1 opens with John on Patmos seeing one like a son of man among seven golden lamp stands. Chapters 2 and 3 are seven letters to the assemblies (Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia, Laodicea), each praising or rebuking and ending with a promise to the one who overcomes. Chapters 4 and 5 move to heaven, with the throne, twenty-four elders, four living creatures, and a sealed scroll that only the Lamb can open. Chapters 6 to 9 and 11 follow the opening of seven seals and the sounding of seven trumpets, bringing conquest, war, famine, death, cosmic upheaval, and plagues, interrupted in chapter 7 by the sealing of 144,000 and a countless multitude in white robes. Chapter 10 gives John a little book to eat, and chapter 11 describes two witnesses, killed and raised, and the kingdom of the world becoming the Lord's. Chapters 12 to 14 portray the woman, the child, the dragon's war and fall, the two beasts, the mark and the number 666, the Lamb with the 144,000, three angels' proclamations, and a harvest of wrath. Chapters 15 and 16 present seven bowls of God's wrath ending at Harmagedon with "It is done!" Chapters 17 and 18 show the prostitute's destruction and Babylon's fall and mourning by kings and merchants. Chapter 19 celebrates the Lamb's wedding and shows the white-horse rider, the Word of God, defeating the beast and false prophet. Chapter 20 binds Satan for a thousand years, describes the saints' reign, Satan's final defeat, and the judgment before the great white throne. Chapters 21 and 22 close with a new heaven and earth, New Jerusalem, the river and tree of life, God dwelling with people and wiping away tears, a warning not to alter the book, Jesus's promise to come soon, and the prayer "Come, Lord Jesus."

Revelation / Chapter 1 body

Chapter 1 opens the Revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave him to show his servants and which was made known through his angel to John. John greets the seven assemblies in Asia with grace and peace from God, the seven Spirits, and Jesus Christ, and declares that Christ is coming with the clouds. On the isle of Patmos, John is in the Spirit on the Lord's day and hears a voice telling him to write what he sees and send it to seven named assemblies. He sees one like a son of man among seven golden lamp stands, holding seven stars, and falls at his feet as though dead. The figure says he is the first and the last, the Living one who was dead and is alive forever, holds the keys of Death and Hades, and explains that the seven stars are the angels of the seven assemblies and the lamp stands are the assemblies.

Chapter 2 body

Chapter 2 contains four letters addressed to the angels of the assemblies in Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, and Thyatira. The speaker, who describes himself with different titles in each letter, says he knows each assembly's works, praising their perseverance, endurance, love, and faith while rebuking specific failings. Ephesus left its first love, Pergamum tolerates the teaching of Balaam and the Nicolaitans, and Thyatira tolerates Jezebel, who seduces his servants into sexual immorality and eating idol sacrifices. Smyrna is told not to fear coming suffering and to be faithful to death. Each letter ends with a call to hear what the Spirit says and a promise to those who overcome.

Chapter 3 body

In Revelation 3, the speaker, who has the seven Spirits of God and the seven stars, dictates messages to the angels of three assemblies: Sardis, Philadelphia, and Laodicea. Sardis is told it has a reputation for being alive but is dead, and is urged to wake up, remember, keep what it received, and repent, though a few there have not defiled their garments. Philadelphia is praised for keeping his word and not denying his name, is given an open door, and is promised protection from the hour of testing. Laodicea is rebuked as lukewarm, self-satisfied, and in fact wretched, poor, blind, and naked, and is counseled to buy gold, white garments, and eye salve, and to repent. Each message closes with a promise to the one who overcomes and a call to hear what the Spirit says to the assemblies.

Chapter 4 body

The narrator sees a door opened in heaven and hears a trumpet-like voice calling him to come up and be shown what must happen after this. Immediately in the Spirit, he sees a throne in heaven with one sitting on it, who looks like jasper and sardius, with an emerald-like rainbow around it. Around the throne sit twenty-four elders in white garments with gold crowns, and lightnings, sounds, thunders, seven lamps of fire, and a sea of glass like crystal are described. Four living creatures full of eyes, resembling a lion, a calf, a man, and a flying eagle, ceaselessly proclaim God's holiness. When they give glory, the twenty-four elders fall down, worship the one who lives forever, throw their crowns before the throne, and declare him worthy as creator of all things.

Chapter 5 body

The narrator sees a book sealed with seven seals in the right hand of him who sat on the throne, and a mighty angel asks who is worthy to open it. No one in heaven, on earth, or under the earth can, and the narrator weeps until one of the elders tells him the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has overcome. The narrator then sees a Lamb standing as though slain, with seven horns and seven eyes, who takes the book from the one on the throne. The four living creatures and twenty-four elders fall before the Lamb and sing a new song praising him for buying people from every tribe, language, people, and nation with his blood and making them kings and priests. Many angels and then every created thing join in praise of the one on the throne and the Lamb, and the living creatures say "Amen" while the elders fall down and worship.

Chapter 6 body

In Chapter 6 the Lamb opens the first six of the seven seals, and each of the first four living creatures calls out "Come" as a differently colored horse appears. The white horse's rider has a bow and a crown and goes out conquering. The red horse's rider is given power to take peace from the earth and a great sword. The black horse's rider holds a balance, and a voice announces the price of wheat and barley. The pale horse's rider is named Death, followed by Hades, and is given authority over a fourth of the earth. At the fifth seal the souls of those killed for the Word of God cry out under the altar and are given white robes and told to rest a while longer. At the sixth seal a great earthquake and cosmic upheaval occur, and all classes of people hide in caves and rocks, asking the mountains to fall on them because the great day of wrath of the one on the throne and the Lamb has come.

Chapter 7 body

The narrator sees four angels at the four corners of the earth holding back the four winds so that no harm comes to earth, sea, or trees. Another angel rising from the sunrise, holding the seal of the living God, commands them to wait until the bondservants of God are sealed on their foreheads. The narrator hears the number sealed, 144,000, with 12,000 from each of twelve tribes of the children of Israel. He then sees an uncountable multitude from every nation, tribe, people, and language standing before the throne and the Lamb in white robes with palm branches, crying out salvation to God and the Lamb, while the angels, elders, and four living creatures worship. One of the elders explains that the multitude came out of the great suffering, washed their robes in the Lamb's blood, and now serve God in his temple, where he shelters them, the Lamb shepherds them, and God wipes away every tear.

Chapter 8 body

When the seventh seal is opened, heaven falls silent for about half an hour, and seven angels standing before God are given seven trumpets. Another angel with a golden censer adds incense to the saints' prayers at the altar, then fills the censer with altar fire and throws it to the earth, causing thunders, sounds, lightnings, and an earthquake. The seven angels prepare to sound, and the first four trumpets bring destruction in turn: hail and fire mixed with blood burn a third of the earth and trees, a burning mountain turns a third of the sea to blood, the star Wormwood embitters a third of the waters, and a third of the sun, moon, and stars is darkened. An eagle flying in mid heaven cries out a threefold woe on those who dwell on the earth because of the three trumpets still to come.

Chapter 9 body

In Chapter 9, the fifth angel sounds and a star fallen from the sky is given the key to the pit of the abyss; he opens it and smoke darkens the sun and air. Locusts with scorpion-like power come out and torment, but do not kill, people without God's seal for five months, and their king is the angel of the abyss, named Abaddon in Hebrew and Apollyon in Greek. After the first woe passes, the sixth angel sounds and a voice from the golden altar orders him to free the four angels bound at the Euphrates. Those angels are freed to kill one third of mankind, and an army of two hundred million horsemen with lion-headed horses kills them by fire, smoke, and sulfur. The rest of mankind does not repent of their idolatry, murders, sorceries, sexual immorality, or thefts.

Chapter 10 body

The narrator sees a mighty angel descend from the sky, clothed with a cloud, with a rainbow on his head, a face like the sun, and feet like pillars of fire. The angel holds a little open book, stands with one foot on the sea and one on the land, and cries out like a roaring lion, whereupon the seven thunders speak. A voice from the sky forbids the narrator to write what the thunders said. The angel swears by the eternal Creator that there will be no more delay and that the mystery of God will be finished in the days of the seventh angel's sounding. The narrator is told to take the little book, eats it, finds it sweet as honey in his mouth but bitter in his stomach, and is told he must prophesy again over many peoples, nations, languages, and kings.

Chapter 11 body

The narrator is given a reed and told to measure God's temple, the altar, and its worshipers, while leaving out the outer court, which has been given to the nations, who will trample the holy city for forty-two months. God's two witnesses prophesy for 1,260 days in sackcloth, wielding fire, power over rain, water, and plagues, until the beast from the abyss kills them. Their bodies lie in the great city, where those who dwell on the earth celebrate for three and a half days, until God's breath enters the witnesses, they stand, and they ascend to heaven in a cloud. A great earthquake then strikes the city, killing seven thousand, and the survivors give glory to God. The seventh angel sounds, voices and the twenty-four elders proclaim that the kingdom of the world has become the Kingdom of the Lord and his Christ, and God's temple in heaven opens, revealing the ark of the covenant amid lightning, thunder, an earthquake, and hail.

Chapter 12 body

Chapter 12 describes a woman clothed with the sun, who is with child and crowned with twelve stars, and a great red dragon with seven heads and ten horns who waits to devour her child. The child is born and caught up to God and his throne, while the woman flees to a place God has prepared in the wilderness. War breaks out in heaven, where Michael and his angels defeat the dragon, the old serpent called the devil and Satan, and he and his angels are thrown down to the earth. A loud voice in heaven proclaims salvation and the Kingdom of God, saying the brothers overcame the accuser by the Lamb's blood and their testimony, and warns the earth of the devil's wrath. The enraged dragon persecutes the woman, who is given eagle's wings and helped by the earth against his river, so he goes off to make war on the rest of her offspring.

Chapter 13 body

The narrator sees a first beast rise out of the sea with ten horns, seven heads, and blasphemous names. The dragon gives it power, a throne, and great authority. Its fatal head wound is healed, the earth marvels and worships both the dragon and the beast, and the beast is permitted to blaspheme God, make war on the saints for forty-two months, and overcome them. A second beast rises out of the earth, speaks like a dragon, exercises the first beast's authority, performs signs including fire from the sky, and deceives those who dwell on the earth into making an image of the first beast. The second beast gives the image breath so it speaks and has those who refuse to worship it killed, forces all people to receive a mark on the right hand or forehead without which none may buy or sell, and the chapter closes by identifying the beast's number as 666, the number of a man.

Chapter 14 body

In Chapter 14 the narrator sees the Lamb standing on Mount Zion with the 144,000, who bear the names of the Lamb and his Father on their foreheads and alone can learn a new song sung before the throne. Three angels then fly or follow with proclamations: the first announces an eternal Good News and the hour of God's judgment, the second announces the fall of Babylon the great, and the third warns that worshippers of the beast and his image, and those who receive his mark, will drink the wine of God's wrath and be tormented forever. A voice from heaven declares blessed the dead who die in the Lord. The chapter closes with a harvest scene in which one like a son of man on a white cloud reaps the earth with a sickle, and another angel gathers the earth's grape clusters and throws them into the great wine press of the wrath of God, from which blood flows up to the horses' bridles for 1,600 stadia.

Chapter 15 body

The narrator sees a great and marvelous sign in heaven: seven angels holding the seven last plagues, which complete God's wrath. He also sees those who overcame the beast, his image, and the number of his name standing on a sea of glass mixed with fire, holding harps of God. They sing the song of Moses and the Lamb, praising God's works and ways and declaring that all nations will come and worship him. The heavenly temple is then opened, and the seven angels come out in pure bright linen with golden sashes. One of the four living creatures gives them seven golden bowls full of God's wrath, and the temple fills with smoke from God's glory and power so that no one can enter until the seven plagues are finished.

Chapter 16 body

A loud voice from the temple commands the seven angels to pour out the seven bowls of God's wrath on the earth. The first four bowls bring sores on those with the mark of the beast, blood in the sea, blood in the rivers and springs, and scorching heat from the sun, while the angel of the waters and the altar affirm God's judgments as righteous. The fifth bowl darkens the beast's throne and kingdom, and the sixth dries up the Euphrates so the kings from the sunrise can come, as three unclean spirits from the dragon, the beast, and the false prophet gather the kings to the place called Harmagedon. The seventh bowl is poured into the air, a voice declares "It is done!", and a great earthquake splits the great city, topples the cities of the nations, and removes islands and mountains. Babylon the great is remembered by God, and huge hailstones fall on people, who still blaspheme God.

Chapter 17 body

One of the seven bowl angels takes the narrator to see the judgment of the great prostitute, who sits on many waters. In the wilderness he sees a woman in purple and scarlet sitting on a scarlet beast with seven heads and ten horns. She holds a golden cup of abominations, bears a mystery name on her forehead, and is drunk with the blood of the saints and martyrs of Jesus. The angel explains the beast, the seven heads as mountains and kings, and the ten horns as ten kings who give their power to the beast and war against the Lamb, who overcomes them. The ten horns and the beast turn on the prostitute and destroy her, as God's words are accomplished, and she is identified as the great city that reigns over the kings of the earth.

Chapter 18 body

In Chapter 18, an angel with great authority descends from the sky and proclaims the fall of Babylon the great, which has become a habitation of demons and unclean spirits because the nations, kings, and merchants shared in her immorality and luxury. A voice from heaven calls God's people to come out of her so they do not share her sins or plagues, and commands that she be repaid double, since she boasts that she sits as a queen and will see no mourning. Her plagues of death, mourning, famine, and burning fire come in one day, and the kings, merchants, ship masters, and mariners stand far off lamenting the loss of her wealth and trade. Heaven, the saints, apostles, and prophets are told to rejoice because God has judged her. A mighty angel casts a millstone into the sea as a sign that Babylon will be thrown down and found no more, with all music, craft, light, and marriage ended in her, because of her sorcery and the blood of prophets, saints, and all the slain found in her.

Chapter 19 body

A great multitude in heaven, together with the twenty-four elders and four living creatures, praises God for judging the great prostitute and avenging his servants, and announces that the wedding of the Lamb has come and his wife has made herself ready in fine linen. An angel tells John to write a blessing on those invited to the wedding supper, and refuses John's attempt to worship him, saying to worship God. John then sees heaven opened and a rider on a white horse called Faithful and True, the Word of God, and KING OF KINGS AND LORD OF LORDS, who judges, makes war, and is followed by armies in heaven. An angel in the sun summons the birds to the great supper of God, and the beast, the kings of the earth, and their armies gather to make war against the rider. The beast and the false prophet are thrown alive into the lake of fire, and the rest are killed by the sword from the rider's mouth while the birds feed on their flesh.

Chapter 20 body

An angel descends from heaven with the key of the abyss and a great chain, seizes the dragon (the old serpent, the devil and Satan), and binds him in the sealed abyss for a thousand years so he cannot deceive the nations. Those who were beheaded for their testimony and refused the beast, its image, and its mark sit on thrones, live, and reign with Christ for a thousand years in the first resurrection, while the rest of the dead wait until the thousand years end. After the thousand years Satan is released, deceives the nations Gog and Magog, and gathers them for war against the camp of the saints and the beloved city, but fire from God devours them. The devil is thrown into the lake of fire and sulfur, where the beast and the false prophet already are. Then, before a great white throne, the dead are judged by their works from the books and the book of life, and Death, Hades, and anyone not written in the book of life are cast into the lake of fire.

Chapter 21 body

The narrator sees a new heaven and a new earth, with the first ones and the sea gone, and the holy city New Jerusalem descending from God like an adorned bride. A voice from heaven declares that God will dwell with people, wipe away every tear, and end death, mourning, crying, and pain. The one on the throne says he makes all things new, names himself the Alpha and Omega, promises the water of life to the thirsty and sonship to those who overcome, and consigns the cowardly, unbelieving, and other wrongdoers to the lake of fire, the second death. One of the seven angels with the bowls then carries the narrator in the Spirit to a high mountain and shows him the bride, the Lamb's wife, measuring the city with a golden reed. The city is described with a wall of jasper, twelve pearl gates bearing the tribes of Israel's names, twelve jeweled foundations bearing the names of the Apostles of the Lamb, and gold streets, and it has no temple or sun because God and the Lamb are its temple and light, while the nations walk in its light and only those written in the Lamb's book of life may enter.

Chapter 22 body

In Revelation 22, an angel shows John a river of the water of life flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb, with the tree of life on either side of it, and describes the city where there is no curse and no night. The angel affirms that the words are faithful and true and rebuffs John when he falls to worship him, saying he is a fellow bondservant and that God alone should be worshiped. Jesus speaks, declaring that he is coming soon with his reward, that he is the Alpha and the Omega, and that he is the root and offspring of David and the Bright and Morning Star. The Spirit and the bride invite the thirsty to take the water of life freely. A warning is given against adding to or taking away from the words of the book, and the book closes with John's prayer "Come, Lord Jesus" and a blessing of grace on the saints.
