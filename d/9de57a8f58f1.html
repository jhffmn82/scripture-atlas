The document is the Book of Joel, in three chapters, centered on the prophet Joel, son of Pethuel, and the LORD's word through him about the coming day of the LORD. Chapter 1 recounts an unprecedented locust devastation that strips vines, fig trees, grain and fields, silences joy, and cuts off offerings from the LORD's house. Joel summons elders, priests and ministers to wear sackcloth, fast, hold a solemn assembly and cry to the LORD, warning that destruction from the Almighty is at hand; he ends with his own cry as fire, drought and suffering animals mark the land. Chapter 2 opens with a trumpet alarm in Zion and the image of a vast, unstoppable army bringing darkness and fire. The LORD then calls his people to return with all their heart, tearing hearts rather than garments, because he is gracious and merciful. After a solemn assembly and the priests' plea, he becomes jealous for his land, removes the northern army, and promises grain, wine, oil, rain and restoration of the years the locusts ate. He also promises to pour out his Spirit on all flesh and save whoever calls on his name. Chapter 3 shifts to judgment: when Judah and Jerusalem are restored, the LORD gathers the nations into the valley of Jehoshaphat to judge them for scattering and selling his people. Tyre, Sidon and Philistia are singled out and promised repayment. The nations are summoned to war at the valley of decision, where the harvest of their wickedness is ripe and the heavens darken as the LORD roars from Zion, a refuge to his people. The book ends with Jerusalem holy, the land abundant, Egypt and Edom desolate for shedding innocent blood, and Judah inhabited forever because the LORD dwells in Zion.

The Book of Joel / Chapter 1 body

Chapter 1 of the Book of Joel opens with the LORD's word coming to Joel, the son of Pethuel, who calls the elders and all inhabitants of the land to hear of a calamity unprecedented in their days and to pass the account to future generations. Successive waves of locusts, grasshoppers and caterpillars, described as a strong, countless nation with lion's teeth, have stripped the vines, fig trees, grain and fields. As a result drunkards lose their wine, farmers and vineyard keepers wail, joy withers, and the meal and drink offerings are cut off from the LORD's house, so the priests mourn. Joel summons the priests and ministers to put on sackcloth, sanctify a fast, call a solemn assembly, and cry to the LORD, warning that the day of the LORD is at hand as destruction from the Almighty. The chapter ends with Joel's own cry to the LORD, as fire devours the wilderness pastures, the water brooks dry up, and the animals groan and pant.

Chapter 2 body

Chapter 2 opens with a trumpet alarm in Zion warning that the day of the LORD is near, describing a vast, unstoppable army that brings darkness, devouring fire, and cosmic shaking. The LORD, however, calls his people to return to him with all their heart, fasting, weeping, and mourning, tearing their hearts rather than their garments, because he is gracious, merciful, and slow to anger. The people, priests, elders, and children are summoned to a solemn assembly, and the priests plead for the LORD to spare his people. The LORD then becomes jealous for his land, pities his people, removes the northern army, and promises grain, new wine, oil, rain, and restoration of the years the locusts ate. He further promises to pour out his Spirit on all flesh, show wonders before the great and terrible day of the LORD, and save whoever calls on his name.

Chapter 3 body

The LORD announces that when he restores the fortunes of Judah and Jerusalem, he will gather all nations into the valley of Jehoshaphat and judge them for scattering Israel, dividing the land, casting lots for his people, and selling them. Tyre, Sidon, and the regions of Philistia are singled out for taking silver, gold, and treasures and for selling Judah's and Jerusalem's children to the Greeks, and the LORD promises to repay them by having Judah's people sell their sons and daughters to Sheba. The nations are summoned to war, to beat plowshares into swords, and to come to the valley of decision, where the LORD will judge them and the harvest and wine press of their wickedness are ripe. The sun, moon, and stars darken as the LORD roars from Zion, yet he is a refuge to his people. The chapter ends with promises that Jerusalem will be holy, the land will flow with wine, milk, and water, Egypt and Edom will become desolate for shedding innocent blood, and Judah will be inhabited forever because the LORD dwells in Zion.
