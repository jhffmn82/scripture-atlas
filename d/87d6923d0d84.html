The First Book of the Maccabees

author unknown · about 99 BC · 17 sections
The First Book of the Maccabees, from the World English Bible, Catholic Edition, dedicated to the public domain by eBible.org. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://ebible.org/eng-web-c/

Abstract written by a model

This document is a sixteen-chapter historical narrative of the Jewish struggle against Greek (Seleucid) rule, and of the rise of the family of Mattathias. It opens with Alexander's conquests and division of his kingdom, from which comes Antiochus Epiphanes. He plunders the Jerusalem temple, bans sacrifice, the Sabbath and circumcision, sets up an abomination of desolation, and burns the law books. Mattathias, a priest at Modin, refuses to sacrifice, kills a Jew who steps up to do so along with the king's officer, and flees to the hills with his five sons. After a thousand die refusing to fight on the Sabbath, his followers decide to resist even then. He dies naming Simon counselor and Judas Maccabaeus captain. Judas defeats Apollonius, Seron, and the armies of Lysias, Gorgias and others, cleanses and rededicates the temple on 25 Chislev in the 148th year, and founds the eight-day festival of dedication. He campaigns against surrounding Gentiles and rescues Jews in Gilead and Galilee. Antiochus dies confessing his evils at Jerusalem, and his young son Eupator, under Lysias, besieges the sanctuary and then makes and breaks a peace. Under Demetrius, Alcimus, the appointed high priest, and Bacchides persecute the faithful. Judas kills Nicanor, a day made a festival, and seeks a treaty with Rome, which sends a bronze-tablet alliance. Judas falls at Elasa fighting Bacchides, and is buried at Modin. His brother Jonathan becomes leader, makes peace with Bacchides, and judges at Michmash. Courted by rival Seleucid claimants Demetrius and Alexander, he becomes high priest under Alexander and later wins confirmation and territory from Demetrius, then from the young Antiochus and Tryphon. He renews ties with Rome and Sparta, but Tryphon seizes him at Ptolemais and later kills him despite a ransom. Simon buries him at Modin, fortifies Judea, and wins release from tribute in the 170th year. He takes Gazara and the Jerusalem citadel, and is honored by the people as leader and high priest until a faithful prophet arises, with Rome and Sparta renewing friendship. Antiochus, son of Demetrius, first confirms Simon's privileges, then repudiates them and sends Cendebaeus against Judea. Simon's sons Judas and John defeat him. Ptolemy son of Abubus, Simon's son-in-law, murders Simon and two sons at a banquet at Dok in the 177th year and tries to seize the country. John, who survives, kills the men sent to kill him, and the narrative points to the chronicles of his high priesthood.

The First Book of the Maccabees front matter

Chapter 1 body

Chapter 1 recounts how Alexander the Macedonian, son of Philip, defeated Darius, conquered much of the earth, and on his deathbed divided his kingdom among his servants, who ruled after him and multiplied evils. From them came Antiochus Epiphanes, who reigned in the 137th year of the Greeks. Under him, transgressors of the law in Israel made a covenant with the Gentiles, built a gymnasium in Jerusalem, and abandoned circumcision. Antiochus conquered Egypt, then plundered the Jerusalem temple, and two years later his chief tribute collector deceitfully attacked and burned the city and fortified the city of David as a citadel. Antiochus decreed that all be one people and forsake their own laws, banned sacrifices, the Sabbath, and circumcision on pain of death, set up an abomination of desolation on the altar, and burned the books of the law, yet many in Israel chose death rather than eat unclean food, and great wrath came upon Israel.

Chapter 2 body

Mattathias, a priest living at Modin with his five sons, laments the desecration of Jerusalem and the Temple. When the king's officers come to Modin to enforce sacrifice and offer him honors for compliance, he refuses publicly, kills a Jew who steps up to sacrifice along with the king's officer, and tears down the altar, then flees with his sons to the mountains. Many faithful refugees who go into the wilderness are attacked on the Sabbath and about a thousand die without resisting, so Mattathias and his friends decide to fight on the Sabbath if attacked. Joined by the Hasidaeans, they raise an army, strike down sinners, pull down altars, circumcise boys by force, and rescue the law from the Gentiles. Before dying in the 146th year, Mattathias exhorts his sons with examples from Israel's heroes, names Simon as counselor and father and Judas Maccabaeus as captain, and is buried at Modin.

Chapter 3 body

Judas Maccabaeus succeeds his father and, with his kindred, fights for Israel, defeating Apollonius and then Seron at the ascent of Bethhoron, where he tells his frightened men that victory comes from heaven rather than numbers. King Antiochus, angered and short of money, leaves for Persia, entrusting half his forces, the elephants, and the charge to destroy Judea to Lysias. Lysias sends Ptolemy, Nicanor, and Gorgias with forty thousand infantry and seven thousand cavalry, who camp near Emmaus while merchants come to buy Israelite captives as slaves. Judas and his people gather at Mizpeh to fast, pray, and consult the book of the law, then organize their ranks, send home those exempted by the law, and camp south of Emmaus. Judas urges his men to be ready to fight, saying it is better to die in battle than to see the nation's and the holy place's calamities, and leaves the outcome to the will of heaven.

Chapter 4 body

Gorgias leads a night march with five thousand infantry and a thousand cavalry to surprise Judas, but Judas slips past him and defeats the king's army at Emmaus, burning its camp and sending Gorgias' men fleeing to Philistine territory. Lysias, hearing of the defeat, gathers sixty thousand infantry and five thousand cavalry the next year, but Judas meets him at Bethsura with ten thousand men after praying, and about five thousand of Lysias' men fall, so Lysias withdraws to Antioch to hire more soldiers. Judas and his kindred then go up to Mount Zion, find the sanctuary desolate, and appoint blameless priests to cleanse it. They pull down the profaned altar, store its stones until a prophet comes, build a new altar of whole stones, and rededicate the temple on the twenty-fifth of Chislev in the 148th year. The people establish an eight-day yearly festival of dedication, and Judas fortifies Mount Zion and Bethsura.

Chapter 5 body

After the Gentiles around Israel grow angry at the rededicated sanctuary and begin attacking Jews, Judas Maccabaeus campaigns against the children of Esau, the children of Baean, and the Ammonites under Timotheus. When Jews in Gilead and Galilee appeal for help, Judas and his brother Jonathan go to Gilead with eight thousand men, while Simon takes three thousand to Galilee, and Joseph son of Zacharias and Azarias are left to guard Judea with orders not to fight. Simon defeats the Gentiles near Ptolemais, and Judas takes Bosora, routs Timotheus, captures other Gilead cities, crosses the brook, destroys Carnaim, and storms Ephron, which had refused him passage, before bringing the Gilead Jews safely to Mount Zion. Meanwhile Joseph and Azarias disobey Judas's order, attack at Jamnia seeking a name for themselves, and are routed by Gorgias with about two thousand dead. Judas and his kindred are glorified, and Judas then strikes Hebron and Azotus, while certain priests who sought exploits are slain.

Chapter 6 body

King Antiochus fails to plunder a rich temple city in Elymais, Persia, then hears that Judas's forces have defeated his armies, torn down the abomination at Jerusalem, and fortified the sanctuary and Bethsura. Grieving, he confesses that his evils at Jerusalem caused his misfortunes, appoints Philip to guide his son, and dies in the 149th year. Lysias sets up the young Antiochus as king, named Eupator, and when Judas besieges the citadel, ungodly Israelites appeal to the king, who marches out with a huge army and elephants. At Bethzacharias Eleazar kills the lead elephant and dies beneath it, but the king's forces take Bethsura and besiege the sanctuary, whose defenders are starving in the seventh year. Hearing that Philip has returned to seize the government, Lysias urges peace; the king swears an oath, breaks it by ordering the wall of Mount Zion pulled down, and returns to Antioch, where he defeats Philip and takes the city.

Chapter 7 body

In the one hundred fifty-first year, Demetrius, son of Seleucus, returned from Rome, took the throne after the army killed Antiochus and Lysias, and heard accusations against Judas from Alcimus and other lawless Israelites. He sent Bacchides and Alcimus, whom he made high priest, to take vengeance; they offered deceitful peace, and Alcimus swore an oath to the Hasidaeans and then had sixty of them killed in one day. Bacchides killed more people, left Alcimus in charge and returned to the king, while Judas struck back at deserters. When Alcimus complained again, the king sent Nicanor, who tried to seize Judas by deceit, lost about five hundred men at Capharsalama, and then mocked the priests in Jerusalem and swore to burn the temple. After the priests prayed and Judas prayed at Adasa, Nicanor was the first to fall in the battle on the thirteenth of Adar, his army was destroyed, his head and right hand were hung beside Jerusalem, and the day was made an annual festival, after which the land had a few days of rest.

Chapter 8 body

Judas hears reports of the Romans' power, including their conquests of the Gauls, Spain, Philip, Perseus, and Antiochus the great king of Asia, as well as their friendship toward allies and their orderly senate and yearly single ruler. Because the Greek kingdom kept Israel in bondage, Judas sends Eupolemus son of John and Jason son of Eleazar to Rome to seek friendship and alliance. The Roman senate is pleased with the request and sends back a treaty written on bronze tables to be kept in Jerusalem. The treaty pledges mutual military help between Rome and the Jews while forbidding supplies to the other side's enemies, and allows amendments by agreement. Rome also states it has written to King Demetrius, asking why he made his yoke heavy on the Jews and warning that it will do them justice and fight him by sea and land if they complain again.

Chapter 9 body

Demetrius sends Bacchides and Alcimus back into Judah, and at Elasa Judas, left with only eight hundred men, refuses to flee and falls in battle after routing Bacchides's right wing. His brothers Jonathan and Simon bury him at Modin, and Israel mourns. The lawless gain power under Bacchides, who persecutes Judas's friends, and the friends choose Jonathan as leader. Jonathan and Simon flee to the wilderness, avenge their brother John against the children of Jambri, escape Bacchides at the Jordan, and later hold Bethbasi against him. After Alcimus dies of a stroke and Bacchides is defeated, Jonathan makes peace with Bacchides, who returns the captives, and Jonathan begins to judge the people at Michmash.

Chapter 10 body

In the 160th year Alexander Epiphanes took Ptolemais, and King Demetrius tried to win the Jew Jonathan with a letter granting authority to raise forces, and the return of hostages. Alexander outbid him by appointing Jonathan high priest and sending a purple robe and golden crown, and Jonathan put on the holy garments at the feast of tabernacles. Demetrius then offered sweeping tax releases and privileges, but Jonathan and the people distrusted him and stayed loyal to Alexander. Alexander was defeated and Demetrius fell in battle, after which Alexander married Ptolemy's daughter Cleopatra at Ptolemais and honored Jonathan there. Later Demetrius, son of Demetrius, returned from Crete, and his officer Apollonius challenged Jonathan, who took Joppa and, with Simon, defeated Apollonius at Azotus, burned the temple of Dagon, and was rewarded by Alexander with a gold buckle and Ekron.

Chapter 11 body

King Ptolemy of Egypt enters Syria with a large force, garrisons its cities, and turns against his son-in-law Alexander, giving his daughter to Demetrius and crowning himself king of Asia at Antioch. Alexander is defeated and beheaded by Zabdiel the Arabian, and Ptolemy dies three days later, leaving Demetrius king. Jonathan besieges the citadel at Jerusalem, wins Demetrius' favor with gifts at Ptolemais, and receives confirmation of the high priesthood and a letter of tax exemptions and added territory for Judea. When Demetrius is threatened by an uprising in Antioch, Jonathan's three thousand men save him, but he then breaks faith with Jonathan, and Tryphon brings the young Antiochus to defeat Demetrius. Antiochus confirms Jonathan's honors and makes Simon governor, and Jonathan and Simon campaign in Gaza, Bethsura and Galilee, where Jonathan rallies his routed army and defeats the foreigners at Hazor before returning to Jerusalem.

Chapter 12 body

Jonathan the high priest sends envoys to Rome and letters to the Spartans to renew friendship, citing an earlier letter from the Spartan king Arius to Onias claiming kinship through Abraham. Jonathan then confronts Demetrius' princes at Hamath, who withdraw at night, and he raids the Zabadaean Arabs and Damascus while Simon takes Ascalon's area and Joppa. Jonathan and Simon fortify Jerusalem, Judea, and Adida. Tryphon, who wants to rule Asia, lures Jonathan to Ptolemais with promises, and the people of Ptolemais seize him and kill the thousand men with him. Jonathan's remaining forces return safely to Judah, where Israel mourns and surrounding Gentiles plan to destroy them.

Chapter 13 body

Simon rallies the frightened people of Judah against Tryphon's invading army, fortifies Jerusalem, and sends Jonathan the son of Absalom to take Joppa. Tryphon, holding Jonathan captive, demands a hundred talents and two of Jonathan's sons; Simon pays out of fear of the people's reaction, but Tryphon does not release Jonathan, and kills him near Bascama before returning to his own land. Simon buries Jonathan at Modin and builds an elaborate monument there for his family. Tryphon kills the young King Antiochus and takes the crown of Asia, while Simon fortifies Judea and wins from King Demetrius a letter granting peace, tax release, and confirmation of the strongholds, which ends the Gentile yoke in the 170th year. Simon then captures Gazara, starves the Jerusalem citadel into surrender and cleanses it, and appoints his son John leader of his forces.

Chapter 14 body

Chapter 14 opens with King Demetrius going into Media to seek help against Tryphon and being captured by Arsaces, king of Persia and Media. It then describes the peace and prosperity of Simon's rule, during which he took Joppa as a harbor, enlarged the nation's borders, took Gazara, Bethsura and the citadel, and glorified the sanctuary. Rome and Sparta, grieved at Jonathan's death, wrote on brass tablets to renew their friendship and alliance with Simon, and Simon sent Numenius to Rome with a gold shield of one thousand minas. In gratitude the people recorded Simon's deeds on bronze tablets set on Mount Zion, confirmed him as leader and high priest forever until a faithful prophet should arise, and set out his authority, including purple and gold and obedience from all. The decree, confirmed earlier by King Demetrius, was accepted by Simon, and copies were to be kept in the sanctuary precinct and the treasury.

Chapter 15 body

King Antiochus, son of Demetrius, writes to Simon the high priest and the Jewish nation confirming tax remissions, the right to coin money, and the freedom of Jerusalem in return for support against Tryphon. Antiochus gathers forces, drives Tryphon to flee to Dor, and besieges the city by land and sea. Envoys led by Numenius arrive from Rome with a letter from the consul Lucius to King Ptolemy and many other rulers and countries, telling them not to harm the Jews and to hand over fugitive troublemakers to Simon. Once the siege is under way, Antiochus rejects Simon's two thousand men and annuls his earlier covenants, then sends Athenobius to demand Joppa, Gazara and the Jerusalem citadel or one thousand talents. Simon refuses and offers one hundred talents, Tryphon escapes by ship to Orthosia, and Antiochus appoints Cendebaeus to attack Judea, where he fortifies Kidron and raids the highways.

Chapter 16 body

John reports from Gazara that Cendebaeus is acting against Judea, and the aged Simon sends his two oldest sons, Judas and John, to fight for the nation. John leads twenty thousand men to defeat Cendebaeus's army near a brook by Modin, pursues it to Kidron and the towers of Azotus, burns them, and returns in peace. Ptolemy son of Abubus, governor of the plain of Jericho and the high priest's son-in-law, plots to seize the country. He receives Simon and his sons Mattathias and Judas at the stronghold of Dok in the eleventh month of the 177th year, and kills them at a banquet. Ptolemy then writes to the king for forces and sends men to kill John at Gazara and to seize Jerusalem, but John learns of it, kills the men sent against him, and the chapter closes by pointing to the chronicles of his high priesthood.
