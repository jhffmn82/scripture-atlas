Genesis

author unknown · about 449 BC · 51 sections
Genesis, from the World English Bible, Catholic Edition, dedicated to the public domain by eBible.org. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://ebible.org/eng-web-c/

Abstract written by a model

This is the book of Genesis, fifty chapters moving from the creation of the world to the settling of one family in Egypt. God creates the heavens and earth in six days, makes man and woman in his image, and rests on the seventh. In Eden the serpent persuades the woman to eat the forbidden fruit, and the pair are expelled. Cain kills Abel, and genealogies run through Seth to Noah. As wickedness and violence grow, God sends a flood, saving Noah, his family and the animals in a ship. After the waters recede he makes a covenant with Noah, sealed by the rainbow. Noah's descendants spread into nations, and at Babel God confuses their language and scatters them. The focus then narrows to Abram, called by the LORD from Haran with promises of land, a great nation and blessing for all families. He is renamed Abraham, and Sarai becomes Sarah. Their story includes Lot's separation, the rescue of Lot, Melchizedek, the covenant of circumcision, Hagar and Ishmael, the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah, and the birth of Isaac. God tests Abraham by commanding him to offer Isaac, then provides a ram instead. After Sarah dies and Abraham buys the cave of Machpelah, a servant finds Rebekah as Isaac's wife. Their twin sons Esau and Jacob quarrel. Esau sells his birthright, and Jacob, helped by Rebekah, obtains Isaac's blessing by deceit. Jacob flees to Laban, dreams at Bethel, marries Leah and Rachel, and fathers twelve sons and a daughter, Dinah. He grows rich, leaves Laban, wrestles with a man at the Jabbok and is renamed Israel. He is reconciled with Esau, but Dinah's violation leads Simeon and Levi to slaughter Shechem's men. Rachel dies giving birth to Benjamin, and Isaac dies. Esau's line in Edom is listed. The last chapters centre on Joseph, Jacob's favored son. His brothers sell him into slavery, and in Egypt he is falsely accused by Potiphar's wife and jailed. After interpreting dreams for the cup bearer and baker, he interprets Pharaoh's dreams of seven years of plenty and seven of famine, and Pharaoh sets him over Egypt. Famine drives his brothers to buy grain. Joseph tests them, and Judah offers himself in Benjamin's place. Joseph then reveals himself, saying God sent him ahead to preserve life. Jacob and seventy of his house move to Goshen. Jacob blesses Ephraim and Manasseh and pronounces on his twelve sons, with Judah exalted. He dies and is buried at Machpelah. Joseph reassures his fearful brothers that God meant their act for good, and he dies in Egypt after making Israel's children swear to carry his bones out.

Genesis front matter

Chapter 1 body

Chapter 1 describes God creating the heavens and the earth over six days. He begins with light and divides it from darkness, then makes the sky, the dry land and seas, and plants. He then makes the sun, moon and stars, the sea creatures and birds, and the land animals. On the sixth day God creates man, male and female, in his own image, gives them dominion and plants for food, and sees that everything he made is very good.

Chapter 2 body

Chapter 2 recounts that the heavens and earth were finished and that God rested on the seventh day, blessing and making it holy. The LORD God formed man from the dust of the ground, breathed life into him, and placed him in a garden in Eden, which was watered by a river that divided into four rivers: Pishon, Gihon, Hiddekel, and the Euphrates. The LORD God commanded the man to eat freely from every tree except the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, on penalty of death. Finding no suitable helper among the animals the man named, the LORD God caused the man to sleep, took one of his ribs, and made a woman, whom the man welcomed as bone of his bones and flesh of his flesh. The chapter closes with the principle that a man will join with his wife and become one flesh, and notes that the two were naked and not ashamed.

Chapter 3 body

The serpent, the most subtle of the animals, persuades the woman to eat the fruit of the tree in the middle of the garden by denying that she will die and promising she will be like God. The woman eats and gives some to her husband, and both realize they are naked and sew fig leaves as coverings. When the LORD God walks in the garden and questions them, the man blames the woman and the woman blames the serpent. The LORD God then pronounces curses on the serpent, the woman, and Adam, and the man names his wife Eve. The LORD God clothes them in animal skins and sends the man out of the garden of Eden, placing cherubim and a flaming sword to guard the way to the tree of life.

Chapter 4 body

Eve gives birth to Cain and then Abel, a tiller of the ground and a keeper of sheep respectively. The LORD respects Abel's offering but not Cain's, and despite the LORD's warning that sin crouches at the door, Cain kills Abel in the field. The LORD curses Cain to be a fugitive and wanderer, marks him with a sign so no one will strike him, and Cain settles in the land of Nod, where he fathers Enoch and builds a city. Cain's descendants include Lamech, whose sons Jabal, Jubal, and Tubal Cain become founders of herding, music, and metalworking, and who boasts of killing a man and of being avenged seventy-seven times. Adam and Eve have another son, Seth, whose son Enosh is born as men begin to call on the LORD's name.

Chapter 5 body

Chapter 5 presents the book of the generations of Adam, recording that God made man in his own likeness and named them Adam. It traces the line from Adam through Seth, Enosh, Kenan, Mahalalel, Jared, Enoch, Methuselah, and Lamech to Noah, giving each man's age at the birth of his heir, the years he lived afterward, and his total lifespan. Each man except Enoch is said to have died. Enoch walked with God and was not found, for God took him. The chapter ends with Lamech naming Noah, who at age five hundred became the father of Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

Chapter 6 body

As men multiplied on the ground, God's sons took wives from among men's daughters, the Nephilim appeared, and the LORD declared that man's days would be one hundred twenty years. The LORD saw that man's wickedness was great and that every thought of his heart was continually evil, and he grieved and resolved to destroy man, animals, creeping things, and birds. Noah, a righteous and blameless man who walked with God and had three sons, Shem, Ham, and Japheth, found favor with the LORD. God, seeing the earth corrupt and filled with violence, told Noah he would end all flesh with a flood, and gave detailed instructions to build a ship of gopher wood, three hundred cubits long, fifty wide, and thirty high. God promised a covenant with Noah and told him to bring his family, two of every kind of creature, and food into the ship, and Noah did all that God commanded.

Chapter 7 body

The LORD tells Noah to enter the ship with his household because he has seen Noah's righteousness, and instructs him to take seven pairs of every clean animal and of the birds and two of each unclean animal, warning that rain will fall for forty days and forty nights after seven days. Noah obeys, and at age six hundred he enters the ship with his sons, wife, and sons' wives, along with the animals that come in pairs, and the LORD shuts him in. On the seventeenth day of the second month the fountains of the great deep burst open and the sky's windows open, and rain falls for forty days. The rising waters lift the ship and cover even the highest mountains by fifteen cubits. Every living thing on dry land dies, including people, livestock, creeping things, and birds, and only Noah and those with him in the ship are left as the waters flood the earth for one hundred fifty days.

Chapter 8 body

God remembers Noah and the animals in the ship and sends a wind over the earth, stopping the fountains of the deep and the windows of the sky so the waters recede. The ship comes to rest on Ararat's mountains in the seventh month, and the mountain tops appear in the tenth month. Noah sends out a raven and then a dove three times; the dove returns with an olive leaf the second time and does not return the third time. Noah removes the ship's covering and sees dry ground, and God tells him to leave the ship with his wife, sons, sons' wives, and all the living creatures. Noah builds an altar and offers burnt offerings of clean animals and birds, and the LORD, pleased by the aroma, resolves never again to curse the ground or strike every living thing, promising that seasons and day and night will continue.

Chapter 9 body

God blesses Noah and his sons, tells them to multiply, gives them every moving living thing for food except flesh with its blood, and declares that anyone who sheds man's blood will have his own blood shed. God then establishes a covenant with Noah, his offspring, and every living creature, promising never again to destroy the earth by flood, and sets his rainbow in the cloud as the sign. The sons of Noah who left the ship are named as Shem, Ham, and Japheth, with Ham the father of Canaan. Noah plants a vineyard, gets drunk, and is uncovered in his tent; Ham sees this and tells his brothers, while Shem and Japheth cover their father walking backwards. Noah, on waking, curses Canaan to be a servant of servants, blesses the God of Shem, and asks that God enlarge Japheth, then lives 350 years after the flood and dies at 950.

Chapter 10 body

Chapter 10 gives the genealogy of the sons of Noah (Shem, Ham, and Japheth), whose descendants were born after the flood. It lists the sons of Japheth, then of Ham, then of Shem, with notes on their lands, languages, and families. Among Ham's descendants, Cush's son Nimrod is singled out as a mighty hunter before the LORD who founded a kingdom beginning at Babel in Shinar and went on to build Nineveh and other cities in Assyria. Among Shem's descendants, Eber's son Peleg is named for the earth being divided in his days, and Joktan's sons are listed with their dwelling place. The chapter ends by stating that the nations divided from the families of Noah's sons on the earth after the flood.

Chapter 11 body

The people of the whole earth, sharing one language, settle on a plain in Shinar and build bricks to construct a city and a tower reaching the sky so they can make a name for themselves and avoid being scattered. The LORD comes down, confuses their language, and scatters them over the earth, so the city is called Babel. The chapter then gives the generations of Shem down through Arpachshad, Shelah, Eber, Peleg, Reu, Serug, and Nahor to Terah, who fathers Abram, Nahor, and Haran. Haran, father of Lot, dies in Ur of the Chaldees, and Abram marries Sarai, who is barren. Terah takes Abram, Lot, and Sarai from Ur toward Canaan, but they settle in Haran, where Terah dies at 205.

Chapter 12 body

The LORD tells Abram to leave his country and relatives for a land he will be shown, promising to make him a great nation and bless all the families of the earth through him. Abram, seventy-five years old, departs Haran with his wife Sarai, his nephew Lot, and their possessions and people, and enters Canaan, where he builds altars at Shechem and near Bethel. A severe famine drives Abram to Egypt, where he asks Sarai to say she is his sister out of fear that the Egyptians will kill him. Pharaoh takes Sarai into his house and treats Abram well, but the LORD afflicts Pharaoh and his house with plagues. Pharaoh confronts Abram, returns Sarai, and has men escort Abram away with his wife and all he has.

Chapter 13 body

Abram leaves Egypt with his wife, Lot, and all his possessions, returning to the South and then to the altar between Bethel and Ai, where he calls on the LORD's name. Because both Abram and Lot have such great livestock holdings, the land cannot support them together, and strife breaks out between their herdsmen. Abram proposes that they separate, offering Lot first choice of direction, and Lot chooses the well-watered Plain of the Jordan and moves his tent toward Sodom, whose men are described as exceedingly wicked. After Lot leaves, the LORD tells Abram to look in every direction, promising him all the land he sees and offspring as numerous as the dust of the earth. Abram then moves his tent to the oaks of Mamre in Hebron and builds an altar to the LORD there.

Chapter 14 body

Four kings led by Chedorlaomer of Elam, along with Amraphel, Arioch, and Tidal, fought five kings of the Salt Sea cities, including those of Sodom and Gomorrah, who had rebelled after twelve years of serving Chedorlaomer. The four kings won in the valley of Siddim, took the goods of Sodom and Gomorrah, and carried off Lot, Abram's brother's son. Abram, living by the oaks of Mamre, led 318 trained men from his house, pursued the captors as far as Dan and Hobah, struck them by night, and brought back Lot, the goods, the women, and the other people. Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, brought out bread and wine and blessed Abram, who gave him a tenth of all. The king of Sodom offered Abram the goods, but Abram refused to take anything of his, except what the young men had eaten and the portion of his allies Aner, Eshcol, and Mamre.

Chapter 15 body

In a vision, the LORD tells Abram not to be afraid and calls himself Abram's shield and great reward. Abram laments that he is childless and that Eliezer of Damascus, a member of his house, would be his heir, but the LORD says Abram's own son will inherit and shows him the stars as a picture of his offspring. Abram believes, and it is credited to him as righteousness. When Abram asks how he will know he will inherit the land, the LORD has him bring and divide animals and birds, and as a deep sleep and terror fall on Abram, the LORD foretells four hundred years of foreign servitude for his offspring before they come out with great wealth. A smoking furnace and flaming torch pass between the pieces, and the LORD makes a covenant giving Abram's offspring the land from the river of Egypt to the Euphrates, naming the peoples living there.

Chapter 16 body

Sarai, Abram's wife, has borne no children, so after Abram has lived ten years in Canaan she gives her Egyptian servant Hagar to him as a wife. Hagar conceives and despises Sarai, and Sarai blames Abram; he tells her Hagar is in her hand, and Sarai deals harshly with Hagar, who flees. The LORD's angel finds Hagar by a fountain on the way to Shur, tells her to return and submit to Sarai, and promises to multiply her offspring. He says she will bear a son named Ishmael, who will be like a wild donkey among men. Hagar names the LORD 'a God who sees,' the well is called Beer Lahai Roi, and Hagar bears Ishmael when Abram is eighty-six.

Chapter 17 body

When Abram is ninety-nine, God Almighty appears to him and establishes an everlasting covenant, renaming him Abraham and promising him many nations, kings, and the land of Canaan. God commands that every male in Abraham's line and household be circumcised at eight days old as the sign of the covenant, on penalty of being cut off. God also renames Sarai as Sarah and promises that she will bear a son, Isaac, with whom the covenant will be established, while Abraham laughs in disbelief and pleads for Ishmael. God promises to bless Ishmael as well, and Abraham circumcises himself, Ishmael, and all the males of his house on the same day.

Chapter 18 body

The LORD appears to Abraham by the oaks of Mamre in the form of three men, and Abraham hospitably runs to feed them with bread, cakes, a calf, butter, and milk. The visitors announce that Sarah will have a son in a year; Sarah, listening from the tent, laughs to herself, then denies it out of fear, and is told she did laugh. The LORD reflects on Abraham's calling to become a great nation and reveals that the cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is great and their sin grievous, so he will go down to see. Abraham stays before the LORD and bargains for Sodom, asking whether he will spare the city for fifty, forty-five, forty, thirty, twenty, and finally ten righteous people, and the LORD agrees each time before departing.

Chapter 19 body

Two angels come to Sodom in the evening, and Lot welcomes them into his house. The men of Sodom surround the house demanding the visitors, Lot offers his two virgin daughters instead, and the angels strike the crowd with blindness and tell Lot to flee. At dawn the angels lead Lot, his wife, and his two daughters out; Lot is allowed to take refuge in Zoar, the LORD rains sulfur and fire on Sodom and Gomorrah, and Lot's wife looks back and becomes a pillar of salt. Abraham sees the smoke the next morning, and God remembers him in saving Lot. Lot then lives in a cave with his daughters, who get him drunk and lie with him to preserve the family line, bearing Moab and Ben Ammi, the ancestors of the Moabites and Ammonites.

Chapter 20 body

Abraham moves to the land of the South and lives as a foreigner in Gerar, where he says his wife Sarah is his sister. Abimelech, king of Gerar, takes Sarah, but God warns him in a dream that he is a dead man because she is a married woman. Abimelech, who had not approached her, protests his innocence, and God confirms this, tells him to restore her, and says Abraham as a prophet will pray for him. Abimelech confronts Abraham, who explains that he feared there was no fear of God in Gerar and that Sarah is his father's daughter and half-sister. Abimelech returns Sarah with livestock, servants, and a thousand pieces of silver, Abraham prays, and God heals Abimelech's household, whose wombs He had closed because of Sarah.

Chapter 21 body

God fulfills his promise to Sarah, who bears Abraham a son, Isaac, when Abraham is one hundred years old; Abraham circumcises him at eight days and holds a great feast when he is weaned. Sarah sees Hagar's son mocking and demands that Abraham cast out Hagar and her son. God tells Abraham to listen to Sarah and promises to make a nation of the servant's son too, so Abraham sends Hagar away with bread and water. In the wilderness of Beersheba, when the water runs out, God hears the boy's voice, the angel of God speaks to Hagar, and God opens her eyes to a well; the boy grows up as an archer in the wilderness of Paran. Abimelech and Phicol ask Abraham to swear fair dealing, Abraham complains about a seized well, and the two make a covenant at Beersheba, sealed with seven ewe lambs, after which Abraham plants a tamarisk tree and calls on the LORD, the Everlasting God.

Chapter 22 body

God tests Abraham by commanding him to offer his only son Isaac as a burnt offering on a mountain in the land of Moriah. Abraham obeys, travels three days with Isaac and two young men, binds Isaac on an altar, and takes up the knife. The LORD's angel stops him from the sky, and Abraham offers a ram caught in the thicket instead, naming the place "The LORD Will Provide." The angel then repeats God's oath to bless Abraham greatly, multiply his offspring, and bless all nations through them. Abraham returns to Beersheba, and he is told of the children born to his brother Nahor by Milcah and by his concubine Reumah, including Bethuel, father of Rebekah.

Chapter 23 body

Sarah dies at age 127 in Kiriath Arba (Hebron) in the land of Canaan, and Abraham mourns and weeps for her. Calling himself a stranger and foreigner, Abraham asks the children of Heth for a burying-place, and they offer him their best tombs. Abraham asks them to intercede with Ephron the son of Zohar to sell him the cave of Machpelah at the end of Ephron's field. Ephron offers to give the field and cave outright, but Abraham insists on paying, and Ephron names a price of four hundred shekels of silver. Abraham weighs out the silver in the hearing of the Hittites, the field, cave, and trees are deeded to him, and he buries Sarah in the cave.

Chapter 24 body

Abraham, old and blessed by the LORD, makes his chief servant swear not to take a Canaanite wife for Isaac but to find one among Abraham's relatives, without taking Isaac back to the old land. The servant travels to the city of Nahor with ten camels and prays at the spring for a sign: the young woman who offers water to him and his camels will be the one appointed for Isaac. Rebekah, daughter of Bethuel, does exactly this, and the servant gives her gold gifts and worships the LORD. He tells her family his story, Laban and Bethuel agree that the matter comes from the LORD, and Rebekah consents to go at once. She travels to Isaac, covers herself with her veil when she sees him, and becomes his wife, and Isaac is comforted after his mother's death.

Chapter 25 body

Abraham takes Keturah as another wife, has children by her, gives everything to Isaac, and sends the sons of his concubines east with gifts. He dies at 175 and is buried by Isaac and Ishmael in the cave of Machpelah beside Sarah. God blesses Isaac, and the chapter lists the twelve sons of Ishmael and notes that Ishmael died at 137. Isaac marries Rebekah at forty, and after he entreats the LORD for her barrenness she conceives twins and is told the elder will serve the younger. Esau and Jacob are born, and Esau, famished, sells his birthright to Jacob for bread and lentil stew.

Chapter 26 body

During a famine, Isaac goes to Gerar, where the LORD tells him not to go to Egypt and renews to him the promise made to Abraham. Isaac passes off Rebekah as his sister out of fear, but Abimelech sees them together, rebukes him, and orders that no one touch Isaac or his wife. Isaac prospers greatly, the Philistines envy him, and Abimelech sends him away; he reopens Abraham's wells and digs new ones, naming them Esek, Sitnah, and Rehoboth. At Beersheba the LORD reassures him, and Abimelech comes with Ahuzzath and Phicol to make a covenant, after which Isaac's servants find water and the well is named Shibah. The chapter closes with Esau marrying Judith and Basemath, Hittite women who grieve Isaac and Rebekah.

Chapter 27 body

The aged and blind Isaac asks his elder son Esau to hunt venison and prepare savory food so that Isaac can bless him before he dies. Rebekah overhears and directs her younger son Jacob to fetch two young goats, which she cooks, and she dresses him in Esau's clothes and goat skins so he can pose as Esau despite his smooth skin. Jacob deceives Isaac, who is suspicious but is convinced by the hairy hands and the smell of the clothing, and receives the blessing of plenty and lordship over his brothers. When Esau returns, Isaac trembles, confirms that the blessing cannot be undone, and gives Esau a lesser blessing of living by the sword and eventually breaking Jacob's yoke. Esau resolves to kill Jacob after Isaac's death, so Rebekah sends Jacob to her brother Laban in Haran and persuades Isaac with her complaint about the daughters of Heth.

Chapter 28 body

Isaac blesses Jacob and commands him not to marry a Canaanite woman, sending him to Paddan Aram to take a wife from the daughters of Laban, his mother's brother. Esau, seeing that Canaanite wives displease Isaac, marries Mahalath, a daughter of Ishmael, in addition to his existing wives. On the way from Beersheba toward Haran, Jacob sleeps at a certain place and dreams of a stairway reaching heaven, where the LORD promises him the land, countless offspring, blessing to all families of the earth, and his own presence. Jacob wakes in awe, sets up his stone pillow as a pillar, anoints it with oil, names the place Bethel, and vows that if God keeps and provides for him and brings him home in peace, the LORD will be his God and he will give a tenth of all.

Chapter 29 body

Jacob travels to the land of the children of the east and meets shepherds at a well, who tell him they are from Haran and know Laban. Rachel, Laban's daughter, arrives with her father's sheep, and Jacob rolls the stone from the well to water them, then weeps and tells her he is her father's relative. Laban welcomes Jacob, and Jacob agrees to serve seven years for Rachel, but Laban gives him Leah in the evening instead, defending this as local custom. Jacob fulfills Leah's week and receives Rachel too, agreeing to serve seven more years, and loves Rachel more than Leah. The LORD sees that Leah is hated and opens her womb, and she bears Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah while Rachel remains barren.

Chapter 30 body

Rachel, barren, demands children from Jacob and gives him her servant Bilhah, who bears Dan and Naphtali; Leah responds by giving Jacob her servant Zilpah, who bears Gad and Asher. Leah then trades her son Reuben's mandrakes to Rachel for a night with Jacob and bears Issachar, Zebulun, and a daughter, Dinah, before God remembers Rachel and she bears Joseph. Jacob then asks Laban to let him leave with his wives and children, but Laban urges him to stay and name his wages. Jacob proposes taking the speckled, spotted, and black animals as his hire, and Laban agrees and removes those animals to his sons, three days' journey away. Jacob uses peeled rods at the watering troughs so the stronger flocks bear streaked and spotted young, and he grows exceedingly rich.

Chapter 31 body

Jacob hears Laban's sons complaining that he has taken their father's wealth and sees that Laban's attitude has cooled, and the LORD tells him to return to the land of his fathers. Jacob explains to Rachel and Leah how God has prospered him and how Laban has cheated him, and they agree to go. Jacob secretly flees with his family and livestock while Laban is shearing sheep, and Rachel steals her father's teraphim. Laban pursues Jacob for seven days to the mountain of Gilead, but God warns him in a dream not to speak to Jacob either good or bad. After Laban searches the tents without finding the teraphim, which Rachel is hiding under her in a camel's saddle, Jacob angrily recounts his twenty years of service, and the two men make a covenant with a heap of stones and a pillar as witness. Jacob offers a sacrifice and a meal, and Laban kisses and blesses his sons and daughters and returns home.

Chapter 32 body

Jacob travels on and is met by angels of God, naming the place Mahanaim. He sends messengers to his brother Esau, who return saying Esau is coming with four hundred men, and Jacob, greatly afraid, divides his company into two, prays to God for deliverance, and prepares a large present of livestock to appease Esau. That night he sends his wives, servants, sons, and possessions across the ford of the Jabbok and is left alone, wrestling with a man until daybreak. The man strains Jacob's thigh, renames him Israel, and blesses him; Jacob names the place Peniel, saying he has seen God face to face, and limps away at sunrise, which is given as the reason the children of Israel do not eat the sinew of the hip.

Chapter 33 body

Jacob sees Esau approaching with four hundred men and arranges his family in order, with the servants first, Leah next, and Rachel and Joseph last. He bows seven times as he approaches, and Esau runs to embrace him, and the two brothers weep. Jacob introduces his children, and each group bows before Esau. Jacob urges Esau to accept his gift, and Esau finally takes it. Esau returns toward Seir, while Jacob goes to Succoth, then to Shechem in Canaan, where he buys a parcel of land from the children of Hamor for one hundred pieces of money and erects an altar called El Elohe Israel.

Chapter 34 body

Dinah, Jacob's daughter, goes out to see the women of the land, and Shechem, son of the Hivite prince Hamor, takes her, lies with her, and humbles her, then asks his father to get her as a wife. Hamor and Shechem propose intermarriage and shared land to Jacob and his sons, and Shechem offers any dowry. Jacob's sons answer deceitfully, demanding that every male of the city be circumcised, and the men of the city agree after Hamor and Shechem persuade them at the gate. On the third day, while the men are sore, Simeon and Levi kill all the males, including Hamor and Shechem, and take Dinah out of Shechem's house. The other sons plunder the city and take its flocks, wealth, women, and children, and Jacob rebukes Simeon and Levi for making him odious among the inhabitants, while they reply by asking whether their sister should be treated like a prostitute.

Chapter 35 body

God tells Jacob to go to Bethel and build an altar, so Jacob has his household give up their foreign gods and rings, which he hides under the oak by Shechem. They travel safely to Bethel, where Jacob builds an altar, Deborah, Rebekah's nurse, dies, and God appears again, renaming Jacob Israel and repeating the promise of land and descendants. Leaving Bethel, Rachel dies in hard labor near Ephrath after giving birth to Benjamin, and Jacob sets up a pillar on her grave. Reuben lies with Bilhah, and the twelve sons of Jacob are listed. Jacob then reaches his father Isaac at Mamre (Hebron), where Isaac dies at one hundred eighty and is buried by Esau and Jacob.

Chapter 36 body

Genesis 36 records the generations of Esau, also called Edom. Esau took wives Adah, Oholibamah, and Basemath, who bore him sons in Canaan, and he then moved with his household and livestock to the hill country of Seir, away from his brother Jacob, because their possessions were too great for them to live together. The chapter lists Esau's descendants and the chiefs from Eliphaz, Reuel, and Oholibamah, including Amalek born to Timna, concubine of Eliphaz. It also lists the sons and chiefs of Seir the Horite, the inhabitants of the land, including Anah who found the hot springs. Finally it names the eight kings who reigned in Edom before any king reigned over Israel, and closes with a list of the chiefs of Edom according to their habitations.

Chapter 37 body

Joseph, seventeen and his father Israel's (Jacob's) favorite, is given a tunic of many colors and tells his brothers two dreams in which they and his parents bow to him, deepening their hatred. Jacob sends him to check on his brothers and the flock; he finds them at Dothan, where they plot to kill him. Reuben persuades them to throw him into an empty pit instead, and Judah then proposes selling him to a passing Ishmaelite caravan; Midianite merchants draw him out and sell him for twenty pieces of silver, taking him to Egypt. The brothers dip his tunic in goat's blood and show it to Jacob, who concludes an animal devoured Joseph and mourns inconsolably. The Midianites sell Joseph in Egypt to Potiphar, Pharaoh's captain of the guard.

Chapter 38 body

Judah leaves his brothers, settles near Hirah the Adullamite, marries Shua's daughter, and has three sons: Er, Onan, and Shelah. Judah gives Er a wife, Tamar, but the LORD kills Er for wickedness, and then kills Onan for refusing to give offspring to his brother through Tamar. Judah tells Tamar to wait as a widow in her father's house until Shelah is grown, but does not give her to him. Tamar veils herself at the gate of Enaim, Judah takes her for a prostitute and gives her his signet, cord, and staff as a pledge, and she conceives. When Tamar is accused of prostitution and sentenced to burning, she produces the pledge items, Judah acknowledges she is more righteous than he, and she bears twins, Perez and Zerah.

Chapter 39 body

Joseph is brought down to Egypt and bought by Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh and captain of the guard, from the Ishmaelites. Because the LORD is with Joseph, he prospers and is made overseer over Potiphar's entire house, and the LORD blesses Potiphar's household for his sake. Potiphar's wife repeatedly asks Joseph to lie with her; he refuses, citing his master's trust and sin against God, and when she catches his garment he flees, leaving it in her hand. She uses the garment to accuse Joseph falsely before the men of her house and then Potiphar, who in wrath puts Joseph into the prison where the king's prisoners are bound. There the LORD is again with Joseph, giving him favor with the keeper of the prison, who puts all the prisoners under Joseph's charge and lets him run everything because the LORD makes his work prosper.

Chapter 40 body

Pharaoh, angry at his chief cup bearer and chief baker, puts them in the prison in the captain of the guard's house, where Joseph is bound and is assigned to care for them. After both men dream in one night, Joseph sees they are sad and, saying interpretations belong to God, asks them to tell him their dreams. He interprets the cup bearer's vine dream to mean restoration to office in three days and asks to be remembered to Pharaoh, then interprets the baker's dream of birds eating bread from baskets to mean he will be hanged in three days. On Pharaoh's birthday both outcomes happen as Joseph said, but the restored cup bearer forgets Joseph.

Chapter 41 body

Pharaoh dreams of seven fat cattle devoured by seven thin cattle and of seven healthy heads of grain swallowed by seven blasted ones, and none of Egypt's magicians and wise men can interpret them. The chief cup bearer recalls Joseph, who is brought from the dungeon and tells Pharaoh that the dream is one dream from God, foretelling seven years of plenty followed by seven years of famine. Joseph advises Pharaoh to appoint a discreet and wise man and overseers to collect a fifth of the produce and store grain in the cities. Pharaoh sets Joseph over all Egypt, gives him his signet ring, robes, a gold chain, the name Zaphenath-Paneah, and Asenath as a wife. Joseph stores grain in vast quantities during the plenty and has two sons, Manasseh and Ephraim. When the famine comes, Egypt has bread, and Joseph sells grain to the Egyptians and to people from all countries.

Chapter 42 body

Jacob sends ten of his sons down to Egypt to buy grain during the famine, keeping Benjamin at home for fear of harm. Joseph, the governor, recognizes his brothers, who do not recognize him, and accuses them of being spies. He holds them in custody for three days, then keeps Simeon bound as a hostage and demands that they bring their youngest brother to prove their honesty. The brothers acknowledge guilt over their treatment of Joseph, and Joseph secretly weeps and returns each man's money to his sack. Back in Canaan, they tell Jacob everything and find the money in their sacks, and Jacob refuses to let Benjamin go despite Reuben's pledge.

Chapter 43 body

The famine in the land forces Israel to tell his sons to return to Egypt for food, and Judah pledges himself as collateral for Benjamin's safety so that their father will let the boy go. Israel agrees, sending a present, double money, and the money returned in the sacks, and the brothers take Benjamin down and stand before Joseph. Joseph has his steward bring them to his house for a noon meal, which frightens the brothers, who fear they will be seized over the returned money; the steward reassures them and brings Simeon out. When Joseph sees Benjamin he is overcome, weeps privately, then composes himself and serves the meal, giving Benjamin a portion five times larger than the others'.

Chapter 44 body

Joseph has his steward fill the men's sacks with food, return their money, and hide his silver cup in the youngest brother's sack. After the men leave, the steward overtakes them and accuses them of theft; they protest and pledge that whoever has the cup will die and the rest will become slaves. The cup is found in Benjamin's sack, and the brothers tear their clothes and return to Joseph's house. Joseph says only the one with the cup will be his slave, and the others may go in peace. Judah then pleads at length, recounting their father's love for the youngest son and fear of losing him, and offers to stay as Joseph's slave in the boy's place.

Chapter 45 body

Joseph, unable to restrain himself, clears the room and reveals his identity to his brothers, weeping so loudly that the Egyptians and Pharaoh's house hear. He tells them not to be grieved, because God sent him ahead to preserve life during the five remaining years of famine, and he instructs them to bring their father to live near him in Goshen. After Joseph embraces and kisses his brothers, Pharaoh approves and orders them to fetch their father and households with wagons, promising the good of Egypt. Joseph sends them off with wagons, provisions, clothing, and extra gifts for Benjamin and for his father. In Canaan the brothers tell Jacob that Joseph is alive and ruling Egypt; he first disbelieves, but the sight of the wagons revives him and he resolves to go see Joseph before he dies.

Chapter 46 body

Israel (Jacob) travels to Beersheba with all his possessions and offers sacrifices, and God speaks to him in a night vision, telling him not to fear going down to Egypt, where God will make him a great nation, go with him, and bring him back up, with Joseph closing his eyes. Jacob, his sons, their wives, little ones, and livestock travel to Egypt in wagons Pharaoh had sent. The chapter lists the names of the descendants who came with him through Leah, Zilpah, Rachel, and Bilhah, totaling seventy souls in the house of Jacob in Egypt. Jacob sends Judah ahead to Goshen, and Joseph goes to meet his father there, weeping on his neck, after which Israel says he is ready to die. Joseph instructs his brothers to tell Pharaoh they are keepers of livestock so they may settle in Goshen, since every shepherd is an abomination to the Egyptians.

Chapter 47 body

Joseph presents his father Jacob and five of his brothers to Pharaoh, who grants the family the land of Goshen (also called the land of Rameses) and offers to put capable brothers in charge of his livestock. Jacob tells Pharaoh his life has been 130 years of few and evil days, and blesses him. As the severe famine continues, Joseph collects all the Egyptians' and Canaanites' money, then their livestock, and finally their land and persons in exchange for bread and seed, making all Egypt's land Pharaoh's except the priests' land and setting a fifth of the harvest as Pharaoh's share. Israel's family prospers in Goshen, and after Jacob has lived 17 years in Egypt and reached age 147, he makes Joseph swear to carry him out of Egypt and bury him with his fathers.

Chapter 48 body

Told that his father is sick, Joseph brings his two sons, Manasseh and Ephraim, to Jacob (also called Israel). Jacob recounts God Almighty's promise at Luz and declares that the two boys are his own, like Reuben and Simeon, and he recalls Rachel's death on the way to Ephrath. With dim eyes, Jacob kisses and embraces the boys, then blesses Joseph and the lads, crossing his hands so that his right hand rests on the younger Ephraim. Joseph objects because Manasseh is the firstborn, but Jacob refuses, saying Manasseh will be great while Ephraim will be greater. Jacob sets Ephraim before Manasseh, tells Joseph that God will bring them back to the land of their fathers, and gives Joseph one portion above his brothers.

Chapter 49 body

Jacob summons his sons to tell them what will happen to them in the days to come, and speaks a blessing or pronouncement over each of the twelve tribes. He rebukes Reuben for defiling his bed, condemns the violence of Simeon and Levi and says he will scatter them, and exalts Judah as a lion's cub whose scepter will not depart. He then characterizes Zebulun, Issachar, Dan, Gad, Asher, Naphtali, Joseph, and Benjamin, giving Joseph the fullest blessing. Jacob charges his sons to bury him with his fathers in the cave of Machpelah in the field Abraham bought from Ephron the Hittite. After finishing his charge, he gathers his feet into the bed, breathes his last, and is gathered to his people.

Chapter 50 body

Joseph mourns his father Israel and has him embalmed, then obtains Pharaoh's permission to bury him in Canaan as Israel had made him swear. A great Egyptian company goes up with Joseph and his brothers, mourns at the threshold floor of Atad (named Abel Mizraim), and Israel's sons bury him in the cave of Machpelah near Mamre. After the return to Egypt, Joseph's brothers fear he will repay them and send a message citing their father's request for forgiveness; Joseph weeps, tells them not to be afraid, says God meant their act for good, and promises to provide for them. Joseph lives to one hundred ten, sees Ephraim's children to the third generation, makes the children of Israel swear to carry his bones up when God visits them, and dies and is embalmed in a coffin in Egypt.
