Yashts (Yasts) I-XII

James Darmesteter · 15 sections
Yashts (Yasts) I-XII, translated by James Darmesteter and L. H. Mills, Sacred Books of the East vols. 4, 23 and 31 (1880-1887), public domain. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://sacred-texts.com/zor/

Abstract written by a model

A sequence of Yasts presents Ahura Mazda’s revelations to Spitama Zarathustra and the worship of powerful holy beings. It begins with the Holy Word: Mazda’s twenty names are declared victorious, healing, and protective, while holiness and good thoughts, words, and deeds are praised. The following units expand worship through sacrifice, prayer, hymns, libations, and blessings to the Amesha-Spentas, especially Asha-Vahista and Haurvatât, whose powers defeat Daêvas, Drug, sickness, Death, the Nasu, and other harms while granting health, prosperity, long life, brightness, and the blissful abode of the holy Ones. The account then turns to Ardvi Sûra Anâhita, the holy, health-giving water. Ahura Mazda commands her worship and descent to protect creation; her channels, appearance, attendants, and benefits for fertility, lands, animals, and people are described. Numerous figures seek her aid. She grants boons to Yôista, Zarathustra, Kavi Vîstâspa, Zairi-vairi, Vafra Navâza, and Vistauru, but refuses Aregat-aspa and Vandaremaini’s request to overcome Vîstâspa and Zairi-vairi. The Sun, Moon, and Tistrya are then honored as forces sustaining the created world. The rising Sun cleanses creation and prevents the Daêvas from destroying the seven Karshvares; the Moon preserves the Bull’s seed and fosters spring plants; Tistrya defeats Apaosha so waters, clouds, rain, fertility, health, and protection may reach the Aryan countries. Drvâspa likewise receives sacrifices from figures including Zarathustra and Kavi Vîstâspa for health of flocks, victory, relief, and advancement of Mazda’s law. Mithra dominates the later units as the sleepless, all-seeing lord of wide pastures: guardian, warrior, judge, and protector who observes thoughts, words, deeds, contracts, sacrifices, and lies. He rewards truthful worshippers with prosperity, strength, victory, wisdom, and protection, while defeating enemies and punishing those who lie. Sraosha is similarly invoked as a fiend-smiting defender whose prayer, truth, and Mazda’s law repel wicked forces. The document closes with Rashnu, praised through Mazda’s explanation of the Holy Word as truthful, healthful, wise, and uniquely powerful to destroy evil; Rashnu embodies justice, discernment, and punishment of thieves.

I. Ormazd Yast. body

This unit concerns Ahura Mazda, Zarathustra, the Amesha-Spentas, and the Holy Word. Zarathustra asks which part of the Holy Word is strongest and most effective, and Ahura Mazda answers that his names are strongest, victorious, glorious, healing, and fiend-smiting. Ahura Mazda reveals twenty names and teaches that reciting them protects a person from weapons, fiends, and harm. The unit also praises holiness, good thoughts, words, and deeds, and invokes protection, health, prosperity, strength, and long life.

II. Haptân Yast. body

The unit concerns worship, sacrifice, prayer, and glorification offered to Ahura Mazda, the Amesha-Spentas, and other holy beings. It names their attributes and associations across the five Gâhs, including Vohu-Manô, Asha-Vahista, Spenta-Ârmaiti, and Sraosha. Zarathustra is told that Yâtus, Daêvas, men, and Drug are crushed or destroyed through the protective power of the Amesha-Spentas and the observance of Mazda's laws. The unit concludes by blessing these acts and asking for brightness, glory, health, strength, welfare, offspring, long life, and the blissful abode of the holy Ones.

III. Ardibehist Yast. body

Ardibehist Yast concerns Ahura Mazda, Spitama Zarathustra, Asha-Vahista, and the Amesha-Spentas. Ahura Mazda instructs Zarathustra to increase Asha-Vahista through hymns, worship, sacrifice, blessings, and adoration, and Zarathustra asks for the proper words. The Airyaman prayer is praised as the greatest and most healing of spells, driving away sickness, Death, Daêvas, and other harmful forces. Angra Mainyu fears Asha-Vahista, while the Drug is driven toward the northern regions and the holy are promised brightness, glory, health, and a blissful abode.

IV. Khordâd Yast. body

Ahura Mazda tells Spitama Zarathustra that Haurvatât provides help, enjoyments, comforts, and pleasures for the faithful. Invoking Haurvatât and the other Amesha-Spentas smites the Nasu and other harmful beings and frees the faithful man from their attacks. The faithful man separates from the wicked by pronouncing Ahura Mazda’s spell, drawing three, six, or nine furrows, and binding and striking down the Drug. The unit prescribes that the spell be transmitted only within specified relationships and concludes with sacrifice, prayer, and blessings for Haurvatât, the prosperity of the seasons, and the years.

V. Âbân Yast. body

This unit concerns Ardvi Sûra Anâhita, the holy, health-giving spring and river created and praised under Ahura Mazda. Ahura Mazda tells Spitama Zarathustra to offer sacrifices to her, describing her waters, channels, powers, and benefits for people, animals, lands, and fertility. Many figures request boons from Ardvi Sûra Anâhita through sacrifice and libations; she grants some requests and refuses others. The unit also recounts her aid to Vafra Navâza and Vistauru and describes conflicts involving Azi Dahâka, Thraêtaona, and the Aryan people.

XX. body

This unit concerns Ardvi Sûra Anâhita, the spring whom Spitama Zarathustra and others worship through sacrifices, libations, gifts, prayers, and entreaties. Ahura Mazda orders Ardvi Sûra Anâhita to descend from the stars to protect creation, and she is described as a powerful water whose channels, palaces, appearance, clothing, and attendants are detailed. She grants requested boons to Yôista, Zarathustra, Kavi Vîstâspa, and Zairi-vairi, but refuses the request of Aregat-aspa and Vandaremaini to conquer Vîstâspa and Zairi-vairi. The text also explains which people may drink her libations and that Daêvas receive libations offered after sunset.

VI. Khôrshêd Yast. body

This unit is a Yast to the undying, shining, swift-horsed Sun and concerns its worship, power, and protection against darkness and harmful beings. When the sun rises, the earth made by Ahura, waters, and holy creatures become clean, while heavenly Yazatas gather and pour its Glory upon the earth. The Daêvas would destroy the things in the seven Karshvares if the sun did not rise. Sacrifice to the Sun is offered to Ahura Mazda, the Amesha-Spentas, and the worshipper's own soul, and Mithra is praised in connection with the Sun.

VII. Mâh Yast. body

The unit concerns Ahura Mazda, the Amesha-Spentas, holiness, sacrifice, and the Moon that keeps in it the seed of the Bull. The Moon waxes for fifteen days and wanes for fifteen days, and its light causes golden-hued plants to grow during spring. The Amesha-Spentas hold the Moon’s glory and pour it upon the earth made by Mazda. Sacrifices, prayers, libations, Haoma, meat, baresma, holy spells, speech, deeds, and rightly-spoken words are offered to the Moon.

VIII. Tîr Yast. body

This unit concerns Tistrya, the bright and glorious star, and his role in bringing waters, rain, fertility, health, and protection to the material world. Ahura Mazda describes Tistrya’s worship, transformations, struggle with the Daêva Apaosha, and victory that enables waters and clouds to reach the Aryan countries. The text explains that sacrifice and prayer, performed according to the law of holiness, strengthen Tistrya and bring protection from hostile hordes, plagues, and other harms.

IX. Gôs Yast. body

This unit is a series of sacrifices and prayers to the powerful, holy Drvâspa, who maintains the health of flocks and assists the faithful. Haoshyangha, Yima Khshaêta, Thraêtaona, Haoma, Husravah, Zarathustra, and Kavi Vîstâspa each seek boons from Drvâspa through sacrifice, offerings, and libations. Their requests concern victory over Daêvas and enemies, prosperity and relief from suffering, the advancement of the Mazdean law, and the defeat or punishment of named foes. The unit concludes by blessing Drvâspa’s sacrifice and prayer and asking for brightness, glory, health, and the blissful abode of the holy Ones.

X. Mihir Yast. body

This unit concerns Ahura Mazda’s revelation to Spitama Zarathustra about Mithra, the lord of wide pastures. Mithra is presented as worthy of sacrifice and prayer, all-seeing, sleepless, protective of truthful people, and destructive toward those who lie to him. The text describes contracts, sacrifices, warfare, and Mithra’s power to grant prosperity, victory, health, and protection while bringing defeat and punishment upon his enemies.

XII. body

This unit concerns Mithra, the lord of wide pastures, who is sleepless, ever awake, powerful, all-knowing, and undeceivable. Ahura Mazda built Mithra a dwelling on Hara Berezaiti, and Mithra watches over the material world, protects those who do not lie, and helps those who call upon him. Mithra grants riches, strength, victory, good conscience, bliss, wisdom, and other boons to the faithful, while those who lie unto Mithra lose strength, glory, and protection. The unit also describes sacrifices offered to Mithra, Haoma’s sacrifice to him, and Mithra’s destruction of enemies, Daêvas, and malice.

XXIV. body

This unit concerns Mithra, the lord of wide pastures, as a sleepless guardian, warrior, judge, and protector of the world. He travels across the earth in a golden chariot, armed with many weapons, and drives away Angra Mainyu, Aêshma, Bûshyãsta, and other fiends. The text teaches that Mithra observes thoughts, words, deeds, sacrifices, and lies, rewarding faithful worshippers and punishing offenders. Ahura Mazda instructs Zarathustra about proper libations and sacrifices offered to Mithra.

XI. Srôsh Yast Hâdhôkht. body

This unit concerns Sraosha, the holy and fiend-smiting master of holiness, and his protection of the world and its faithful worshippers. Good prayer, the Ahuna Vairya, the word of truth, and the Law of the worshippers of Mazda are described as forces that drive away the Daêvas, Drug, and other wicked beings. Sraosha guards the creation of Mazda and the material world, fights the Mâzainya Daêvas, and protects people from fear, harm, and evil. The text offers sacrifices, prayers, libations, blessings, and glorification to Sraosha and associated holy beings.

XII. Rashn Yast. body

This unit concerns Zarathustra questioning Ahura Mazda about the qualities and power of the Holy Word. Ahura Mazda declares that the most glorious Holy Word is true, progress-making, discerning, healthful, wise, happy, and more powerful to destroy than all other creatures. Ahura Mazda instructs that Rashnu be invoked for friendship toward the var prepared, fire, baresma, boiling milk, oil, and plant sap. Rashnu is praised as true, knowing, discerning, far-seeing, and the best doer of justice and smiter of thieves, wherever he may be.
