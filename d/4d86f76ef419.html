The Second Book of Chronicles

author unknown · about 424 to 399 BC · 36 sections
The Second Book of Chronicles, from the World English Bible, Catholic Edition, dedicated to the public domain by eBible.org. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://ebible.org/eng-web-c/

Abstract written by a model

The Second Book of Chronicles narrates the reigns of Solomon and the kings of Judah, from the height of the united kingdom to the destruction of Jerusalem and a hopeful close. The central theme is that kings prosper when they seek the LORD and suffer when they forsake him, with the temple at the center of the story. Chapters 1-9 cover Solomon. God grants him wisdom at Gibeon, and he gathers great wealth. With Huram of Tyre supplying timber and the craftsman Huram-abi, Solomon builds the temple on Mount Moriah, makes its furnishings, and brings in the ark. God's glory fills the house. Solomon prays at the dedication, fire falls from heaven, and the LORD warns that forsaking him will bring uprooting. The queen of Sheba visits, and Solomon dies after forty years, succeeded by Rehoboam. Chapters 10-12 describe the split of the kingdom. Rehoboam rejects the elders' counsel and answers the people harshly, so Israel departs under Jeroboam. Rehoboam abandons the law, and Shishak of Egypt plunders Jerusalem. Abijah (ch. 13) defeats Jeroboam's larger army by relying on God. Chapters 14-20 cover Asa and Jehoshaphat. Asa removes idols, defeats Zerah's million-strong force, and renews the covenant, but late in life relies on Syria, imprisons the seer Hanani, and seeks only physicians in his illness. Jehoshaphat teaches the law and appoints judges. He allies with Ahab, who dies in battle despite Micaiah's warning, and Jehoshaphat is rebuked by Jehu. When Moab and Ammon attack, Judah is saved by praise and trust. Chapters 21-28 trace decline. Jehoram, Ahaziah, and Athaliah follow Ahab's house, and Athaliah destroys the royal offspring until Jehoiada hides and later crowns Joash. Joash repairs the temple but turns to idols after Jehoiada dies and has Zechariah stoned. Amaziah worships Edomite gods and is defeated by Israel. Uzziah prospers but is struck with leprosy for burning incense. Jotham does right. Ahaz is the worst, sacrificing children and closing the LORD's house. Chapters 29-35 describe revival. Hezekiah cleanses the temple, keeps a great Passover, and reorganizes the priests' support. He and Isaiah pray, and an angel destroys Sennacherib's army. Manasseh sins grievously, is taken to Babylon in chains, repents, and is restored. Amon is killed. Josiah purges the land, finds the book of the law, and keeps a great Passover, but dies fighting Neco at Megiddo. Chapter 36 recounts the last kings, Jehoahaz, Jehoiakim, Jehoiachin, and Zedekiah. The people mock the prophets, Nebuchadnezzar's Chaldeans burn the temple and Jerusalem, and survivors are exiled to Babylon for seventy years. The book ends with Cyrus of Persia proclaiming that the LORD's house is to be built in Jerusalem and that God's people may go up.

The Second Book of Chronicles / Chapter 1 body

Solomon son of David is established in his kingdom, with the LORD his God making him exceedingly great. He gathers Israel's leaders and the assembly and goes to the high place at Gibeon, where the Tent of Meeting and the bronze altar made by Bezalel stand, and offers one thousand burnt offerings. That night God appears and invites him to ask, and Solomon requests wisdom and knowledge to judge the people, so God grants these and promises riches, wealth, and honor unmatched by any other king. Solomon returns to Jerusalem and reigns over Israel, gathering 1,400 chariots and 12,000 horsemen, making silver and gold as common as stones, and trading horses and chariots from Egypt and Kue to the Hittite and Syrian kings.

Chapter 2 body

Solomon resolves to build a house for the LORD's name and a house for his kingdom, and assigns seventy thousand burden bearers, eighty thousand stone cutters, and three thousand six hundred overseers. He sends a message to Huram, king of Tyre, asking for a skilled craftsman and for cedar, cypress, and algum timber from Lebanon, and he offers wheat, barley, wine, and oil in payment. Solomon explains that the house will be great because his God is greater than all gods, though heaven cannot contain him. Huram answers in writing, blesses the LORD, sends the skilled man Huram-abi, agrees to cut the wood and float it by sea to Joppa, and asks that the provisions be sent. Solomon then counts the foreigners in Israel, finding 153,600, and sets them to the same labor of bearing burdens, cutting stone, and overseeing.

Chapter 3 body

Chapter 3 describes Solomon beginning to build the LORD's house at Jerusalem on Mount Moriah, on the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite, in the second month of the fourth year of his reign. It gives the foundation dimensions of sixty by twenty cubits and details the porch, the larger room, and the lavish gold overlay and decoration with precious stones, palm trees, chains, and cherubim. It describes the most holy place, where two carved cherubim with wings spanning twenty cubits stood facing the house, and a veil of blue, purple, crimson, and fine linen. Finally, Solomon sets up two thirty-five-cubit pillars before the temple, topped with chains and one hundred pomegranates, and names them Jachin on the right and Boaz on the left.

Chapter 4 body

Chapter 4 describes the furnishings Solomon had made for God's house. These include a bronze altar, a great molten sea resting on twelve oxen, ten basins, ten gold lamp stands, ten tables, and a hundred gold basins, along with the court of the priests and its bronze-overlaid doors. Huram (also called Huram-abi) made the pots, shovels, forks, basins, pillars, capitals, networks, and pomegranates for King Solomon. The king cast the bronze vessels in the clay ground of the Jordan plain between Succoth and Zeredah, in such abundance that the bronze could not be weighed. The chapter ends by listing the golden altar, tables, lamp stands, and utensils of pure gold, and the gold doors of the temple.

Chapter 5 body

Solomon finishes all the work on the LORD's house and places the things his father David had dedicated, the silver, gold, and vessels, in the treasuries of God's house. He assembles the elders, tribal heads, and fathers' household princes of Israel at Jerusalem in the seventh month to bring the ark of the LORD's covenant up from David's city, Zion. The Levites and Levitical priests carry up the ark, the Tent of Meeting, and the holy vessels, while Solomon and the congregation sacrifice innumerable sheep and cattle. The priests set the ark in the most holy place under the cherubim's wings; it contains only the two tablets Moses put there at Horeb. When the priests come out and the Levite singers and trumpeters praise the LORD as one voice, a cloud fills the house so that the priests cannot stand to minister, for the LORD's glory fills God's house.

Chapter 6 body

Solomon addresses the assembly of Israel, blessing the LORD for fulfilling his word to David by letting Solomon, David's son, build the house for the LORD's name. Standing on a bronze platform with hands spread toward heaven, he prays that the LORD keep his promise of a throne to David's line if his children walk in the LORD's law. He acknowledges that heaven cannot contain God, much less the house, yet asks God to hear prayers made toward it. He then lists situations in which people may pray toward the house, including oaths, defeat, drought, famine, foreigners, battle and captivity, asking the LORD to hear from heaven and forgive. He closes by asking the LORD to arise into his resting place and to remember his loving kindnesses to David.

Chapter 7 body

When Solomon finishes praying, fire comes down from heaven and consumes the offerings, and the LORD's glory fills the house so that the priests cannot enter. Solomon and all the people offer huge sacrifices, dedicate the house, and hold a seven-day dedication of the altar followed by a seven-day feast, with Levites and priests making music and giving thanks. On the twenty-third day of the seventh month Solomon sends the people home joyful. The LORD then appears to Solomon by night, saying he has chosen the house, promising to hear and heal if his people humble themselves and pray, and promising to establish Solomon's throne if he walks as David did. He also warns that if they forsake him and serve other gods, he will uproot them from the land and cast the house out of his sight, making it a byword.

Chapter 8 body

After twenty years of building the LORD's house and his own house, Solomon rebuilt the cities Huram had given him, settled Israelites in them, and prevailed against Hamath Zobah. He built Tadmor, Beth Horon the upper and lower, Baalath, storage cities, and cities for chariots and horsemen, and conscripted forced labor from the Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites, while making no servants of Israelites, who served as warriors and officers. He moved Pharaoh's daughter out of David's city to a house he built for her, because places where the LORD's ark had come were holy. Solomon offered burnt offerings on the LORD's altar according to Moses' commandment and set the priests and Levites in their divisions according to David's ordinance, and the LORD's house was completed. He then went to Ezion Geber and Eloth, where Huram sent ships and servants, and they brought four hundred fifty talents of gold from Ophir to him.

Chapter 9 body

The queen of Sheba comes to Jerusalem with a great caravan to test King Solomon with hard questions, and she is overwhelmed by his wisdom, house, and court. She blesses the LORD, gives Solomon gold, spices, and precious stones, and receives all she desired before returning to her land. The chapter then describes Solomon's vast wealth: 666 talents of gold in a year, gold shields, an ivory and gold throne with lions, gold vessels, and ships of Tarshish bringing treasure with Huram's servants. Kings of the earth seek him out and bring tribute, and he rules from the River to the land of the Philistines and the border of Egypt. The chapter closes by citing the prophetic records of his acts, noting he reigned forty years, was buried in David's city, and was succeeded by his son Rehoboam.

Chapter 10 body

All Israel gathers at Shechem to make Rehoboam king, and Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who had fled to Egypt from Solomon, returns at their call. Jeroboam and the people ask Rehoboam to lighten the heavy yoke Solomon put on them in exchange for their service. Rehoboam consults the old men who served Solomon, who advise kindness, but he abandons their counsel and follows the young men who grew up with him, who urge him to add to the yoke. He answers the people roughly, and the text says this was brought about by God to establish his word spoken by Ahijah the Shilonite to Jeroboam. Israel renounces any portion in David and departs to their tents, only the people in Judah's cities remain under Rehoboam, and when Rehoboam sends Hadoram over forced labor, Israel stones him to death and Rehoboam flees to Jerusalem.

Chapter 11 body

Rehoboam gathers 180,000 chosen warriors from Judah and Benjamin to fight Israel, but the LORD sends Shemaiah the man of God to forbid it, and the men return home. Rehoboam lives in Jerusalem and fortifies fifteen cities in Judah and Benjamin with captains, food stores, shields and spears. Priests, Levites and faithful Israelites leave Jeroboam, who had cast the Levites off and appointed his own priests for the high places and calf idols, and come to Judah and Jerusalem, strengthening Rehoboam for three years. Rehoboam takes eighteen wives and sixty concubines, loves Maacah most, and appoints her son Abijah as chief prince intending to make him king. He also disperses some of his sons to the fortified cities and provides them with food and wives.

Chapter 12 body

Once Rehoboam's kingdom was established and strong, he and all Israel abandoned the LORD's law. In Rehoboam's fifth year, Shishak king of Egypt invaded with a vast army, took Judah's fortified cities, and came to Jerusalem. The prophet Shemaiah told Rehoboam and the princes that the LORD had left them in Shishak's hand, and they humbled themselves, so the LORD granted some deliverance but made them Shishak's servants. Shishak took the treasures of the LORD's house and the king's house, including Solomon's gold shields, which Rehoboam replaced with bronze ones. Rehoboam reigned seventeen years, did evil by not seeking the LORD, fought continually with Jeroboam, and was succeeded by his son Abijah.

Chapter 13 body

In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam, Abijah began to reign over Judah for three years; his mother was Micaiah the daughter of Uriel of Gibeah. War broke out between Abijah, with 400,000 chosen men, and Jeroboam, with 800,000. From Mount Zemaraim, Abijah told Jeroboam and Israel that the LORD gave the kingdom to David's line, that Israel had adopted golden calves and false priests, and that Judah still kept the LORD's worship. Jeroboam ambushed Judah from behind, but Judah cried to the LORD, the priests sounded trumpets, and God struck Jeroboam and Israel, so that 500,000 Israelites fell. Abijah took Bethel, Jeshanah, and Ephron with their villages, Jeroboam never recovered and was struck by the LORD and died, and Abijah grew mighty with fourteen wives and thirty-eight children.

Chapter 14 body

Abijah dies and is buried in David's city, and his son Asa becomes king, enjoying ten years of quiet in the land. Asa does what is good in the LORD's eyes by removing foreign altars, high places, pillars, Asherah poles and sun images, and by commanding Judah to seek the LORD. Because the LORD gives rest, Asa has Judah build fortified cities with walls, towers, gates and bars, and he musters 300,000 men from Judah and 280,000 from Benjamin. Zerah the Ethiopian comes against them at Mareshah with a million troops and three hundred chariots, and Asa cries to the LORD for help. The LORD strikes the Ethiopians, who flee, and Asa and his people pursue them to Gerar, plunder the surrounding cities and the livestock tents, and return to Jerusalem.

Chapter 15 body

Azariah the son of Oded, moved by the Spirit of God, meets King Asa and all Judah and Benjamin and tells them that the LORD is with those who seek him, recalling Israel's past distress without the true God, a teaching priest, or law. Encouraged, Asa removes the abominations from Judah and Benjamin and from captured cities in Ephraim, and renews the LORD's altar. In the third month of his fifteenth year, people from Judah, Benjamin, Ephraim, Manasseh, and Simeon gather at Jerusalem, sacrifice seven hundred cattle and seven thousand sheep, and swear a covenant to seek the LORD with all their heart, on pain of death for those who do not. Asa removes his mother Maacah as queen mother for making an abominable image for an Asherah, and destroys the image. The high places remain, but Asa's heart is perfect, he brings dedicated gifts into God's house, and there is no war until his thirty-fifth year.

Chapter 16 body

In the thirty-sixth year of his reign, Asa of Judah faced Baasha of Israel, who built Ramah to block movement in and out of Judah. Asa sent silver and gold from the treasuries of the LORD's house and the king's house to Ben Hadad of Syria, who broke his treaty with Baasha and attacked Israelite cities, so Baasha stopped building Ramah. Asa then carried away Ramah's stones and timber to build Geba and Mizpah. The seer Hanani rebuked Asa for relying on the king of Syria rather than the LORD, and Asa put him in prison and oppressed some of the people. Asa later suffered a severe foot disease, sought only physicians, died in his forty-first year, and was buried in his own tomb in David's city with spices and a great fire.

Chapter 17 body

Jehoshaphat succeeds his father as king of Judah and strengthens himself against Israel by placing forces and garrisons in Judah's fortified cities and in the cities of Ephraim that Asa had taken. The LORD is with him because he follows the ways of his father David, seeks God, and rejects the Baals and the ways of Israel, so the kingdom is established and Judah brings him tribute, riches, and honor. He removes the high places and Asherah poles and, in his third year, sends princes, Levites, and priests with the book of the LORD's law to teach throughout the cities of Judah. The fear of the LORD falls on surrounding kingdoms, so they make no war on him, and the Philistines and Arabians bring presents and tribute. Jehoshaphat grows great, builds fortresses and store cities, and keeps large armies of mighty men of valor from Judah and Benjamin under named captains.

Chapter 18 body

Jehoshaphat of Judah, allied with Ahab of Israel, visits Samaria and agrees to join Ahab in war against Ramoth Gilead, but insists on first inquiring of the LORD. Ahab's four hundred prophets, with Zedekiah's iron horns, promise victory, while Micaiah, whom Ahab hates, first echoes them sarcastically and then reports a vision of Israel scattered and of a lying spirit sent into the prophets' mouths. Zedekiah strikes Micaiah, and Ahab orders him imprisoned on bread and water until Ahab returns in peace. Ahab disguises himself in battle, but a randomly shot arrow wounds him and he dies at sunset, while Jehoshaphat, mistaken for Ahab by the Syrian chariot captains, is saved when he cries out and the LORD helps him.

Chapter 19 body

Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, returns in peace to Jerusalem, where Jehu the son of Hanani the seer confronts him for helping the wicked and loving those who hate the LORD, warning that wrath is on him, yet acknowledging the good in him for removing the Asheroth and seeking God. Jehoshaphat then goes among the people from Beersheba to the hill country of Ephraim and brings them back to the LORD. He sets judges in all the fortified cities of Judah and charges them to judge for the LORD, in fear of him, without iniquity, partiality, or bribes. In Jerusalem he appoints Levites, priests, and heads of fathers' households to give judgment for the LORD and settle controversies, instructing them to warn people against guilt toward the LORD. He names Amariah the chief priest over the matters of the LORD and Zebadiah the son of Ishmael over the king's matters, and tells them to deal courageously.

Chapter 20 body

Moab, Ammon, and some Meunites come against King Jehoshaphat of Judah, and he responds by proclaiming a fast and praying before the assembly in the LORD's house, admitting Judah has no might and that their eyes are on God. The Levite Jahaziel, moved by the LORD's Spirit, tells them the battle is God's and that they need only stand still and watch. Judah goes out with Levites singing praise at the front, and the LORD sets ambushers so that Ammon and Moab destroy the people of Mount Seir and then each other. Judah gathers plunder for three days, blesses the LORD in Beracah Valley, and returns to Jerusalem rejoicing, and the surrounding kingdoms fear God. The chapter closes with a summary of Jehoshaphat's reign and his alliance with Ahaziah king of Israel to build ships, which the LORD destroys after Eliezer prophesies against him.

Chapter 21 body

Jehoshaphat dies and his firstborn son Jehoram becomes king of Judah, then kills his brothers and some princes of Israel. Following the ways of Ahab's house, to which he is tied by marriage, he does evil and leads Judah into idolatry, while the LORD spares David's house because of his covenant. Edom and Libnah revolt, and Elijah sends a letter announcing a plague and a fatal bowel disease. The Philistines and Arabians raid Judah and carry off the king's possessions, sons, and wives, leaving only Jehoahaz, and Jehoram dies of the disease after two years, unmourned and not buried in the kings' tombs.

Chapter 22 body

The inhabitants of Jerusalem make Ahaziah, the youngest son of Jehoram, king of Judah after raiders have slain his older brothers. Ahaziah follows the wicked counsel of Ahab's house, including his mother Athaliah, and goes with Jehoram of Israel to war against Hazael of Syria, where Joram is wounded. While visiting the wounded Jehoram in Jezreel, Ahaziah is caught by Jehu, whom the LORD had anointed to cut off Ahab's house, and is killed, though he is buried because of his father Jehoshaphat's faithfulness. Athaliah then destroys all the royal offspring of Judah, but Jehoshabeath, wife of Jehoiada the priest, hides Joash in God's house, where he remains six years while Athaliah reigns.

Chapter 23 body

In the seventh year, the priest Jehoiada made a covenant with five captains of hundreds, gathered the Levites and the heads of Israel's households to Jerusalem, and organized them to guard the LORD's house and the king's son. They armed the people with David's spears and shields, crowned the king's son, gave him the covenant, anointed him, and proclaimed 'Long live the king!' When Athaliah heard the noise, came to the LORD's house, and cried 'Treason!', Jehoiada ordered her taken out between the ranks and killed outside the house, and she was slain at the horse gate. Jehoiada then made a covenant that the people would be the LORD's people, and the people destroyed Baal's house and killed Mattan the priest of Baal. Jehoiada restored the temple order of priests, Levites, offerings, and gatekeepers, and the king was set on the throne, with the people rejoicing and the city quiet.

Chapter 24 body

Joash became king of Judah at age seven and did right while the priest Jehoiada lived. He ordered the repair of the LORD's house, which had been damaged by the sons of Athaliah, and when the Levites were slow he had a chest set at the temple gate to collect Moses' tax; the people gave abundantly, workmen restored the house, and leftover money made vessels. After Jehoiada died at 130 and was buried among the kings, the princes of Judah persuaded Joash to abandon the LORD for Asherah poles and idols. Zechariah, Jehoiada's son, rebuked the people and was stoned at the king's command, after which a small Syrian army defeated Judah, wounded Joash, and his own servants Zabad and Jehozabad killed him in his bed, and Amaziah his son succeeded him.

Chapter 25 body

Amaziah begins his twenty-nine-year reign over Judah, doing right but not with a perfect heart, and executes his father's killers while sparing their children according to the law of Moses. He musters 300,000 men of Judah and Benjamin and hires 100,000 men from Israel, but dismisses the Israelites at a man of God's warning, which enrages them. He defeats ten thousand of Seir's children in the Valley of Salt, yet then brings home and worships the gods of Seir, ignoring a prophet's rebuke. He challenges Joash king of Israel, rejects Joash's warning, and is defeated at Beth Shemesh; Joash breaks down part of Jerusalem's wall and plunders the temple and palace. Amaziah later flees a conspiracy to Lachish, where he is killed, and he is buried in the City of Judah.

Chapter 26 body

Uzziah, sixteen years old, is made king by the people of Judah in place of his father Amaziah and reigns fifty-two years in Jerusalem. He does right in the LORD's eyes, seeks God in the days of Zechariah, and prospers: he defeats the Philistines and others, receives tribute from the Ammonites, builds towers and cisterns, farms, and equips a large army with weapons and siege devices. Once strong, his heart is lifted up and he enters the LORD's temple to burn incense on the altar of incense. Azariah the priest and eighty priests resist him, saying only the consecrated sons of Aaron may burn incense, and Uzziah's anger is met by leprosy breaking out on his forehead. He lives as a leper in a separate house until his death while his son Jotham judges the people, and he is buried with his fathers in the kings' burial field, with Isaiah the prophet, son of Amoz, having written his acts.

Chapter 27 body

Chapter 27 describes the reign of Jotham, who became king of Judah at twenty-five and ruled sixteen years in Jerusalem; his mother was Jerushah daughter of Zadok. He did right in the LORD's eyes like his father Uzziah, though he did not enter the temple and the people still acted corruptly. He built the upper gate of the LORD's house, worked on the wall of Ophel, and built cities, fortresses and towers in Judah. He fought the king of the children of Ammon and prevailed, receiving silver, wheat and barley as tribute for three years. After he died he was buried in David's city and his son Ahaz succeeded him.

Chapter 28 body

Chapter 28 describes the sixteen-year reign of Ahaz over Judah, who did evil by making images for the Baals, burning his children in the fire, and sacrificing at high places. As a result, the LORD delivered him to the king of Syria and to the king of Israel, whose commander Pekah killed 120,000 men of Judah in one day, and Israel took 200,000 captives to Samaria. The prophet Oded, backed by several Ephraimite leaders, persuaded the army to release the captives, who were clothed, fed, and returned to Jericho. Ahaz then appealed to the king of Assyria while Edomites and Philistines attacked Judah, but Tilgath-pilneser troubled him rather than helping him. Ahaz sacrificed to the gods of Damascus, cut up the vessels of God's house, shut the LORD's house, and set up altars and high places everywhere, and when he died he was buried in Jerusalem but not in the tombs of the kings, with Hezekiah reigning after him.

Chapter 29 body

Hezekiah, a 25-year-old king of Judah who does right in the LORD's eyes, opens and repairs the doors of the LORD's house in the first month of his first year. He summons the priests and Levites, tells them to sanctify themselves and remove filthiness from the temple, and explains that the fathers' unfaithfulness brought the LORD's wrath. Named Levites and their brothers cleanse the house in sixteen days and report that the altar, the table of show bread, and the vessels Ahaz threw away are prepared. Hezekiah then gathers the city's princes and offers seven bulls, seven rams, seven lambs, and seven male goats as a sin offering for the kingdom, sanctuary, and Judah, with music according to David's commandment. The assembly brings sacrifices, thank offerings, and burnt offerings, the Levites help skin them because the priests are too few, and Hezekiah and the people rejoice that the service of the house was set in order.

Chapter 30 body

King Hezekiah sends letters and couriers throughout Israel and Judah, from Beersheba to Dan, inviting people to Jerusalem to keep the Passover in the second month because the priests had not sanctified themselves in sufficient number earlier. Many in Ephraim and Manasseh ridicule the couriers, but some men of Asher, Manasseh, and Zebulun humble themselves and come, and God gives Judah one heart to obey. A very great assembly removes the altars in Jerusalem, kills the Passover on the fourteenth day, and keeps the feast of unleavened bread, with Hezekiah praying for the unclean who ate otherwise than prescribed, and the LORD healing the people. The assembly joyfully keeps seven more days, supported by large gifts of bulls and sheep from Hezekiah and the princes. The priests bless the people and their prayer reaches heaven, and the joy is unmatched in Jerusalem since Solomon.

Chapter 31 body

After the Passover and related events, all Israel who were present went through Judah, Benjamin, Ephraim and Manasseh destroying pillars, Asherah poles, high places and altars, then returned to their own cities. King Hezekiah organized the priests and Levites into divisions for offerings and service, and provided the king's own portion for the regular and festival burnt offerings. He commanded the people in Jerusalem to give the priests' and Levites' portion, and the people responded with abundant first fruits and tithes, laid in heaps from the third to the seventh month. Azariah the chief priest told Hezekiah that there was plenty left over because the LORD had blessed his people, so Hezekiah had storerooms prepared, and Conaniah, Shimei, Kore and others were appointed to oversee and distribute the goods. The chapter concludes that Hezekiah did what was good, right and faithful, and prospered in all he did to seek his God.

Chapter 32 body

Sennacherib king of Assyria invades Judah and threatens Jerusalem, so Hezekiah stops the springs, strengthens the wall, arms the people, and encourages them to trust that the LORD is with them. Sennacherib's servants mock Hezekiah and the LORD with speeches and letters, comparing the LORD to the powerless gods of other nations. Hezekiah and the prophet Isaiah pray, and the LORD sends an angel who cuts off the Assyrian leaders, so Sennacherib returns home in shame and is killed by his own sons in his god's house. Afterward Hezekiah is exalted and grows rich, but pride after his illness and healing brings wrath that he and Jerusalem avert by humbling themselves, and God tests him regarding the Babylonian ambassadors. Hezekiah dies and is buried with honor, and Manasseh his son reigns in his place.

Chapter 33 body

Chapter 33 recounts how Manasseh became king of Judah at twelve and reigned fifty-five years, rebuilding high places, raising altars to Baals and the army of the sky in the LORD's house, setting up an engraved idol there, sacrificing his children, and practicing sorcery. The LORD spoke to him and his people, but they did not listen, so Assyrian captains took Manasseh in chains to Babylon. In distress he humbled himself and prayed, and the LORD restored him to Jerusalem, after which he built an outer wall, placed captains in fortified cities, removed the foreign gods and idol, rebuilt the LORD's altar, and commanded Judah to serve the LORD, though the people still sacrificed at the high places. After Manasseh's death his son Amon reigned two years, did evil and did not humble himself, and was killed by his servants. The people of the land then executed the conspirators and made Josiah, Amon's son, king.

Chapter 34 body

Chapter 34 describes Josiah, who became king of Judah at age eight and began seeking God in his eighth year, then purged Judah, Jerusalem, and the lands of Israel of high places, Asherah poles, and images in his twelfth year. In his eighteenth year he sent Shaphan, Maaseiah, and Joah to repair the house of the LORD, where Hilkiah the priest found the book of the law given by Moses. Shaphan read it to Josiah, who tore his clothes and sent Hilkiah and others to inquire of the prophetess Huldah. Huldah declared that the LORD would bring the written curses on the place, but that Josiah, because he humbled himself and wept, would be gathered to his grave in peace without seeing the evil. Josiah then gathered the people, read them the book of the covenant, and made a covenant before the LORD, and the people did not depart from the LORD all his days.

Chapter 35 body

Josiah keeps a great Passover in Jerusalem, setting the priests and Levites in their offices and providing thousands of lambs, young goats, and bulls from his own substance, with his princes and the chiefs of the Levites also giving generously. The priests sprinkle the blood and the Levites skin the animals and prepare the offerings, so that the singers and gatekeepers need not leave their posts. The text says no Passover like it had been kept since the days of Samuel, and it took place in Josiah's eighteenth year. Afterward Neco king of Egypt goes up to fight at Carchemish, warns Josiah that God has commanded him, and Josiah refuses to listen and fights him in the valley of Megiddo. Archers wound Josiah, he is taken to Jerusalem and dies, and Judah, Jerusalem, and Jeremiah mourn him.

Chapter 36 body

Chapter 36 recounts the last kings of Judah. The people make Jehoahaz king, but the king of Egypt removes him after three months, fines the land, and installs Eliakim, renamed Jehoiakim, while Neco carries Joahaz to Egypt. Jehoiakim does evil and Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon binds him and takes vessels of the LORD's house to Babylon; his son Jehoiachin reigns briefly and is also taken to Babylon, and Zedekiah is made king. Zedekiah, the priests, and the people rebel and pollute the LORD's house, mock the prophets, and so the Chaldeans burn the temple and Jerusalem and carry survivors to Babylon for seventy years. In Cyrus of Persia's first year, the LORD stirs him to proclaim that he is to build the LORD a house in Jerusalem and that God's people may go up.
