Abstract written by a model

This record summarizes Book I of the Akaranga Sutra, Hermann Jacobi’s translation of Sudharman’s discourse heard from Mahâvîra. Across eight lectures, it sets out a religious path of non-injury, restraint, renunciation, and liberation from karman and samsâra. The First Lecture teaches that humans and other beings possess souls, suffer birth, rebirth, and pain, and must renounce harm to earth-, water-, fire-, wind-, plant-, and animal lives. The Second through Fifth Lectures depict carelessness, attachment, desire, sensuality, wealth, ignorance, violence, and sinful acts as sources of grief and continued birth and death; they praise the wise, houseless sage who abandons possessions, controls passions, follows truth, and seeks the opposite shore. Righteousness is defined especially by refusing to slay, abuse, torment, or drive away any breathing, living, sentient creature. The text contrasts this teaching with claims of some Brâhmanas or Sramanas, while presenting the Seer and heroic sages as disciplined, equanimous, and free of worldly weakness. It also teaches that the Self is the knower and that the liberated has no body, qualities, or material conditions. The Sixth and Seventh Lectures turn to religious discipline: awakened teachers, monks, ascetics, disciples, householders, mendicants, food, illness, vows, penance, and religious death. They call for patience, abstinence, right faith, rejection of goods gained through injury or theft, endurance of pain, reduced food, and unwavering restraint so that karman may be dissolved and Nirvâna entered. The Eighth Lecture offers Mahâvîra as the culminating example. During thirteen years of wandering and meditation, he abandoned his robe and endured deprivation, attacks, abuse, cold, heat, and pain without seeking comfort or medical treatment. He carefully avoided harm to all forms of life and proceeded undisturbed toward Nirvâna.

Akaranga Sutra (Âkârâṅga Sûtra), Book I / First Book. / First Lecture, Called Knowledge of the Weapon. / First Lesson. .. Seventh Lesson. body

The Akaranga Sutra, translated by Hermann Jacobi, records Sudharman’s discourse heard from Mahâvîra in the First Lecture, called Knowledge of the Weapon. It teaches that men and other beings possess souls, undergo birth and rebirth, experience pain and suffering, and are affected by sinful acts. The unit instructs people to comprehend and renounce harm toward earth-bodies, water-bodies, fire-bodies, plants, animals, and wind-bodies, and to practise self-control, religious faith, and houselessness. It presents non-injury toward the aggregate of six kinds of lives as connected with reward and final liberation.

Second Lecture, Called Conquest of the World. / First Lesson. .. Sixth Lesson. body

The Second Lecture, Called Conquest of the World, teaches that people suffer through carelessness, attachment, desire, pleasure, wealth, and worldly relations. It instructs the wise man, hero, houseless, and sage to renounce possessions, avoid violence, practice control, and pursue religious life and final liberation. The text describes birth and death, samsara, bodily decay, and the loss or destruction of wealth as causes of grief. It praises non-killing, true knowledge, detachment, and crossing to the opposite shore.

Third Lecture, Called Hot and Cold. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson. body

The Third Lecture, Called Hot and Cold, teaches that sages and heroes should understand the misery caused by karman, violence, sensual attachment, and careless action. It instructs people to restrain themselves, avoid killing and other sinful acts, follow truth and the highest good, and enter religious life. The text explains that conquering passions and understanding the connections among love, hate, delusion, birth, death, and samsâra leads toward freedom from karman and danger. The doctrine of the Seer is described as non-injury and the ending of acts and samsâra.

Fourth Lecture, Called Righteousness. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson. body

The Fourth Lecture, Called Righteousness, teaches that all breathing, existing, living, sentient creatures should not be slain, treated with violence, abused, tormented, or driven away. It explains that understanding this law, pain, karman, and the consequences of carelessness leads toward restraint, renunciation, and freedom from acts. The text contrasts the claims of some Brâhmanas or Sramanas with the doctrine of the teachers, who affirm that harming living beings is wrong. It describes the heroes and the Seer as disciplined, equanimous, knowledgeable, and free from worldly weakness.

Fifth Lecture, Called Essence of the World. / First Lesson. .. Sixth Lesson. body

The Fifth Lecture, called Essence of the World, concerns the dangers of ignorance, attachment, sensuality, injurious deeds, and careless conduct, and teaches the path toward liberation. It describes how fools remain in delusion and samsâra, while monks and sages practice restraint, repentance, sagedom, and obedience to the law. It teaches that the Self is the knower and that the liberated has no body, qualities, or material conditions. The lecture also explains the currents of sin, the abandonment of worldly attachments, and the nature of the religious life taught by the noble ones.

Sixth Lecture, Called the Cleaning. / First Lesson. .. Fifth Lesson. body

The Sixth Lecture, Called the Cleaning, concerns awakened teachers, monks, ascetics, disciples, parents, and other beings in relation to religious discipline and liberation. It describes the diseases, pains, passions, attachments, and acts that keep men and other beings bound in samsâra. It teaches renunciation, abstinence, patience, non-injury, right faith, and steadfast observance of the law. It praises great heroes and sages who endure hardships, destroy the effects of works, and reach liberation.

Seventh Lecture, Called Liberation. / First Lesson. .. Eighth Lesson. body

The Seventh Lecture, Called Liberation, concerns mendicants, householders, conduct toward heretics, restraint from harming living beings, clothing, illness, food, and practices leading to liberation. It teaches that mendicants should reject goods obtained through injury, theft, force, or other sinful acts, and should follow their vows without deviation. The unit describes penance, reducing food, enduring bodily pain, controlling passions and motions, and several forms of religious death. It concludes that a restrained ascetic should remain unattached, bear hardships, dissolve karman, and choose one of the good methods of entering Nirvâna.

Eighth Lecture, (Called) the Pillow of Righteousness. / First Lesson. .. Fourth Lesson. body

This unit concerns Mahâvîra, called the Venerable One, and his thirteen years of severe wandering, meditation, self-control, and renunciation. He gave up his robe, endured hunger, thirst, cold, heat, attacks, abuse, and bodily pain, and avoided medical treatment and bodily comforts. He carefully avoided harming living beings, understood earth-bodies, water-bodies, fire-bodies, wind-bodies, lichens, seeds, and sprouts as imbued with life, and restrained himself from sinful acts and karman. The lessons describe his conduct in resting-places and in Lâdha and conclude that he proceeded undisturbed toward Nirvâna.
