Abstract written by a model

The document is the Book of Tobit, a narrative in fourteen chapters about a righteous Naphtali exile in Nineveh, his family, and God's intervention through the angel Raphael. Tobit, carried captive from Thisbe in the days of Enemessar, stays faithful to the law, gives alms, and buries slain Israelites. After Sennacherib's reign he is informed on, loses his goods, and is later restored through his kinsman Achiacharus. He then buries another murdered man, is blinded when sparrow dung falls in his eyes, and is rebuked by his wife Anna over alms. Grieved, he prays for death. On the same day in Ecbatana, Sarah, daughter of Raguel, whose seven husbands were killed by the demon Asmodaeus, prays likewise. Both prayers are heard, and Raphael is sent to heal Tobit, give Sarah to Tobias, and bind the demon. Tobit instructs his son Tobias about burial, alms, marriage within the kindred, and ten talents of silver left with Gabael in Rages of Media. Tobias hires a companion who claims to be Azarias, actually Raphael, and travels with him and his dog. At the Tigris a fish is caught; its heart and liver are kept to drive off demons and its bile to heal eyes. At Ecbatana Raguel recognizes Tobias as Tobit's kin and gives him Sarah under a contract. Tobias burns the heart and liver, the demon flees to Egypt and is bound, and the couple survive. Raphael fetches the money from Gabael while a fourteen-day feast is held. Tobias leaves with Sarah and half of Raguel's goods. Anna, who has been waiting anxiously, sees him coming. Tobias anoints Tobit's eyes with the bile and restores his sight. Tobit offers Raphael half the goods, and Raphael reveals his identity as one of the seven holy angels, urges thanks to God, tells them to record events, and ascends. Tobit then writes a prayer of praise, foretelling Israel's regathering and a glorious rebuilt Jerusalem. In his last charge he tells his family to leave Nineveh before its prophesied fall, foretells Jerusalem's destruction and restoration, and urges righteousness. Tobit dies at 158. Tobias buries him and Anna, moves to Media, and dies at 127 after hearing of Nineveh's destruction.

Tobit / Chapter 1 body

Tobit, a Naphtali man of the line of Tobiel, introduces himself as one carried captive from Thisbe to Nineveh in the days of Enemessar. He recounts that while his tribe turned to sacrificing to Baal, he alone went to Jerusalem for the feasts and gave his tithes and first fruits, and he later married Anna and fathered Tobias. In captivity he refused the Gentiles' bread, gained favor with Enemessar and served as his purchasing agent, and left ten talents of silver with Gabael in Rages of Media. Under Sennacherib he gave to the needy and secretly buried slain Israelites until a Ninevite informed on him, forcing him to flee and costing him all his goods except his wife Anna and son Tobias. After Sennacherib was killed by two of his sons, Sarchedonus reigned and appointed Tobit's kinsman Achiacharus over the kingdom's accounts, and Achiacharus secured Tobit's return to Nineveh.

Chapter 2 body

The narrator, who has returned home with his wife Anna and son Tobias restored to him, sits down to a Pentecost feast and sends Tobias to find a poor kinsman who remembers the Lord. Tobias reports that a man of their race has been strangled and cast out in the marketplace, so the narrator takes the body into a chamber until sunset and then buries it, recalling Amos's prophecy about feasts turning to mourning. His neighbors mock him for burying the dead again despite the danger. After sleeping by his courtyard wall, sparrows drop warm dung into his open eyes and white films form, and physicians cannot help him, though Achiacharus supports him until he goes to Elymais. Anna weaves cloth for wages and brings home a kid as a gift, but the narrator suspects it is stolen and demands she return it, and she rebukes him by asking where his alms and righteous deeds are.

Chapter 3 body

In Chapter 3, Tobit, grieved and weeping, prays to the Lord, acknowledging His righteous judgments, asking Him not to take vengeance for his sins and those of his fathers, and requesting release from life through death. The same day in Ecbatana of Media, Sarah the daughter of Raguel is reproached by her father's maidservants because Asmodaeus the evil spirit has killed her seven husbands before they lay with her. Sarah considers hanging herself but refrains out of concern for her father's old age, and instead prays by the window, declaring her purity and asking to be released from the earth or shown pity. Both prayers are heard before the glory of God, and Raphael is sent to heal Tobit's eyes, give Sarah as wife to Tobias son of Tobit, and bind Asmodaeus.

Chapter 4 body

Tobit, believing he may die, remembers the money he left in trust with Gabael in Rages of Media and calls his son Tobias to instruct him. He tells Tobias to bury him, honor his mother and bury her in the same grave, and to remember the Lord God and do righteousness all his days. He urges generous alms-giving according to one's means, marriage to a wife from the seed of his fathers rather than a strange wife, prompt payment of wages, avoiding drunkenness, and seeking wise counsel. Tobit then explains the ten talents of silver left with Gabael the son of Gabrias, and reassures Tobias that though they are poor, he has much wealth if he fears God and avoids sin.

Chapter 5 body

Tobias tells his father Tobit he will obey, but asks how he can collect the money since he does not know the man who holds it. Tobit gives him the handwriting and tells him to find a companion to travel with him, promising wages. Tobias finds Raphael, who is an angel though Tobias does not know it, and Raphael says he knows the way to Rages of Media. Tobit questions the man's tribe, accepts his claim to be Azarias, son of Ananias, agrees to pay a drachma a day plus necessities, and sends the two off with a blessing, the young man's dog going with them. Tobit's wife Anna weeps at losing their son, but Tobit reassures her that a good angel will accompany Tobias and bring him back safe, and she stops weeping.

Chapter 6 body

On their journey, the young man (later named Tobias) and the angel, who is called Brother Azarias, stop at the river Tigris, where a fish leaps out to swallow the young man. At the angel's command he seizes it, hauls it ashore, cuts it open and keeps the heart, liver and bile; they roast and eat the fish. The angel explains that burning the heart and liver drives away a demon or evil spirit, and that the bile heals white films in the eyes. Near Rages the angel says they will lodge with Raguel, a kinsman whose only daughter Sarah should be given to Tobias as wife. Tobias fears the demon that killed Sarah's seven earlier husbands, but the angel tells him to burn the heart and liver in the bride-chamber and to pray to God, and Tobias comes to love Sarah.

Chapter 7 body

Tobias and his companion, whom Tobias calls Azarias, arrive at Ecbatana and are welcomed at the house of Raguel by Sarah. Raguel notices that Tobias resembles his cousin Tobit, and when the travelers say they are Naphtali's descendants from Nineveh and that Tobit is alive and well, Tobias reveals Tobit is his father. Raguel and his household weep on hearing Tobit is blind, then feast on a ram. Tobias asks that the matter discussed on the road be settled, and Raguel warns that seven relatives who married Sarah died in the night, but Tobias refuses to eat until a covenant is made. Raguel gives Sarah to Tobias as wife under the law of Moses and writes and seals a contract, and afterward Edna prepares another chamber and comforts the weeping Sarah.

Chapter 8 body

After supper, Tobias is brought to his bride. Remembering Raphael's instructions, he burns the fish's heart and liver on incense ashes, and the smell makes the demon flee to the uppermost parts of Egypt, where the angel binds it. Tobias and his bride then pray together for God's mercy, and they sleep. Meanwhile Raguel digs a grave, fearing Tobias will die, but a maidservant finds them both alive, so Raguel blesses God and has the grave filled. He holds a fourteen-day wedding feast and swears that Tobias must stay until it ends, after which Tobias will receive half of Raguel's goods and go safely to his father.

Chapter 9 body

Tobias calls Raphael, whom he addresses as Brother Azarias, and asks him to take a servant and two camels to Gabael in Rages of Media to collect money for him and bring Gabael to the wedding feast. Tobias explains that Raguel has sworn he must not leave, and that his father counts the days and will be very grieved if he is delayed. Raphael goes, lodges with Gabael, and hands over the handwriting, and Gabael brings out the sealed bags of money and gives them to him. Raphael and Gabael rise early the next morning and travel together to the wedding feast, where Tobias blesses his wife.

Chapter 10 body

Tobit counts the days of his son Tobias's journey and worries when Tobias does not return, fearing he is detained or that Gabael has died. Tobit's wife is convinced the child has perished, goes out daily to the road, refuses to eat by day, and weeps through the nights. When the fourteen days of the wedding feast that Raguel had sworn are over, Tobias insists on leaving despite Raguel's offer to send word to his parents instead. Raguel gives Tobias his wife Sarah along with half his goods, servants, cattle and money, and blesses them. Raguel and Edna charge Sarah to honor her in-laws, and Edna asks Tobias not to cause Sarah grief.

Chapter 11 body

Tobias, blessing God for a prosperous journey, leaves Raguel and Edna and travels with Raphael toward Nineveh. Raphael tells Tobias to run ahead with the bile of the fish and explains how to use it on his father's eyes. Anna sees her son coming and runs to embrace him, and Tobias anoints Tobit's eyes with the bile so that the white films peel away and Tobit sees his son. Tobit blesses God and his holy angels, then goes out to the gate of Nineveh to welcome his daughter-in-law Sarah, and the wedding feast of Tobias is kept for seven days with joy among the kindred, including Achiacharus and Nasbas.

Chapter 12 body

Tobit tells his son Tobias to pay the man who accompanied him his wages, and Tobias suggests giving him half of everything brought back, citing the safe journey, the cure of his wife and Tobit, and the recovered money. Tobit agrees and the angel is told to take half. The companion then privately urges both to bless and thank God, teaches that alms, prayer with fasting, and righteousness are good, and reveals that he is Raphael, one of the seven holy angels, who presented their prayers and was with Tobit when he buried the dead. Tobit and Tobias fall on their faces in fear, but Raphael reassures them, says he came by God's will, that he only appeared to eat and drink, and that he is ascending to the one who sent him, and tells them to write down all that has happened. He vanishes, and they confess the great works of God.

Chapter 13 body

Tobit writes a prayer of rejoicing blessing God, the everlasting King, who both scourges and shows mercy. He calls the children of Israel to give thanks among the Gentiles, where they have been scattered, and promises that God will gather them again if they turn to him with their whole heart and soul. Tobit himself gives thanks in the land of his captivity and urges sinners to turn and do righteousness. He then addresses Jerusalem, the holy city, which will be scourged for the works of its sons but shown mercy again, and foretells that many nations will come with gifts. He closes by describing Jerusalem rebuilt with sapphires, emeralds, pure gold and paved streets of beryl, carbuncle and stones of Ophir, all crying 'Hallelujah!'

Chapter 14 body

Tobit, after regaining his sight and growing very old, calls his son Tobias and his six grandsons and gives a final charge. He tells them to leave Nineveh for Media, because Jonah's prophecy of Nineveh's overthrow will come true, and he foretells the scattering of his kindred, the desolation and burning of Jerusalem and the house of God, and their later restoration with all nations turning to the Lord God. He urges them to keep the law, be merciful and righteous, and bury him and Anna decently, citing Aman and Achiacharus to show what alms and righteousness do. Tobit dies at 158, Tobias buries him and Anna, moves to Ecbatana in Media to Raguel, inherits the family possessions, and dies at 127 after hearing of the destruction of Nineveh by Nebuchadnezzar and Ahasuerus.
