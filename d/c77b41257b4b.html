Its central concern is how believers should live faithfully, holy and hopeful, while suffering, with Jesus Christ as the model and basis of their salvation. Chapter 1 blesses God for causing believers to be born again to a living hope and a heavenly inheritance. Trials test faith like gold in fire. Readers are urged to be holy as God is holy, since they were redeemed by the precious blood of Christ, the lamb without blemish, and to love one another fervently. Chapter 2 tells them to put away wickedness and long for pure spiritual milk. Christ is the living stone and chief cornerstone, and believers are a spiritual house, royal priesthood, and holy nation. They are to behave well among the nations, submit to the king, governors, and masters, and honor all. Christ's unjust suffering, without sin or retaliation, is their example, and they have returned to the Shepherd and Overseer of their souls. Chapter 3 instructs wives and husbands in their conduct, with Sarah and Abraham as the example, then urges all to be like-minded and to bless rather than return evil. Suffering for righteousness is a blessing, and believers should answer for their hope with humility and a good conscience. Baptism is described as a symbol that saves through his resurrection. Chapter 4 calls believers to arm themselves with Christ's mind and abandon the lusts of the Gentiles. Because the end of all things is near, they are to be sober, loving, hospitable, and to serve one another with their gifts. They should not be astonished at the fiery trial, should rejoice in sharing Christ's sufferings, and should entrust their souls to a faithful Creator while doing good, since judgment begins with God's household. 1 Peter / Chapter 1 body

Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, writes to chosen ones living as foreigners in the Dispersion across Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia. He blesses God the Father of Jesus Christ for causing believers to be born again to a living hope and an inheritance reserved in Heaven, and for guarding them through faith for a coming salvation. He says their trials test their faith like gold in fire, and that the prophets and angels longed to understand this salvation. He then urges the readers to prepare their minds, to be holy as God is holy, and to live in reverent fear, since they were redeemed by the precious blood of Christ, the lamb without blemish. He closes by calling them to love one another fervently, having been born again through the enduring word of God, which is the Good News preached to them.

Chapter 2 body

Chapter 2 urges believers to put away wickedness, deceit, hypocrisy, envy and evil speaking, and to long for pure spiritual milk in order to grow. It describes Jesus Christ as a living stone, rejected by men but chosen and precious to God, and as the chief cornerstone, with believers built up as a spiritual house and holy priesthood, while the disobedient stumble over him. Believers are called a chosen race, royal priesthood, holy nation and God's people, who now have mercy and are to live as foreigners and pilgrims with good behavior among the nations. They are told to submit to the king, governors and masters, to honor all, love the brotherhood and fear God, living as free people who are bondservants of God. The chapter ends with Christ's example of suffering unjustly without sin or retaliation, bearing sins on the tree so that believers might live to righteousness and return to the Shepherd and Overseer of their souls.

Chapter 3 body

Chapter 3 first instructs wives to be in subjection to their husbands so that disobedient husbands may be won by their behavior, urging inner adornment of a gentle and quiet spirit over outward adornment, with Sarah and Abraham as the example. Husbands are told to live with their wives according to knowledge, honoring them as the weaker vessel and as joint heirs of the grace of life, so that their prayers are not hindered. All the addressees are then urged to be like-minded, loving, and to bless rather than return evil, citing a quotation about seeking peace and about the Lord's eyes being on the righteous. The chapter says that suffering for righteousness is a blessing, that believers should be ready to answer for their hope with humility and a good conscience, and that it is better to suffer for doing right than for doing evil. It closes with Christ, who suffered once for sins, was made alive in the Spirit, preached to the spirits in prison from the days of Noah, and is now at God's right hand with angels, authorities and powers subject to him, while baptism is described as a symbol that saves through his resurrection.

Chapter 4 body

Chapter 4 urges the addressees to arm themselves with the same mind as Christ, who suffered in the flesh, and to stop living for the lusts of the Gentiles, whose past conduct they describe, in favor of God's will. Those who think it strange that the believers no longer join their excess speak evil of them but will give account to God, who is ready to judge the living and the dead. Because the end of all things is near, the believers are told to be sober, earnest in love, hospitable, and to use their gifts to serve one another so that God is glorified through Jesus Christ. The chapter then tells them not to be astonished at the fiery trial, to rejoice in sharing Christ's sufferings, to avoid suffering as wrongdoers, and not to be ashamed if they suffer as Christians. Judgment begins with the household of God, and those who suffer according to God's will are to entrust their souls to a faithful Creator while doing good.

Chapter 5 body

The author, who calls himself a fellow elder and a witness of Christ's sufferings, urges the elders to shepherd God's flock willingly and as examples, not for dishonest gain or by lording over others. He tells the younger ones to be subject to the elder and tells everyone to clothe themselves with humility, since God resists the proud and gives grace to the humble. The readers are told to cast their worries on God, to be sober and watchful against the devil who prowls like a roaring lion, and to withstand him steadfastly, knowing their brothers elsewhere suffer the same. The author prays that the God of all grace will perfect, establish, strengthen, and settle them after a little suffering. He closes by naming Silvanus as the one through whom he wrote, sending greetings from the woman in Babylon and from Mark, and giving a blessing of peace.
