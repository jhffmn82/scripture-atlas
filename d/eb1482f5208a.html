Abstract written by a model

This is the second letter to Timothy, written by Paul, an imprisoned apostle who knows his death is near. It is a personal and pastoral charge to Timothy, his beloved child in the faith, urging him to remain faithful and carry on the work. Chapter 1 opens with Paul's thanks for Timothy's sincere faith, which lived first in his grandmother Lois and mother Eunice. Paul urges him to stir up God's gift, since God gave a spirit of power, love, and self-control rather than fear. Timothy must not be ashamed of the Lord or of Paul the prisoner, because Christ abolished death and brought life to light through the Good News. Paul notes that all in Asia, including Phygelus and Hermogenes, turned from him, while Onesiphorus refreshed him and sought him out in Rome. Chapter 2 calls Timothy to be strong in Christ's grace, to entrust the teaching to faithful men who can teach others, and to endure hardship like a soldier, athlete, and farmer. Paul suffers to the point of chains, yet God's word is not chained. He quotes a trustworthy saying about dying, enduring, and reigning with Christ, warns against word-quarrels, and names Hymenaeus and Philetus as teaching error about the resurrection. Timothy is to flee youthful lusts, pursue righteousness, and correct opponents gently. Chapter 3 warns of grievous last days, when people will be lovers of self, money, and pleasure, with a form of godliness but no power. Timothy should turn from them, as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses. Contrasted with deceivers, Timothy has followed Paul's teaching and sufferings, including persecutions at Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra. He should continue in the God-breathed Scriptures he has known since infancy, which equip for every good work. Chapter 4 gives Paul's solemn charge to preach the word patiently, even as people seek teachers who please itching ears. Paul says he has fought the good fight, finished the course, and kept the faith, and a crown of righteousness awaits him. He asks Timothy to come soon with Mark, his cloak, and the books and parchments, since Demas deserted him and only Luke remains. He warns against Alexander the coppersmith, recalls the Lord standing by him at his first defense, and ends with greetings.

2 Timothy / Chapter 1 body

Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus by God's will, greets Timothy, his beloved child, and says he thanks God and remembers Timothy's tears and the sincere faith that lived first in Timothy's grandmother Lois and mother Eunice. He urges Timothy to stir up the gift of God received through the laying on of Paul's hands, reminding him that God gave a spirit of power, love, and self-control rather than fear. Paul tells Timothy not to be ashamed of the Lord's testimony or of Paul as prisoner, because God saved and called people according to his own purpose and grace, revealed through Christ Jesus, who abolished death and brought life and immortality to light through the Good News. Paul says he was appointed preacher, apostle, and teacher of the Gentiles, suffers for this, yet is not ashamed, and charges Timothy to hold sound words and guard the good thing committed to him through the Holy Spirit. He notes that all in Asia, including Phygelus and Hermogenes, turned away from him, while he asks mercy for the house of Onesiphorus, who refreshed him, was not ashamed of his chain, and sought him out in Rome.

Chapter 2 body

The speaker urges his child, the addressee, to be strengthened in Christ's grace, to pass on what he has heard to faithful men who can teach others, and to endure hardship like a soldier, athlete, and farmer. He tells him to remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, and says he himself suffers to the point of chains, though God's word isn't chained, and endures all things for the chosen ones. He quotes a trustworthy saying about dying, living, enduring, and reigning with Christ, and about Christ remaining faithful even when people are faithless. He warns against arguing about words and empty chatter, naming Hymenaeus and Philetus as men who say the resurrection is already past and overthrow some people's faith. He contrasts vessels of honor and dishonor in a large house, tells the addressee to flee youthful lusts and pursue righteousness, and describes the Lord's servant as gentle and patient in correcting opponents, in hope that God may grant them repentance.

Chapter 3 body

The chapter warns that in the last days grievous times will come, when men will be lovers of self, money, and pleasure, holding a form of godliness while denying its power. The speaker tells the addressee to turn away from such people, who creep into houses and captivate gullible women, and who oppose the truth as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses. The speaker says their folly will become evident and they will go no further, though evil men and impostors will grow worse, deceiving and being deceived. The addressee is contrasted as one who followed the speaker's teaching, conduct, and sufferings, including persecutions at Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra from which the Lord delivered the speaker. The addressee is urged to remain in what was learned, grounded in the holy Scriptures known since infancy, which are God-breathed and equip people for every good work.

Chapter 4 body

Paul solemnly charges Timothy before God and Christ to preach the word with patience, warning that people will come to reject sound doctrine for teachers who please their itching ears and for fables. Paul says his death is near, declaring that he has fought the good fight, finished the course and kept the faith, and that a crown of righteousness awaits him and all who love Christ's appearing. He asks Timothy to come soon, bringing Mark, the cloak left with Carpus at Troas, and the books and parchments, since Demas has deserted him, others have gone elsewhere, and only Luke remains. Paul warns of Alexander the coppersmith, recalls that no one stood with him at his first defense while the Lord strengthened and delivered him, and closes with greetings to Prisca, Aquila and the house of Onesiphorus, news of Erastus and Trophimus, and greetings from Eubulus, Pudens, Linus, Claudia and the brothers.
