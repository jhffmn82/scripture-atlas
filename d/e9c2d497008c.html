Exodus

author unknown · about 1439 to 1399 BC · 40 sections
Exodus, from the World English Bible, Catholic Edition, dedicated to the public domain by eBible.org. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://ebible.org/eng-web-c/

Abstract written by a model

This document is the book of Exodus (chapters 1-40), narrating Israel's passage from Egyptian bondage to the establishment of worship at the tabernacle. The most important figures are the LORD (God), Moses, his brother Aaron, and Pharaoh, with supporting roles for Miriam, Jethro, Joshua, Bezalel and Oholiab. It begins with the Israelites multiplying in Egypt, a new king oppressing them with hard labor, and Pharaoh ordering newborn boys killed. Moses is hidden, rescued by Pharaoh's daughter, and raised as her son, then flees to Midian after killing an Egyptian, marrying Zipporah. At the burning bush, God reveals himself as 'I AM WHO I AM' and sends Moses, with Aaron as spokesman, to demand Israel's release. Pharaoh refuses and increases their burdens, and a series of plagues follows: blood, frogs, lice, flies, livestock pestilence, boils, hail, locusts, and darkness, with Pharaoh's heart repeatedly hardened. The final plague kills Egypt's firstborn, while Israel, marked by Passover lamb's blood, is spared and leaves, with ordinances for Unleavened Bread and the firstborn. Led by pillars of cloud and fire, Israel is pursued to the sea, which divides; Pharaoh's army drowns, and Moses and Miriam lead songs of praise. In the wilderness the people murmur over bitter water, hunger, and thirst, and God provides sweetened water, quail, manna with the Sabbath rest, and water from the rock; Israel defeats Amalek, and Jethro advises Moses to appoint judges. At Sinai, Israel agrees to the covenant, and God speaks the commandments, followed by detailed civil, moral, and religious laws, and the covenant is ratified with blood. Moses ascends the mountain for forty days, where God gives instructions for the tabernacle, ark, altars, priestly garments, consecration, incense, and anointing oil, and names Bezalel and Oholiab as craftsmen. Meanwhile the people have Aaron make a golden calf; Moses breaks the tablets, the Levites kill about three thousand, and Moses intercedes. God relents, renews the covenant on new tablets, reveals his merciful name, and Moses' face shines. The closing chapters describe the people's generous offerings, the craftsmen building everything as commanded, and Moses setting up the tabernacle in the second year's first month. The cloud covers the Tent and God's glory fills it, and Israel thereafter journeys only when the cloud lifts.

Exodus / Chapter 1 body

Exodus 1 lists the sons of Israel who came into Egypt with Jacob, seventy souls in all, with Joseph already there. After Joseph and his generation died, the Israelites multiplied greatly and filled the land. A new king of Egypt who did not know Joseph feared their numbers and set taskmasters over them, forcing them into hard labor building the storage cities Pithom and Raamses. When they kept multiplying, the king ordered the Hebrew midwives Shiphrah and Puah to kill newborn boys, but the midwives feared God and let them live, and God gave them families. Pharaoh then commanded all his people to cast every newborn son into the river while saving the daughters alive.

Chapter 2 body

A Levite woman hides her newborn son for three months, then places him in a tar-coated papyrus basket among the reeds by the river, while his sister watches. Pharaoh's daughter finds him, takes pity, and through the sister hires the child's own mother as nurse; he becomes the princess's son and she names him Moses. As an adult, Moses kills an Egyptian who was striking a Hebrew, and when a Hebrew challenges him the next day, he realizes the deed is known; Pharaoh seeks to kill him and he flees to Midian. There he helps the priest Reuel's seven daughters at a well, is welcomed by Reuel, marries Zipporah, and has a son named Gershom. Meanwhile the king of Egypt dies, the Israelites cry out under bondage, and God hears, remembers his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and sees them.

Chapter 3 body

While keeping the flock of his father-in-law Jethro, Moses comes to Horeb, God's mountain, and sees a bush that burns without being consumed. God calls to him from the bush, identifies himself as the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and says he has seen the affliction of his people in Egypt. God sends Moses to Pharaoh to bring the children of Israel out toward a land flowing with milk and honey. Moses objects and asks what name to give the people, and God answers 'I AM WHO I AM' and calls himself the LORD, the God of their fathers. God instructs Moses to gather the elders, predicts that the king of Egypt will refuse until God strikes Egypt with wonders, and promises that the Israelites will plunder the Egyptians when they leave.

Chapter 4 body

Moses fears the Israelites will not believe him, so the LORD gives him signs: his rod turns into a snake and back, his hand becomes leprous and is restored, and river water will turn to blood. When Moses protests that he is slow of speech and asks the LORD to send someone else, the LORD becomes angry and appoints his brother Aaron as spokesman. Moses gets permission from Jethro, his father-in-law, to return to Egypt with his wife and sons, carrying God's rod, and the LORD tells him he will harden Pharaoh's heart and that Pharaoh's firstborn will be killed if he refuses. On the way the LORD meets Moses and wants to kill him until his wife Zipporah circumcises her son. Aaron meets Moses at God's mountain, and together they gather the elders, perform the signs, and the people believe and worship.

Chapter 5 body

Moses and Aaron come to Pharaoh with the LORD's demand to let Israel go to hold a feast in the wilderness, but Pharaoh refuses, saying he does not know the LORD. He accuses the people of idleness and orders his taskmasters and officers to stop supplying straw while demanding the same number of bricks. The people scatter across Egypt to gather stubble, and the Israelite officers are beaten when the quota is not met. The officers appeal to Pharaoh, who rejects them and repeats that the people are idle. The officers then blame Moses and Aaron for making them a stench to Pharaoh, and Moses asks the LORD why he has brought trouble on the people and has not rescued them.

Chapter 6 body

The LORD tells Moses he will see what is done to Pharaoh, and identifies himself as the God who appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as God Almighty but was not known to them by the name the LORD. He declares he has heard Israel's groaning, remembered his covenant, and promises to redeem the children of Israel from Egyptian bondage and bring them to the land sworn to the patriarchs. Moses relays this, but the people do not listen because of anguish and cruel bondage. When the LORD commands Moses to speak to Pharaoh, Moses objects that he has uncircumcised lips. A genealogy of the heads of fathers' houses of Reuben, Simeon, and Levi follows, tracing Aaron and Moses through Amram and Jochebed, and the chapter closes with the LORD repeating the command and Moses repeating his objection.

Chapter 7 body

The LORD tells Moses he has been made as God to Pharaoh, with Aaron as his prophet, and announces that he will harden Pharaoh's heart and bring the children of Israel out of Egypt by great judgments. Moses (eighty) and Aaron (eighty-three) go to Pharaoh, where Aaron's rod becomes a serpent and swallows the rods of the Egyptian magicians, who had done the same with their enchantments. Pharaoh's heart stays hardened, so the LORD sends Moses to meet him at the river's bank with a demand to let the people go. Aaron then stretches out the rod and the waters of Egypt turn to blood, killing the fish and making the river foul. The magicians duplicate this too, Pharaoh turns away unmoved, the Egyptians dig for drinking water, and seven days pass.

Chapter 8 body

The LORD sends Moses to demand that Pharaoh let his people go, and brings three plagues on Egypt when Pharaoh refuses. Frogs cover the land, and Pharaoh promises to release the people if they are removed, but he hardens his heart once the frogs die. Aaron strikes the dust with his rod and it becomes lice on people and animals; the magicians cannot imitate this and say it is God's finger, yet Pharaoh still refuses. The LORD then sends swarms of flies on Egypt while setting apart the land of Goshen, and Pharaoh offers a compromise, but after Moses prays and the flies are removed he again refuses to let the people go.

Chapter 9 body

In Exodus chapter 9, the LORD sends Moses to Pharaoh with a demand to let the Hebrews go, and Pharaoh's refusal brings three more plagues on Egypt. A pestilence kills all of Egypt's livestock while sparing Israel's, then ashes sprinkled by Moses become boils on people and animals, including the magicians. The LORD then warns of an unprecedented hail and tells Pharaoh he was raised up to show God's power, and the hail strikes Egypt's fields, people, animals, flax, barley and trees but spares Goshen. Pharaoh confesses sin and promises to release Israel, but once Moses stops the hail he hardens his heart again and refuses, as the LORD had said.

Chapter 10 body

The LORD tells Moses to go to Pharaoh because he has hardened Pharaoh's heart so that his signs may be shown. Moses and Aaron warn Pharaoh of locusts, and Pharaoh's servants urge him to let the men go; Pharaoh offers to release only the men, and they are driven out. The LORD brings an east wind and locusts devour all remaining greenery in Egypt, Pharaoh confesses sin and asks for forgiveness, and the LORD removes the locusts with a west wind into the Red Sea, then hardens Pharaoh's heart. The LORD then sends a thick darkness over Egypt for three days while Israel has light, and Pharaoh offers to let everyone go except the flocks and herds, which Moses refuses. The LORD hardens Pharaoh's heart again, and Pharaoh orders Moses out, threatening him with death if he sees his face again.

Chapter 11 body

The LORD tells Moses that one more plague will fall on Pharaoh and Egypt, after which Pharaoh will drive the Israelites out, and instructs him to have the people ask their neighbors for silver and gold jewels. Moses, who is held in high regard by the Egyptians and Pharaoh's servants, announces to Pharaoh that at midnight the LORD will go through Egypt and kill all the firstborn, from Pharaoh's own to the servant behind the mill, and the livestock's firstborn. A great cry will rise over Egypt, while Israel will be untouched, showing that the LORD distinguishes between Egypt and Israel. Moses leaves Pharaoh in hot anger, and the LORD says Pharaoh will not listen so his wonders may be multiplied. The chapter closes noting that Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh, but the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart and he did not let Israel go.

Chapter 12 body

In Exodus 12 the LORD instructs Moses and Aaron in Egypt to establish the Passover: each household is to take a year-old unblemished lamb on the tenth day of the first month, kill it at evening on the fourteenth, put its blood on the door posts and lintel, and eat it roasted with unleavened bread and bitter herbs. Moses relays the instructions to the elders, and the LORD strikes all the firstborn of Egypt at midnight while passing over the houses marked with blood. Pharaoh and the Egyptians urge Moses and the Israelites to leave, and the Israelites depart from Rameses to Succoth with unleavened dough, plunder from the Egyptians, and a mixed multitude, after 430 years in Egypt. The chapter also sets out lasting ordinances for the Feast of Unleavened Bread and rules on who may eat the Passover, including circumcision requirements for servants and strangers.

Chapter 13 body

In Exodus 13 the LORD tells Moses to sanctify all firstborn of the children of Israel, man and animal, as his own. Moses instructs the people to remember the day they left Egypt, to eat unleavened bread for seven days with a feast on the seventh, and to tell their sons why, once they are in the land of the Canaanite and other peoples. Moses also gives rules for the firstborn: males of animals are the LORD's, a donkey's firstborn is redeemed with a lamb or has its neck broken, and human firstborn sons are redeemed, in memory of the LORD killing Egypt's firstborn after Pharaoh refused to let Israel go. After Pharaoh releases the people, God leads them by the wilderness road by the Red Sea rather than the Philistine road so they will not turn back at the sight of war, and Moses carries Joseph's bones. They camp at Etham, and the LORD goes before them in a pillar of cloud by day and a pillar of fire by night.

Chapter 14 body

The LORD instructs Moses to have the children of Israel turn back and encamp by the sea before Pihahiroth, saying he will harden Pharaoh's heart so that Pharaoh pursues them and the LORD gains honor over him. Pharaoh and his servants regret letting Israel go and chase them with six hundred chosen chariots and all the chariots of Egypt, overtaking the Israelites by the sea. The frightened Israelites complain to Moses, who tells them to stand still and see the LORD's salvation. At the LORD's command Moses stretches out his hand, a strong east wind divides the sea, and Israel crosses on dry ground while the pillar of cloud stands between them and the Egyptians. The Egyptians follow into the sea, the LORD confuses them and removes their chariot wheels, and when Moses stretches out his hand again the waters return and drown Pharaoh's entire army, after which Israel fears and believes in the LORD and in Moses.

Chapter 15 body

Moses and the children of Israel sing a song to the LORD celebrating his triumph in drowning Pharaoh's horses, chariots, and army in the Red Sea, and anticipating that he will bring his people to his holy dwelling while the nations of Philistia, Edom, Moab, and Canaan tremble. Miriam the prophetess leads the women with tambourines and dances, repeating the song's opening line. Moses then leads Israel from the Red Sea into the wilderness of Shur, where after three days without water they reach Marah, whose waters are bitter. The people murmur, Moses cries to the LORD, and the LORD shows him a tree that sweetens the water when thrown in. There the LORD sets a statute and ordinance, promises healing and freedom from Egypt's diseases in return for obedience, and Israel then camps at Elim with twelve springs and seventy palm trees.

Chapter 16 body

The children of Israel arrive in the wilderness of Sin and murmur against Moses and Aaron, wishing they had died in Egypt rather than starve. The LORD promises bread from the sky and meat, and Moses and Aaron tell the people that their murmurings are really against the LORD, whose glory appears in the cloud. Quail cover the camp in the evening, and in the morning the people find manna, which they gather by the omer according to their eating; leftovers spoil except on the sixth day, when a double portion is gathered. The LORD establishes the Sabbath rest on the seventh day, when no manna is found, and Aaron lays up an omer of manna before the Testimony. The Israelites eat manna for forty years until they reach the borders of Canaan.

Chapter 17 body

The children of Israel travel from the wilderness of Sin to Rephidim at the LORD's command, find no water, and quarrel with Moses, who fears they will stone him. The LORD tells Moses to take the elders and his rod and strike the rock in Horeb, and water comes out; Moses names the place Massah and Meribah. Amalek then attacks Israel at Rephidim, and Joshua fights while Moses, Aaron, and Hur go up the hill. Israel prevails while Moses holds up his hand, so Aaron and Hur support his hands until sunset, and Joshua defeats Amalek with the sword. The LORD orders Moses to write the event in a book and declares he will blot out the memory of Amalek, and Moses builds an altar called The LORD our Banner.

Chapter 18 body

Jethro, priest of Midian and Moses' father-in-law, hears of what God did for Moses and Israel and brings Moses' wife Zipporah and her two sons, Gershom and Eliezer, to Moses at the Mountain of God. Moses greets him and recounts the LORD's deliverance of Israel from Pharaoh and the Egyptians, and Jethro rejoices, praises the LORD as greater than all gods, and offers a burnt offering and sacrifices, with Aaron and the elders eating bread with him. The next day Jethro sees Moses judging the people alone from morning to evening and tells him this is not good because the work is too heavy for him. He advises Moses to teach the people God's statutes, to represent them before God, and to appoint able, God-fearing, truthful men as rulers of thousands, hundreds, fifties and tens who will judge small matters while Moses handles great ones. Moses follows the counsel, appoints such rulers, and sends Jethro on his way back to his own land.

Chapter 19 body

In the third month after leaving Egypt, the children of Israel arrive at the wilderness of Sinai and camp before the mountain. The LORD calls to Moses from the mountain and offers Israel the status of his own possession, a kingdom of priests and a holy nation, if they obey his voice and keep his covenant. The people answer together that they will do all the LORD has spoken, and the LORD instructs Moses to sanctify them, set bounds around the mountain, and prepare for the third day. On that day thunder, lightning, a thick cloud, a loud trumpet, fire, and smoke and quaking mark the LORD's descent on Mount Sinai, and Moses goes up at his call. The LORD then sends Moses back down to warn the people and priests not to break through to gaze, and Moses goes down to tell them.

Chapter 20 body

God speaks the commandments to the people, identifying himself as the LORD who brought them out of Egypt. He forbids other gods, idols, misuse of his name, and murder, adultery, theft, false testimony, and coveting, and commands keeping the Sabbath holy and honoring parents. The people, terrified by thunder, lightning, trumpet sound, and smoke on the mountain, stay at a distance and ask Moses to speak with God for them. Moses reassures them that God has come to test them, then approaches the thick darkness where God is. The LORD then instructs Moses to tell Israel not to make gods of silver or gold and gives rules for building an altar of earth or uncut stone, without steps.

Chapter 21 body

Chapter 21 opens a set of ordinances to be set before the people, beginning with rules for Hebrew servants, who serve six years and go free in the seventh, and for a daughter sold as a female servant, who is protected from being sold abroad and must receive food, clothing and marital rights. It then lists laws on violence: death for murder, attacking or cursing parents, and kidnapping, with provision of a place of refuge for unintentional killing, and compensation for non-fatal injuries. Masters who injure servants face punishment or must free them for a lost eye or tooth, while a pregnant woman harmed in a fight brings a fine or, if further harm follows, life for life, eye for eye and so on. The chapter closes with rules on liability for a goring bull, an owner's responsibility when he knew of the habit, an uncovered pit, and one bull killing another.

Chapter 22 body

Chapter 22 sets out laws on theft, property damage, and entrusted goods. A thief who steals an ox or sheep and kills or sells it pays five oxen or four sheep, while one found with the live animal pays double. Owners of animals or fires that damage another's field must make restitution, and disputes over goods kept by a neighbor are settled before God by oath or judgment. The chapter then turns to sexual and religious offenses, including seduction of a virgin, sorcery, bestiality, and sacrifice to other gods. It ends with commands to protect aliens, widows, fatherless children, and the poor, to honor God and rulers, to give firstfruits and firstborn to the LORD, and to avoid eating torn meat.

Chapter 23 body

In Exodus 23, God gives the Israelites laws on honest testimony and impartial justice, including bans on false reports, following a crowd to pervert justice, bribes, and oppressing aliens, along with a duty to return an enemy's stray animal and help his fallen donkey. He then sets a sabbatical year of rest for the land and a weekly Sabbath rest, and requires three annual feasts: unleavened bread, harvest, and ingathering. God promises to send an angel before the people to bring them into the land and to drive out the Amorite, Hittite, Perizzite, Canaanite, Hivite, and Jebusite little by little. He forbids serving or covenanting with their gods and promises blessing, health, and a border from the Red Sea to the sea of the Philistines if the Israelites obey.

Chapter 24 body

The LORD tells Moses to come up with Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders to worship from a distance, while Moses alone may come near. Moses relays the LORD's words and ordinances, and the people promise to do all that the LORD has said. Moses writes the words down, builds an altar with twelve pillars, has young men offer burnt and peace offerings, reads the book of the covenant to the people, and sprinkles them with blood, declaring it the blood of the covenant. Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and the seventy elders then go up and see the God of Israel, and they eat and drink. Moses goes further up Mount Sinai with Joshua, leaving Aaron and Hur in charge of disputes, and stays in the cloud of the LORD's glory for forty days and forty nights.

Chapter 25 body

The LORD instructs Moses to tell the children of Israel to bring a freewill offering of materials such as gold, silver, bronze, fabrics, skins, acacia wood, oil, spices, and stones, so they can make a sanctuary where he may dwell among them. He then gives detailed specifications for the furnishings: an acacia-wood ark overlaid with gold and carried by poles, a gold mercy seat with two hammered-gold cherubim, a gold-overlaid table for the bread of the presence, and a hammered pure-gold lamp stand with six branches and seven lamps. The LORD says he will meet with Moses and speak from above the mercy seat, between the cherubim. Everything must follow the pattern shown to Moses on the mountain.

Chapter 26 body

Chapter 26 gives instructions for constructing the tabernacle. Ten curtains of fine twined linen with blue, purple, scarlet and cherubim are coupled in two sets of five by fifty blue loops and fifty gold clasps, and eleven goats' hair curtains are coupled with bronze clasps to cover the tent, with coverings of rams' skins dyed red and sea cow hides above. The framework is made of upright acacia boards (twenty on the south, twenty on the north, eight on the west) set in silver sockets and held by gold-overlaid acacia bars. A veil of blue, purple, scarlet and linen hung on four gold-overlaid pillars separates the holy place from the most holy, where the ark of the covenant and mercy seat are placed. The table and lamp stand are set outside the veil, and a screen with five pillars on bronze sockets is made for the door of the Tent.

Chapter 27 body

This chapter gives instructions for making the altar of acacia wood, which is square, five cubits by five cubits and three cubits high, with horns of one piece with it, overlaid with bronze. It lists the bronze vessels, a bronze grating with four rings, and the acacia poles overlaid with bronze for carrying it, and says it is to be made hollow with planks as shown on the mountain. It then specifies the court of the tabernacle: hangings of fine twined linen on the south, north, west and east sides, with pillars set in bronze sockets, silver hooks and fillets, and a twenty-cubit embroidered screen for the gate. Finally, the children of Israel are commanded to bring pure olive oil beaten for the light so a lamp burns continually, and Aaron and his sons are to keep it in order in the Tent of Meeting from evening to morning before the LORD as a statute forever.

Chapter 28 body

In Exodus 28, the LORD instructs that Aaron, his brother, and Aaron's sons Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar be set apart from the children of Israel to minister in the priest's office. Wise-hearted workers are to make holy garments for glory and beauty: a breastplate, ephod, robe, fitted tunic, turban, and sash, using gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine linen. The ephod carries two engraved onyx stones with the tribes' names on its shoulders, and the breastplate of judgment holds twelve engraved stones and the Urim and the Thummim over Aaron's heart. The robe has pomegranates and golden bells, and a gold plate engraved 'HOLY TO THE LORD' is worn on Aaron's forehead. Aaron's sons receive tunics, sashes, headbands, and linen pants, and all are to be anointed, consecrated, and sanctified, with the pants worn so they do not bear iniquity and die.

Chapter 29 body

Chapter 29 gives the LORD's instructions for consecrating Aaron and his sons as priests: washing, clothing, and anointing Aaron, then offering a young bull as a sin offering, one ram as a burnt offering, and a second ram of consecration whose blood marks the priests. The priests receive portions of the wave offerings, eat the consecration ram and bread at the door of the Tent of Meeting, and any leftovers are burned. Aaron's holy garments pass to his sons after him, and the seven-day consecration includes daily sin offerings to cleanse and sanctify the altar. The chapter ends with the daily offering of two lambs, morning and evening, with flour, oil, and wine, and the LORD's promise to meet Israel, sanctify the place by his glory, and dwell among the children of Israel as their God.

Chapter 30 body

In Exodus 30 the LORD gives Moses instructions for further tabernacle furnishings and rites. He describes an acacia-wood altar of incense overlaid with gold, on which Aaron is to burn incense every morning and evening. He sets a half-shekel atonement ransom for each Israelite counted in a census, a bronze basin for Aaron and his sons to wash their hands and feet so they do not die, and a holy anointing oil of myrrh, cinnamon, cane, cassia and olive oil for consecrating the Tent of Meeting, its furnishings, and the priests. Finally he specifies a holy incense of sweet spices, gum resin, onycha, galbanum and frankincense, and forbids making either the oil or the incense for ordinary use, on penalty of being cut off from the people.

Chapter 31 body

The LORD tells Moses that he has called Bezalel, son of Uri of the tribe of Judah, and filled him with the Spirit of God and skill in all kinds of craftsmanship. He appoints Oholiab, son of Ahisamach of the tribe of Dan, to work with Bezalel, and puts wisdom in all the wise-hearted so they can make everything commanded, including the Tent of Meeting, the ark, the furniture, the priestly garments, the anointing oil and the incense. The LORD then tells Moses to instruct the children of Israel to keep his Sabbaths as a perpetual sign and covenant, on pain of death for anyone who works on that day, because he made heaven and earth in six days and rested on the seventh. When he finishes speaking with Moses on Mount Sinai, he gives him the two stone tablets of the covenant, written with God's finger.

Chapter 32 body

When Moses delays on the mountain, the people of Israel ask Aaron to make gods, and Aaron collects their golden earrings and fashions a molded calf, proclaiming a feast to the LORD. The LORD tells Moses of their corruption and threatens to consume them, but Moses pleads, citing the Egyptians and the promise to Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, and the LORD relents. Moses comes down carrying the two tablets, sees the calf and the dancing, breaks the tablets, burns and grinds the calf and makes the people drink it, and confronts Aaron. The sons of Levi respond to Moses' call and kill about three thousand men at his command, and Moses then returns to the LORD to seek atonement, offering to be blotted out of the LORD's book. The LORD says he will blot out whoever sinned, tells Moses to lead the people on with his angel going before them, and strikes the people for what they did with the calf.

Chapter 33 body

The LORD tells Moses to lead the Israelites from Horeb toward the land promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, with an angel going ahead, but says he will not go among them because they are stiff-necked. The people mourn and strip off their jewelry. Moses pitches the Tent of Meeting outside the camp, where the pillar of cloud descends and the LORD speaks with him face to face while the people worship at their tent doors. Moses pleads for the LORD's presence to go with them, and the LORD agrees. Moses then asks to see the LORD's glory, and the LORD promises to pass his goodness before him and proclaim his name, but says Moses may see only his back, hidden in a cleft of the rock, not his face.

Chapter 34 body

The LORD commands Moses to chisel two new stone tablets and come alone to Mount Sinai, where the LORD descends in a cloud and proclaims his name as merciful, gracious, and just. Moses bows and asks the LORD to go among the stiff-necked people and pardon them, and the LORD responds by making a covenant, promising marvels and the driving out of the Amorite, Canaanite, Hittite, Perizzite, Hivite, and Jebusite. Israel is commanded to make no covenant with the land's inhabitants, to destroy their altars, pillars, and Asherah poles, to make no cast idols, and to keep the feasts, the Sabbath, and the firstborn and offering laws. Moses spends forty days and nights with the LORD and writes the covenant words, the ten commandments, on the tablets. When he comes down, his face shines, the Israelites fear to approach, and he wears a veil except when speaking with the LORD.

Chapter 35 body

Moses gathers the whole congregation of the children of Israel and relays the LORD's commands: observe the Sabbath as a day of solemn rest with no work or fire-kindling, and bring a willing offering of materials for the tabernacle and the priestly garments. He lists the materials and everything the wise-hearted are to make, from the ark and table to the altars, court hangings, and holy garments for Aaron and his sons. The people, men and women, return and bring gold jewelry, fabrics, skins, metals, wood, spices, oil, and stones, with the women spinning thread and the rulers bringing onyx and other stones. Moses then announces that the LORD has called Bezalel of Judah and Oholiab of Dan, filling them with wisdom and skill in all kinds of craftsmanship and the ability to teach.

Chapter 36 body

Moses calls Bezalel, Oholiab, and every wise-hearted man whom the LORD has gifted with wisdom to do the work of the sanctuary according to the LORD's commands. The workers receive the Israelites' daily free will offerings from Moses until they report that the people have brought much more than enough, so Moses has a proclamation made that restrains the people from bringing more. The wise-hearted workers then make the tabernacle from ten curtains of fine linen with cherubim, joined by fifty blue loops and fifty gold clasps, plus eleven goats' hair curtains joined by bronze clasps, and coverings of rams' skins and sea cow hides. They also make the acacia boards with silver sockets for the south, north, and west sides, the bars with gold-covered rings, the veil with four gold-overlaid pillars on silver sockets, and the door screen with five pillars on bronze sockets.

Chapter 37 body

Chapter 37 describes Bezalel's construction of the sacred furnishings. He makes the ark of acacia wood overlaid with gold, with rings and poles for carrying it, and a pure gold mercy seat with two beaten-work cherubim whose wings cover it. He then makes the table of acacia wood with its gold overlay, border, rings, poles, and gold vessels. He crafts the lamp stand of beaten pure gold with six branches, seven lamps, and accessories, made from a talent of gold. Finally he makes the acacia altar of incense overlaid with gold, along with the holy anointing oil and pure incense.

Chapter 38 body

Chapter 38 describes the construction of the bronze altar of burnt offering, with its horns, vessels, grating, rings, poles and bronze basin made from the mirrors of the ministering women. It then details the tabernacle court, with linen hangings, bronze sockets, silver hooks and fillets, pillars on each side, and an embroidered gate screen. Bezalel of Judah and Oholiab of Dan are named as the craftsmen who made everything the LORD commanded Moses. The chapter closes with an accounting, overseen by Ithamar son of Aaron, of the gold, the silver from the census of counted men, and the bronze, and how the silver and bronze were used for sockets, hooks, fillets, and other fittings.

Chapter 39 body

Chapter 39 describes the Israelites making the priestly garments for Aaron and his sons exactly as the LORD commanded Moses. They craft the ephod with gold, blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen, with onyx stones engraved with the names of the children of Israel on its shoulder straps. They make the breastplate set with twelve stones for the twelve tribes and bind it to the ephod, then make the blue robe with bells and pomegranates, the linen tunics, turban, trousers and sash, and the gold plate inscribed "HOLY TO THE LORD". With all the work of the tabernacle finished, the people bring the tabernacle and all its furnishings and the garments to Moses, who sees that it was done as the LORD commanded and blesses them.

Chapter 40 body

The LORD instructs Moses to raise up the tabernacle on the first day of the first month, arrange its furnishings, anoint it and its furniture, and wash, clothe, and anoint Aaron and his sons for an everlasting priesthood. Moses carries out every command: in the first month of the second year he sets up the tabernacle, places the ark, table, lamp stand, golden altar, altar of burnt offering, and basin, and raises the court. Once Moses finishes the work, the cloud covers the Tent of Meeting and the LORD's glory fills the tabernacle, so Moses cannot enter. The chapter closes by explaining that Israel journeyed onward only when the cloud lifted, and stayed put when it did not, with the cloud by day and fire in it by night.
