Abstract written by a model

The Book of Habakkuk is a three-chapter prophetic text centered on the prophet Habakkuk and the LORD, with the Chaldeans as the instrument of judgment. In chapter 1, Habakkuk cries out against violence, iniquity, and perverted justice. The LORD answers that he is raising up the Chaldeans, a fierce, feared nation whose strength is its god. Habakkuk affirms the LORD's everlasting nature but questions why one too pure to look on evil tolerates treachery, and he likens the oppressor to a fisherman who nets people and kills nations without mercy. In chapter 2, Habakkuk takes a watchman's post to await an answer. The LORD tells him to write the vision on tablets and wait, for it will surely come and the righteous will live by faith. A series of woes follows against the plundering, arrogant man, those who shame neighbors, and idol makers who trust lifeless wood, stone, gold, and silver. The chapter ends with the earth filled with knowledge of the LORD's glory and all the earth silent before him in his holy temple. Chapter 3 is Habakkuk's prayer, set to music. He asks the LORD to renew his work and remember mercy, recalls God's awesome coming from Teman and Mount Paran, and describes God saving his people and crushing the land of wickedness. Though trembling and waiting for the day of trouble, Habakkuk resolves to rejoice in the LORD even if crops and flocks fail, declaring that God is his strength and enables him to walk in high places.

The Book of Habakkuk / Chapter 1 body

The prophet Habakkuk receives a revelation and cries out to the LORD, asking how long he must call for help against violence, iniquity, and perverted justice while the wicked surround the righteous. The LORD answers that he is raising up the Chaldeans, a bitter, hasty, and feared nation that marches across the earth seizing dwellings that are not theirs, mocking kings and strongholds, and taking prisoners like sand. The Chaldeans are called guilty because their strength is their god. Habakkuk responds by affirming that the LORD is everlasting and has appointed them for judgment, yet he questions why the LORD, whose eyes are too pure to look on evil, tolerates treachery and is silent when the wicked swallow up those more righteous. He closes by describing the oppressor as a fisherman who nets people, sacrifices to his net, and lives luxuriously, and asks whether he will keep emptying his net and killing nations without mercy.

Chapter 2 body

The speaker takes a watchman's post to await the LORD's answer to his complaint. The LORD tells him to write the vision plainly on tablets and to wait for it, since it will surely come at its appointed time, and that the righteous will live by faith. The LORD then pronounces a series of woes on an arrogant, insatiable man who plunders nations, enriches himself by extortion, builds cities with blood, and shames his own house, warning that the plundered peoples will rise and plunder him. The passage also condemns the one who makes his neighbor drunk to gaze on his nakedness, and the maker who trusts in lifeless idols of wood and stone overlaid with gold and silver. It closes by declaring that the earth will be filled with knowledge of the LORD's glory and that the LORD is in his holy temple, before whom all the earth must be silent.

Chapter 3 body

Chapter 3 is a prayer of Habakkuk the prophet, set to victorious music. Habakkuk asks the LORD to renew his work and to remember mercy in wrath, then recalls God's awesome coming from Teman and Mount Paran, with plague, shaking earth, trembling nations and mountains, and rivers and sea stirred as God marched in wrath. God went out to save his people and his anointed, crushing the land of wickedness and piercing the heads of its warriors with their own spears. Habakkuk trembles and must wait quietly for the day of trouble when invaders come, yet he resolves to rejoice in the LORD even if fig trees, vines, olives, fields, flocks, and herds all fail. He ends by declaring that God is his strength, making his feet like deer's feet and enabling him to walk in high places.
