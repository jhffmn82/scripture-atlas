Abstract written by a model

Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara) presents Allah’s guidance, revelation, power, judgment, and commands for mankind. It distinguishes believers, disbelievers, and those who only claim belief; recounts Adam and his wife in the Garden; and follows the Children of Israel through covenant, disobedience, the Exodus, Pharaoh’s drowning, Moses’ command to sacrifice a cow, and Saul’s campaign in which David slew Goliath by Allah’s leave. Abraham’s leadership, the raising of the House with Ishmael, and prayers connected with the Inviolable Place of Worship establish worship and the qiblah. Believers are told to establish worship, pay the poor-due, give charity, fast, seek help through steadfastness and prayer, eat what is lawful, keep treaties, compete in good works, and act patiently and kindly. The text gives guidance on righteousness, pilgrimage, warfare, retaliation, marriage, divorce, nursing, waiting periods, inheritance, charity, usury, and debts. It warns against concealed revelation, inherited error, associating rivals with Allah, sin, aggression, injustice, mischief, covenant-breaking, the Fire, and rejection of faith. It ends with believers affirming faith and seeking forgiveness, mercy, and victory.

Surah 2: The Cow (Al-Baqara) body

The unit concerns Allah's guidance, revelation, creation, judgment, and commands to mankind, including believers, disbelievers, and those who merely claim belief. It recounts Adam and his wife in the Garden, the Children of Israel's covenant and repeated disobedience, and Pharaoh's folk being drowned after the Exodus from Egypt. It also presents Moses' dealings with the Children of Israel, including the command to sacrifice a cow, and warns about sin, covenant-breaking, the Fire, and the Hereafter.

This unit concerns Allah’s guidance, revelation, worship, the qiblah, and the conduct of believers, disbelievers, the people of the Scripture, Jews, and Christians. It recounts Abraham’s leadership, his raising of the House with Ishmael, and the prayers associated with the House and the Inviolable Place of Worship. It instructs believers to establish worship, pay the poor-due, seek help through steadfastness and prayer, eat what is lawful, and compete in good works. It warns against concealing revelation, following inherited error, associating rivals with Allah, and rejecting faith, while promising guidance, mercy, and reward to those who submit and do good.

This unit concerns Allah’s guidance on righteousness, worship, charity, fasting, pilgrimage, warfare, marriage, divorce, and social obligations. Believers are instructed to trust Allah, observe worship and fasting, give wealth to those in need, keep treaties, and act with patience and kindness. The text addresses retaliation, warfare, the Inviolable Place of Worship, pilgrimage, Ramadan, marriage, divorce, nursing, waiting periods, and inheritance-related duties. It describes Allah as forgiving, merciful, knowing, and severe in punishment, and warns against sin, aggression, injustice, and mischief.

This unit concerns Allah’s commands about prayer, fighting, charity, usury, debts, belief, and accountability. It recounts how Saul led the Children of Israel against Goliath, and how David slew Goliath by Allah’s leave. It describes Allah’s power to give life, including bringing people back to life, and presents signs, revelations, and teachings for believers. It concludes with believers affirming faith and asking Allah for forgiveness, mercy, and victory over disbelieving folk.
