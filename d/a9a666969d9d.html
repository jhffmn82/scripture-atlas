The Song of Solomon

Abstract written by a model

The document is the Song of Solomon, eight chapters of love poetry attributed to Solomon, voiced mainly by a woman (the Beloved, once called the Shulammite) and her Lover, with interjections from Friends, the daughters of Jerusalem, and her Brothers. The Beloved and Lover matter most; Solomon appears as the named author and as a king in a wedding procession, and he is contrasted with her own vineyard at the end. Chapter 1 opens with the Beloved longing for her lover's kisses, calling his love better than wine, and describing herself as dark but lovely, made keeper of vineyards by her mother's sons. She asks where he pastures his flock; he answers and compares her to a steed in Pharaoh's chariots, and Friends promise gold earrings. Chapter 2 has her call herself a rose of Sharon, faint with love. She charges the daughters of Jerusalem not to awaken love before it desires, and recalls her beloved calling her away because winter is past. She declares he is hers and she his. Chapter 3 recounts her night search for him, her encounter with the watchmen, finding him and bringing him to her mother's house. A procession then comes up from the wilderness: Solomon's carriage, guarded by sixty mighty men, on his wedding day. Chapter 4 is the lover's praise of his beloved, called sister and bride, through images of doves, goats, pomegranate, and David's tower. He likens her to a locked garden, and she invites him into it. In Chapter 5 he enters his garden. She tells how she hesitated to open the door, found him gone, and was beaten by the watchmen. Asked by the daughters of Jerusalem what makes him special, she describes him as white and ruddy and altogether lovely. Chapter 6 has others offering to seek him with her; she says he has gone to his garden, and he praises her as unique among sixty queens, eighty concubines and countless virgins. Chapter 7 is an admiring description of her from feet to hair, comparing her stature to a palm tree. She replies that she is her beloved's and invites him to the fields and vineyards. Chapter 8 closes the book. She wishes he were her brother so she could kiss him openly, asks to be set as a seal on his heart, and declares that love is as strong as death and cannot be quenched by many waters. Her Brothers plan for their little sister, and she answers that she is a wall with breasts like towers. She ends by urging her lover to come away like a gazelle on the mountains of spices.

The Song of Solomon / Chapter 1 body

Chapter 1 of the Song of Solomon opens as a song attributed to Solomon, in which the Beloved, a woman, longs for her lover's kisses and praises his love as better than wine. She describes herself as dark but lovely, explaining that the sun scorched her and that her mother's sons made her keeper of the vineyards, so she has not kept her own. She asks where her lover grazes his flock, and the Lover answers by telling her to follow the sheep's tracks, then compares her to a steed in Pharaoh's chariots. Friends, who speak as a group, promise to make her earrings of gold with studs of silver and say they will rejoice in the king. The Beloved and Lover then exchange praise, calling each other beautiful and describing their shared couch and the cedar and fir of their house.

Chapter 2 body

Chapter 2 is an exchange between a Lover and a Beloved. The speaker calls herself a rose of Sharon and a lily of the valleys, and describes sitting in her beloved's shadow, being brought to the banquet hall, and being faint with love. She charges the daughters of Jerusalem not to stir up or awaken love until it so desires. She then relates how her beloved came leaping over the mountains and called her to rise and come away because winter is past and spring has arrived. The chapter closes with the call to catch the little foxes that plunder the vineyards, the declaration that the beloved is hers and she is his, and a plea that he turn and be like a roe or young deer on the mountains of Bether until the day is cool.

Chapter 3 body

In Chapter 3, the speaker describes seeking the one whom her soul loves at night on her bed and not finding him. She rises to search the city's streets and squares, is met by the watchmen, and soon finds him, holding him until she brings him to her mother's house. She charges the daughters of Jerusalem not to stir up or awaken love until it so desires. A procession then comes up from the wilderness, perfumed with myrrh and frankincense, and is identified as Solomon's carriage, guarded by sixty mighty men of Israel armed with swords. The chapter closes with a description of the carriage's silver, gold, and purple materials and a call to the daughters of Zion to see King Solomon crowned by his mother on his wedding day.

Chapter 4 body

In Chapter 4, the lover praises his beloved, whom he calls his love, sister, and bride, describing her eyes, hair, teeth, lips, temples, neck, and breasts through images of doves, goats, shorn sheep, scarlet thread, pomegranate, David's tower, and fawns. He declares there is no spot in her and says he will go to the mountain of myrrh and the hill of frankincense until the day is cool. He calls her to come with him from Lebanon, from the peaks of Amana, Senir, and Hermon, and says she has ravished his heart and that her love is better than wine. He likens her to a locked garden, sealed fountain, and orchard of spices and precious fruits. At the end she calls on the north and south winds to blow on her garden so that her beloved may come into it and taste its fruits.

Chapter 5 body

In Chapter 5 the beloved declares he has come into his garden to gather myrrh, spice, honeycomb, honey, wine and milk, and friends urge him to eat and drink abundantly. The bride recounts that while she slept, her heart awake, her beloved knocked and asked her to open, but she hesitated over her robe and washed feet. When she rose to open, her hands dripping with myrrh, he had gone and did not answer her calls, and the watchmen of the city beat and bruised her and took her cloak. She charges the daughters of Jerusalem to tell him she is faint with love if they find him. When they ask how her beloved is better than others, she describes him from head to legs, as white and ruddy, golden-headed, and altogether lovely, her beloved and her friend.

Chapter 6 body

Chapter 6 opens with others asking the Beloved where her beloved has gone so they can seek him with her. She answers that he has gone down to his garden to pasture his flock and gather lilies, and she declares that she is his and he is hers. The Lover then praises her as beautiful as Tirzah, lovely as Jerusalem, and awesome as an army with banners, describing her hair, teeth, and temples. He contrasts the sixty queens, eighty concubines, and countless virgins with his unique dove, whom her mother favored and whom the daughters, queens, and concubines praised. A speaker recounts going down to the nut tree grove and being carried among royal chariots, and Friends call on the Shulammite to return so they may gaze at her, to which the Lover responds by asking why they wish to gaze at her.

Chapter 7 body

Chapter 7 is a speech of admiration addressed to a woman called the prince's daughter. The speaker praises her feet, thighs, body, waist, breasts, neck, eyes, nose, head and hair with a series of comparisons, and says the king is held captive in her tresses. He likens her stature to a palm tree and says he will climb it and take hold of its fruit. Her mouth is compared to the best wine that goes down smoothly for the beloved. The speaker then shifts, declaring that she belongs to her beloved and that his desire is toward her, and invites him to go out to the fields and villages and to the vineyards, where she will give him her love and where fruits stored up for him are at the doors.

Chapter 8 body

In Chapter 8, the Beloved wishes the Lover were like her brother so she could kiss him openly, and she imagines leading him to her mother's house and having him embrace her. She adjures the daughters of Jerusalem not to stir up love before it desires, and Friends ask who is coming up from the wilderness leaning on her beloved. The Beloved asks to be set as a seal on his heart and arm, declaring that love is as strong as death and cannot be quenched by many waters. The Brothers speak of their little sister with no breasts and plan to build a silver turret or enclose her in cedar, and the Beloved answers that she is a wall with breasts like towers. She contrasts Solomon's leased vineyard at Baal Hamon with her own vineyard, and the chapter ends with the Lover being called to hear her voice and the Beloved urging him to come away like a gazelle on the mountains of spices.
