The Second Book of Kings

author unknown · 25 sections
The Second Book of Kings, from the World English Bible, Catholic Edition, dedicated to the public domain by eBible.org. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://ebible.org/eng-web-c/

Abstract written by a model

The Second Book of Kings (chapters 1-25) is a narrative history of the kingdoms of Israel and Judah, tracing both from the aftermath of Ahab's death to the fall of Jerusalem and exile in Babylon. Its central figures are the prophets Elijah and Elisha, a long line of kings, and the LORD, whose word repeatedly proves true. The opening chapters center on prophecy. Elijah pronounces death on Ahaziah of Israel, calls down fire, and is taken up by a whirlwind, and Elisha takes his mantle and double portion of spirit. Elisha then works many miracles: healing water, multiplying oil, raising the Shunammite's son, healing Naaman of leprosy, floating an ax head, and blinding a Syrian army. He also prophesies victory over Moab and relief from the Syrian siege of Samaria. His servant Gehazi is struck with leprosy for greed. Elisha's word brings Hazael to the Syrian throne and prompts the anointing of Jehu. Jehu kills Joram, Ahaziah of Judah, and Jezebel, whom dogs eat as Elijah had said. He then destroys Ahab's house and the worshipers of Baal, yet keeps the sins of Jeroboam. In Judah, Athaliah seizes power until the priest Jehoiada crowns the hidden child Joash; Joash later repairs the temple but pays tribute to Hazael and is assassinated. Elisha dies, and a dead man revives on touching his bones. Israel's kings then follow in swift, violent succession, with Assyria intruding. Hoshea's rebellion leads Assyria to take Samaria and deport Israel, which the text blames on idolatry and ignored prophets. Judah survives longer. Hezekiah trusts the LORD, and when Sennacherib's envoy Rabshakeh threatens Jerusalem, Isaiah promises deliverance and an angel strikes 185,000 Assyrians. Hezekiah is healed and given fifteen more years, but Isaiah rebukes him for showing Babylon's envoys his treasures and foretells Babylonian conquest. Manasseh's grave evil seals Judah's fate. Josiah finds the book of the law, makes a covenant, purges idolatry, and keeps Passover, yet the LORD's wrath stays because of Manasseh. Josiah is killed by Pharaoh Necoh. His successors do evil, and Nebuchadnezzar subjugates Judah, deports Jehoiachin and many others, and installs Zedekiah. Zedekiah rebels, Jerusalem falls, the temple is burned, Zedekiah is blinded and taken to Babylon, and the governor Gedaliah is murdered, after which people flee to Egypt. The book ends with a small note of grace: Evilmerodach releases Jehoiachin from prison and honors him.

The Second Book of Kings / Chapter 1 body

After Ahab's death, Moab rebelled against Israel. Ahaziah, injured in a fall through the lattice of his upper room in Samaria, sent messengers to ask Baal Zebub, the god of Ekron, whether he would recover. The LORD's angel sent Elijah the Tishbite to intercept them with a message that Ahaziah would die, and Elijah twice called down fire from the sky that consumed a captain and his fifty men sent to bring him to the king. A third captain begged for mercy, the angel told Elijah to go with him, and Elijah delivered the same message of death to Ahaziah in person. Ahaziah died as the LORD had said, and Jehoram began to reign in his place because he had no son.

Chapter 2 body

Elijah, about to be taken up into heaven by a whirlwind, travels with Elisha from Gilgal to Bethel, Jericho, and the Jordan, and Elisha refuses three times to leave him despite warnings from the sons of the prophets. Elijah strikes the Jordan with his rolled-up mantle, the waters divide, and after Elisha asks for a double portion of his spirit, a chariot of fire and horses of fire separate them and Elijah goes up by a whirlwind. Elisha tears his clothes, takes up the fallen mantle, strikes the Jordan, and crosses over, and the sons of the prophets at Jericho recognize that Elijah's spirit rests on him. After fifty men search three days in vain for Elijah, Elisha heals Jericho's bad water by throwing salt into its spring at the LORD's word. On the way to Bethel, Elisha curses youths who mock him as 'baldy,' and two female bears maul forty-two of them before he continues to Mount Carmel and Samaria.

Chapter 3 body

Jehoram son of Ahab becomes king of Israel, doing evil though removing Baal's pillar, and holds to the sins of Jeroboam. When Mesha king of Moab, who had supplied Israel with lambs and wool, rebels after Ahab's death, Jehoram enlists Jehoshaphat of Judah and the king of Edom, and the three march seven days into a waterless wilderness. At Jehoshaphat's urging they consult Elisha, who rebukes Jehoram but, for Jehoshaphat's sake, prophesies that trenches dug in the valley will fill with water and that Moab will be delivered to them. Water comes at dawn, Moab mistakes it for blood and is routed, and Israel devastates the land and besieges Kir Hareseth. Mesha, failing to break through, offers his oldest son as a burnt offering on the wall, and great wrath against Israel causes them to withdraw.

Chapter 4 body

Chapter 4 recounts four episodes involving Elisha, the man of God. A prophet's widow, threatened by a creditor who would take her two sons as slaves, fills borrowed containers from her one pot of oil at Elisha's direction and sells the oil to pay her debt. A prominent woman of Shunem builds a room for Elisha, is promised a son by him, and bears the child; when the boy later dies, she rides to Elisha at Mount Carmel, and Elisha, after prayer and stretching himself on the child, restores him to life. At Gilgal during a famine, Elisha makes poisoned stew safe by adding meal. Finally, he feeds a hundred men with twenty loaves of first fruits bread, with some left over according to the LORD's word.

Chapter 5 body

Naaman, the Syrian army captain who is a leper, learns from a captive Israelite girl that a prophet in Samaria could heal him, and the king of Syria sends him to the king of Israel with a letter and gifts. The king of Israel tears his clothes in alarm, but Elisha tells Naaman to wash seven times in the Jordan. Naaman is first enraged but, persuaded by his servants, obeys and is healed, then confesses that there is no God but in Israel and offers Elisha a gift, which Elisha refuses. Elisha's servant Gehazi secretly runs after Naaman, lies to obtain silver and clothing, and then lies to Elisha about it. Elisha rebukes him and declares that Naaman's leprosy will cling to Gehazi and his offspring forever, and Gehazi leaves leprous, white as snow.

Chapter 6 body

Elisha helps the sons of the prophets build a new dwelling by the Jordan, making a borrowed ax head float after it falls into the water. The king of Syria, at war with Israel, finds his plans repeatedly revealed to the king of Israel by Elisha, and sends an army to surround Elisha in Dothan. Elisha's servant sees the hosts of fire protecting them, the Syrians are struck blind and led to Samaria, and Elisha has them fed rather than killed, so the Syrian raids stop. Later Benhadad besieges Samaria, causing a terrible famine in which two women have eaten one woman's son, and the king of Israel, hearing this, vows to behead Elisha. Elisha foresees the messenger's coming, and the king's messenger arrives declaring that this evil is from the LORD.

Chapter 7 body

Elisha announces that within a day flour and barley will sell cheaply in the gate of Samaria, and the captain on whose hand the king leans scoffs and is told he will see it but not eat of it. Four leprous men, deciding to surrender to the Syrians rather than die, find the Syrian camp deserted because the Lord made the Syrians hear a great army and flee. After eating and taking goods, the lepers tell the city gatekeepers, and the king, suspecting a trap, sends riders who confirm the Syrians' flight along the path to the Jordan. The people plunder the camp, prices fall as Elisha said, and the captain is trampled to death in the gate.

Chapter 8 body

Elisha warns the woman whose son he restored to flee a seven-year famine, and on her return the king, while hearing Gehazi recount Elisha's deeds, orders her house and land restored. Elisha then goes to Damascus, where the sick Benhadad sends Hazael to ask whether he will recover; Elisha says Benhadad will die and that Hazael will be king and bring terrible violence on Israel. Hazael tells Benhadad he will recover, then smothers him with a wet cloth and reigns. The chapter also records the evil reign of Jehoram of Judah, marked by Edom's revolt, and the one-year evil reign of his son Ahaziah, who joins Joram of Israel against Hazael and visits the wounded Joram at Jezreel.

Chapter 9 body

Elisha the prophet sends a young prophet to Ramoth Gilead to anoint Jehu son of Jehoshaphat as king over Israel, and the young man pronounces the LORD's judgment on the house of Ahab and Jezebel before fleeing. Jehu's fellow captains proclaim him king, and he conspires against Joram and rides to Jezreel, where Joram is recovering from wounds and Ahaziah king of Judah is visiting. Jehu shoots Joram dead on Naboth the Jezreelite's land, and Ahaziah is struck down as he flees and dies at Megiddo. Jehu then goes to Jezreel's gate, where Jezebel taunts him, her eunuchs throw her from the window, and Jehu tramples her. The dogs eat her body so that only her skull, feet, and palms remain, fulfilling the word spoken by Elijah the Tishbite.

Chapter 10 body

Jehu writes to the rulers and elders of Samaria and Jezreel who raise Ahab's seventy sons, and they, afraid of him, kill the sons and send their heads to Jezreel in baskets. Jehu then kills the remainder of Ahab's house in Jezreel and in Samaria, and kills forty-two brothers of Ahaziah king of Judah at the shearing house pit. He takes Jehonadab the son of Rechab into his chariot, then deceptively gathers all the worshipers of Baal into the house of Baal and has them killed, destroying the house and making it a latrine. The LORD promises Jehu's descendants the throne to the fourth generation, but Jehu does not leave the sins of Jeroboam or walk in the LORD's law, and Hazael begins to strike Israel's eastern territories. Jehu reigns twenty-eight years, dies, and is succeeded by his son Jehoahaz.

Chapter 11 body

After Ahaziah's death, Athaliah destroyed the royal offspring, but Jehosheba took Ahaziah's son Joash and hid him with his nurse in the LORD's house for six years while Athaliah reigned. In the seventh year the priest Jehoiada made a covenant with the captains of hundreds of the Carites and the guard, showed them the king's son, and organized them in watches around the king, arming them with David's spears and shields. Jehoiada crowned and anointed Joash, and the people clapped and said, "Long live the king!" Athaliah came to the LORD's house, cried "Treason!", and was led out and slain at the horses' entry to the king's house. Jehoiada made covenants between the LORD, the king, and the people, the people destroyed the house of Baal and killed its priest Mattan, and Joash, seven years old, took the throne while the city was quiet.

Chapter 12 body

Chapter 12 describes Jehoash, who began to reign in the seventh year of Jehu and ruled forty years in Jerusalem, doing right while Jehoiada the priest instructed him, though the high places remained. By Jehoash's twenty-third year the priests had not repaired the damage to the LORD's house, so the king confronted them, and Jehoiada set up a chest beside the altar to collect the money, which was counted and paid to the workers without demanding an accounting. When Hazael king of Syria took Gath and set his face toward Jerusalem, Jehoash sent him the holy things and the gold from the LORD's house and the king's house, and Hazael left. Jehoash's servants Jozacar son of Shimeath and Jehozabad son of Shomer conspired against him and killed him at the house of Millo, and Amaziah his son reigned in his place.

Chapter 13 body

Chapter 13 recounts the reigns of Jehoahaz the son of Jehu and his son Jehoash (Joash) over Israel in Samaria. Both did evil and followed the sins of Jeroboam, and under Jehoahaz the LORD delivered Israel to Hazael king of Syria and Benhadad his son until Jehoahaz begged the LORD and a savior was given. Elisha, sick with the illness of which he died, told King Joash to shoot an arrow of victory over Syria and strike the ground; Joash struck only three times, so Elisha said he would defeat Syria just three times. After Elisha died, a man thrown into his tomb revived on touching his bones. Hazael died, Benhadad reigned, and Jehoash defeated Benhadad three times and recovered Israel's cities, as the LORD showed grace because of his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

Chapter 14 body

Chapter 14 recounts the reign of Amaziah, who became king of Judah at twenty-five in the second year of Joash of Israel and did right but not like David, leaving the high places in place. He executed the servants who had killed his father but spared their children, and he struck down ten thousand Edomites and took Sela. Emboldened, he challenged Jehoash king of Israel, who answered with a parable of a thistle and a cedar; Judah was defeated at Beth Shemesh, Amaziah was captured, Jerusalem's wall was broken down for four hundred cubits, and the temple and royal treasures were taken to Samaria. Jehoash died and was succeeded by Jeroboam, and Amaziah later fled a conspiracy to Lachish, where he was killed, after which Azariah, aged sixteen, became king. Jeroboam son of Joash reigned forty-one years in Samaria, did evil, yet restored Israel's borders as spoken by the prophet Jonah son of Amittai, and was succeeded by his son Zechariah.

Chapter 15 body

Chapter 15 traces the kings of Judah and Israel. Azariah of Judah reigns fifty-two years, is struck with leprosy by the LORD, and is succeeded by his son Jotham, who also does right but leaves the high places standing. In Israel a rapid series of kings follows: Zechariah is killed by Shallum, Shallum by Menahem, Menahem is succeeded by Pekahiah, Pekahiah is killed by Pekah, and Pekah is killed by Hoshea. During this period Assyria intervenes, with Pul receiving silver from Menahem and Tiglath Pileser capturing northern territories and carrying people captive under Pekah. Near the end, the LORD begins sending Rezin of Syria and Pekah against Judah, and Jotham is succeeded by Ahaz.

Chapter 16 body

Chapter 16 describes Ahaz son of Jotham, who became king of Judah at twenty and reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem, doing evil by following the kings of Israel, making his son pass through the fire, and sacrificing at the high places. Rezin king of Syria and Pekah king of Israel besieged Jerusalem but could not overcome Ahaz, though Rezin recovered Elath for Syria. Ahaz sent silver and gold from the LORD's house and the king's treasuries to Tiglath Pileser king of Assyria, who took Damascus, carried its people to Kir, and killed Rezin. Ahaz then saw the altar at Damascus and had Urijah the priest build a copy, which Ahaz used for offerings while moving the bronze altar aside and stripping parts of the temple furnishings because of the king of Assyria. Ahaz died and was buried in David's city, and his son Hezekiah reigned in his place.

Chapter 17 body

Hoshea the son of Elah becomes king of Israel in Samaria, rules nine years, and does evil, though less than earlier kings. Shalmaneser king of Assyria makes him a tributary, then imprisons him after discovering that Hoshea sent messengers to So king of Egypt and withheld tribute. The king of Assyria besieges Samaria for three years and takes it in Hoshea's ninth year, carrying Israel off to Halah, the Habor, and the cities of the Medes. The text explains this as the LORD's judgment for Israel's idolatry, child sacrifice, high places, and rejection of the prophets' warnings, and notes that Judah also followed Israel's ways. The king of Assyria then resettles Samaria with people from Babylon, Cuthah, Avva, Hamath, and Sepharvaim, who are attacked by lions, are taught to fear the LORD by a returned priest, yet keep serving their own gods.

Chapter 18 body

Chapter 18 introduces Hezekiah, who began to reign over Judah in the third year of Hoshea, did right in the LORD's eyes, removed the high places, broke the bronze serpent Nehushtan, trusted the LORD, prospered, and rebelled against Assyria. It recounts how Shalmaneser king of Assyria besieged and took Samaria and carried Israel away to Assyria because they disobeyed the LORD and transgressed his covenant. In Hezekiah's fourteenth year, Sennacherib took Judah's fortified cities, and Hezekiah paid him silver and gold, stripping the temple doors and pillars. Sennacherib then sent Tartan, Rabsaris, and Rabshakeh with a great army to Jerusalem, where Rabshakeh mocked Hezekiah's trust in Egypt and the LORD and urged the people to surrender. The people stayed silent as the king commanded, and Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah returned to Hezekiah with torn clothes to report his words.

Chapter 19 body

King Hezekiah of Judah, hearing Rabshakeh's taunts, tears his clothes, puts on sackcloth, and sends Eliakim, Shebna, and the priests' elders to Isaiah, who answers that the LORD will make the king of Assyria hear news and fall by the sword in his own land. Sennacherib, king of Assyria, then sends Hezekiah a threatening letter claiming no god has saved any nation from Assyria. Hezekiah spreads the letter before the LORD and prays for deliverance so all kingdoms will know the LORD alone is God. Isaiah relays the LORD's reply: Sennacherib's arrogance will be punished, he will be turned back by the way he came, and he will not enter or shoot an arrow at Jerusalem. That night the LORD's angel strikes 185,000 in the Assyrian camp, Sennacherib returns to Nineveh, and his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer kill him while he worships in the house of Nisroch, after which Esar Haddon reigns.

Chapter 20 body

Hezekiah, sick and dying, is told by the prophet Isaiah that the LORD says he will die, but after Hezekiah prays and weeps, the LORD sends Isaiah back to promise healing, fifteen more years, and deliverance of the city from the king of Assyria. Isaiah has a cake of figs laid on the boil, and Hezekiah recovers. As a sign, the LORD, at Isaiah's cry, makes the shadow go back ten steps on the sundial of Ahaz, as Hezekiah chose. Berodach Baladan, king of Babylon, sends letters and a present, and Hezekiah shows the envoys all his treasures and armor. Isaiah rebukes this, prophesying that everything will be carried to Babylon and some of Hezekiah's sons will become eunuchs there; Hezekiah calls the word good, hoping for peace and truth in his days, and he later dies and is succeeded by his son Manasseh.

Chapter 21 body

Chapter 21 describes Manasseh, who became king of Judah at age twelve and reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem, doing evil by rebuilding the high places, raising altars for Baal and the army of the sky, making an Asherah, and practicing child sacrifice and sorcery. He placed altars and the Asherah image in the LORD's house and shed much innocent blood, leading Judah into worse evil than the nations the LORD had destroyed. The LORD, speaking through the prophets, declared he would bring disaster on Jerusalem and Judah, wiping Jerusalem like a dish and giving the remnant of his inheritance to their enemies. Manasseh died and was buried in the garden of Uzza, and his son Amon reigned two years, following his father's evil ways and abandoning the LORD. Amon's servants conspired and killed him in his house, but the people of the land killed the conspirators and made his son Josiah king.

Chapter 22 body

Josiah becomes king of Judah at age eight, reigns thirty-one years in Jerusalem, and does what is right in the LORD's eyes, following David. In his eighteenth year he sends Shaphan the scribe to Hilkiah the high priest to arrange counting and delivering money to the workers repairing the LORD's house. Hilkiah announces he has found the book of the law, and Shaphan reads it to the king, who tears his clothes. Josiah sends Hilkiah, Ahikam, Achbor, Shaphan, and Asaiah to inquire of the LORD through the prophetess Huldah. She declares that the LORD will bring evil on Jerusalem and its people for forsaking him and burning incense to other gods, but that Josiah, because he humbled himself, will be gathered to his grave in peace and not see the evil.

Chapter 23 body

King Josiah gathers the elders, priests, prophets, and people of Judah and Jerusalem, reads them the book of the covenant found in the LORD's house, and makes a covenant before the LORD. He then purges Judah, Jerusalem, Bethel, and the cities of Samaria of idolatrous objects, priests, altars, and high places, and commands the people to keep a Passover unlike any before it. Although no king before or after him turned to the LORD as he did, the LORD does not turn from his wrath over Manasseh's provocations and says he will remove Judah and cast off Jerusalem. Josiah is killed at Megiddo by Pharaoh Necoh, and the people of the land make his son Jehoahaz king. Necoh binds Jehoahaz, who dies in Egypt, places Eliakim on the throne as Jehoiakim, and imposes a tribute on the land, and both kings are described as doing evil in the LORD's sight.

Chapter 24 body

Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up against Judah, and Jehoiakim served him three years before rebelling, after which the LORD sent bands of Chaldeans, Syrians, Moabites and Ammonites against Judah because of Manasseh's sins and innocent bloodshed. Jehoiakim died and his son Jehoiachin, eighteen years old, reigned three months in Jerusalem and did evil. Babylon's servants besieged Jerusalem, and Jehoiachin surrendered with his mother, servants, princes and officers; Nebuchadnezzar took the temple and royal treasures, cut up Solomon's gold vessels, and deported the leading people, mighty men and craftsmen to Babylon, leaving only the poorest. Nebuchadnezzar made Mattaniah, Jehoiachin's father's brother, king and renamed him Zedekiah, who was twenty-one, reigned eleven years, did evil, and rebelled against the king of Babylon.

Chapter 25 body

Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon besieges Jerusalem until famine breaks the city in King Zedekiah's eleventh year; Zedekiah flees, is captured by the Chaldeans, watches his sons killed, is blinded, and is taken to Babylon. Nebuzaradan, captain of the guard, burns the LORD's house, the king's house and the city's houses, breaks down the walls, carries off the people and the bronze of the temple, and sends the chief officials to be executed at Riblah. Gedaliah is made governor over those left in Judah but is killed by Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, and the people flee to Egypt in fear of the Chaldeans. The chapter ends with Evilmerodach king of Babylon releasing Jehoiachin king of Judah from prison in the thirty-seventh year of his captivity and giving him a place of honor and a daily allowance for life.
