Abstract written by a model

The Book of Ether, recorded by Moroni from twenty-four plates, tells the history of the Jaredites from their departure after the confusion of language at the great tower to their final destruction. Jared and his brother lead their families and friends toward a choice land after the Lord preserves their language, guides them through the wilderness, and prepares them to cross the great sea. The brother of Jared’s faith is central: the Lord makes sixteen stones shine, reveals himself as Jesus Christ, and commands him to write and seal revelations for a future faithful people. Moroni also teaches that the sealed record, witnesses, repentance, baptism, faith, hope, charity, and coming unto Christ matter in God’s work. The people reach the promised land after 344 days in barges and establish kingship with Orihah. Their history becomes a repeated cycle of righteous rule, prosperity, rebellion, secret combinations, captivity, prophetic warning, repentance, and war. Kings including Kib, Shule, Omer, Jared, Emer, Heth, Shez, Riplakish, Morianton, Lib, Com, and others rule amid conflicts over power. Prophets warn that wickedness, idolatry, and secret combinations will destroy the people; at intervals, humility and repentance bring preservation, rain, prosperity, industry, and peace. In the final decline, Ether prophesies to Coriantumr’s people, calling them to believe in God and repent. Moroni uses Ether’s ministry to teach that miracles follow faith and that weakness can become strength through humility and faith. The people reject warnings and continue in war, secret societies, famine, pestilence, and violence. Ether records events while hidden in a rock. A curse, continuing conspiracies, and massive battles reduce the nation to ruin. Coriantumr eventually mourns nearly two million dead and seeks peace, but Shiz refuses. Their armies annihilate one another until only Coriantumr and Shiz remain; Coriantumr kills Shiz and collapses, fulfilling Ether’s words as Ether completes his record.

The Book of Ether / Chapter 1 body

The Book of Ether is Moroni’s account of the Jaredites, drawn from the twenty and four plates found by the people of Limhi. It records the genealogy of Ether and describes Jared, the brother of Jared, and their people after the Lord confounded the language of the people at the great tower. The brother of Jared asked the Lord to preserve their language and the language of their friends, and the Lord directed them to gather their families, flocks, and seeds. The Lord promised to lead them from the valley which is northward into a choice land and to raise up a great nation from their descendants.

Chapter 2 body

Jared, the brother of Jared, their families, and their friends travel with animals, fowls, fish, bees, seeds, and other things into the wilderness under the direction of the Lord. They reach the great sea, call the place Moriancumer, and dwell there for four years before the Lord chastens the brother of Jared for failing to call upon him. The brother of Jared and his brethren build tight, light barges according to the Lord’s instructions, but the barges have no light and require holes for air. The Lord asks what should be done to provide light and explains that the people must be prepared to cross the great deep amid waves, winds, rains, and floods.

Chapter 3 body

The brother of Jared asks the Lord to touch sixteen small stones so they will shine in the vessels and provide light while crossing the sea. The Lord touches the stones, reveals his finger, and then shows himself to the brother of Jared as Jesus Christ. Jesus Christ teaches that because of the brother of Jared’s faith, he is redeemed from the fall and brought into the Lord’s presence. The Lord shows him the inhabitants of the earth and commands him to write and seal what he has seen.

Chapter 4 body

The Lord commands the brother of Jared to write and seal the things he saw, including the interpretation, and says they will remain hidden until people repent and become clean. Jesus Christ promises to manifest greater revelations and the things seen by the brother of Jared to those who exercise faith and rend the veil of unbelief. The Gentiles and house of Israel are invited to come unto Christ, believe in his gospel, and receive knowledge of the things hidden from them. The Father’s work has commenced, and those who believe and are baptized in Christ’s name shall be saved.

Chapter 5 body

Moroni records the words he was commanded to write and says the sealed things must not be touched for translation except when God gives wisdom. He says the plates may be shown to those who assist in bringing forth the work, and that three witnesses will see them by the power of God. The testimony of the three witnesses, the work, and the records of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost will stand against the world at the last day, while those who repent and come to the Father in the name of Jesus will be received into the kingdom of God.

Chapter 6 body

Moroni records the voyage of Jared, the brother of Jared, and their people across the great waters to the promised land. The Lord makes stones shine in the vessels or barges, causes a furious wind to drive them toward the promised land, protects them on the sea, and brings them safely to shore after 344 days. After landing, the people till the earth, multiply, and become strong, while Jared and the brother of Jared gather them before their deaths. The people request a king, and Orihah is anointed and begins to reign.

Chapter 7 body

Orihah is succeeded by Kib, who is taken captive after his son Corihor rebels, captures the king in Moron, and draws away many people. Shule strengthens himself, defeats Corihor, restores the kingdom to Kib, and later reigns in righteousness, while Noah rebels against Shule, captures him, and is slain by Shule’s sons. The land is divided between the kingdom of Shule and the kingdom of Cohor, but Shule defeats Cohor and receives the kingdom through Nimrod. Prophets warn that the people’s wickedness and idolatry will bring destruction, and Shule’s judgments cause the people to repent, after which they prosper and have no more wars during his days.

Chapter 8 body

Jared rebelled against his father, Omer, gained half the kingdom through flattering many people, and took Omer into captivity. Esrom and Coriantumr defeated Jared's army, but Jared was spared and became sorrowful over losing the kingdom. Jared's daughter devised a plan, danced before Akish, and persuaded Jared to promise her in marriage if Akish brought Omer's head. Akish formed a secret combination through oaths, and Moroni warned that such combinations bring murder, destruction, and the loss of freedom, while urging the Gentiles to repent.

Chapter 9 body

This unit concerns the overthrow of Omer’s kingdom through the secret combinations of Akish and his friends, followed by Jared’s reign, Akish’s conspiracies, and a war that destroys nearly all the people. Omer escapes with most of his family, later returns, and is succeeded by Emer, whose house prospers greatly under the Lord’s blessings. Coriantum, Com, and Heth then reign in succession, while wickedness, famine, poisonous serpents, and destruction return to the land. After the people repent and humble themselves, the Lord sends rain and preserves them from famine.

Chapter 10 body

Shez, a descendant of Heth, rebuilt a broken people and established a righteous kingdom, while his eldest son rebelled and was killed by a robber. Riplakish later oppressed the people through heavy taxation, prisons, forced labor, and abominations, leading to rebellion, war, his death, and the expulsion of his descendants. Morianton gained control of the land, eased the people’s burdens, and made them prosperous, but later rulers led the kingdom into captivity until Lib’s reign brought righteousness, industry, commerce, mining, and prosperity. In Lib’s time the poisonous serpents were destroyed, the land northward became densely inhabited, and Com later fought both the king Amgid and organized robbers.

Chapter 11 body

This unit concerns Com, Shiblom, Seth, Ahah, Ethem, Moron, Coriantor, and Ether during the decline and destruction of their people. Prophets repeatedly warned the people to repent of their wickedness, but the people rejected them and followed wicked combinations and secret societies. Wars, calamity, famines, pestilences, rebellion, captivity, and overthrow afflicted the land and its kingdoms. Coriantor died in captivity after begetting Ether, while prophets foretold that the Lord would destroy the people and bring another people to possess the land.

Chapter 12 body

Ether prophesies to the people in the days of Coriantumr, urging them to believe in God and repent. Moroni explains that faith concerns things hoped for and not seen, and that miracles and divine manifestations occur after faith. The Lord teaches Moroni that weakness can become strength through humility and faith, and that faith, hope, and charity lead people to God. Moroni testifies of Jesus Christ and commends the Gentiles to seek Him, with the grace of God, Jesus Christ, and the Holy Ghost abiding in them.

Chapter 13 body

Moroni finishes his record about the destruction of the people and recounts Ether’s prophecies concerning this land, the New Jerusalem, Jerusalem, the house of Israel, and the remnant of the seed of Joseph. Ether was rejected and expelled, hid in the cavity of a rock, and recorded the destruction he saw. Coriantumr and the people continued in war and wickedness despite Ether’s warning that repentance would spare them. Shared temporarily captured Coriantumr, but Coriantumr’s sons restored the kingdom, and Coriantumr later killed Shared before being wounded and unable to fight for two years.

Chapter 14 body

A curse came upon all the land because of the iniquity of the people, causing tools and swords to disappear and people to guard their property. Coriantumr fought the brother of Shared, Gilead, Lib, and Shiz in a prolonged succession of battles, gaining and losing ground across the land. Lib, a man of great stature, became king after a secret combination murdered Gilead, but Coriantumr later killed Lib. Shiz then pursued Coriantumr, and the war caused widespread destruction, death, famine-like disturbance from the scent of unburied bodies, and the deaths of men, women, and children.

Chapter 15 body

Coriantumr remembered Ether’s words, mourned the destruction of nearly two million people, repented, and asked Shiz to spare the people. Shiz refused unless Coriantumr surrendered, and the people of Coriantumr and the people of Shiz continued gathering for battle. After years of preparation, the armies fought until nearly all were slain, leaving only Coriantumr and Shiz, who fought until Coriantumr killed Shiz and then collapsed. The Lord told Ether to go forth, and Ether saw that the Lord’s words had been fulfilled and finished his record.
