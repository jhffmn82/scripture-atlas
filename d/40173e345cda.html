Yasna

L.H. Mills · 43 sections
Yasna, translated by James Darmesteter and L. H. Mills, Sacred Books of the East vols. 4, 23 and 31 (1880-1887), public domain. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://sacred-texts.com/zor/

Abstract written by a model

This document summarizes Yasna XXVIII–LXXII, a sequence of prayers, teachings, worships, and ritual formulas centered on Ahura Mazda, Zarathustra Spitama, Righteousness (Asha), Good Mind, Piety, the Bountiful Immortals, and the Mazdayasnian Faith. It begins with Zarathustra seeking grace, revelation, understanding, life, and power, while the Soul of the Kine appeals for protection from violence and receives Zarathustra as spiritual master for the diligent tiller. The early units establish the central conflict between the better and worse primeval spirits: Righteousness, truth, and good thoughts, words, and deeds lead toward Ahura Mazda’s Kingdom, Welfare, Immortality, Heaven, and salvation; the Lie, Evil Mind, Daêvas, violence, and false teachers lead to ruin, Hell, and the Lie’s abode. Zarathustra repeatedly asks for divine instruction about creation, righteous rule, protection of people, settlements, cattle, waters, plants, and the poor, while opposing Grehma, the Karpans, Kavis, Bendva, rapacious rulers, and other enemies of the Faith. Named supporters include Kavi Vîstâspa, Frashaostra Hvôgva, and Gâmâspa Hvôgva. The middle portion broadens into worship and sacrifice offered to Ahura Mazda, Fire, the Kine, earth, waters, stars, plants, saints, Haoma, and sacred powers, with confessions, petitions for prosperity and health, and care for the whole clean creation. Later units emphasize the Saoshyant, future saviours, final judgment, the Kinvat Bridge, and the eventual overcoming of evil. Yasna LIII adds marriage counsel for Pourukista and the faithful. The closing sections focus on the Gâthas, Sraosha, ritual worship, mutual blessings, household prosperity, prayers against enemies of holy law, offerings to Fire and waters including Ardvi Sûra Anâhita, and the saving force of correctly recited Yasna words. Yasna LXXII simply directs the reader back to Yasna LXI.

Yasna / Yasna XXVIII. body

Yasna XXVIII consists of prayers chiefly for grace and the words of revelation. Zarathustra addresses Ahura Mazda, Righteousness, Good Mind, and Piety, seeking spiritual gifts, understanding, long-lasting life, and power to overcome torment. The prayers concern both corporeal and mental worlds, the Soul of the Kine, and the advancement of the imperishable Kingdom. Zarathustra also asks to be taught how the primeval world arose so he may declare it to the waiting people.

Yasna XXIX. body

The Soul of the Kine cries to Ahura and Asha about violence, desolation, and the lack of a capable guardian and pasture-giver. Ahura asks Asha who was appointed to protect the Kine, and Asha answers that no suitable chieftain could be found. Zarathustra intervenes, and Ahura names Zarathustra Spitama as the spiritual master and speaker for the diligent tiller of the ground. The unit asks Ahura, Asha, Good Mind, and Sovereign Power to grant aid, protection, gladness, and peaceful homes to the righteous and their disciples.

Yasna XXX. body

The Doctrine of Dualism concerns the choice between two primeval spirits, one better and one worse, in thought, word, and deed. It teaches that the better spirit chose Righteousness, while the worse spirit chose evil, and that the Demon-gods chose the Worst Mind and allied themselves with the Demon of Fury. Ahura Mazda, the Good Mind, Righteousness, Âramaiti, and the Sovereign Power oppose falsehood and work toward the perfection of the world. The unit promises Heaven and salvation for the righteous and Hell and the worst life for the wicked.

Yasna XXXI. body

The unit concerns Ahura Mazda, the prophet, and the struggle between Righteousness and the Lie. It declares that Ahura Mazda reveals truth, appoints rule, supports the Good Mind and Righteous Order, and grants the Kingdom, Universal Weal, and Immortality to the faithful. It contrasts the husbandman, who chooses the good creed, with the thieving nomad, the wicked, and the evil-doer, whose actions bring ruin and punishment. It calls the living believers to hear the truthful teaching, reject harmful commands, and follow Righteous Order in thought, word, and deed.

Yasna XXXII. body

The unit concerns a rival-monarch and his allies, whose false religion, violence, and teachings threaten Zarathustra, his people, the Kine, and the doctrines of Ahura Mazda. Ahura Mazda answers through the Good Mind and Righteousness, accepting good Piety and condemning the Daêvas, Evil Mind, and Evil Spirit. The rival forces are associated with Grehma, the Karpans, and the Kavis, who oppose truth and damage life. Zarathustra declares that he will establish Ahura Mazda’s precepts and drive away the Karpans’ and Kavis’ disciples.

Yasna XXXIII. body

Yasna XXXIII. concerns Zarathustra’s prayers and self-consecration to Ahura Mazda, seeking justice, guidance, power, and communion with the divine attributes. It rejects disobedience, the Evil Mind, arrogance, falsehood, and neglect of the Kine, while upholding the Righteous Order, Good Mind, Obedience, and Âramaiti. Zarathustra asks for insight, prosperity, salvation, and the gifts of Immortality and Welfare, and offers his body, speech, deeds, and spiritual priority to Mazda.

Yasna XXXIV. body

The unit concerns Ahura Mazda, the gifts and powers associated with the Righteous Order, Good Mind, Âramaiti, and the Kingdom of Welfare, and the worship offered by faithful servants. It asks what Ahura Mazda’s Kingdom, riches, regulations, praise, and offerings are, and seeks signs, instruction, and protection through Righteousness. It describes sacrifice, prayer, Fire, and good actions as means of approaching Ahura Mazda and receiving blessed rewards. It also concerns the Kine, the settlements, the poor, and those who oppose or abandon the divine order.

Yasna XXXV. body

Yasna XXXV., also called Yasna Haptanghâiti, praises Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Righteousness, and the Mazdayasnian Faith. It concerns the sacrifice and homage offered to the divine powers and to the entire clean, spiritual, and mundane creation. It calls for good thoughts, good words, and good actions, and for the care and comfort of the Kine. It also asks that the faith, its teachings, and its ritual order be proclaimed, practiced, and further diffused.

Yasna XXXVI. body

This unit concerns Ahura Mazda and the Fire of Ahura Mazda. It asks for approach, aid, zeal, blessing, praise, and protection from pollution. It also acknowledges Ahura Mazda’s Good Mind, Righteousness, good wisdom, stars as His body, and the highest of the high.

Yasna XXXVII. body

The unit concerns worship of Ahura Mazda, the creator of the Kine (the living creation), Righteousness, waters, wholesome plants, stars, earth, and all good objects. Ahura Mazda is worshipped for His Sovereign Power, greatness, and beneficence, and under His name as Lord and Mazda dear. The Fravashis of the Just, Righteousness the Best, the Bountiful Immortals, the Good Mind, Good Faith, and Piety are also worshipped. The worship is offered with the body and life, and Righteousness is praised as beauteous and endowed with light in all things good.

Yasna XXXVIII. body

This unit concerns worship of the earth, Ahura Mazda, and the waters. The earth is honored as the bearer of those who worship, together with the wives of Ahura Mazda, who are praised for their sanctity and pious qualities. The waters are worshipped in their various forms as beneficent, productive, maternal beings that serve, nourish, and aid people. Ahura Mazda is said to have given the waters their names when the good was bestowed.

Yasna XXXIX. body

The unit concerns worship offered to the Kine's soul, her created body, cattle, tame and wild beasts, and the souls of saints. The saints are described as men and women whose good consciences have conquered, are conquering, or will conquer the Daêvas. The Bountiful Immortals, both male and female, are worshipped as ever-living and helpful beings dwelling beside the pious. Ahura Mazda is praised and worshipped for thinking, speaking, determining, and doing what is good, and prayers are directed to Him with confessions of sin.

Yasna XL. body

The unit consists of prayers addressed to Ahura Mazda for wise action, bounty, tenderness, and appointed reward. It asks for fellowship with Ahura Mazda and Righteousness for all duration. It requests righteous men, lovers and producers of the Right, and trained beasts for helpful companionship and enduring vigour. It also asks for a kinsman lord, labourers of the village, and clients or peers, through whose help the petitioners may arise and remain holy and true to Ahura Mazda.

Yasna XLI. body

Yasna XLI. is a prayer praising Ahura Mazda and Righteousness the Best. It asks Ahura Mazda to rule benevolently, grant life, bodily vigor, power, salvation, and protection, and allow worshippers to attain his good Kingdom. It seeks long life and the reward appointed for souls, for both this life and that of mind. The unit concludes by offering sacrifice to Yasna Haptanghâiti as a brave, holy, and chief ritual.

Yasna XLII. body

The text worships the Bountiful Immortals through the entire collection of the Yasna, Haptanghâiti. It offers sacrifices to waters, rivers, roads, hills, lakes, corn, earth, heaven, the stormy wind Mazda made, Haraiti, and other good things. It also honors Zarathustra, the Lord, the Good Mind in the living, spirits of the saints, Vouru-kasha, and various sacred beings. Haoma is praised as golden-flowered, restorative, helpful to the world's advance, and able to drive death away.

Yasna XLIII. body

Yasna XLIII concerns Zarathustra’s reflections on Ahura Mazda’s bountifulness and the universal salvation offered to believers. Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda for Righteousness, Piety, the Good Mind, holy blessings, spiritual glory, and long life. Ahura Mazda’s messengers question and instruct Zarathustra, who pledges to oppose the wicked, help the righteous, and prepare people for Ahura Mazda’s Kingdom. The unit describes final judgment, the role of Âramaiti and Sraosha, and the blessings associated with Ahura Mazda’s Realm.

Yasna XLIV. body

The speaker asks Ahura Mazda to explain the origins and workings of creation, including the Righteous Order, the sun, stars, moon, earth, waters, plants, winds, lights, darkness, sleep, and dawns. The speaker seeks instruction on serving Ahura Mazda, increasing goodness, hallowing the Faith of Ahura Mazda's people, and attaining perfection, Welfare, and Immortality. The speaker asks how the Righteous Order and Good Mind will prevail over the Demon-of-the-Lie and its followers when the two hosts meet. The unit contrasts the friends and worshippers of Righteousness with Daêva-worshippers, Karpan, Usig, and the Kavian, who are associated with rapine and failure to bring waters and blessings to the fields.

Yasna XLV. body

Yasna XLV, titled the Doctrine of Dualism, concerns the declaration of Ahura Mazda’s teaching and the opposition between the two first spirits. It presents Ahura Mazda as the all-wise Lord and creator who establishes Righteousness, Good Mind, Piety, Weal, Immortality, and royal power. It teaches that the two spirits differ in thoughts, commands, beliefs, deeds, consciences, and souls, and that obedience to the Mãthra brings blessing while wickedness brings woe. It also condemns the Daêva-gods and describes the Saoshyant’s bounteous Faith as saving and beneficent.

Yasna XLVI. body

Zarathustra describes his lack of allies, reduced flocks, and need for help in establishing the Faith. He appeals to Ahura Mazda for guidance, protection, and the wealth of the Good Mind, while condemning evil rulers who obstruct the Righteous Order and harm the settlements. He asks for righteous judges and helpers, naming Kavi Vîstâspa, Frashaostra Hvôgva, Gâmâspa Hvôgva, and others who may advance the cause. He teaches that the righteous will receive blessings, while the wicked will fall into misery and the abode of the Lie.

Yasna XLVII. body

This unit concerns Ahura Mazda’s gifts to His chosen saint through His Bountiful Spirit, Best Mind, Righteous moral Order, Sovereign Power, and Âramaiti. The gifts include Universal Weal and Immortality, brought forth through Good Mind, Âramaiti, and Fire. Ahura Mazda is described as the father of the righteous Order and the maker of the joy-creating Kine. The wicked foemen are harmed and remain aligned with the Evil Mind, while Piety and righteous Truth instruct those who seek them.

Yasna XLVIII. body

The unit concerns prayers to Ahura Mazda for victory of the Righteous Order over the Demon-of-the-Lie, evil heretics, rapine, envy, and oppressive rulers. It asks for strength in conflicts, the rule of good kings, prosperity for the Kine, and refuge for the faithful. It looks toward the future appearance of the Saoshyant, perfected Piety, men of mind's perfection, and Saviours of the Provinces, who will advance the Holy Order through the Good Mind.

Yasna XLIX. body

Yasna XLIX concerns opposition from Bendva, appeals to Ahura Mazda for help through Good Mind and Righteousness, and the struggle between the faithful and the wicked. It rejects those who support Wrath of Rapine and the Daêvas, while praising those who guard Faith and live according to the Holy Order. Frashaostra is given headship of the Holy Order, and Gâmâspa and the “hero” are joined in the best recompense. The souls of evil men are destined for the Lie’s abode.

Yasna XLIX, 12-L. body

The speaker, the supplicant, asks Ahura Mazda what aids of grace are granted through the Righteous Order and Good Mind. The unit concerns the protection of flocks, herds, the Kine, and lands, and calls for righteous settlers and servants to bring prosperity and justice. Zarathustra and other Mãthra-speakers are urged to proclaim and teach the righteous way and the regulations of Ahura Mazda. The speaker offers sacrifice, worship, Yasnas, praises, and prayer, seeking the path to the Home-of-song and help for the cause.

Yasna LI. body

The unit is an appeal to Ahura Mazda and the Assembly of the Faithful for Good Government, Sovereign Power, wealth, salvation, and spiritual blessings through Asha, Good Mind, and Âramaiti. It presents Zarathustra as the speaker and asks who supports him, while praising Kavi Vîstâspa, Frashaostra, Gâmâspa, and others associated with the great cause and the good Religion. It contrasts the rewards of the righteous with the punishment of the wicked and the Lie, and describes the Kine, waters, and plants as creations requiring protection. It also speaks of judgment at the Kinvat Bridge, the consequences of sacrifice and worship, and the promised blessings of Ahura Mazda.

Yasna LII (Sp. LI). body

Yasna LII (Sp. LI) presents A Prayer for Sanctity and Its Benefits. The speaker prays for benefits, goodness, and sanctity for the entire creation of the holy and the clean, including present, emerging, and future generations. Sanctity is described as bringing prosperity, shelter, healing virtue, good gifts, and success while overcoming the harmful malice of the Daêvas. The prayer also concerns the sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise of the Bountiful Immortals, the prosperity of the holy creation, and opposition to the entire evil creation through Righteousness.

Yasna LIII. body

Yasna LIII. concerns the answered prayer of Zarathustra Spitâma, the devotion of Kavi Vîstâspa, Zarathustra Spitâma, and Frashaostra, and the founding of the Faith of the Saoshyant by Ahura Mazda. It presents marriage counsel for Pourukista and for maidens, bride and bridegroom, emphasizing mutual care in Righteousness and service to the Good Mind, Asha, and Mazda. It describes the protection of the faithful from the Lie-demon, the role of righteous women in overcoming temptation, and the defeat of sinners and deceivers. It concludes by affirming Ahura Mazda’s power, Kingdom, and highest blessings for the right-living poor.

Yasna LIV (Sp. LIII). body

Yasna LIV (Sp. LIII), titled The Airyemâ-Ishyô, concerns prayers and sacrifices connected with the Airyaman and the Airyemâ-ishyô. It asks that the Airyaman draw near to the men and women taught of Zarathustra for the joyful grace of the Good Mind. It seeks the sacred reward of the ritual order and asks that Ahura Mazda grant or increase it. It also sacrifices to the powerful Airyemâ-ishyô, the bounteous Gâthas, and the Praises of the Yasna.

Yasna LV (Sp. LIV). body

Yasna LV (Sp. LIV) concerns the conclusion of the worship of the Gâthas and the beginning of that of the Staota Yêsnya. We offer our riches, persons, bodies, consciousness, souls, and Fravashi to the bountiful Gâthas and worship them with the divine Righteousness and the Good Mind. The Gâthas are described as guardians, defenders, and spiritual food and clothing for souls, bringing rewards, health, protection, progress, sanctity, and gifts. The unit also worships the Praises of the Yasna in their remembered, recited, learned, taught, repeated, chanted, and offered forms, which further the world through grace.

Yasna LVI (Sp. LV). body

Yasna LVI (Sp. LV) introduces the Srôsh Yast. It repeatedly calls for Sraosha, identified with attentive or listening Obedience, to be present for the worship of Ahura Mazda, the good waters, the Fravashis of the saints and their souls, the good and bountiful Immortals, and Blessedness. Sraosha is described as good, holy, blessed, and stately, and as one who smites with victory and furthers settlements in their advance.

Yasna LVII (Sp. LVI). body

Yasna LVII (Sp. LVI), called The Srôsh Yast, concerns worship and praise of Sraosha, or Obedience the blessed. Sraosha is described as a victorious ritual lord who protects settlements, guards Ahura Mazda’s creatures, battles the Daêvas, and aids the righteous. The text recounts Sraosha’s association with Ahura Mazda, Zarathustra, the Bountiful Immortals, Haoma, the Gâthas, and the Mãthra. It asks Sraosha to protect people in both the corporeal and mental worlds and to grant strength, soundness, speed, and victory.

Yasna LVIII (Sp. LVII). body

Yasna LVIII (Sp. LVII), called The Fshûshô-Mãthra, presents worship as protection from the Daêva and the evil-minded man. It entrusts settlements and persons to worship for protection, care, guarding, and oversight, and connects worship with Blessedness, Piety, and good thoughts, words, and deeds. The unit praises the owner of herds, asks the Bountiful Immortals to save holy men and saintly women, and offers thoughts, words, actions, herds, and men to the Bountiful Spirit. It also praises Ahura Mazda, the Righteous Order, the Fire of Ahura Mazda, and the Praises of the Yasna.

Yasna LIX (Sp. LVIII). body

Yasna LIX, also called Sp. LVIII, is titled “Mutual Blessings.” It concerns worship of Verethraghna, Saoshyant, the Baresman, souls, and Fravashis, followed by blessings spoken by the Ratu and Zaotar. It also praises Ahû, Ratu, Mazda, Asha, Asha Vahista, the Fshûshô-mãthra, and the Yasna Praises.

Yasna LX (Sp. LIX). body

Yasna LX (Sp. LIX) concerns prayers for the dwelling of the sacrificer. It asks for blessings, prosperity, righteous order, divine sovereignty, the Zarathustrian Faith, and the presence of holy beings within the house. It calls for obedience, peace, generosity, reverence, and truthful speech to overcome their opposites. It concludes by asking that the sacrificers attain Heaven, the best world of the saints, and companionship with Ahura Mazda.

Yasna LXI (Sp. LX). body

The unit concerns sending forth the Ahuna-vairya, Asha Vahista, YêNhê hâtãm, and the prayer of the pious man between heaven and earth. It calls for encounter with and displacement of Angra Mainyu, Kahvaredha, Kayadhas, thieves, robbers, Zandas, sorcerers, covenant breakers, and others opposed to the holy ritual laws. It also calls for the overthrow of murderers of the saints, persecutors, ritual opponents, the tyrant full of death, and the wicked. Zarathustra Spitama and the prophets who are yet to serve and save thy people are concerned with driving the Demon of the Lie from the seven Karshvars and dislodging the entire evil world.

Yasna LXII (Sp. LXI). body

This unit concerns the Fire, addressed as Ahura Mazda's son, and the offerings, care, blessings, and admonitions associated with it. The man who worships the Fire is promised salvation when he approaches it truthfully with wood, Baresman, flesh, and a mortar. The Fire is asked to remain aflame and growing in the house until the heroic renovation is complete, and to grant glory, nourishment, booty, understanding, power, and a capable offspring. The Fire blesses those who bring properly prepared wood and other sacred materials, while Ahura Mazda's Fire addresses an admonition to those for whom it cooks meals.

Yasna LXIII (Sp. LXII). / Yasna LXIV (Sp. LXIII). / Yasna LXV (Sp. LXIV). body

Yasna LXV (Sp. LXIV) praises Ardvi Sûra Anâhita and the waters for their healing, purifying, life-sustaining, and prosperity-bringing powers. It describes the waters flowing from Hukairya to the sea Vouru-kasha, dividing toward the seven Karshvars, and purifying the seed, wombs, and milk of women. The text excludes evil people from the waters’ benefits and prescribes the proper invocations and offerings taught by Ahura Mazda through Zarathustra. It asks the waters and many holy beings to grant wealth, worthy offspring, Immortality, and Welfare.

Yasna LXVI (Sp. LXV). body

Yasna LXVI (Sp. LXV) concerns an offering addressed to the Ahurian One. The speaker offers the Zaothra with sanctity, together with the Haoma and the flesh, and the Hadhânaêpata lifted up with sacred regularity. The offering is made for the propitiation of Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Sraosha (Obedience) the blessed, and the Fire of Ahura Mazda.

Yasna LXVII (Sp. LXVI). body

Yasna LXVII (Sp. LXVI) is identified as a unit of text. Sections 1-4 refer to Y. XXIII, 1-4, with “I desire to approach with sanctity” replaced by “I offer with sanctity,” and also refer to Y. VII, 24. Sections 5-7 refer to Y. XXXVIII, 3-5.

Yasna LXVIII (Sp. LXVII). body

The unit concerns offerings and prayers to the Ahurian One, Ahura Mazda, the Zaothra, and the good waters. The worshippers ask for health, healing, growth, prosperity, offspring, protection, glory, and the best life of the saints. The waters are praised as those created by Mazda and are invoked to support fertility, plants, settlements, and worshippers. The unit also concerns the Yasna, the Mazdayasnians who perform it, and blessings for villages through the Good Mind and Righteous Order.

Yasna LXIX (Sp. LXVIII). body

Yasna LXIX (Sp. LXVIII) is identified as a chapter. The chapter is composed of fragments. It refers to Y. XV, 2, and Y. LI, 1 and 22.

Yasna LXX (Sp. LXIX). body

The unit concerns worship of the Bountiful Immortals, especially Ahura Mazda, and Zarathustra Spitâma. It declares the institutions associated with Ahura Mazda, the Good Mind, Asha Vahista, Khshatra-vairya, Bountiful Âramaiti, Weal and Immortality, the Kine, Fire, and other sacred beings and prayers. It seeks good religious speech, prophetic service, good thoughts, words, and deeds, and praises the good waters, Apãm-napât, and Sraosha.

Yasna LXXI (Sp. LXX). body

Yasna LXXI concerns Frashaostra’s question to Zarathustra about the memorised recital of the rites and the completed delivery of the Gâthas. Zarathustra answers by describing sacrifices and worship directed to Ahura Mazda, Zarathustra, the Fravashis, the Bountiful Immortals, the Yazads, the Mazdayasnian Faith, the Gâthas, and the holy creation. Ahura Mazda promises that whoever pronounces the prescribed words at the last ending of life will have the soul kept away from Hell, cross the Kinvat Bridge in holiness, and enter Heaven. The unit also praises the Yasna, its ending words, its Gâthas, and the Staota-Yêsnya hymns.

YASNA LXXII. (See Y. LXI.) body

The unit is titled YASNA LXXII and refers the reader to YASNA LXI. No further subject, event, or teaching is provided.
