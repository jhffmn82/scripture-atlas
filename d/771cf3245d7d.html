The Book of Ezekiel

author unknown · about 592 to 570 BC · 49 sections
The Book of Ezekiel, from the World English Bible, Catholic Edition, dedicated to the public domain by eBible.org. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://ebible.org/eng-web-c/

Abstract written by a model

This is the book of Ezekiel, in 48 chapters: the account of Ezekiel, a priest and son of Buzi, who is among the Judean captives by the river Chebar in the Chaldeans' land. It centers on the LORD, who commissions Ezekiel as a prophet, watchman and living sign to a rebellious Israel, and on the fate of Jerusalem, Israel and the surrounding nations. It opens with Ezekiel's vision of the LORD's glory on a sapphire throne above four living creatures and wheels full of eyes (ch. 1). He is commanded to eat a scroll of lamentation, is hardened against a stubborn people, and is appointed watchman, made mute until the LORD opens his mouth (2-3). Through sign-acts such as a besieged tile, lying on his sides, scarce bread, a shaved head and an acted exile, and through oracles, the LORD announces Jerusalem's coming siege, famine, sword and exile for idolatry, bloodshed and false prophecy (4-7, 12-24). A visionary tour of the temple shows abominations there; the LORD's glory leaves the temple and the city, and a man in linen marks those who grieve (8-11). Chapters 14-23 add the parables of the vine, the foundling bride, the eagles, the lions, the two sisters Oholah and Oholibah, and a teaching on individual responsibility (18). On the day Babylon besieges Jerusalem, Ezekiel's wife dies and he may not mourn (24). Judgments follow on Ammon, Moab, Edom, the Philistines, Tyre and its prince, Sidon, and Egypt and Pharaoh, with Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon as the LORD's instrument (25-32). After a fugitive reports Jerusalem's fall, Ezekiel's mouth is opened and he is again named watchman (33). The tone then turns to hope. The LORD condemns Israel's false shepherds and promises David as one shepherd (34), judges Edom (35), and promises a new heart, his Spirit, and a land like Eden (36). The valley of dry bones revives, and Judah and Joseph are joined as one nation under David with an everlasting covenant (37). Gog of Magog's invasion ends in his defeat and the nations see the LORD's glory (38-39). The last chapters (40-48) give a detailed vision of a restored temple, measured by a guide. The LORD's glory returns through the east gate, and the chapters set out rules for the priests of Zadok, the prince, offerings and feasts. A river flows from the temple and heals the sea, and the land is divided among the tribes. The book ends with the city named 'The LORD is there.'

The Book of Ezekiel front matter

Chapter 1 body

Ezekiel the priest, son of Buzi, is among the captives by the river Chebar in the Chaldeans' land in the fifth year of King Jehoiachin's captivity when the heavens open and he sees visions of God. The LORD's word comes to him and the LORD's hand is on him. He sees a stormy wind and fiery cloud from the north, out of which come four living creatures, each with four faces and four wings, moving straight forward wherever the spirit goes. Beside them are wheels within wheels with rims full of eyes, moving with the creatures because the creature's spirit is in the wheels. Above an awesome crystal-like expanse is a sapphire throne with a man-like figure of glowing metal and fire surrounded by rainbow-like brightness, which Ezekiel identifies as the likeness of the LORD's glory, and he falls on his face and hears a voice speaking.

Chapter 2 body

In Chapter 2, a speaker addresses the "son of man" and tells him to stand, and the Spirit enters the narrator and sets him on his feet. The Lord GOD sends the son of man to the children of Israel, described as a rebellious, impudent, stiff-hearted nation whose fathers and who themselves have transgressed to this day. He is to speak the words "This is what the Lord GOD says," whether they hear or refuse, so that they will know a prophet has been among them. He is told not to fear them or their words, though he dwells among briers, thorns, and scorpions, and not to be rebellious himself but to open his mouth and eat what is given. A hand holds out a scroll, which is spread before him, written on both sides with lamentations, mourning, and woe.

Chapter 3 body

In Chapter 3, the LORD commands Ezekiel, addressed as "son of man," to eat a scroll, which tastes as sweet as honey, and to speak the LORD's words to the house of Israel. The LORD warns that Israel will not listen because they are obstinate and hard-hearted, so he makes Ezekiel's face and forehead hard like a diamond. The Spirit lifts Ezekiel up and carries him in bitterness to the captives at Tel Aviv by the river Chebar, where he sits overwhelmed for seven days. The LORD then appoints Ezekiel a watchman, explaining that he will be held responsible for warning the wicked and the righteous. Finally, the LORD tells Ezekiel to shut himself in his house, where he will be bound and made mute until the LORD opens his mouth to speak.

Chapter 4 body

In Chapter 4, the LORD instructs a man addressed as 'son of man' to portray Jerusalem on a tile and enact a siege against it, with an iron pan as a wall between himself and the city, as a sign to the house of Israel. He is told to lie on his left side 390 days to bear the iniquity of the house of Israel, then on his right side 40 days to bear the iniquity of the house of Judah, bound with ropes and facing the siege with his arm uncovered. He must eat bread made of wheat, barley, beans, lentils, millet, and spelt by weight, and drink water by measure, baking it over dung in the people's sight. The speaker objects that he has never been polluted, so the LORD permits cow's dung instead of human dung. The LORD explains that this foreshadows Israel eating unclean bread among the nations and that He will break the staff of bread in Jerusalem, leaving the people dismayed and pining away in their iniquity.

Chapter 5 body

The Lord GOD commands the prophet, addressed as 'son of man', to shave his head and beard with a sharp sword and divide the hair by weight into thirds: one burned in the city, one struck with the sword, and one scattered to the wind, with a few strands bound in his robe and some of those burned again. The Lord GOD explains that the hair represents Jerusalem, which He set in the middle of the nations but which rebelled against His ordinances and statutes more than the surrounding countries. Because Jerusalem defiled His sanctuary with detestable things, He declares He is against her, will execute unparalleled judgments before the nations, and will show no pity. A third of the people will die by pestilence and famine, a third will fall by the sword, and a third will be scattered with a sword drawn after them, including the horror of fathers and sons eating one another. Jerusalem will become a desolation, reproach, taunt and warning to the surrounding nations, as the Lord sends famine, evil animals, pestilence, blood and the sword until His wrath is accomplished.

Chapter 6 body

The LORD commands the son of man to set his face toward the mountains of Israel and prophesy judgment against them. The Lord GOD declares he will bring a sword, destroy the high places, break the altars and incense altars, and strew the slain and bones of the children of Israel before their idols. Yet he promises to leave a remnant who escape among the nations, remember him, and loathe themselves for their idolatry. The son of man is told to strike his hand, stamp his foot, and cry "Alas!" over Israel's abominations, since the people will die by sword, famine, and pestilence according to their distance or circumstance. The LORD says he will make the land desolate from the wilderness toward Diblah, so that they will know that he is the LORD.

Chapter 7 body

In Ezekiel 7, the LORD tells the prophet, called 'son of man', to announce to the land of Israel that the end has come on its four corners. The LORD declares he will pour out his anger, judge the people according to their ways, and bring their abominations upon them without pity, so that they will know he is the LORD. The coming day brings tumult, with the sword outside and pestilence and famine within, leaving survivors moaning on the mountains like doves, with weak hands, sackcloth, shame, and baldness. Silver and gold become unclean and cannot deliver them, and the LORD gives their treasured ornament and holy places to strangers and the wicked of the earth, who profane them. With the land full of bloody crimes and violence, the worst of the nations will possess their houses, rumor will pile on rumor, the law will perish from the priest, and the king, prince, and people will mourn and be troubled.

Chapter 8 body

In the sixth year, on the fifth day of the sixth month, Ezekiel sits in his house with the elders of Judah when the Lord GOD's hand falls on him. A fiery figure lifts him by a lock of his head in visions to Jerusalem, where he is shown a series of escalating abominations. He sees the image of jealousy at the north gate, seventy elders of Israel burning incense before idols and creeping things portrayed on a hidden chamber wall, women weeping for Tammuz at the north gate of the LORD's house, and about twenty-five men worshiping the sun with their backs to the LORD's temple. The Lord declares that Judah has filled the land with violence and provoked him, and that he will deal in wrath, showing no pity and refusing to hear their cries.

Chapter 9 body

In this chapter the LORD summons those in charge of the city, and six men with destroying weapons arrive with one man clothed in linen carrying a writer's inkhorn, and they stand beside the bronze altar. The LORD's glory moves from the cherub to the threshold of the house, and he commands the man in linen to mark the foreheads of those who sigh and cry over the city's abominations. He orders the other six to follow and kill the old, young, virgins, children and women without pity, sparing anyone marked, beginning at his sanctuary with the old men before the house. The narrator, left alone, falls on his face and asks whether all the remnant of Israel will be destroyed. The LORD answers that the iniquity of Israel and Judah is great, the land full of blood and the city full of perversion, and he will bring their way on their heads; the man in linen then reports that he has done as commanded.

Chapter 10 body

The narrator sees a sapphire-like throne in the expanse above the cherubim. The speaker commands the man clothed in linen to go between the whirling wheels and take coals of fire from between the cherubim to scatter over the city; a cherub puts the fire into his hands and he goes out. The narrator describes the cherubim and the four wheels beside them, which are full of eyes, move in four directions without turning, and are moved by the spirit of the living creature. Each cherub has four faces (cherub, man, lion, eagle) and four wings, and the narrator identifies them as the living creature he saw by the river Chebar. The LORD's glory rises from the cherub to the threshold of the house, which fills with cloud and brightness, then leaves the threshold and stands over the cherubim, which rise up and stop at the east gate of the LORD's house with the glory of the God of Israel above them.

Chapter 11 body

The Spirit brings Ezekiel to the LORD's east gate, where he sees twenty-five men, including the princes Jaazaniah son of Azzur and Pelatiah son of Benaiah, who give wicked counsel in the city and claim they are the meat in the cauldron. Ezekiel is told to prophesy against them: the LORD says the slain they have multiplied are the meat, and the men will be brought out, delivered to strangers, and judged at the border of Israel. Pelatiah dies as Ezekiel prophesies, and Ezekiel cries out asking whether the remnant of Israel will be fully ended. The LORD answers that He will be a sanctuary to the scattered exiles, gather them, give them the land of Israel, and give them one heart and a new spirit, while those devoted to detestable things will bear the consequences. The LORD's glory then leaves the city and stands on the mountain east of it, and Ezekiel, carried back to the captives in Chaldea, tells them everything he was shown.

Chapter 12 body

In Ezekiel 12, the LORD tells Ezekiel, the son of man living among a rebellious house, to act out an exile by packing baggage, digging through a wall, and carrying his things out in the dark with his face covered. Ezekiel does so, and the LORD explains that the act is a sign concerning the prince in Jerusalem and the house of Israel, who will go into exile, with the prince captured in a net and brought to Babylon, where he will die without seeing it. The LORD says a few will be spared so they can declare their abominations among the nations. Ezekiel is also told to eat and drink with trembling as a sign of the fear that will come on Jerusalem's inhabitants and the desolation of the land. Finally, the LORD declares an end to the proverb that visions fail and are far off, saying his word will be performed without delay.

Chapter 13 body

The LORD commands Ezekiel, called son of man, to prophesy against the prophets of Israel who speak from their own spirit and have seen nothing, and who claim 'The LORD says' though he has not sent them. They are compared to foxes in waste places, they did not repair the gaps in the wall, and they promised 'Peace' when there was none, plastering a flimsy wall with whitewash. The LORD declares that a stormy wind, flooding shower and hailstones will bring the wall down and consume those who plastered it. Ezekiel is then told to set his face against the women who prophesy from their own heart and sew magic bands and veils to hunt souls for handfuls of barley and pieces of bread. The LORD vows to tear off these bands and veils, free his people from their hands, and end false visions and divination, so that they will know he is the LORD.

Chapter 14 body

Elders of Israel come to the speaker, and the LORD tells him they have taken idols into their hearts and set their iniquity before them, so he will answer them according to their many idols and calls the house of Israel to return from idols. Anyone, Israelite or stranger, who does this and then inquires of a prophet will be answered by the LORD himself, set apart as a sign and proverb, and cut off; a deceived prophet is destroyed too, and both bear their iniquity. The LORD then says that when a land sins and he sends famine, evil animals, sword, or pestilence, even Noah, Daniel, and Job would save only their own souls and no sons or daughters. He declares that these four severe judgments will come on Jerusalem, yet a remnant of sons and daughters will be carried out and will comfort the speaker's audience, showing that the LORD acted not without cause.

Chapter 15 body

The LORD's word comes to a 'son of man' with a question about the vine tree: what makes its wood better than that of any other forest tree? The wood is useless for making anything, not even a pin to hang a vessel on, and once the fire has burned both its ends and its middle it is even less useful. The LORD applies this to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, saying he will give them to the fire as he gave the vine wood. He will set his face against them so that the fire still devours them, and they will know he is the LORD. He will make the land desolate because they have acted unfaithfully.

Chapter 16 body

In Ezekiel 16 the Lord GOD tells Ezekiel to make Jerusalem know her abominations. He recounts how she was born of a Canaanite land with an Amorite father and Hittite mother, cast out and abhorred, then rescued, made to live, adorned, and covenanted to him. She trusted in her beauty, made high places and images from the gifts he gave her, prostituted herself with Egyptians, Assyrians and Chaldeans, and sacrificed her children. The Lord GOD declares he will gather her lovers to strip, stone, and burn her, and compares her to her sisters Samaria and Sodom, whom she has outdone in corruption. He nevertheless promises to restore all three sisters, remember his covenant, and establish an everlasting covenant so she will know he is the LORD, when he has forgiven her.

Chapter 17 body

The LORD tells Ezekiel to give the house of Israel a riddle about two great eagles, a cedar, and a vine. The first eagle takes the top of Lebanon's cedar to a city of merchants and plants seed in fertile soil, where it becomes a low spreading vine; the vine then turns toward a second eagle, and the LORD asks whether it will prosper. The LORD explains that the king of Babylon took Jerusalem's king and princes, made a covenant and oath with one of the royal offspring, but that king rebelled by sending ambassadors to Egypt for horses and troops. The LORD vows that this king will die in Babylon, that Pharaoh's army will not help him, and that his broken oath and covenant will fall on his own head. The chapter closes with the LORD promising to plant a tender twig from the cedar's top on Israel's high mountain, where it will become a good cedar sheltering birds, so that all the trees will know the LORD has spoken and acted.

Chapter 18 body

In Ezekiel 18 the Lord GOD rejects the proverb the house of Israel uses, that fathers have eaten sour grapes and the children's teeth are set on edge. He declares that all souls are his and that the soul who sins shall die. He illustrates this with a just man who lives, his robber son who dies, and that son's son who sees his father's sins, refuses to repeat them, and lives. He then says the wicked who turn from sin shall live, while the righteous who turn to iniquity shall die, answers Israel's charge that his way is unfair, and urges Israel to return, make a new heart and spirit, and live.

Chapter 19 body

Chapter 19 gives a lamentation for the princes of Israel in two images. In the first, their mother is a lioness who raises two cubs among lions; each becomes a young lion that catches prey and devours men. The nations trap the first cub in a pit and bring him with hooks to Egypt. When the lioness sees her hope lost, she raises a second cub, who lays waste cities and roars until the nations net him, cage him, and bring him to the king of Babylon so his voice is no longer heard on the mountains of Israel. In the second image, their mother is a fruitful vine planted by waters with strong branches for rulers' scepters, but it is plucked up in fury, dried by the east wind, burned by fire, and planted in a dry wilderness with no branch left to be a scepter. The text ends by declaring this a lamentation.

Chapter 20 body

In the seventh year, elders of Israel come to inquire of the LORD, but he refuses to be inquired of by them and tells the son of man to judge them by recounting the abominations of their fathers. The LORD recalls choosing Israel in Egypt, bringing them out, and giving statutes, ordinances and Sabbaths, yet Israel repeatedly rebelled in Egypt and the wilderness, and the LORD acted for his name's sake, withheld full destruction, and swore to scatter them. The people continue the idolatry of their fathers, including sacrificing at high places and passing sons through the fire. The LORD declares he will be king over them with a mighty hand, gather them from the nations, judge them face to face in the wilderness of the peoples, purge rebels, and finally accept the faithful on his holy mountain and bring them into the land. The chapter ends with a prophecy against the forest of the south, where the LORD will kindle an unquenchable fire, and the prophet laments that people call him a speaker of parables.

Chapter 21 body

In Ezekiel 21 the LORD tells Ezekiel, addressed as son of man, to prophesy against Jerusalem and the land of Israel, announcing that he has drawn his sword to cut off the righteous and the wicked and that it will not return. Ezekiel is told to sigh with a broken heart, to cry out, and to strike his thigh and hands, because the coming news will make every heart melt. The sword is described as sharpened and polished for slaughter, set against the gates, and falling on the LORD's people and the princes of Israel. The king of Babylon stands at a fork in the road and uses divination, and the lot for Jerusalem decides his attack, though Ammon's Rabbah is also a possible target. The wounded wicked prince of Israel is told to remove his turban and crown, and the LORD says he will overturn the kingdom until the one whose right it is comes. Finally the LORD turns the sword against Ammon, promising judgment, wrath, and destruction by brutish men.

Chapter 22 body

In Ezekiel 22 the LORD commands the son of man to judge the bloody city and make known her abominations: bloodshed, idolatry, contempt for parents, oppression of foreigners, orphans and widows, sexual sins, bribery, usury, and profaning of the Sabbaths. He declares he will scatter the people among the nations to purge their filthiness. In a second word, the house of Israel is called dross in the furnace, and the LORD will gather them into Jerusalem to melt them with the fire of his wrath. In a third word, the land is described as uncleansed, with conspiring prophets, violent priests, wolf-like princes, and oppressive people of the land. The LORD sought a man to stand in the gap but found no one, so he poured out his indignation on them.

Chapter 23 body

The LORD gives Ezekiel an allegory of two sisters, Oholah (Samaria) and Oholibah (Jerusalem), who were unfaithful in Egypt in their youth and afterward. Oholah doted on the Assyrians and was handed over to them, who uncovered her nakedness, took her children, and killed her with the sword. Oholibah, more corrupt than her sister, lusted after the Assyrians and then the Babylonians (Chaldeans), and the LORD announces that these alienated lovers will attack her, strip her, and mutilate her, and that she will drink her sister's cup. The sisters are also charged with adultery with idols, shedding blood, sacrificing their sons to idols, and profaning the LORD's sanctuary and Sabbaths on the same day. The LORD says a mob will stone them, kill their children, and burn their houses so that lewdness ceases and they know he is the Lord GOD.

Chapter 24 body

In the ninth year, the LORD tells Ezekiel to record the day the king of Babylon drew close to Jerusalem and to speak a parable to the rebellious house about a cauldron set on the fire, filled with choice pieces and bones and boiled until its rust is consumed. The cauldron stands for the bloody city, whose uncleansed filthiness and unhidden blood bring the LORD's unsparing judgment. The LORD then announces that he will take the desire of Ezekiel's eyes with one stroke and forbids him to mourn, and that evening Ezekiel's wife dies. When the people ask what this means, Ezekiel explains that the LORD will profane his sanctuary and their children will fall by the sword, and that they will likewise be unable to mourn but will pine away in their iniquities. The LORD says that Ezekiel will be a sign to them, and that when a survivor comes to report the news, Ezekiel's mouth will be opened and he will no longer be mute.

Chapter 25 body

In Chapter 25 the LORD instructs the prophet, addressed as 'Son of man', to prophesy against four neighboring peoples who mocked or harmed Israel and Judah. The children of Ammon are condemned for saying 'Aha!' over the profaned sanctuary, the desolated land of Israel, and Judah's captivity, and will be handed to the children of the east and cut off. Moab and Seir are judged for saying Judah is like all the nations, and Moab's frontier cities will be opened to invaders. Edom is condemned for taking vengeance on Judah and will be made desolate from Teman to Dedan by the hand of Israel. The Philistines, who acted with perpetual hostility, will be cut off along with the Cherethites and the remnant of the sea coast, so that all will know that He is the LORD.

Chapter 26 body

In the eleventh year, the LORD tells the prophet that because Tyre gloated over Jerusalem's fall, hoping to be replenished by it, the Lord GOD is against Tyre. He will bring many nations against it like waves, and specifically Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon with horses, chariots, and a large army from the north. The walls and towers will be broken, the people and its daughters in the field killed, its riches plundered, and its songs silenced, leaving a bare rock for spreading nets. The princes of the sea will mourn and tremble, and the islands will be dismayed at its fall. Tyre will be brought down to the pit with those of old, will never be inhabited or found again, while the Lord GOD sets glory in the land of the living.

Chapter 27 body

The LORD instructs Ezekiel, addressed as son of man, to take up a lamentation over Tyre, the merchant city at the entry of the sea that boasted 'I am perfect in beauty.' The chapter pictures Tyre as a magnificent ship, built with cypress from Senir, a Lebanon cedar mast, Bashan oak oars, and Egyptian linen sails, and crewed by men of Sidon, Arvad, and Gebal. Tyre's army included Persia, Lud, Put, and Arvad, and its trade drew merchants from Tarshish, Javan, Judah, Israel, Damascus, Arabia, Sheba, and many other places, dealing in metals, horses, spices, wool, and precious stones. The ship is then broken by the east wind in the heart of the seas, and all its wealth, crew, and company sink in the day of its ruin. Mariners and pilots mourn on the shore with dust, ashes, shaved heads, and sackcloth, while island inhabitants and kings are horrified and the merchants hiss at Tyre's terrible end.

Chapter 28 body

The LORD instructs the prophet to speak against the prince of Tyre, who claims to be a god and is proud of the wisdom and riches he has gained through trading; the LORD declares that foreign strangers will bring him down to a violent death. A lamentation is then taken up over the king of Tyre, who was once perfect, wise, and beautiful in Eden, the garden of God, and an anointed covering cherub on God's holy mountain. Unrighteousness and violence from his commerce led to his being cast out, laid before kings, and burned to ashes by a fire from within him. The LORD then sets his face against Sidon, promising pestilence, bloodshed, and the sword so that they will know he is the LORD. The chapter closes with a promise that Israel will be gathered, freed from scornful neighbors, and will dwell securely in the land given to Jacob.

Chapter 29 body

In Chapter 29 the LORD tells Ezekiel to prophesy against Pharaoh king of Egypt, who is called a great monster in his rivers for claiming the river as his own. The LORD says he will hook Pharaoh and cast him into the wilderness as food for animals and birds, and will bring a sword on Egypt and make it a desolation from the tower of Seveneh to the border of Ethiopia for forty years. The Egyptians will be scattered, then gathered back to Pathros as a lowly kingdom that will never again rule the nations or be Israel's confidence. A later word, dated the twenty-seventh year, says Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon received no wages from his hard service against Tyre, so the LORD gives him Egypt as payment. The chapter ends with the LORD promising to make a horn sprout for the house of Israel and to open Ezekiel's mouth among them.

Chapter 30 body

In Ezekiel 30 the LORD commands the prophet to wail over the coming day of the LORD, a day of clouds and a time of the nations, when a sword will fall on Egypt and its allies Ethiopia, Put, Lud, Cub and others. The LORD names Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon as the agent who will destroy Egypt's multitude, dry up its rivers, and fill the land with the slain. Egyptian idols, cities including Memphis, Pathros, Zoan, No, Sin, Aven, Pibeseth and Tehaphnehes, and its princes are to be destroyed or taken into captivity. In a second oracle dated the seventh day of the first month of the eleventh year, the LORD says he has broken Pharaoh's arm and will break both arms, making the sword fall from his hand. He will strengthen the king of Babylon with his sword and scatter the Egyptians among the nations, so that they will know that he is the LORD.

Chapter 31 body

In the eleventh year, on the first day of the third month, the LORD tells the prophet to address Pharaoh king of Egypt and his multitude, asking whom he resembles in greatness. The LORD answers with an image of the Assyrian as a great cedar in Lebanon, nourished by many waters, sheltering birds, animals and nations, and envied by all the trees of Eden in the garden of God. Because its heart was lifted up in its height, the LORD delivers it to the mighty one of the nations, and foreigners cut it down so that its branches fall across the mountains and valleys and its shadow is abandoned. Its descent to Sheol causes mourning, makes the nations shake, and comforts the trees of Eden in the lower parts of the earth, where its allies also go down to those slain by the sword. The passage ends by telling Pharaoh that he will likewise be brought down among the uncircumcised slain by the sword, declaring that the tree is Pharaoh and all his multitude.

Chapter 32 body

In Ezekiel 32, the LORD commands Ezekiel to take up a lamentation over Pharaoh king of Egypt, who is likened to a young lion of the nations and a monster in the seas. The LORD says he will catch Pharaoh in his net, leave him on the open field for birds and animals, darken the heavens, and bring the sword of the king of Babylon against Egypt, using the ruthless of the nations to destroy its multitude. In a second word, given on the fifteenth day of the same month, Ezekiel is told to wail for Egypt's multitude and cast them down to the pit among the uncircumcised slain by the sword. The chapter lists nations already in Sheol: Asshur, Elam, Meshech, Tubal, Edom, the princes of the north, and the Sidonians, all of whom caused terror in the land of the living and now lie slain. Pharaoh will see them and be comforted, but he and all his multitude will be laid among the uncircumcised slain by the sword.

Chapter 33 body

The LORD tells Ezekiel (the 'son of man') that he is a watchman for the house of Israel, using a parable of a watchman who either blows the trumpet at the approach of the sword or fails to, so that responsibility for blood falls on the hearer or on the watchman. The LORD declares he takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked but wants them to turn and live, and he explains that neither past righteousness nor past wickedness settles a person's fate, since each is judged by his own ways, against the people's charge that his way is not fair. A fugitive from Jerusalem arrives in the twelfth year of the captivity announcing the city's defeat, and Ezekiel's mouth, opened the evening before, is no longer mute. The LORD then rebukes those in the waste places of the land of Israel who claim inheritance because they are many, citing their blood-eating, idolatry, bloodshed and adultery, and promises desolation by sword, beasts and pestilence. The people enjoy Ezekiel like a lovely song but do not act on his words, until events show that a prophet has been among them.

Chapter 34 body

In Chapter 34 the LORD commands the prophet to speak against the shepherds of Israel, who feed themselves, eat the fat, and wear the wool while neglecting the sheep, ruling them with force so that they are scattered and become prey. The LORD declares that he is against these shepherds, will require his sheep from them, and will stop them from feeding on the flock. He promises to search for and gather his scattered sheep from the peoples and countries, bring them to their own land, and shepherd them with good pasture, healing the weak and destroying the fat and strong. He will judge between fat and lean sheep, rams and male goats, because the strong trample the pasture and push the diseased away. He will set up his servant David as one shepherd and prince, make a covenant of peace, bless the land, break the yoke of their enslavers, and affirm that they are his people and he is their God.

Chapter 35 body

In Chapter 35 the LORD commands the prophet, addressed as 'Son of man', to set his face against Mount Seir and prophesy against it. The LORD declares that He is against Seir and will make it a perpetual desolation, laying its cities waste and filling its mountains, hills, and valleys with the slain. The reasons given are Seir's perpetual hostility, its handing the children of Israel over to the sword in their calamity, and its claim that the 'two nations and two countries' would be its own, although the LORD was there. The LORD also says He has heard Seir's insults and boasts against the mountains of Israel. Because Seir rejoiced over Israel's desolate inheritance, the LORD will make Seir and all Edom desolate, so that they will know He is the LORD.

Chapter 36 body

In Ezekiel 36 the Lord GOD commands the prophet to speak to the mountains of Israel, promising to punish Edom and the residue of the nations who mocked and seized the land. He promises that the mountains will bear fruit, be tilled, and be repopulated by the house of Israel, and will no longer devour or bereave their people. He explains that Israel defiled the land, was scattered among the nations, and profaned his holy name there. He declares he will act for the sake of his name, gathering them, cleansing them, giving them a new heart and his Spirit, and restoring the land to be like the garden of Eden so the nations will know he is the LORD.

Chapter 37 body

In a vision, the LORD's Spirit sets the prophet, called son of man, in a valley full of very dry bones and tells him to prophesy over them. The bones assemble, gain sinews, flesh and skin, and then live and stand as a great army when breath comes at his prophesying to the wind. The LORD explains that the bones are the whole house of Israel, who say their hope is lost, and promises to open their graves, put his Spirit in them, and bring them into the land of Israel. He then has the prophet join two sticks, one for Judah and one for Joseph/Ephraim, as a sign that the divided nations will become one nation under one king. The LORD promises that David his servant will be their prince forever, that they will be cleansed from idols, and that he will make an everlasting covenant of peace and set his sanctuary among them.

Chapter 38 body

In Ezekiel 38, the LORD tells Ezekiel (addressed as son of man) to prophesy against Gog, prince of Rosh, Meshech, and Tubal, of the land of Magog. The LORD says he will turn Gog around, bring him out with a vast army and allies including Persia, Cush, Put, Gomer, and Togarmah, and send him in the latter years against Israel, a land restored and dwelling securely in unwalled villages. Gog will devise an evil plan to seize plunder, while Sheba, Dedan, and the merchants of Tarshish question his motives. The LORD declares that he is bringing Gog against his land so the nations may know him when he is sanctified in Gog, and that his wrath will cause a great shaking, with swords turned against brothers, pestilence, blood, torrential rain, hailstones, fire, and sulfur. Through this judgment the LORD will magnify and sanctify himself and be known to many nations.

Chapter 39 body

The Lord GOD commands the prophet to prophesy against Gog, prince of Rosh, Meshech, and Tubal, announcing that he will draw Gog from the far north onto the mountains of Israel, disarm him, and cause him and his hordes to fall and be devoured by birds and animals. Israel will burn the enemy weapons for seven years and bury Gog's multitude in the valley of Hamon Gog for seven months to cleanse the land, with continual searchers marking any remaining bones. The birds and animals are summoned to a great sacrifice on the mountains of Israel, where they will eat the flesh and drink the blood of mighty men, princes, horses, and charioteers. The Lord GOD declares that the nations will see his glory and judgment and will understand that Israel went into captivity for its iniquity. He promises to reverse the captivity of Jacob, show mercy to the whole house of Israel, gather them to their land, and pour out his Spirit on them, never hiding his face from them again.

Chapter 40 body

Chapter 40 opens in the twenty-fifth year of the captivity, when the LORD's hand brings the speaker, addressed as son of man, in visions of God to a very high mountain in the land of Israel, where there is something like the frame of a city. A man with the appearance of bronze, holding a line of flax and a measuring reed, charges him to see, hear, and set his heart on all he is shown and to declare it to the house of Israel. The man measures the wall, the east gate with its lodges, porch, posts, windows, and palm trees, then the outer court with its pavement and thirty rooms, and the north and south gates of the outer court, each fifty cubits long and twenty-five wide with seven steps. He then measures the matching gates of the inner court, each reached by eight steps, along with the rooms where burnt offerings were washed, the eight tables for killing sacrifices, and the four cut stone tables. Finally he describes the rooms for singers and for priests, identifying the sons of Zadok as those who minister to the LORD, and measures the square inner court, the altar before the house, and the porch of the house.

Chapter 41 body

Chapter 41 describes a guide measuring the parts of the temple for the narrator, beginning with the nave, its posts, and its entrance, and then the inner room he calls the most holy place. It details the wall of the house and the three-story side rooms built into it, along with the raised base, open area, and the separate place and building at the west. The guide gives overall dimensions of one hundred cubits for the temple, the separate place with its building, and the east face. The walls and doors are decorated with cherubim and palm trees, each cherub having a man's face and a young lion's face. The chapter ends with the wooden altar, called the table before the LORD, and the two-leaved doors of the temple and sanctuary.

Chapter 42 body

In Ezekiel 42 the speaker is led by a guide into the outer court toward the north, where he sees a building of rooms arranged in three stories of galleries, with the upper rooms set back and shorter than the lower ones. The north building is one hundred cubits long and fifty wide, with an entry on the east side, and matching rooms lie to the south and east. The guide explains that these are holy rooms where priests who are near the LORD eat and lay the most holy things: the meal offering, sin offering, and trespass offering. Priests must leave their ministering garments in these rooms and put on other garments before approaching the people. After finishing the inner house, the guide measures the whole surrounding area through the east gate, finding five hundred reeds on each of the four sides, and notes a wall five hundred cubits by five hundred cubits that separates the holy from the common.

Chapter 43 body

In Chapter 43 the narrator is brought to the east gate, where the glory of the God of Israel returns from the east, enters the house by that gate, and fills the house after the Spirit lifts him into the inner court. A voice from the house declares it the place of God's throne where he will dwell forever among the children of Israel, provided they stop defiling his name with prostitution and the dead bodies of their kings. The narrator is told to show the house's form and laws to the house of Israel so they may be ashamed and keep its ordinances. The altar's measurements are given, including its base, ledges, four horns, twelve-cubit hearth, and east-facing steps. Finally, God sets out a seven-day rite of sin offerings, a young bull, and a ram, with blood and salt, for the Levitical priests of Zadok's offspring to cleanse and consecrate the altar, after which God will accept the people's offerings on the eighth day.

Chapter 44 body

In Chapter 44, the speaker is brought back to the outer east gate of the sanctuary, which the LORD declares must stay shut because the LORD, the God of Israel, entered by it; only the prince may sit in its porch to eat bread before the LORD. At the north gate the speaker sees the LORD's glory fill the house and falls on his face, then is told to mark carefully all the house's ordinances. The LORD rebukes the house of Israel for bringing uncircumcised foreigners into the sanctuary and bars them from entering. The Levites who went astray after idols will bear their iniquity and be limited to guarding the gates and serving the people, while the Levitical priests, the sons of Zadok, who kept the sanctuary's duty, may approach the LORD to offer fat and blood. The chapter ends with rules for those priests' clothing, hair, wine, marriage, teaching, judging, contact with the dead, inheritance, portions of offerings, and forbidden food.

Chapter 45 body

Chapter 45 gives instructions for dividing the land by lot, with a holy portion of 25,000 by 10,000 reeds set aside as an offering to the LORD. Within it lie the sanctuary and its pasture lands, a section for the priests, and a section for the Levites, alongside a city possession for the whole house of Israel and land on either side for the prince. The LORD rebukes the princes of Israel for violence, plunder, and dispossessing his people, and commands just balances and consistent measures for the ephah, bath, homer, shekel, and mina. The people are to give a set offering of wheat, barley, oil, and lambs, and the prince is made responsible for providing the burnt, meal, drink, sin, and peace offerings at feasts, new moons, and Sabbaths. The chapter ends with the cleansing of the sanctuary on the first day of the first month, the Passover on the fourteenth day with seven days of unleavened bread and daily offerings, and a matching seven-day feast in the seventh month.

Chapter 46 body

Chapter 46 gives the Lord GOD's regulations for worship and land in the restored temple. The east gate of the inner court is shut on the six working days but opened on the Sabbath and the new moon, when the prince worships at its threshold while the priests prepare his offerings and the people of the land worship at its door. Specified burnt offerings and meal offerings are set for the Sabbath, the new moon, and the feasts, along with a daily morning lamb as a continual burnt offering. The prince may give gifts from his own inheritance to his sons permanently, to servants only until the year of liberty, and must never take the people's inheritance. A guide then shows the narrator the holy rooms for the priests, where sin and trespass offerings and meal offerings are cooked, and the corner courts with boiling houses where the ministers boil the people's sacrifices.

Chapter 47 body

In Ezekiel's vision, a man with a measuring line leads him back to the temple door, where water flows out from beneath the threshold eastward, south of the altar. The man measures off four stretches of one thousand cubits, and the water deepens from ankle-deep to knee-deep to waist-deep to a river too deep to cross. Along its banks grow very many trees, and the man explains that the waters will flow into the Arabah and the sea, healing it so that every living creature and a great multitude of fish live, though the swamps and marshes will be given up to salt. The Lord GOD then sets out the borders of the land (north, east, south, and west) to be divided by lot among the twelve tribes, with Joseph having two portions. The aliens living among Israel and fathering children there are to be treated as native-born and receive an inheritance in whatever tribe they live.

Chapter 48 body

Chapter 48 describes how the land is divided among the tribes of Israel by lot, with seven tribal portions from Dan to Judah in the north, and five more from Benjamin to Gad in the south. Between Judah and Benjamin lies a central square offering of twenty-five thousand by twenty-five thousand reeds, containing the holy offering for the priests of the sons of Zadok with the sanctuary in its middle, an adjoining portion for the Levites, and the city with its farmland. The prince receives the remaining land on either side of the offering and the city. The chapter also lists the city's measurements and its twelve gates named after the tribes, and closes by giving the city the name 'The LORD is there.'
