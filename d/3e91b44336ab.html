Abstract written by a model

This sequence from the Vendidad presents Ahura Mazda’s teachings to Spitama Zarathustra, beginning with creation and ending in detailed rules for purity and atonement. In Fargard I, Ahura Mazda lists beautiful lands he created, including Airyana Vaêgô, Sughdha, Môuru, and Bâkhdhi, while Angra Mainyu counters each with evils such as serpents, sin, and winter. Fargard II turns to Yima, who declines to receive the law but agrees to protect the material world; after enlarging the overcrowded earth three times, he builds the Vara to preserve selected people, animals, plants, and fires from fatal winters. Zarathustra and Urvatad-nara rule there, and Karshipta brings Mazda’s law. The following units establish religious and social order. Fargard III explains what delights or grieves the Earth: worship, households, cultivation, herds, and care for the faithful are good, while corpses, Dakhmas, fiendish burrows, and captivity bring grief. It also connects confession and Mazda’s law with cleansing sin. Fargard IV regulates contracts, breaches, assault, restitution, and escalating penalties, including severe punishment for lying before truth-knowing water. Fargards V through VIII concentrate on death pollution, especially the Drug Nasu, and on protecting water, fire, land, people, animals, and sacred things. They prescribe where bodies and bones may be placed, how polluted ground and objects are treated, how corpses are removed from water or secured from it, and how people, clothing, utensils, animals, food, and places are cleansed. They include rules for still-born children, Dakhmas, healers and their payments, sexual conduct, and confession. The final record specifically addresses a fire used to burn a corpse, its dispersal, rewards for bringing fire to the Dâityô-gâtu, and cleansing a corpse-touched person with gômêz, water, running, and assistance, alongside further penalties and atonements.

Vendidad (Vendîdâd) / Fargard I. body

Ahura Mazda tells Spitama Zarathustra that he created every land dear to its dwellers and describes the good lands and countries he made. The first sixteen lands include Airyana Vaêgô, the plains in Sughdha, the strong, holy Môuru, and the beautiful Bâkhdhi with high-lifted banners. Angra Mainyu counter-created harmful beings, sins, and conditions in each land, including the serpent in the river and winter in Airyana Vaêgô. The passage concludes that there are still other beautiful, desirable, and thriving lands and countries.

Fargard II. body

Zarathustra asks Ahura Mazda who first received the law of Ahura, and Ahura Mazda identifies Yima, who instead undertakes to nourish, rule, and watch over the material world. Under Yima’s rule, the earth becomes overcrowded, so he enlarges it three times. Ahura Mazda then warns Yima of fatal winters and commands him to build the Vara, where selected men and women, animals, plants, and fires will be preserved. The Vara is completed, has its own lights and inhabitants, and is ruled by Urvatad-nara and Zarathustra, while Karshipta brings the law of Mazda there.

Fargard III. body

Fargard III concerns the Earth and the teachings of Ahura Mazda to Zarathustra about what brings the Earth happiness or grief. The Earth is happiest where the faithful worship, build thriving households, cultivate land, increase herds, and produce dung, while it grieves over corpses, Dakhmas, fiendish burrows, and captive families. Ahura Mazda teaches rules concerning carrying and burying corpses, repentance, cultivation, eating, and giving to the faithful. The unit also describes the law of Mazda as cleansing confessed sins and removing their penalties.

Fargard IV. body

Fargard IV concerns contracts, their breach, assaults, atonement, and penalties. Ahura Mazda defines six contracts, explains how each is redeemed by a higher contract, and assigns escalating atonements and stripes for breaking them. It also classifies threatening or assaultive acts as Âgerepta, Avaoirista, or Aredus, with repeated or unatoned offenses making the offender a Peshôtanu. The unit further describes agreements involving goods, wives, and knowledge, and imposes severe punishment for knowingly lying before the truth-knowing water.

Fargard V. body

This unit concerns Ahura Mazda’s teachings to Zarathustra about corpses, death, water, fire, purity, and the law of Mazda and Zarathustra. It explains rules for handling corpses, dead matter, houses for the dead, Dakhmas, contaminated objects, and a still-born child. It describes the effects of the Drug Nasu on people and dogs, and the pollution caused by an Ashemaogha. It also teaches about the movement and cleansing of water and the greatness of purity and the law.

Fargard VI. body

Ahura Mazda instructs Zarathustra on keeping ground fallow for a year after the death of dogs or men and on penalties for violating or failing to inspect it. He specifies penalties for placing bones or bodies on the ground, and procedures for removing corpses from ponds, wells, snow, hail, and running streams so the water can become clean. He explains how touched Haoma may be purified, where the dead and their bones should be placed, and how corpses must be secured against being carried to water or trees.

Fargard VII. body

Fargard VII concerns the pollution caused by death and the rules for cleansing people, clothing, utensils, animals, wood, corn, and ground. Ahura Mazda tells Zarathustra when the Drug Nasu attacks the dead and how far its pollution extends. It also sets rules for healing, payments to healers, Dakhmas and fiends, the treatment of a woman with a still-born child, and offerings defiled by the dead.

Fargard VIII. body

Fargard VIII concerns the treatment of dogs and men who die, corpse pollution, purification, penalties, sexual conduct, and the law of Mazda. Ahura Mazda instructs the worshippers of Mazda where to place dead bodies, how to move them, how to purify people and places, and how to drive away the Drug Nasu. It also describes punishments for covering a dead body, rules concerning involuntary and voluntary emission of seed, and the cleansing power of confession under the law of Mazda. The unit identifies the Daêva through sexual acts between men and women and traces the Drug Nasu’s movement during ritual washing.

VIII. body

Ahura Mazda instructs worshippers of Mazda how to deal with a corpse-burning fire, including killing the man responsible, removing the cauldron and tripod, and dispersing the fire. The text assigns rewards for bringing different kinds of fire to the Dâityô-gâtu, measured in fire-brands. It explains how a man who has touched a corpse may be cleansed using gômêz, water, running, and assistance from others. It also prescribes penalties and atonements involving water, trees, the Aspahê-astra, and the Sraoshô-karana.
