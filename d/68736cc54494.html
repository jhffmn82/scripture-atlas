Kojiki

Kojiki, translated by Basil Hall Chamberlain (1882), public domain. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://sacred-texts.com/shi/kj/

Abstract written by a model

A chronological imperial record begins with His Augustness Kamu-yamato-ihare-biko, later Emperor Jim-Mu, advancing east from Himuka, defeating resistance, founding rule at Kashibara, and losing his elder brother Itsu-se in battle. Guided by the Great-High-Integrating-Deity, a cross-sword, and an eight-foot crow, he pacifies the Empire; after his death, Kamu-nuna-kaha-mimi kills Tagishi-mimi and succeeds as Emperor Sui-Zei. The record then traces successive rulers through marriages, children, palaces, reign lengths, mausoleums, and the noble lineages descending from them: Sui-Zei, an-Nei, I-Toku, Ko-Sho, Koan, Kō-Rei, Kō-Gen, and Kai-Kuwa. It notes the pacification of Kibi by Oho-kibi-tsu-hiko and Waka-take-kibi-tsu-hiko, and expands on descendants who become ancestors of regional rulers, grandees, chiefs, and dukes. Emperor Sū-Jin’s reign includes pestilence ended through worship of the Great-Master-of-Things, suppression of King Take-hani-yasu’s rebellion, tribute, and public works. Under Sui-Nin, the deaths of Saho-biko and Saho-bime, Prince Homu-chi-wake’s worship at Idzumo, clan foundations, and Tajima-mori’s return from the Eternal Land precede the Emperor’s death. Emperor Kei-Kō’s reign centers on Yamato-take’s campaigns against Kumaso, Idzumo, Sagamu, and other opponents; Yamato-take dies after encountering the Deity of Mount Ibuki and transforms into a white dotterel. Sei-Mu appoints Take-uchi Prince Minister and establishes countries’ rulers, boundaries, and departmental lords. Under Chiū-Ai and Empress Jin-Gō, divine instructions lead to Korea’s submission and later victory over Yamato opposition. Finally, Ō-Jin’s reign records genealogies, foreign contact, succession roles for Oho-yama-mori, Oho-sazaki, and Uji-no-waki-iratsuko, conflict after his death, and Ama-no-hi-boko’s descendants and eight precious treasures.

Kojiki front matter

Sect. XLIV.—Reign of the Emperor Jim-Mu (Part I.—His Progress Eastward, and Death of His Elder Brother) .. Sect. LIV.—Emperor Jim-Mu (Part XI.—His Age and Place of Burial). body

His Augustness Kamu-yamato-ihare-biko, later identified as Emperor Jim-Mu, progresses eastward from Himuka, defeats resisting forces, and establishes rule at Kashibara near Unebi. His elder brother His Augustness Itsu-se dies after being wounded in the battle with Nagasune-biko of Tomi. With guidance from the Great-High-Integrating-Deity, the cross-sword, and the eight-foot crow, His Augustness Kamu-yamato-ihare-biko conquers and pacifies the Empire. After his death, His Augustness Kamu-nuna-kaha-mimi kills His Augustness Tagishi-mimi, and the imperial mausoleum is placed on the Kashi Spur by Mount Unebi.

Sect. LV.—Emperor Sui-Zei. body

Emperor Sui-Zei, also called His Augustness Kamu-nuna-kaha-mimi, dwelt at the palace of Takawoka in Kadzuraki and ruled the Empire. He wedded Kaha-mata-bime, ancestress of the Departmental Lords of Shiki, and they begot His Augustness Shiki-tsu-hiko-tama-demi. His august years were forty-five, and his mausoleum is on the Mound of Tsukida.

Sect. LVI.—Emperor an-Nei. body

His Augustness Shiki-tsu-hiko-tama-de-mi dwelt in the palace of Ukiana at Kata-shiha and ruled the Empire. He wedded Akuto-hime and begot three august children, including His Augustness Oho-yamato-hiko-suki-tomo and His Augustness Shiki-tsu-hiko. His Augustness Oho-yamato-hiko-suki-tomo later ruled the Empire, while His Augustness Shiki-tsu-hiko had two Kings as children, including His Augustness Wa-chi-tsumi. His Augustness Wa-chi-tsumi dwelt in the palace of Miwi in Ahaji and had two daughters, and His Augustness Shiki-tsu-hiko-tama-de-mi’s august years were forty-nine.

Sect. LVII.—Emperor I-Toku. body

Emperor I-Toku, also called His Augustness Oho-yamato-hiko-suki-tomo, dwelt in the palace of Sakahiwo at Karu and ruled the Empire. He wedded Princess Futo-ma-waka, also called Princess Ihi-bi, and begot Mi-ma-tsu-hiko-wake-shine and Tagishi-hiko. His august years were forty-five, and his mausoleum was above the Vale of Manago by Mount Unebi.

Sect. LVIII.—Emperor Ko-Sho. body

Emperor Ko-Sho, His Augustness Mi-ma-tsu-hiko-kawe-shine, dwelt at the palace of Waki-no-kami in Kadzuraki and ruled the Empire. He wedded Her Augustness Princess Yoso-taho, younger sister of Oki-tsu-yoso, and begot His Augustness Ame-oshi-tarashi-hiko and His Augustness Oho-yamato-tarashi-hiko-kuni-oshi-bito. Oho-yamato-tarashi-hiko-kuni-oshi-bito afterwards ruled the Empire, while Ame-oshi-tarashi-hiko became ancestor of numerous grandees, dukes, and rulers. Emperor Ko-Sho's august years were ninety-three, and his August mausoleum was on Mount Hakata at Waki-no-kami.

Sect. LIX.—Emperor Koan. body

Sect. LIX concerns Emperor Koan, identified as His Augustness Oho-yamato-tarashi-hiko-kuni-oshi-bito. He dwelt at the palace of Akidzushima at Muro in Kadzuraki and ruled the Empire. He wedded his niece, Her Augustness Princess Oshika, and had two august children, including Oho-yamato-ne-ko-hiko-futo-ni, who later ruled the Empire. His august years were one hundred and twenty-three, and his mausoleum was on the Mound of Tamade.

Sect. LX.—Emperor Kō-Rei. body

Emperor Kō-Rei, His Augustness Oho-yamato-ne-ko-hiko-futo-ni, dwelt at the Palace of Ihodo at Kuruda and ruled the Empire. His son Oho-yamato-ne-ko-hiko-kuni-kuru was the one who afterwards ruled the Empire. Oho-kibi-tsu-hiko and Waka-take-kibi-tsu-hiko set sacred jars at the front of the River Hi in Harima and, making Harima the mouth of the road, subdued and pacified the Land of Kibi. Emperor Kō-Rei's august years were one hundred and six, and his mausoleum was at Umasaka at Kotawoka.

Sect. XLI.—Emperor Kō-Gen. body

Emperor Kō-Gen, His Augustness Oho-yamato-ne-ko-hiko-kuni-kuru, dwelt at Sakaki-bara at Karu and ruled the Empire. He had five august children, including Oho-biko and Waka-yamato-ne-ko-hiko-oho-bibi, who afterwards ruled the Empire. Oho-biko’s descendants and those of Noble Take-Uchi are identified as ancestors of numerous Grandees, Chiefs, and Dukes. Emperor Kō-Gen’s august years were fifty-seven, and his mausoleum was on the mound in the middle of the Pool of Tsurugi.

Sect. LXII.—Emperor Kai-Kuwa. body

Emperor Kai-Kuwa ruled the Empire from the palace of Izakaha at Kasuga and fathered five august children, including Mima-kiri-biko-iniwe and King Hiko-imasu. King Hiko-imasu fathered eleven Kings, including King Tatatsu-michi-no-ushi, and his descendants became ancestors of various regional rulers and noble families. King Tatatsu-michi-no-ushi fathered Princess Hibasu, Princess Matonu, Princess Oto-hime, and King Mi-kado-wake. King Noble Okinaga fathered Princess Okinaga-tarashi, Sora-tsu-hime, and King Prince Okinaga, and his descendants included rulers of Tajima.

Sect. LXIII.—Emperor Sū-Jin (Part I.—Genealogies). .. Sect. LXVIII.—Emperor Sū-Jin (Part VI.—His Age and Place of Burial). body

The unit concerns Emperor Sū-Jin's genealogies, reign, military campaigns, restoration of peace, and burial. His Augustness Mima-ki-iri-biko-iniwe ruled from the Palace of Midzu-gaki, and His Augustness Ikume-iri-biko-isachi succeeded him. A great pestilence was ended through worship of the Great Deity the Great-Master-of-Things, while rebellions led by King Take-hani-yasu were suppressed. The Empire was pacified, tribute was established, pools were made, and Emperor Sū-Jin's age and burial place were recorded.

Sect. LXIX.—Emperor Sui-Nin (Part I.—Genealogies) .. Sect. LXXV.—Emperor Sui-Nin (Part VII.—His Death and That of the Empress Hibasu.) body

This unit concerns Emperor Sui-Nin, his genealogies, the conspiracy of King Saho-biko and Princess Saho-bime, and the birth and later speech of Prince Homu-chi-wake. It recounts King Saho-biko and Princess Saho-bime's deaths, Homu-chi-wake's worship of the Great Deity of Idzumo, and the establishment of several clans. It also describes Tajima-mori's return from the Eternal Land with the fruit of the everlasting fragrant tree, followed by the death of Emperor Sui-Nin and the burial of Princess Hibasu.

Sect. LXXVI.—Empress Kei-Ko (Part I,—Genealogies) .. Sect. XCIII.—Emperor Kei-Kō (Part XVIII. His Age and Place of Burial). body

The unit concerns the reign and genealogy of the Heavenly Sovereign Oho-tarashi-hiko-oshiro-wake, also called Emperor Kei-Kō, and the deeds, death, transformation, and descendants of His Augustness Yamato-take. His Augustness Wo-usu, later called Yamato-take, subdues the Kumaso bravoes, the Idzumo bravo, rulers in Sagamu, and various savage Deities and unsubmissive people during his eastern expedition. Yamato-take dies after suffering illness following his encounter with the Deity of Mount Ibuki, then becomes a white dotterel and is buried in several august mausoleums. The unit also records Emperor Kei-Kō’s age as one hundred and thirty-seven and gives the genealogy of Yamato-take’s descendants.

Sect. XCIV.—Emperor Sei-Mu. body

Emperor Sei-Mu, identified in the text as the Heavenly Sovereign Wata-tarashi-hiko, dwelt at the palace of Taka-anaho at Shiga in Chika-tsu-Afumi and ruled the empire. He wedded Lady Oho-takara and begot King Wata-nuke. He raised Take-uchi to be Prince Minister and established the rulers, boundaries, and departmental lords of the countries. His august years were ninety-five, and his mausoleum was at Tatanami near Saki.

Sect. XCV.—Emperor Chiū-Ai (Part I.—Genealogies). .. Sect. CIII.—Emperor Chiū-Ai (Part IX.—His Death and That of the Empress Jin-Gō). body

This unit concerns Emperor Chiū-Ai, Empress Jin-Gō, their Heir Apparent, and the events surrounding the promised conquest of Korea. Empress Jin-Gō receives divine instructions, leads the crossing to Korea, secures the submission of Shiragi and Kudara, and later subdues Yamato. King Kagosaka dies while seeking an omen, and King Oshikuma dies after opposing the Empress's forces. The Heir Apparent exchanges names with Great Deity Izasa-wake, receives great august liquor, and Emperor Chiū-Ai and Empress Jin-Gō are recorded as dead and buried.

Sect. CIV.—Emperor Ō-Jin (Part 1.—Genealogies). .. Sect. CXVIII.—Emperor Ō-Jin (Part XV.—His Age and Place of Burial). body

This unit concerns Emperor Ō-Jin, his genealogies, reign, succession arrangements, foreign contacts, and burial. His Augustness Oho-yama-mori, His Augustness Oho-sazaki, and Uji-no-waki-iratsuko receive roles in governing the mountains, sea, Empire, and succession, but conflict follows Emperor Ō-Jin’s death. Ama-no-hi-boko crosses to Japan, and his descendants and eight precious treasures are described. Emperor Ō-Jin is said to have lived 130 years and was buried on the mound of Mofusu at Wega in Kafuchi.
