The Good News According to Mark

author unknown · about AD 70 · 17 sections

Abstract written by a model

This document is the Gospel of Mark ("The Good News According to Mark"), sixteen chapters presenting Jesus Christ, the Son of God, from his baptism to his resurrection. Jesus is the central figure; the twelve disciples, especially Peter, James, John and Andrew, are his constant companions, while John the Baptizer, the scribes, Pharisees, chief priests, Herod and Pilate shape the conflict around him. Chapter 1 opens with John baptizing in the Jordan. Jesus is baptized, declared the beloved Son, tempted in the wilderness, then preaches God's Kingdom in Galilee, calls his first followers, casts out an unclean spirit and heals many. Chapters 2 and 3 show healings, the forgiving of a paralytic's sins, the call of Levi, and disputes over fasting and the Sabbath. Opposition hardens as the Pharisees and Herodians plot to destroy him, he appoints the twelve, and scribes accuse him of having Beelzebul. Chapters 4 to 6 bring parables of the sower and the Kingdom, the calming of the storm, the Gadarene demoniac, Jairus's daughter and the woman with the flow of blood. Rejected in his own country, he sends the twelve out. The account of John's beheading by Herod follows, then the feeding of five thousand and Jesus walking on the sea. Chapters 7 and 8 treat the tradition of the elders and what truly defiles, the Syrophoenician woman's daughter, healing the deaf and the blind, and a feeding of four thousand. At Caesarea Philippi Peter declares Jesus the Christ, and Jesus foretells his suffering, death and resurrection, calling followers to take up their cross. Chapters 9 and 10 mark the turn toward Jerusalem: the transfiguration, the boy with a mute spirit, further predictions of his death, teaching on greatness, children, divorce and riches, the request of James and John, and the healing of Bartimaeus. Chapters 11 to 13 cover the entry into Jerusalem, the cursed fig tree, the cleansing of the temple, challenges to his authority, the vineyard parable, questions about taxes, resurrection and the greatest commandment, the widow's coins, and his prophecy of the temple's fall and the coming of the Son of Man, with a call to watch. Chapters 14 and 15 narrate the Passion: the plot, anointing, Judas's betrayal, the last supper, Gethsemane, arrest, condemnation, Peter's three denials, trial before Pilate, Barabbas's release, crucifixion, death, and burial by Joseph of Arimathaea. Chapter 16 closes with the women finding the empty tomb and hearing that Jesus has risen, his appearances, the commission to preach to all creation, and his ascension, after which the disciples preach everywhere.

The Good News According to Mark / Chapter 1 body

Mark chapter 1 opens the Good News of Jesus Christ, the Son of God, citing the prophets' promise of a messenger. John baptizes in the Jordan with a baptism of repentance, draws crowds from Judea and Jerusalem, and announces a mightier one coming. Jesus is baptized by John, is declared the beloved Son by a voice from the sky, and is driven by the Spirit into the wilderness for forty days of temptation by Satan. After John is taken into custody, Jesus preaches God's Kingdom in Galilee, calls Simon, Andrew, James and John to follow him, and in Capernaum teaches with authority, casts out an unclean spirit, heals Simon's wife's mother and many others, and cleanses a leper, whose spreading of the news keeps Jesus in deserted places.

Chapter 2 body

Jesus returns to Capernaum, where a crowd gathers at his house and four people lower a paralytic through the roof to him. Jesus forgives the man's sins, answers the scribes' charge of blasphemy, and heals him so that he walks out, leaving everyone amazed. Jesus then calls Levi, the son of Alphaeus, from the tax office and eats at his house with many tax collectors and sinners, which draws criticism from the scribes and Pharisees. When asked why his disciples do not fast, Jesus answers with images of the bridegroom, unshrunk cloth, and new wine in old wineskins. Finally, the Pharisees object that his disciples pluck grain on the Sabbath, and Jesus cites David and declares that the Son of Man is lord even of the Sabbath.

Chapter 3 body

In a synagogue, Jesus heals a man with a withered hand on the Sabbath while the Pharisees watch to accuse him, and they then conspire with the Herodians to destroy him. Jesus withdraws to the sea, where a great multitude from many regions follows him, and unclean spirits declare him the Son of God before he warns them to be silent. He goes up the mountain and appoints twelve, naming them, to be with him, preach, and have authority over sicknesses and demons. When his friends think he is insane and scribes from Jerusalem say he has Beelzebul, Jesus answers in parables about a divided Satan and a bound strong man, and warns of the unforgivable blasphemy against the Holy Spirit. His mother and brothers arrive outside, and he declares that whoever does the will of God is his brother, sister, and mother.

Chapter 4 body

In Chapter 4, Jesus teaches a large multitude by the seaside from a boat, telling the parable of the farmer who sows seed on four kinds of ground. Alone with those around him and the twelve, he explains that the seed is the word and that the soils represent different responses to it, and he says the mystery of God's Kingdom is given to them while outsiders receive parables. He adds sayings about a lamp, the measure given, and two Kingdom parables, the seed growing by itself and the mustard seed. That evening he crosses the sea with his disciples, sleeps in the boat during a wind storm, calms the wind and sea with a command, and asks why they are afraid and have no faith. The disciples are greatly afraid and ask who he is that the wind and sea obey him.

Chapter 5 body

Jesus crosses the sea to the country of the Gadarenes, where he frees a man living in the tombs from an unclean spirit named Legion, sending the demons into a herd of about two thousand pigs that drown in the sea. The townspeople, afraid, beg Jesus to leave, while the healed man is told to go home and proclaim what the Lord has done, which he does in Decapolis. Back across the sea, Jairus, a synagogue ruler, begs Jesus to heal his dying little daughter. On the way, a woman with a twelve-year flow of blood touches Jesus' clothes and is healed, and Jesus tells her that her faith has made her well. News comes that Jairus's daughter has died, but Jesus tells Jairus to believe, enters with Peter, James, John and the parents, and raises the twelve-year-old girl, ordering that she be given something to eat.

Chapter 6 body

In Chapter 6, Jesus teaches in his own country's synagogue, where people take offense at him and he can heal only a few sick people. He sends the twelve out two by two with authority over unclean spirits, and they preach repentance, cast out demons, and anoint and heal the sick. King Herod, hearing of Jesus, believes John the Baptizer has risen; the text recounts how Herod, at his birthday supper, was pressed by an oath to have John beheaded at the request of Herodias and her daughter. The apostles return, Jesus takes them to a deserted place, and he feeds five thousand men with five loaves and two fish. Jesus then walks on the sea to his straining disciples and calms the wind, and at Gennesaret the people bring the sick to him to touch his garment and be made well.

Chapter 7 body

Pharisees and scribes from Jerusalem fault Jesus' disciples for eating with unwashed hands, breaking the tradition of the elders. Jesus answers by quoting Isaiah, accusing them of hypocrisy, and showing that through the Corban practice they set aside God's commandment to honor father and mother. He then teaches the multitude and, privately, his disciples that what enters a man cannot defile him, but evil things proceeding from the heart do. Jesus goes to the borders of Tyre and Sidon, where a Greek Syrophoenician woman begs him to cast a demon out of her daughter; after her reply about the dogs and the crumbs, he tells her the demon has gone, and she finds the child on the bed. Returning to the sea of Galilee through Decapolis, Jesus heals a deaf man with a speech impediment, and though told to be silent, the people proclaim it and are astonished.

Chapter 8 body

Jesus has compassion on a hungry multitude of about four thousand and feeds them with seven loaves and a few small fish, leaving seven baskets of leftovers. He then crosses to Dalmanutha, where the Pharisees ask for a sign from heaven and he refuses, and in the boat he warns his disciples about the yeast of the Pharisees and Herod, rebuking their misunderstanding by recalling the two feedings. At Bethsaida he heals a blind man in two stages. Near Caesarea Philippi Peter declares Jesus is the Christ, Jesus foretells his suffering, death and resurrection, rebukes Peter for objecting, and calls the multitude and his disciples to deny themselves, take up their cross and follow him.

Chapter 9 body

In Mark 9, Jesus takes Peter, James, and John up a high mountain, where he is changed in appearance, Elijah and Moses appear, and a voice from a cloud calls him the beloved Son. Coming down, he tells them to keep silent until the Son of Man has risen, and he explains that Elijah has already come. Jesus then casts a mute spirit out of a boy whom his disciples could not help, after the boy's father cries out, "I believe. Help my unbelief!" Passing through Galilee, Jesus twice foretells his death and resurrection, but the disciples do not understand. They argue about who is greatest, and Jesus teaches them about being last and servant of all, receiving little children, not forbidding others who act in his name, and avoiding causes of stumbling.

Chapter 10 body

In Mark 10, Jesus travels into Judea and beyond the Jordan, where Pharisees test him about divorce and he answers by pointing to God's creation of male and female and the union of husband and wife. He receives little children over the disciples' objection, then tells a man with great possessions who asks about eternal life to sell everything and follow him, and the man goes away sorrowful. Jesus teaches his disciples about the difficulty of riches entering God's Kingdom and for the third time predicts his condemnation, death, and rising on the way to Jerusalem. James and John ask for seats at his right and left, which prompts Jesus to teach that greatness means serving. At Jericho he restores the sight of the blind beggar Bartimaeus, who then follows him.

Chapter 11 body

Near Jerusalem, Jesus sends two disciples to fetch a young donkey, rides it into the city while crowds spread garments and branches and cry out "Hosanna," and enters the temple before going out to Bethany with the twelve. The next day he curses a leafy fig tree that has no fruit, then returns to the temple, throws out buyers and sellers, overturns the money changers' tables, and declares it was meant to be a house of prayer but has become a den of robbers. The chief priests and scribes seek to destroy him, fearing the astonished multitude. The next morning the disciples find the fig tree withered, and Jesus teaches about faith, prayer, and forgiveness. Back in the temple, the chief priests, scribes, and elders challenge his authority, and he counters by asking whether John's baptism was from heaven or from men; they refuse to answer, so he refuses to tell them his authority.

Chapter 12 body

Jesus tells a parable of a man who planted a vineyard and rented it to farmers who beat and killed his servants and finally killed his beloved son; Jesus says the lord of the vineyard will destroy them and give the vineyard to others, and quotes Scripture about the rejected stone. The leaders perceive the parable is against them and, after sending Pharisees and Herodians to trap him over paying taxes to Caesar, Jesus answers 'Give to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's.' The Sadducees, who deny the resurrection, pose a question about a woman who married seven brothers, and Jesus answers that they err in not knowing the Scriptures or God's power. A scribe asks which commandment is greatest, and Jesus names loving the Lord God and loving one's neighbor, telling the scribe he is not far from God's Kingdom. Jesus then questions how the Christ can be David's son, warns against the scribes, and praises a poor widow who gave two small coins into the treasury as giving more than the rich.

Chapter 13 body

As Jesus leaves the temple, a disciple admires its stones and buildings, and Jesus predicts that not one stone will be left on another. On the Mount of Olives, Peter, James, John, and Andrew privately ask when this will happen and what the sign will be. Jesus warns them about deceivers, wars, earthquakes, famines, persecution before councils and rulers, family betrayal, and hatred for his name, while the Good News is preached to all nations. He describes the abomination of desolation, a time of unparalleled oppression that is shortened for the chosen ones, and false christs and prophets, followed by cosmic signs and the coming of the Son of Man, who gathers the chosen ones. He teaches the fig tree parable, says no one but the Father knows the day or hour, and closes with the parable of a traveling man, urging everyone to watch.

Chapter 14 body

Two days before the Passover, the chief priests and scribes plot to seize and kill Jesus, while a woman anoints him at Bethany and Judas Iscariot goes to the chief priests to betray him. Jesus eats the Passover meal with the twelve, predicts that one of them will betray him and that all will stumble, and gives them the bread and the cup as his body and blood of the new covenant. At Gethsemane he prays in distress while Peter, James, and John fall asleep, and Judas arrives with an armed crowd and identifies Jesus with a kiss, after which the disciples flee. Jesus is taken to the high priest, where false testimony fails to agree, and he answers that he is the Christ and is condemned for blasphemy. Meanwhile Peter, as Jesus predicted, denies him three times before the rooster crows a second time, and weeps.

Chapter 15 body

In the morning the chief priests, elders, scribes and council bind Jesus and deliver him to Pilate, who questions him about being King of the Jews while Jesus gives little answer. Pilate, perceiving the priests' envy but wishing to please the crowd, releases the insurgent Barabbas and hands Jesus over to be flogged and crucified. The soldiers mock Jesus with a purple cloak and crown of thorns, then lead him out, compelling Simon of Cyrene to carry his cross to Golgotha. Jesus is crucified between two robbers, mocked by passersby, priests and the robbers, and after darkness falls he cries out and dies, the temple veil tears, and the centurion declares him the Son of God. Joseph of Arimathaea asks Pilate for the body, wraps it in linen and lays it in a rock-cut tomb, while Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses watch.

Chapter 16 body

After the Sabbath, Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James, and Salome bring spices to anoint Jesus and find the stone rolled away from the tomb. A young man in a white robe tells them Jesus has risen and instructs them to tell his disciples and Peter that he goes before them into Galilee. The women flee in fear and say nothing to anyone. Jesus appears first to Mary Magdalene, then to two others on a walk, and finally to the eleven, whom he rebukes for unbelief before commissioning them to preach the Good News to all creation and describing signs that will accompany believers. Jesus is received up into heaven and sits at God's right hand, and the disciples go out and preach everywhere.
