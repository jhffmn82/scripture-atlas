Abstract written by a model

This document is a five-chapter letter from Paul, Silvanus, and Timothy to the assembly of the Thessalonians, a young community of believers. It combines thanksgiving, personal recollection, pastoral exhortation, and teaching about the Lord's return. Chapter 1 opens with greeting and thanks to God for the Thessalonians' work of faith, labor of love, and perseverance of hope. The Good News came to them in power and the Holy Spirit. They imitated the writers and the Lord, received the word amid affliction with joy, and became an example across Macedonia and Achaia. Others report how they turned from idols to serve a living God and await his Son Jesus from heaven. Chapter 2 recalls the writers' visit. Despite suffering at Philippi, they spoke boldly and without flattery, greed, or desire for glory. They were gentle like a nursing mother and exhorting like a father, and they worked night and day to avoid burdening anyone. The believers accepted the message as God's word and suffered from their own countrymen, as the assemblies in Judea did. The writers longed to see them again, but Satan hindered them. They call the believers their hope, joy, and crown. Chapter 3 explains that, unable to bear the separation, the writers stayed in Athens and sent Timothy to strengthen and comfort the believers, fearing the tempter had undone their labor. Timothy returned with good news of the believers' faith and love, which comforted the writers. They give thanks, pray night and day to see them, and ask that the Lord make the believers increase in love and be blameless in holiness at Jesus' coming. Chapter 4 exhorts the believers to please God and be sanctified: avoid sexual immorality, control their bodies honorably, not wrong a brother or sister, grow in brotherly love, live quietly, and work with their own hands. It then addresses those who have fallen asleep: believers should not grieve without hope. The Lord will descend with a shout and God's trumpet, the dead in Christ will rise first, and the living will be caught up with them to meet the Lord. They are to comfort one another with these words. Chapter 5 says the day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night, bringing sudden destruction on those saying "Peace and safety." The believers, children of light, are to stay awake and sober, wearing faith, love, and the hope of salvation. God appointed them to salvation through Christ, who died so they may live with him. A series of exhortations follows: respect leaders, be at peace, support the weak, rejoice always, pray without ceasing, give thanks, and test all things. The letter closes with a prayer for complete sanctification, a request for prayer, greetings, a command to read it to all, and a blessing of grace.

1 Thessalonians / Chapter 1 body

Paul, Silvanus, and Timothy greet the assembly of the Thessalonians in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, wishing them grace and peace. They give thanks to God for the Thessalonians, remembering their work of faith, labor of love, and perseverance of hope. The writers know the Thessalonians are chosen because the Good News came to them in power, in the Holy Spirit, and with much assurance. The Thessalonians imitated the writers and the Lord, received the word amid affliction with joy, and became an example to believers in Macedonia and Achaia. Their faith has become known everywhere, and others report how they turned from idols to serve a living and true God and wait for his Son from heaven, Jesus, who delivers from the wrath to come.

Chapter 2 body

In this chapter the speaker, writing as "we" and naming Paul once, reminds the Thessalonian believers that their visit was not in vain. Despite earlier suffering and shameful treatment at Philippi, the group boldly told them the Good News of God, without flattery, greed, or seeking glory, and behaved gently like a nursing mother and exhorting like a father. They worked night and day so as not to burden anyone. The writer thanks God that the believers accepted the message as God's word, and notes they suffered from their own countrymen just as the assemblies in Judea suffered from the Jews, who are described as having killed the Lord Jesus and the prophets and driven the group out. The group longed to see the believers again, Paul tried more than once but Satan hindered them, and the believers are called their hope, joy, crown of rejoicing, and glory.

Chapter 3 body

The authors explain that, unable to bear their separation any longer, they stayed behind at Athens alone and sent Timothy, their brother and God's servant in the Good News of Christ, to establish and comfort the Thessalonian believers in their faith. They recall having warned the believers beforehand that affliction was coming, and say they feared the tempter had tempted them and made their labor vain. Timothy has now returned with glad news of the believers' faith, love, and good memories of the authors, which comforts the authors in all their distress and affliction. The authors express thanksgiving and joy before God, pray night and day to see the believers and to perfect what is lacking in their faith, and ask God and the Lord Jesus to direct their way to them. They close by praying that the Lord will make the believers increase in love and establish their hearts blameless in holiness at the coming of Jesus with all his saints.

Chapter 4 body

In Chapter 4, the writers exhort the Thessalonian brothers to keep walking in the way they were taught so as to please God, and to abound more and more. They state that God's will is their sanctification, including abstaining from sexual immorality, controlling their own bodies in honor, and not wronging a brother or sister, since the Lord is an avenger in these matters. They commend the believers' brotherly love toward all the brothers in Macedonia while urging them to abound further, lead a quiet life, mind their own business, and work with their own hands so they walk properly before outsiders and need nothing. The writers then address those who have fallen asleep, telling the believers not to grieve like those without hope, because God will bring with him those who have fallen asleep in Jesus. They describe the Lord descending with a shout, the archangel's voice and God's trumpet, the dead in Christ rising first, and the living being caught up together with them to meet the Lord in the air, and they close by telling the believers to comfort one another with these words.

Chapter 5 body

Chapter 5 addresses the brothers about the times and seasons, reminding them that the day of the Lord comes like a thief in the night, bringing sudden destruction on those who say "Peace and safety." The writer contrasts the brothers, who are children of light and of the day, with those of the night, and urges them to stay awake and sober, wearing faith, love, and the hope of salvation as armor. God has appointed them to obtain salvation through the Lord Jesus Christ, who died for them so that they may live with him whether awake or asleep. The letter then gives a series of exhortations: respect those who labor among them, be at peace, admonish the disorderly, support the weak, do not return evil for evil, rejoice always, pray without ceasing, give thanks, and test all things. It closes with a prayer that the God of peace sanctify them completely, a request for prayer, greetings, a command that the letter be read to all, and a blessing of grace.
