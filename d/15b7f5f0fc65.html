Numbers

author unknown · 1439 to 1399 BC · 37 sections
Numbers, from the World English Bible, Catholic Edition, dedicated to the public domain by eBible.org. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://ebible.org/eng-web-c/

Abstract written by a model

This is the Book of Numbers, a narrative and legal record of Israel's organization at Sinai and forty years of wilderness travel to the edge of Canaan. Moses, Aaron and later Eleazar and Joshua lead under the LORD's direct commands. In the first part, at Sinai, Moses counts the fighting men (603,550), arranges the camps around the Tent of Meeting by tribal standards, and sets apart the Levites in place of the firstborn, assigning the clans of Gershon, Kohath and Merari their tabernacle duties. Laws on uncleanness, restitution, jealousy, Nazirite vows and the priestly blessing follow, then the princes' dedication offerings, the cleansing of the Levites, the Passover, and the guiding cloud and silver trumpets. The journey then turns to repeated rebellion. The people complain, crave meat and suffer a plague. Miriam and Aaron oppose Moses, and Miriam is struck with leprosy. After twelve spies explore Canaan, only Caleb and Joshua urge conquest. The people's refusal brings the decree that the adult generation will die in the wilderness over forty years. Korah's revolt ends with the earth swallowing the rebels and a plague, and Aaron's budding rod confirms the priesthood. Further rules cover offerings, the red heifer and tithes. Near the end of the wanderings Miriam and Aaron die, and Moses, having struck the rock, is barred from entering the land. Israel defeats Arad, Sihon and Og, and a bronze serpent heals those bitten by snakes. In Moab, Balak hires Balaam to curse Israel, but Balaam, after his donkey speaks, blesses Israel three times and foretells a star from Jacob. Israel then sins with Baal Peor, and Phinehas stops the plague and receives a covenant of peace. A second census (601,730) shows that only Caleb and Joshua survive from the first generation. The final chapters prepare for settlement. Zelophehad's daughters win inheritance rights, Joshua is commissioned to succeed Moses, and festival offerings and vow laws are set out. Israel avenges itself on Midian, Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh settle east of the Jordan, and the journey's stages are recounted. The LORD gives the land's borders, appoints men to divide it, assigns Levite cities and cities of refuge, and rules that daughters' inheritances must stay within their tribe.

Numbers front matter

Chapter 1 body

In the wilderness of Sinai, on the first day of the second month of the second year after leaving Egypt, the LORD commands Moses and Aaron to count every male of the children of Israel aged twenty and upward who is able to go to war, by families and fathers' houses. A man who heads his fathers' house is named from each tribe to stand with them, and the census is carried out as commanded. The tribe-by-tribe totals are given, from Manasseh's thirty-two thousand two hundred to Judah's seventy-four thousand six hundred, and the whole count comes to six hundred three thousand five hundred fifty. The Levites are excluded from the count and appointed instead to care for, carry, take down, set up, and encamp around the Tabernacle of the Testimony, so that no wrath falls on the congregation. The other tribes are to camp each by their own standard, and the children of Israel do all that the LORD commanded.

Chapter 2 body

The LORD instructs Moses and Aaron on how the children of Israel are to camp and travel around the Tent of Meeting, each man by his own standard and his fathers' house. Four camps of three tribes each are assigned to the east (Judah with Issachar and Zebulun), south (Reuben with Simeon and Gad), west (Ephraim with Manasseh and Benjamin), and north (Dan with Asher and Naphtali), each with a named prince and a counted total. The camps set out in the order Judah, Reuben, the Tent of Meeting with the Levites in the middle, Ephraim, and Dan last. The total counted of all the camps is 603,550, while the Levites are not counted among the children of Israel. The children of Israel do all that the LORD commanded Moses.

Chapter 3 body

Chapter 3 lists the sons of Aaron (Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, Ithamar), noting that Nadab and Abihu died childless for offering strange fire. The LORD tells Moses to give the tribe of Levi to Aaron and his sons to serve the tabernacle, taking the Levites in place of Israel's firstborn, who are the LORD's because he struck down Egypt's firstborn. Moses counts the Levites by their three clans, Gershon, Kohath, and Merari, assigning each a camp position and tabernacle duties, for a total of twenty-two thousand males from a month old. Moses then counts Israel's firstborn males, finding twenty-two thousand two hundred seventy-three, and collects five shekels each for the two hundred seventy-three who exceed the Levites, a total of one thousand three hundred sixty-five shekels, which he gives to Aaron and his sons.

Chapter 4 body

In Numbers 4, the LORD instructs Moses and Aaron to count the Levite clans of Kohath, Gershon, and Merari, aged thirty to fifty, who are eligible for service in the Tent of Meeting. The Kohathites are assigned to carry the most holy things after Aaron and his sons have covered them, and they must not touch or see them lest they die; Eleazar oversees the oil, incense, meal offering, and anointing oil. The Gershonites carry the curtains, coverings, screens, hangings, and cords under Ithamar, while the Merarites carry the boards, bars, pillars, sockets, pins, and cords, also under Ithamar. Moses, Aaron, and the princes count 2,750 Kohathites, 2,630 Gershonites, and 3,200 Merarites, totaling 8,580. Each man is counted and assigned according to his service and burden, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Chapter 5 body

In Numbers 5 the LORD gives Moses three commands for the children of Israel. First, lepers, those with discharges, and those unclean by a corpse, male and female, are to be put outside the camp so they do not defile the LORD's dwelling place, and the people do so. Second, anyone who trespasses against the LORD must confess, make full restitution plus a fifth, and give it to the wronged party or, if there is no kinsman, to the priest, along with a ram of atonement; holy gifts brought to the priest belong to him. Third, the law of jealousy provides that a husband who suspects his wife of unfaithfulness brings her to the priest, who sets her before the LORD with a barley meal offering and has her swear an oath and drink the water of bitterness; if guilty she will swell and her thigh fall away, and if innocent she will be free and conceive. The husband is free from iniquity, while the wife bears hers.

Chapter 6 body

The LORD instructs Moses to tell the children of Israel the rules for a Nazirite vow, which a man or woman may take to separate themselves to the LORD. The Nazirite must abstain from wine, grape products, cutting the hair, and contact with dead bodies, even of close family. If someone dies suddenly nearby, the Nazirite is defiled and must shave the head, bring birds and a lamb as offerings, and restart the days of separation. When the vow period ends, the Nazirite brings a set of offerings to the Tent of Meeting, shaves the head and burns the hair, and may then drink wine. The LORD then tells Moses to have Aaron and his sons bless the children of Israel with a three-part blessing, putting his name on them.

Chapter 7 body

On the day Moses finished setting up and anointing the tabernacle, its furniture, and the altar, the princes of Israel, heads of the tribes, brought six covered wagons and twelve oxen before the tabernacle. At the LORD's command Moses gave them to the Levites according to their service: two wagons and four oxen to the sons of Gershon, four wagons and eight oxen to the sons of Merari under Ithamar son of Aaron, and none to the sons of Kohath, who carried the sanctuary on their shoulders. The LORD directed that each prince offer on his own day for the altar's dedication, beginning with Nahshon son of Amminadab of Judah and continuing through twelve days to Ahira son of Enan of Naphtali. Each prince gave the identical offering of a silver platter, silver bowl, golden ladle, burnt, sin, and peace offerings, and the chapter totals them. It ends with Moses entering the Tent of Meeting and hearing the LORD's voice from above the mercy seat between the two cherubim.

Chapter 8 body

The LORD instructs Moses to tell Aaron to light the seven lamps in front of the lamp stand, and Aaron does so; the lamp stand is described as beaten gold made according to the pattern the LORD showed Moses. The LORD then commands Moses to cleanse the Levites by sprinkling water, shaving, and washing clothes, and to offer bulls as a meal offering, sin offering, and burnt offering. The children of Israel lay hands on the Levites, and Aaron offers them as a wave offering so they may serve the LORD in place of the firstborn of Israel, whom the LORD sanctified when he struck Egypt's firstborn. Moses, Aaron, and the congregation carry out these commands, and the Levites then serve in the Tent of Meeting before Aaron and his sons. The LORD finally sets the Levites' service age from twenty-five years, with retirement at fifty, after which they assist their brothers but perform no service.

Chapter 9 body

In the wilderness of Sinai, in the first month of the second year after leaving Egypt, the LORD tells Moses that the children of Israel are to keep the Passover at its appointed time, and they do so on the fourteenth day at evening. Certain men made unclean by a dead body could not keep it and ask Moses and Aaron why they are kept back, and Moses waits to hear from the LORD. The LORD answers by establishing a second-month Passover on the fourteenth day for those unclean or on a distant journey, warns that a clean man who is not traveling and fails to keep it will be cut off, and allows foreigners to keep it under the same statute. The chapter then describes how the cloud covered the tabernacle, appearing as fire at night, and how the children of Israel traveled or encamped according to whether the cloud lifted or remained, for any length of time, at the LORD's commandment by Moses.

Chapter 10 body

In Numbers 10, the LORD instructs Moses to make two beaten silver trumpets to be blown by the sons of Aaron for assembling the congregation, directing the camps' journeys, going to war, and marking feasts and sacrifices. In the second year, second month, on the twentieth day, the cloud lifts from the tabernacle and Israel departs the wilderness of Sinai, with the tribes setting out in order under their named leaders, beginning with Judah and ending with Dan as rear guard. Moses urges Hobab, son of Reuel the Midianite, to come with them as their eyes in the wilderness, but Hobab says he will return to his own land and relatives. The ark of the LORD's covenant goes ahead three days' journey from the Mount of the LORD to seek a resting place, with the cloud over the people by day. Moses speaks a prayer whenever the ark sets out and whenever it rests.

Chapter 11 body

In Numbers 11 the people of Israel complain, and the LORD's fire burns the outskirts of the camp until Moses prays; the place is named Taberah. The mixed multitude and the Israelites then crave meat, longing for the food of Egypt and disdaining the manna. Moses, overwhelmed, tells the LORD the burden is too heavy and asks to die, so the LORD has him gather seventy elders and puts some of the Spirit on Moses onto them, and they prophesy. Eldad and Medad prophesy in the camp, Joshua asks Moses to forbid them, and Moses says he wishes all the LORD's people were prophets. The LORD sends a wind that brings quails, but while the people are still eating, he strikes them with a plague, and the place is named Kibroth Hattaavah before the people move on to Hazeroth.

Chapter 12 body

Miriam and Aaron speak against Moses because of the Cushite woman he had married, asking whether the LORD has spoken only through Moses. The LORD hears them, summons all three to the Tent of Meeting, and declares that Moses is faithful in all his house and is spoken to mouth to mouth, unlike other prophets. The LORD's anger burns against Miriam and Aaron, and Miriam becomes leprous, as white as snow. Aaron confesses their foolish sin to Moses, and Moses cries to the LORD to heal her. The LORD orders Miriam shut outside the camp for seven days, and the people do not travel until she is brought back, after which they move from Hazeroth to the wilderness of Paran.

Chapter 13 body

The LORD instructs Moses to send one leader from each tribe to spy out the land of Canaan, and Moses sends twelve named men from the wilderness of Paran, renaming Hoshea son of Nun as Joshua. Moses tells them to observe the land, its people, cities, and fertility, and to bring back fruit. The spies travel from the wilderness of Zin to Rehob and Hebron, cut a huge cluster of grapes at the valley of Eshcol, and return after forty days to Moses, Aaron, and the congregation at Kadesh. They report that the land flows with milk and honey but that its people are strong, its cities fortified, and the children of Anak live there. Caleb urges immediate conquest, but the other spies give an evil report, claiming the land devours its inhabitants and that they felt like grasshoppers beside the Nephilim.

Chapter 14 body

After the spies' report, the whole congregation of the children of Israel weeps and murmurs against Moses and Aaron, wishing to die in Egypt or choose a leader to return there. Joshua and Caleb tear their clothes and urge the people not to rebel or fear the people of the land, but the congregation threatens to stone them. The LORD threatens to strike the people with pestilence, but Moses intercedes by appealing to the LORD's reputation and his own words about loving kindness, and the LORD pardons yet decrees that the adult generation will die in the wilderness over forty years, sparing only Caleb and Joshua. The spies who brought the evil report die by plague, and when the people then presume to go up the mountain against Moses' warning, the Amalekites and Canaanites defeat them as far as Hormah.

Chapter 15 body

In Numbers 15 the LORD gives Moses instructions for the children of Israel about life in the land they will enter. He specifies the meal and drink offerings that accompany lambs, rams, and bulls offered by fire, and declares one law for native-born Israelites and for the foreigner living among them. He commands a wave offering from the first of their dough, and provides sacrifices and atonement for unwitting sins, while the one who sins with a high hand is to be cut off. A man found gathering sticks on the Sabbath is held in custody, then stoned outside the camp by the whole congregation at the LORD's command. The chapter ends with the command to make fringes with a blue cord on garment borders so the people will remember and do all the LORD's commandments.

Chapter 16 body

Korah, a Levite, joins Dathan, Abiram, and On of Reuben and 250 princes of the congregation in challenging Moses and Aaron, claiming all the congregation is holy. Moses proposes a test with censers before the LORD, while Dathan and Abiram refuse to come and accuse him of leading them out of a land flowing with milk and honey to die in the wilderness. When Moses warns the people to move away from the rebels' tents, the ground splits and swallows Korah, Dathan, Abiram, and their households alive into Sheol, and fire from the LORD consumes the 250 men offering incense. Eleazar hammers the rebels' bronze censers into a covering for the altar as a memorial that no outsider to Aaron's offspring should burn incense. The next day the congregation complains that Moses and Aaron killed the LORD's people, and a plague kills 14,700 until Aaron, at Moses' direction, runs among them with incense and makes atonement.

Chapter 17 body

The LORD instructs Moses to collect twelve rods, one from each prince of the children of Israel, with Aaron's name written on Levi's rod, and to lay them before the covenant in the Tent of Meeting so that the rod of the chosen man will bud and the murmuring will cease. Moses does so, and the next day Aaron's rod has sprouted, budded, blossomed, and borne ripe almonds. Moses brings all the rods out, and each man takes his own. The LORD commands that Aaron's rod be put back before the covenant as a token against the rebellious, to end their complaining so they do not die. The children of Israel respond in fear, saying they perish and that everyone who approaches the LORD's tabernacle dies.

Chapter 18 body

The LORD addresses Aaron and his sons, making them responsible for bearing the iniquity of the sanctuary and the priesthood, while the Levites are joined to them to serve the Tent of Meeting but may not approach the sanctuary vessels or altar. The LORD gives Aaron, his sons, and his daughters the wave offerings, most holy things, first fruits, devoted things, and firstborn offerings as a perpetual portion, with firstborn humans and unclean animals to be redeemed for five shekels. Aaron is told he will have no land inheritance, since the LORD is his portion. The Levites receive all of Israel's tithe as their inheritance in return for their service, and the children of Israel are barred from approaching the Tent of Meeting lest they bear sin and die. Speaking to Moses, the LORD instructs that the Levites must offer a tithe of the tithe as a wave offering to Aaron the priest, after which the rest is theirs to eat anywhere without incurring sin.

Chapter 19 body

The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron and gives a statute for a red heifer without defect that has never been yoked. Eleazar the priest oversees its killing outside the camp, sprinkles its blood toward the Tent of Meeting seven times, and has it burned with cedar wood, hyssop, and scarlet. A clean man gathers the ashes and stores them outside the camp for use in water for cleansing impurity, and those who handle the ritual become unclean until evening. The chapter then sets out the rules for touching a dead body, dying in a tent, or touching a bone or grave: seven days of uncleanness, with purification on the third and seventh days by water mixed with the ashes. Anyone who does not purify himself defiles the LORD's sanctuary and is cut off from Israel.

Chapter 20 body

The Israelites arrive in the wilderness of Zin and stay at Kadesh, where Miriam dies and is buried. With no water, the people quarrel with Moses and Aaron, and the LORD tells Moses to take the rod and speak to the rock so it pours out water. Moses instead rebukes the people and strikes the rock twice; water comes out, but the LORD tells Moses and Aaron they will not bring the assembly into the promised land because they did not believe him. Moses asks the king of Edom for passage along the king's highway, but Edom refuses and comes out against Israel with a strong hand, so Israel turns away. At Mount Hor the LORD announces Aaron's death, Moses transfers Aaron's garments to Eleazar, Aaron dies on the mountain, and the whole house of Israel mourns him thirty days.

Chapter 21 body

In Numbers 21, Israel vows to destroy the cities of the Canaanite king of Arad after he takes some of them captive, and the LORD delivers the Canaanites to them, so the place is named Hormah. While traveling around Edom, the people complain against God and Moses, and the LORD sends venomous snakes that kill many; when the people confess, Moses prays and makes a bronze serpent on a pole that heals those who look at it. Israel continues its journey through several camps to Beer, where the LORD provides water and the people sing a song to the well. Israel asks Sihon king of the Amorites for passage, he refuses and attacks, and Israel defeats him and settles in Heshbon and the Amorite cities. Israel then takes Jazer's villages and defeats Og king of Bashan at Edrei after the LORD tells Moses not to fear him.

Chapter 22 body

Israel encamps in the plains of Moab, and Balak, king of Moab, fears them after seeing what they did to the Amorites. He sends elders of Moab and Midian with rewards of divination to summon Balaam the son of Beor to curse Israel. God first forbids Balaam to go and says Israel is blessed, but after a second, more honorable delegation God allows him to go if he speaks only God's word. On the way the LORD's angel blocks Balaam three times with a drawn sword, seen first only by Balaam's donkey, which the LORD enables to speak; Balaam then sees the angel, confesses sin, and is told to go on and speak only what he is given. Balak meets Balaam, offers sacrifices, and takes him up to the high places of Baal, where Balaam sees part of the people.

Chapter 23 body

Balaam instructs Balak to build seven altars and offer a bull and a ram on each, then goes to a bare height where God meets him and puts a word in his mouth. Returning to Balak and the princes of Moab, Balaam declares he cannot curse whom God has not cursed, and instead praises Israel as a numerous people dwelling alone. Balak protests, then takes Balaam to the top of Pisgah in the field of Zophim, where the altars and offerings are repeated and Balaam again speaks the LORD's word: God does not lie or repent, has commanded a blessing, and sees no iniquity in Jacob. Balak tells him neither to curse nor bless, but Balaam insists he must do all the LORD speaks. Balak then takes him to the top of Peor, where Balaam again asks for seven altars, seven bulls and seven rams, and Balak complies.

Chapter 24 body

Balaam, seeing that the LORD is pleased to bless Israel, stops using divination and looks toward the wilderness, where he sees Israel camped by tribes and is seized by the Spirit of God. He delivers a parable praising Israel's tents, its strength, its king, and its safety, declaring that those who bless Israel are blessed and those who curse it are cursed. Balak angrily strikes his hands together, rebukes Balaam for blessing his enemies three times, and tells him to flee, while Balaam answers that he can only say what the LORD says. Balaam then gives further parables foretelling a star and scepter from Jacob and Israel that will crush Moab, Edom, and Seir, and pronounces destruction on Amalek, the Kenite, Asshur, and Eber. Balaam and Balak then each go their own way.

Chapter 25 body

While Israel stayed in Shittim, the people took part in sacrifices to the gods of the daughters of Moab and joined themselves to Baal Peor, provoking the LORD's anger. The LORD told Moses to hang the chiefs, and Moses told the judges to kill those who had joined Baal Peor. When Zimri, a Simeonite prince, brought the Midianite woman Cozbi before Moses and the congregation, Phinehas, son of Eleazar, speared them both, which stopped the plague that had killed twenty-four thousand. The LORD then gave Phinehas a covenant of peace and an everlasting priesthood for his offspring because he was jealous for his God and made atonement. The LORD also commanded Moses to harass and strike the Midianites for deceiving Israel in the matter of Peor and Cozbi.

Chapter 26 body

After the plague, the LORD tells Moses and Eleazar the priest to take a census of Israelite men twenty years old and upward who can go to war, on the plains of Moab by the Jordan at Jericho. The chapter lists each tribe's families and counts, including Reuben, Simeon, Gad, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun, Manasseh, Ephraim, Benjamin, Dan, Asher, and Naphtali, for a total of 601,730. The LORD directs that the land be divided by lot among the tribes, with larger tribes receiving more inheritance and smaller ones less. The Levites are counted separately, 23,000 males from a month old and upward, because they receive no inheritance among the Israelites. The chapter ends by noting that none of those counted earlier at Sinai remained, except Caleb son of Jephunneh and Joshua son of Nun, because the LORD had said they would die in the wilderness.

Chapter 27 body

The five daughters of Zelophehad (Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah) come before Moses, Eleazar the priest, the princes and the congregation to ask for a possession among their father's brothers, since he died without sons. Moses brings the case to the LORD, who affirms they speak rightly and gives a statute for the inheritance of those who die without sons, passing it to daughters, then brothers, then father's brothers, then the nearest kinsman. The LORD then tells Moses to go up Mount Abarim to see the land, and that he will be gathered to his people as Aaron was, because he rebelled at the waters of Meribah of Kadesh in the wilderness of Zin. Moses asks the LORD to appoint a man over the congregation so it will not be like sheep without a shepherd. The LORD names Joshua the son of Nun, and Moses commissions him before Eleazar and the congregation by laying his hands on him.

Chapter 28 body

In Numbers 28 the LORD speaks to Moses and commands him to instruct the children of Israel to present the LORD's offerings made by fire in their due season. The chapter sets out the continual daily burnt offering of two year-old male lambs, one in the morning and one at evening, with fine flour, oil and a drink offering. It then specifies larger offerings for the Sabbath and for the beginning of each month, the latter including bulls, a ram, lambs and a male goat as a sin offering. Finally it lays out the offerings for the Passover on the fourteenth day of the first month, the seven-day feast of unleavened bread starting on the fifteenth, and the day of the first fruits at the feast of weeks, each with holy convocations, no regular work on the key days, and offerings that are in addition to the continual burnt offering.

Chapter 29 body

Chapter 29 sets out the offerings the LORD commands for the holy days of the seventh month. On the first day there is a holy convocation and a day of blowing of trumpets, with a burnt offering of one bull, one ram and seven lambs plus a goat for a sin offering. On the tenth day the people are to afflict their souls and do no work, offering the same animals and a sin offering in addition to the sin offering of atonement. From the fifteenth day a seven-day feast is kept, with bulls decreasing daily from thirteen to seven, and a solemn assembly on the eighth day. The chapter ends with Moses telling the children of Israel all that the LORD commanded him.

Chapter 30 body

Moses addresses the heads of the tribes of Israel with the LORD's command about vows. A man who vows or swears an oath to the LORD must not break his word and must do all that comes out of his mouth. A young woman in her father's house, or a married woman, has her vows stand if her father or husband hears them and says nothing, but they are void if he forbids her on the day he hears. The vows of a widow or divorced woman stand against her, and a husband who nullifies a vow after the day he heard it bears her iniquity.

Chapter 31 body

The LORD commands Moses to avenge Israel on the Midianites, and Moses sends twelve thousand men, a thousand from each tribe, with Phinehas son of Eleazar the priest. They kill every male, including five Midianite kings and Balaam son of Beor, burn the cities and encampments, and bring captives and plunder back to the camp. Moses is angry that the women were kept alive and orders the killing of every male child and every woman who has known a man, while the girls who have not known a man are kept alive. Moses and Eleazar set out purification rules for the soldiers, captives, and goods, and the LORD directs how the plunder is divided between the soldiers and the congregation, with tribute set aside for Eleazar and the Levites. The officers report that not one man is missing and bring a gold offering of 16,750 shekels, which Moses and Eleazar place in the Tent of Meeting as a memorial.

Chapter 32 body

The children of Reuben and the children of Gad, who own very great multitudes of livestock, ask Moses, Eleazar the priest, and the princes to give them the land east of the Jordan, including Jazer and Gilead, and not bring them over the Jordan. Moses rebukes them, comparing them to their fathers who discouraged Israel at Kadesh Barnea and caused the LORD to make the people wander forty years. The two tribes promise to build folds and cities for their families and livestock but to go armed before the children of Israel until all have received their inheritance. Moses accepts on condition that they cross the Jordan armed and help subdue the land, otherwise they will receive possessions in Canaan, and he gives them, along with the half-tribe of Manasseh, the kingdoms of Sihon and Og. The tribes rebuild and rename cities, and Machir, Jair, and Nobah take Gilead and its villages.

Chapter 33 body

Numbers 33 records the stages of the children of Israel's journey from Rameses in Egypt to the plains of Moab by the Jordan at Jericho, which Moses wrote down at the LORD's command. It recalls that they left on the fifteenth day of the first month, the day after the Passover, while the Egyptians buried their firstborn and the LORD executed judgments on Egyptian gods. The list names encampments including Succoth, Marah, Elim, Rephidim, the wilderness of Sinai, Kadesh, and Mount Hor, where Aaron died at age 123 in the fortieth year. At the plains of Moab the LORD tells Moses to instruct Israel that, once across the Jordan into Canaan, they must drive out the inhabitants, destroy their idols and high places, and divide the land by lot. He warns that any inhabitants left in place will become pricks in their eyes and thorns in their sides, and that he will do to Israel what he planned to do to them.

Chapter 34 body

The LORD speaks to Moses and gives him the boundaries of the land of Canaan, which will fall to the children of Israel as an inheritance. The south, west, north, and east borders are described through a series of named landmarks, including the Salt Sea, the great sea, Mount Hor, Hazar Enan, and the Jordan. Moses relays to the people that the land is to be inherited by lot by the nine tribes and the half-tribe, since Reuben, Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh have already received their inheritance east of the Jordan. The LORD then names Eleazar the priest and Joshua the son of Nun to divide the land, together with one prince from each of the nine and a half tribes, and the chapter lists those princes by name.

Chapter 35 body

In the plains of Moab by the Jordan at Jericho, the LORD commands Moses to have the children of Israel give the Levites forty-eight cities with surrounding pasture lands, taken in proportion to each tribe's inheritance. Six of these are to be cities of refuge, three east of the Jordan and three in Canaan, where a man slayer who kills unintentionally may flee from the avenger of blood until the congregation judges him. The chapter distinguishes murder, committed with iron, stone, or wood weapons or out of hatred and ambush, from killing without hostility or intent. A man slayer must stay in his city of refuge until the high priest dies, and if he leaves, the avenger may kill him without guilt. Murderers must be executed on the testimony of more than one witness, no ransom is allowed, and the land, where the LORD dwells, is polluted by blood that only the blood of the shedder can atone for.

Chapter 36 body

The heads of the fathers' households of the family of the children of Gilead, of the tribe of Manasseh, approach Moses and the princes with a concern about the inheritance of Zelophehad's daughters. They argue that if the daughters marry men from other tribes, their land will pass to those tribes and be lost to Manasseh's lot, and it will stay lost even at the jubilee. Moses, speaking according to the LORD's word, affirms that the tribe of Joseph's sons speak rightly and commands that the daughters may marry whom they think best, but only within the family of their father's tribe. The rule is that no inheritance may move from tribe to tribe. The daughters of Zelophehad obey by marrying their father's brothers' sons, so their inheritance stays in the tribe of Manasseh, and the chapter closes by summarizing the commandments given in the plains of Moab.
