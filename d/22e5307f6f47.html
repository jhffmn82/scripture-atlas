Yasna

author unknown · 27 sections
Yasna, translated by James Darmesteter and L. H. Mills, Sacred Books of the East vols. 4, 23 and 31 (1880-1887), public domain. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://sacred-texts.com/zor/

Abstract written by a model

Yasna I–XXVII is a connected cycle of Mazdayasnian ritual worship, sacrifice, confession, offerings, and sacred commentary centered on Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Righteousness, the Fire, Haoma, the waters, plants, Fravashis, and the many Yazads. The speaker repeatedly declares allegiance to Mazda-worship and Zarathustra’s order, rejects the Daêvas and wicked powers, confesses possible failures of thought, word, and deed, and seeks to complete the Yasna rightly. The early units establish the ritual: approaching the Zaothra and Baresman; offering Haoma, Myazda, milk, water, wood, plants, meat, and prayers; honoring ritual lords, sacred times and places, and priestly authority shared by the Zaotar and Ratu. The rites are presented as benefiting homes, herds, people, descendants, settlements, and the righteous. They include blessings, protection, prosperity, health, victory, wisdom, and the repression of the wicked. Yasna IX–XI focus on H(a)oma: its revelation to Zarathustra, its preparers Vîvanghvant, Âthwya, Thrita, and Pourushaspa, its association with Yima, Thraêtaona, Keresâspa, and Zarathustra, and its powers of healing, victory, protection, and sacred exhilaration. Yasna XII gives the Mazdayasnian Confession, explicitly rejecting the Daêvas and affirming Mazda, Asha, the Kine, sacred creation, Zarathustra’s creed, and future Saoshyants. Later units continue dedications to Ahura Mazda, Zarathustra, the Faith, Fire, waters, mountains, sacred words, and the Fravashis of saints, including the dead, living, unborn, teachers, and children. Yasna XVIII petitions Mazda for Immortality, Weal, and Spirit bounteous. Yasna XIX–XXI shift into commentary on the Ahuna-vairya, Ashem Vohû, and Yênhê Hâtãm, explaining their righteousness, salvation, structure, recitation, and sacrificial use. The collection closes by renewing ritual offerings and honoring Fravashis, then affirming Ahura Mazda’s kingdom and the sacred power of the Ahuna-vairya and Asha Vahista against Angra Mainyu, Aêshma, and wicked Daêvas.

Yasna / Yasna I. body

Yasna I is a series of announcements, completions, sacrifices, homages, propitiations, and praises directed to Ahura Mazda and many holy beings and powers. It concerns the Good Mind, Righteousness the Best, the Fire of Ahura Mazda, Hâvani and other lords of the ritual order, Mithra, the Fravashis of the saints, and the Yazads. The speaker confesses himself a Mazda-worshipper of Zarathustra’s order and a foe to the Daêvas. He also confesses possible offenses by thought, word, or deed and makes up for any deficiency in his Yasna and homage.

Yasna II. body

The speaker repeatedly approaches the Zaothra and Baresman together with worship and praise. The unit invokes Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Mithra, the Fire, waters, plants, mountains, festivals, seasons, Fravashis, and all holy Yazads. It especially names the lords of the ritual order and their proper times, including Hâvani, Sâvanghi, Vîsya, and many others. Mithra is praised as having wide pastures, a thousand ears, and myriad eyes, while Ahura Mazda is praised as radiant and glorious and identified as the land-ruler.

Yasna III. body

Yasna III concerns the approach, praise, sacrifice, and propitiation of sacred beings, ritual objects, times, places, and creatures. It names offerings including the Baresman, Zaothra, Myazda, Haoma, Parahaoma, wood, water, milk, plants, and meat. It invokes Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Sraosha, the Fravashi of Spitâma Zarathustra, Fire, Mithra, Righteousness, and many other Yazads and ritual lords. It concludes with the Mazdayasnian confession and an exchange between the Zaotar and the Ratu concerning the proper priestly recitation.

Yasna IV. body

The unit concerns the offering of good thoughts, good words, good deeds, ritual substances, prayers, recitations, and praises through celebrations. These offerings are presented to Ahura Mazda, Sraosha, the Bountiful Immortals, the Fravashis of the saints, Fire, and many other holy beings and entities. The offerings are declared beneficial for the house, its herds, its people, and those yet to be born. The unit concludes by worshipping the Bountiful Immortals, the superior male and female beings known to Ahura Mazda, and all good Yazads fit for sacrifice, homage, propitiation, and praise.

Yasna V. body

Yasna V. consists only of a note stating that this chapter is identical with Yasna XXXVII. It concerns the relationship between Yasna V. and Yasna XXXVII. No other content or details are provided.

Yasna VI. body

Yasna VI continues the sacrifice with fuller expression by worshipping Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, the lords of the ritual order, and many divine beings and sacred things. It concerns Mithra, Fire (Ahura Mazda's son), Sraosha, the Fravashis of the saints, Mãthra Spenta, the Mazdayasnian Religion, Haoma, Zarathustra, waters, plants, lands, mountains, festivals, and celestial bodies. The text concludes with the Ratu and the Zaotar speaking about the Ratu from Righteousness.

Yasna VII. body

Yasna VII presents offerings and blessings made by the priest for Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Sraosha, the Fravashi of Zarathustra Spitâma, the Fire, the waters which are Mazda-made, and many other holy beings and sacred things. It names ritual offerings including the Myazda meat-offering, Haurvatât, Ameretatât, the Kine of blessed gift, Haoma, Haoma-juice, wood-billets, perfume, and the Baresman. It invokes the holy Yazads, the ritual lords of times and places, the Fravashis of the saints, celestial bodies, the Zarathustrian law, the good Religion, and the Mazdayasnian faith. The unit concludes with prayers for protection and reward, sacrifices to the Ahuna-vairya and other sacred prayers, and a declaration of sacrifice to righteous male and female beings.

Yasna VIII. body

The unit concerns the offering of the Myazda and the faithful partaking of it under the guidance of Righteousness. It presents Righteousness as the Best and calls for offerings to Ahura Mazda, Haoma, the waters, plants, and other sacred beings. The Ratu commands deserving men to eat, while Mazdayasnians are urged to practice and teach the Religion of Ahura. Zarathustra prays for the freedom of the holy and the repression of the wicked, and the Zaotar speaks before the Ratu.

Yasna IX. body

The Hôm Yast recounts H(a)oma’s revelation to Zarathustra and the blessings, powers, and protections associated with him. H(a)oma names Vîvanghvant, Âthwya, Thrita, and Pourushaspa as the first four men who prepared him for the corporeal world, describing the sons born to them, including Yima, Thraêtaona, Keresâspa, and Zarathustra. The unit recounts victories over dragons, tyrants, demon-gods, and other enemies, and asks H(a)oma for health, strength, prosperity, wisdom, victory, and protection. It also connects Mazda and the Mazdayasnian Faith with H(a)oma and invokes H(a)oma’s mace against forces that harm the righteous saints and settlements.

Yasna X. body

The unit concerns H(a)oma, its growth, praise, healing powers, and ritual use. It describes H(a)oma growing on mountains and being carried by birds to high peaks, where it appears in milky-white and golden forms. H(a)oma is said to bring victory, health, friendship, prosperity, and protection from curses and death. Zarathustra praises Mazda-made H(a)oma, its plants, teachings, and ritual words, and also praises Righteousness and the Kine.

Yasna XI. body

The cow, horse, and H(a)oma curse those who misuse or withhold them. An offering of tongue and left eye is said to have been chosen by Ahura for H(a)oma, and those who deny it face misfortune in their household. Zarathustra praises H(a)oma as Mazda-made and offers himself to H(a)oma and the sacred exhilaration it bestows. The speaker affirms the value of good thoughts, good words, good deeds, and Righteousness.

Yasna XII (Sp. XIII). body

Yasna XII (Sp. XIII), titled The Mazdayasnian Confession, presents a confession of allegiance to Mazda-worship and Zarathustra's order. It rejects the Daêvas, their shelter, headship, thoughts, words, actions, seed, sorcerers, and adherents. It praises Ahura Mazda, Piety, Asha, the Kine, the waters, plants, and the Mazdayasnian villages, while rejecting robbery, violence, drought, wandering, wasting, and withering. It affirms the religious sanctity, Faith of Mazda, and Zarathustrian creed associated with Zarathustra, Kavi Vîstâspa, Frashaostra, Gâmâspa, and future Saoshyants.

Yasna XIII (Sp. XIV). body

Yasna XIII invokes Ahura Mazda, the Mazdayasnian Faith, divine and human leaders, the earth, and other sacred beings. It offers worship, sacrifice, gifts, prayers, and confessions of debt to Ahura Mazda and the Bountiful Immortals. The unit recalls that the two spirits thought, spoke, and acted, and it associates approach to Ahura Mazda with good kinship, Righteousness, thrift-law, and Piety. It also worships several Fravashis and sacred figures, and concludes with worship of the Ahuna-vairya and Asha Vahista and sacrifice to Hâ fraoreti.

Yasna XIV (Sp. XV). body

Yasna XIV (Sp. XV) presents dedications to the Bountiful Immortals and the holy beings of the ritual order. The speaker offers praise, priestly service, recitation, sacrifice, homage, bodily flesh, and the blessings of a prospered life. The speaker approaches the holy Yazads and identifies as a Mazdayasnian of Zarathustra's order. The Zaotar and Ratu then speak about the Zaotar and the Ratu's righteous authority to speak forth.

Yasna XV (Sp. XVI). body

Yasna XV (Sp. XVI), titled The Sacrifice Continues, concerns the worship of the Bountiful Immortals and the continuation of the sacrifice. The speaker calls upon the Bountiful Immortals with precept, praise, delight, and blessings from the good ritual and Mazdayasnian Faith. The speaker worships those who have lived and continue to live by their names, while choosing the Good Kingdom. Sraosha (Obedience) is invited to be present for the sacrifice of Ahura Mazda, and the Âtarevakhsha calls for the Ratu to speak from righteousness.

Yasna XVI (Sp. XVII). body

This unit concerns the sacrifice and worship of Ahura Mazda, Zarathustra Spitâma, and many heavenly and earthly Yazads. Ahura Mazda is praised as the holy lord of the ritual order, creator of good creatures, and giver of beneficent gifts. Zarathustra Spitâma is worshipped along with his Fravashi, utterances, religion, faith, and lore. The dwelling-place is identified with the bountiful Âramaiti and is asked to provide a healthy home where helpful influences may remain through summer and winter.

Yasna XVII. body

This unit concerns worship of the Fire, waters, plants, sacred words, mountains, prayers, and holy beings. The Fire is honored as Ahura Mazda’s son and through several named fires, including Nairya-sangha. The Mazda-made and holy waters and plants are worshipped, along with Mãthra-spenta and the Zarathustrian law against the Daêvas. Mount Ushi-darena, other holy mountains, prayers, and the Fravashis of the saints are also praised, invoked, and glorified.

Yasna XVIII. body

The speaker asks Mazda, who is described as the maker of the Kine, plants, and waters, to grant Immortality. The speaker also asks Mazda to grant Weal and Spirit bounteous. The unit concerns Mazda and the requested gifts of Immortality, Weal, and Spirit bounteous.

Yasna XIX. body

Yasna XIX. presents Ahura Mazda’s commentary on the Ahuna-vairya, the sacred formula pronounced before creation and containing the seed of righteousness. Zarathustra asks about its origin, power, wording, and meaning, and Ahura Mazda explains its spiritual effects and structure. The text identifies its measures with good thought, good word, and good deed, and connects it with four classes and five chiefs. It concludes with a sacrifice to the parts, memorized recital, chanting, and use of the Ahuna-vairya in the full Yasna.

Yasna XX. body

Yasna XX. presents commentary on the Ashem Vohû, identifying Righteousness as the best blessing and connecting it with weal. It attributes the prayer’s qualities and words to Asha and Ahura Mazda, and explains how the worshipper ascribes the Mãthra and righteousness to Asha Vahista and the prophets. A catechetical addition describes Mazda’s announcement of a holy one who is heavenly and earthly, and of a holy, best man who rules without capricious tyranny. The passage concludes with sacrifices to the parts, recital, memorising, chanting, and sacrificial use of the Asha Vahista prayer.

Yasna XXI. body

Yasna XXI. is a Catechetical Zand, or commentary upon the Yênhê Hâtãm. It explains that the Yasna and sacrificial worship are offered to Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, and all holy beings, including male and female saints. It presents questions and answers about salvation, stating that Ahura grants salvation and that Mazda, the best One, answers with the best and holy answer. It identifies the YêNhê hâtãm as a prominent and holy Yast.

Yasna XXII. body

The Sacrifice continues with offerings and praise directed to Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, and other holy beings and things. The Baresman, Zaothras, Haoma, fresh milk, Hadhânaêpata, Haoma-water, mortars, wood, and perfume are approached with praise and sanctity. The rites seek the propitiation of Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Fire, the beneficent waters, the Mazdayasnian Faith, Mãthra Spenta, and many other sacred powers. The unit also invokes the Gâthas, prayers for blessings, the memorised recital and fulfilment of the Mazdayasnian law, and all good Mazda-made objects.

Yasna XXIII. body

This unit concerns the Fravashis of the Saints and prayers for approaching them with praise and worship. It names the Fravashis of houses, villages, communities, provinces, Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Gaya Maretan, Zarathustra Spitâma, Kavi Vîstâspa, Isat-vâstra, and other holy persons. The Fravashis are said to hold heaven, water, land, cattle, and children in the wombs safely apart. The speaker also approaches the Fravashi of his own soul and all good Yazads because of Righteousness the Best.

Yasna XXIV. body

Yasna XXIV. presents Haomas, Zaothras, the Baresman, Hadhânaêpata, food, milk, ritual objects, prayers, recollection, and other Mazda-made objects in worship. These are presented to Ahura Mazda, the good waters, the Fire of Ahura Mazda, Sraosha (Obedience), the Bountiful Immortals, and the Fravashis and souls of the saints. The offerings also concern Zarathustra Spitâma, people who love Righteousness, unborn men, future prophets, and the progress and renovation of the world. The unit associates the offerings with sacrifice, homage, propitiation, praise, and the seed of Righteousness.

Yasna XXV. body

The unit concerns worship offered to Ahura Mazda, the Bountiful Immortals, Haoma, Fire, the good Mazdayasnian Religion, the good Mazdayasnian law, and the Mãthra Spenta. It describes ritual objects and substances, including Haoma, waters, mortars, Baresman, prayers, Gâthas, and perfumed wood-billets. It praises Mazda-made and holy knowledge, law, fire, winds, mountains, and Yazads. It also identifies the Mãthra Spenta with the law pronounced against the Daêvas, the Zarathustrian law, and Mazda-made understanding.

Yasna XXVI. body

The unit praises, invokes, and worships the good, heroic, and bountiful Fravashis of the saints and related holy beings. It specifically honors the Fravashi of Ahura Mazda, the Fravashis of named holy figures, and the Fravashis, souls, and other faculties of holy men and women, including the dead, living, unborn, teachers, disciples, and children. It worships the Fravashis of the saints from Gaya Maretan to Saoshyant, the victorious.

Yasna XXVII. body

This unit concerns the worship and furtherance of Ahura Mazda, the greatest lord and master, and the smiting of Angra Mainyu, Aêshma, and wicked Daêvas. It presents the Ahuna-vairya as a sacred gift and recital that offers saving wisdom and worship. It also declares that the Kingdom belongs to Ahura and concludes by worshipping the Ahuna-vairya and Asha Vahista.
