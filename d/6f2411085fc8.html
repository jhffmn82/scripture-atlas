Peter’s Second Letter / Chapter 1 body

Simon Peter, a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ, writes to those who share a precious faith with him, wishing them grace and peace through knowledge of God and Jesus. He explains that God's divine power has granted all things pertaining to life and godliness and given great promises so believers may partake of the divine nature. He urges his readers to add to their faith a series of virtues, ending in love, warning that lacking them means blindness while having them secures their calling and entrance into the eternal Kingdom. Peter says he will keep reminding them because his departure is coming soon, as Jesus made clear to him. He grounds his message in eyewitness testimony of Christ's majesty, including the voice from heaven on the holy mountain, and affirms that Scripture's prophecy came from men moved by the Holy Spirit, not private interpretation.

Chapter 2 body

Chapter 2 warns that false teachers, like the false prophets of old, will secretly bring destructive heresies among the readers, deny the Master, and exploit people through greed and deceptive words. It cites God's past judgments as proof that punishment is certain: the angels who sinned were cast into Tartarus, the ancient world was flooded while Noah and seven others were preserved, and Sodom and Gomorrah were turned to ashes while righteous Lot was delivered. The text concludes that the Lord knows how to deliver the godly and keep the unrighteous under punishment for the day of judgment. The false teachers are described as lustful, arrogant, and despising authority, following the way of Balaam, and likened to waterless wells and storm-driven clouds. They promise liberty while being slaves of corruption, and those who knew the way of righteousness and turned back are compared to a dog returning to its vomit and a washed sow wallowing in the mire.

Chapter 3 body

The writer addresses his beloved readers in a second letter, reminding them of the words of the holy prophets and the apostles' commandment. He warns that in the last days mockers will walk after their own lusts and ask where the promise of Christ's coming is, willfully forgetting that God's word created the earth and flooded the world. The same word has reserved the present heavens and earth for fire on the day of judgment. The writer explains that the Lord is patient, not wishing anyone to perish but all to come to repentance, though the day of the Lord will come as a thief. He urges holy living, diligence to be found in peace, and watchfulness against error, and he closes by calling the readers to grow in the grace and knowledge of the Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, to whom be glory.
