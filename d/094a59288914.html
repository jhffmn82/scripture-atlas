The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma

author unknown · 64 sections
The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma, from The Book of Mormon, public domain in the United States, from Project Gutenberg. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/17

Abstract written by a model

This record recounts the reign of the judges among the people of Nephi, centered on Alma’s religious leadership, missionary work, and later wars for liberty. After Mosiah’s death, Alma judges Nehor for priestcraft and murder, then leads the Nephites against Amlici and the Lamanites. Though wars bring great loss, the church is restored; later pride, inequality, and persecution cause Alma to give up the judgment-seat to Nephihah and devote himself to preaching repentance, faith in Jesus Christ, humility, care for the poor, and preparation for judgment. Alma ministers in Zarahemla, Gideon, Melek, Ammonihah, and Sidom. With Amulek, he confronts Ammonihah’s wickedness, Zeezrom’s accusations, and teachings that deny God. Alma and Amulek teach Christ’s redemption, resurrection, justice, mercy, repentance, and priesthood. Believers are burned and Alma and Amulek imprisoned, but they are delivered; Ammonihah is later destroyed, while Sidom prospers in faith. The account follows the sons of Mosiah among the Lamanites. Ammon serves king Lamoni and helps convert him; Aaron converts Lamoni’s father. Thousands become the Anti-Nephi-Lehies, bury their weapons rather than shed blood, suffer massacre, and later find Nephite refuge in Jershon as the people of Ammon. Alma counters Korihor’s anti-Christ teachings, then ministers among poor Zoramites with Amulek, teaching faith as a seed, Christ’s atonement, continual prayer, and urgent repentance. Alma counsels Helaman, Shiblon, and Corianton, entrusting sacred records to Helaman and explaining conversion, resurrection, restoration, justice, mercy, and the atonement. War then dominates the account: Moroni defends Nephite liberty, lands, families, and worship against Amalickiah, Ammoron, dissenters, and Lamanite armies. He creates the title of liberty, fortifies cities, wins battles with Lehi, Teancum, Helaman, and others, and confronts governmental neglect. Helaman’s two thousand and sixty sons of the people of Ammon fight faithfully and survive despite wounds. After Pahoran and Moroni reclaim Zarahemla from rebels, the Lamanites are driven from the land, peace and the church are restored, and Helaman dies. The record closes with transfers of sacred things, Moroni’s and Shiblon’s deaths, Hagoth’s voyages northward, renewed Lamanite conflict, and the ending of the account of Alma, Helaman, and Shiblon.

The Book of Alma, the Son of Alma front matter

Chapter 1 body

After Mosiah’s death, Alma presides in the first year of the reign of the judges over the people of Nephi under Mosiah’s laws. Nehor preaches priestcraft, universal salvation, and support for priests, gains followers and wealth, establishes a church, and kills Gideon with a sword. Alma condemns Nehor, who acknowledges that his teachings opposed the word of God and is executed. Despite persecution and internal conflict, the church restores order, shares with the needy, prospers, and the people have peace through the fifth year of the reign of the judges.

Chapter 2 body

Amlici draws many people after him and seeks to become king, but the voice of the people rejects him, so his followers consecrate him as king and become Amlicites. Amlici commands his people to fight the Nephites, leading to war at the hill Amnihu and later across the river Sidon. Alma leads the Nephites, kills Amlici, and helps defeat the Amlicites and Lamanites. The Nephites pursue their enemies into the wilderness, where many die from wounds and are devoured by beasts and vultures.

Chapter 3 body

The Nephites bury their dead after battles with the Lamanites and Amlicites, then return to their lands and establish peace after driving out another Lamanite army. The Lamanites and Amlicites are described by their markings, appearance, traditions, rebellion, and warfare against the Nephites. The Lord God’s promises concerning the curse and mark are fulfilled as the Amlicites mark themselves and bring the curse upon themselves. The wars and contentions in the fifth year of the reign of the judges result in thousands and tens of thousands dying according to the spirit they obeyed.

Chapter 4 body

In the sixth year of the reign of the judges, the people of Nephi suffered losses from the Lamanites and were awakened to their duty, leading to renewed establishment of the church of God and many baptisms. In the eighth year, the church became proud and contentious, persecuting others and setting a harmful example that caused the church to fail in its progress. Alma saw inequality, afflictions, and persecution among the people, while humble followers succored the poor and endured afflictions for Christ’s sake. At the commencement of the ninth year, Alma appointed Nephihah chief judge, relinquished the judgment-seat, retained the high priesthood, and devoted himself to preaching the word of God and bearing pure testimony.

Chapter 5 body

Alma delivers the word of God to the people in Zarahemla and throughout the land, calling them to remember how God delivered their fathers and changed their hearts. He teaches that faith in Jesus Christ, repentance, and works of righteousness are necessary for salvation and preparation to meet God. Alma warns the people against pride, envy, persecution, riches, neglecting the poor, and following the devil, while inviting them to follow the good shepherd. He testifies by the Holy Spirit of God that Jesus Christ will come to take away the sins of those who steadfastly believe on his name.

Chapter 6 body

Alma ordained priests and elders to preside over and watch over the church in the city of Zarahemla. Those outside the church who repented were baptized and received, while members who refused to repent and humbled themselves were rejected and had their names blotted out. The children of God were commanded to gather often, fast, and pray for those who did not know God. Alma then went to the valley of Gideon and declared the word of God to the church there, testifying of Jesus Christ, the Son of God, who should come to redeem his people from their sins.

Chapter 7 body

The speaker comes to address the beloved brethren and urges them to repent, be baptized, keep God’s commandments, and walk in righteousness. The speaker testifies that the Son of God will come to earth, be born of Mary at Jerusalem, suffer for the pains and sins of his people, and overcome death. The speaker perceives that the beloved brethren have faith and are walking in the paths leading to the kingdom of God. The speaker exhorts them to be humble, patient, diligent, thankful, and full of faith, hope, and charity so they may enter the kingdom of heaven.

Chapter 8 body

Alma teaches in Melek, is rejected and expelled from Ammonihah, and is commanded by an angel of the Lord to return and warn the people to repent or be destroyed. He returns, is fed by Amulek, and stays with him before beginning to preach again. Alma and Amulek preach to the people of Ammonihah with the power of the Holy Ghost, and they cannot be confined or slain until they are bound and imprisoned. The people of Ammonihah grow more wicked and seek to destroy the liberty of the people of Nephi.

Chapter 9 body

Alma and Amulek preach repentance to the people who were in the city of Ammonihah, who challenge Alma and become angry at his testimony. Alma reminds them of God’s commandments, the deliverance and blessings given to the people of Nephi, and the consequences of continuing in wickedness. He declares that the Son of God will soon come, redeem those baptized unto repentance, and judge all people according to their works. Amulek then begins preaching to them, though only part of his words is recorded.

Chapter 10 body

Amulek recounts how an angel of the Lord sent him home to feed Alma, a holy prophet, and how Alma’s presence blessed his household. He testifies that Alma’s teachings are true and warns the people of Ammonihah that their wickedness, lawyers, and judges are laying the foundation for destruction. The people and lawyers oppose Amulek, while Zeezrom becomes the foremost accuser of Amulek and Alma. Amulek declares that the prayers of the righteous are sparing the people from famine, pestilence, and the sword.

Chapter 11 body

The unit explains the law of Mosiah concerning judges’ wages, monetary reckoning, debt enforcement, and the motives of those who stirred up disturbances for gain. Zeezrom questions Amulek and offers him six onties of silver to deny the existence of a Supreme Being, but Amulek affirms that there is one true and living God. Amulek teaches that the Son of God will come to redeem those who believe, will not save people in their sins, and is the Eternal Father of heaven and earth. He further teaches that all people will be resurrected, restored, and judged before God according to their works.

Chapter 12 body

Alma explains to Zeezrom and the people that Zeezrom lied to men and God under the influence of the devil, and that hardened hearts receive less of the word and become subject to destruction. He teaches that death, resurrection, judgment, spiritual death, and the consequences of sin are part of God's plan, while repentance and faith in the Only Begotten Son bring mercy and entry into God's rest. Alma explains to Antionah that Adam's Fall made mankind fallen, established a probationary state, and made the plan of redemption necessary. He urges the people to repent, harden not their hearts, and avoid provoking God's wrath.

Chapter 13 body

Alma teaches that the Lord God ordained priests and high priests after the holy order of the Only Begotten Son to teach the commandments and help the people look forward to redemption. He explains that holy calling and high priesthood are prepared for those who choose good, exercise faith, repent, and work righteousness, while many are sanctified and enter into the rest of the Lord. Alma recounts that Melchizedek preached repentance in the land of Salem, and the people repented and received peace. He urges the people to repent without delay, humble themselves, pray continually, and prepare for the coming of the Son, which angels declare with glad tidings.

Chapter 14 body

Alma and Amulek are bound, imprisoned, mocked, deprived, and questioned after testifying against the wickedness of the people. Zeezrom repents and pleads for them, while believers, women and children, and the holy scriptures are cast into a fire. The Lord permits the innocent to die, but later gives Alma and Amulek strength according to their faith in Christ, enabling them to break their bonds. The prison collapses, killing the chief judge, lawyers, priests, teachers, and every soul inside except Alma and Amulek, and the people flee in fear.

Chapter 15 body

Alma and Amulek departed from Ammonihah and came to the land of Sidom, where they found people cast out for believing Alma. Zeezrom, sick with a burning fever because of his sins and anguish, was healed after affirming his belief in Christ and Alma prayed for him. Alma baptized Zeezrom, who then preached, and Alma established a church in Sidom where many people were baptized. The people of Ammonihah remained hard-hearted, while the people in Sidom humbled themselves, worshipped, and prayed.

Chapter 16 body

The Lamanites invaded Ammonihah and surrounding areas, destroying the city and taking captives before Zoram and his sons led the Nephite armies to recover them. Alma received revelation that the Lamanites would cross the river Sidon in the south wilderness, where they would be defeated and the captives returned. Ammonihah was left desolate, and its dead were associated with the Desolation of Nehors. Alma, Amulek, and other priests preached repentance, the word of God, the coming and resurrection of the Son of God, and the church became established throughout the land amid peace and spiritual blessings.

Chapter 17 body

Alma meets the sons of Mosiah and rejoices that they remain strong in the knowledge of the truth after fourteen years of teaching among the Lamanites. The sons of Mosiah recount their journey from the land of Zarahemla, their afflictions, and the Lord’s command to establish his word among the Lamanites. Ammon becomes a servant of king Lamoni in the land of Ishmael and is assigned to watch Lamoni’s flocks. When Lamanites scatter the flocks at the water of Sebus, Ammon defends them with his sling and sword and returns the flocks to the king.

Chapter 18 body

King Lamoni’s servants testify that Ammon defended the king’s flocks and defeated those who attacked them, causing Lamoni to believe that Ammon is the Great Spirit. Ammon teaches Lamoni and his servants about God, the creation, the fall of man, their history, the scriptures, the plan of redemption, and the coming of Christ. Lamoni believes Ammon’s words, prays for mercy, and falls to the earth as if dead, after which his servants carry him to his wife.

Chapter 19 body

Ammon visits king Lamoni after Lamoni has lain as if dead for two days and tells the queen that he will rise. Lamoni awakens, declares that he has seen his Redeemer, and the queen, Ammon, and the servants are overcome by the Spirit and fall to the earth. Abish raises the queen and king, after which Lamoni, Ammon, and the servants teach the people, many of whom believe, are baptized, and establish a church. The work of the Lord begins among the Lamanites as the Lord pours out his Spirit upon them.

Chapter 20 body

Ammon is directed by the Lord to go to the land of Middoni to deliver Aaron and his other brethren from prison instead of accompanying Lamoni to the land of Nephi. Lamoni travels with Ammon, defends him before his father, and refuses to kill him or return to the land of Ishmael. Ammon defeats Lamoni’s father in combat and obtains the release of his brethren and the continuation of Lamoni’s rule. In Middoni, Ammon’s brethren are released after suffering hunger, thirst, affliction, beatings, and imprisonment, but remaining patient.

Chapter 21 body

Aaron and his brethren preach the word of God among the Lamanites, Amalekites, and Amulonites in Jerusalem, Ani-Anti, and Middoni, teaching repentance, Christ, the resurrection of the dead, and redemption through his death and sufferings and the atonement of his blood. The Amalekites reject and mock Aaron, and Aaron and some of his brethren are imprisoned in Middoni before Lamoni and Ammon deliver them. The missionaries continue preaching and bring many to a knowledge of the truth, convincing them of their sins and incorrect traditions. In Ishmael, Lamoni establishes religious liberty, builds synagogues, teaches his people, and Ammon exhorts them daily to keep the commandments of God.

Chapter 22 body

Aaron and his brethren go to the palace of the king who was Lamoni’s father and teach him about God, the Great Spirit, repentance, Christ, and the plan of redemption. The king prays to know God, offers to give away his sins, and is struck as if dead, but Aaron raises him and the king and his household are converted unto the Lord. The king pacifies the people, commands Aaron and his brethren to preach, and sends a proclamation throughout the land. The unit also describes the locations and boundaries of the lands of the Lamanites and Nephites, including Bountiful, Desolation, the land northward, and the land southward.

Chapter 23 body

The king of the Lamanites issued a proclamation protecting Ammon, Aaron, Omner, Himni, and their brethren so they could preach the word of God freely throughout the land. Aaron and his brethren established churches and taught the Lamanites, bringing thousands to the knowledge of the Lord, and those who believed did not fall away. The converted Lamanites laid down their weapons of rebellion, became righteous, and took the name Anti-Nephi-Lehies. They became industrious, friendly with the Nephites, and were no longer followed by the curse of God.

Chapter 24 body

The Amalekites, Amulonites, and unconverted Lamanites stir up anger against the people of Anti-Nephi-Lehi, and the Lamanites prepare to destroy them. Anti-Nephi-Lehi and his people refuse to take up arms, bury their weapons, and covenant with God to give their own lives rather than shed the blood of their brethren. The Lamanites massacre 1,005 people of Anti-Nephi-Lehi, but many Lamanites repent, abandon their weapons, and join the people of God. The unit teaches that those who fall away after enlightenment become more hardened than those who never knew the truth.

Chapter 25 body

The Lamanites swear vengeance against the Nephites, destroy the people in the land of Ammonihah, and later return to their own land after being driven and slain in battles. Many Lamanites remember the words preached by Aaron and his brethren, believe in the Lord, and join the people of Anti-Nephi-Lehi, while rulers from the remnant of the children of Amulon cause believers to be put to death by fire. The seed of Amulon and his brethren are hunted, scattered, and slain, fulfilling the words of Abinadi. The converted Lamanites bury their weapons of war, become a righteous people, keep the law of Moses, and look forward to the coming of Christ through faith.

Chapter 26 body

Ammon rejoices with his brethren over the blessings God granted through their journey from Zarahemla to Nephi and their preaching among the Lamanites. He explains that many Lamanites were brought from darkness into God's light, converted, and gathered into God's fold. Ammon recounts the afflictions he and his brethren endured and credits God’s power, mercy, and word for their success. He teaches that repentance, faith, good works, and continual prayer bring knowledge of God’s mysteries and lead to salvation.

Chapter 27 body

The Lamanites returned to the land of Nephi after failing to destroy the Nephites, while the Amalekites stirred them to attack the people of Anti-Nephi-Lehi. Ammon and his brethren led the people to seek refuge among the Nephites, and the Lord directed them to leave the land. The Nephites granted them the land of Jershon, protected them with armies, and called them the people of Ammon. The people of Ammon were known for their repentance, faith in Christ, honesty, zeal, and refusal to take up arms against their brethren.

Chapter 28 body

After the people of Ammon and a church were established in the land of Jershon, the Lamanites followed their brethren into the wilderness and fought the Nephites in a tremendous battle. The Lamanites were slain, driven, and scattered, while the Nephites also suffered a great slaughter and returned to their land. The fifteenth year of the reign of the judges ended with widespread mourning, fasting, and prayer, as many thousands had died and their kindred mourned. The unit teaches that sin, transgression, and the power of the devil bring destruction and sorrow, while the light of Christ brings hope, joy, and rejoicing.

Chapter 29 body

The speaker wishes to declare repentance and the plan of redemption to every people but recognizes that he should be content with the work allotted to him. He teaches that God grants people according to their desires and gives nations His word in wisdom. The speaker glories in being an instrument in the hands of God, rejoices in the repentance of others, and remembers that God delivered his fathers from captivity and bondage. He also rejoices in the success of his brethren who went to the land of Nephi and prays that they and the fruit of their labors may enter the kingdom of God.

Chapter 30 body

After the people of Ammon were established in the land of Jershon and the Lamanites were driven out, continual peace prevailed among the Nephites while the people observed the commandments and the law of Moses. In the latter end of the seventeenth year, Korihor, an Anti-Christ, preached against the prophecies of the prophets and denied Christ, God, and the atonement, leading many into wickedness. Alma confronted Korihor, who demanded a sign, and God struck him dumb; Korihor then confessed that the devil had deceived him. Korihor was cast out, later trodden down and killed among the Zoramites, and the people were convinced of his wickedness and returned to the Lord.

Chapter 31 body

Alma learned that the Zoramites were perverting the ways of God and feared that they would ally with the Lamanites. He and Ammon, Aaron, Omner, Amulek, Zeezrom, Shiblon, and Corianton went among the Zoramites to preach the word of God. The Zoramites worshipped at the Rameumptom, prayed that they were chosen, rejected the tradition of belief in Christ, and set their hearts on riches and pride. Alma prayed for strength, comfort, wisdom, and success, and after he blessed his companions they were filled with the Holy Spirit and strengthened to labor among the people.

Chapter 32 body

Alma teaches the poor in heart, who have been cast out of the synagogues because of their poverty, that God can be worshiped anywhere and that humility can lead to repentance and salvation. He explains that faith is hope in things not seen and that knowledge comes through an experiment upon the word. Alma compares the word to a seed that grows into a tree when nourished by faith, diligence, patience, and care. The tree yields precious fruit and springs up unto everlasting life.

Chapter 33 body

Alma teaches his listeners that they can worship God despite being cast out of their synagogues and urges them to search the scriptures. He cites Zenos, Zenock, Moses, and the type raised in the wilderness as witnesses of the Son of God and his mercy, atonement, resurrection, and judgment. Alma tells his listeners to plant the word in their hearts and nourish it by faith so it will become a tree leading to everlasting life and lighten their burdens through the joy of the Son of God.

Chapter 34 body

Amulek teaches that Christ will come as the Son of God to atone for the sins of the world through an infinite and eternal sacrifice. He explains that mercy can satisfy the demands of justice for those who exercise faith unto repentance, while all mankind would otherwise perish. He exhorts the people to call upon God continually in prayer, care for the needy and afflicted, and bring forth fruit unto repentance. He teaches that this life is the time to prepare to meet God and warns that delaying repentance until death leads to the final state of the wicked.

Chapter 35 body

After Amulek finished speaking, Alma and his brethren came to the land of Jershon, where many Zoramites who favored their words had been cast out. The people of Ammon received the poor Zoramites, giving them food, clothing, lands, and help, which angered the Zoramites and led them to stir up the Lamanites against the people of Ammon and the Nephites. The people of Ammon moved to the land of Melek so Nephite armies could use Jershon, and war began between the Lamanites and the Nephites. Alma, Ammon, their brethren, and Alma's two sons returned to Zarahemla after bringing many Zoramites to repentance, while Alma grieved over the people's iniquity, wars, bloodshed, contentions, and hardening hearts.

Chapter 36 body

Alma teaches his son Helaman that keeping the commandments of God brings prosperity, while disobedience cuts people off from God’s presence. He recounts how God sent a holy angel to stop him and the sons of Mosiah from destroying the church of God, leading to Alma’s conversion. Alma describes suffering torment for his sins and iniquities, then receiving joy and relief through Jesus Christ. He says God has supported and delivered him and will raise him up at the last day.

Chapter 37 body

Helaman is commanded by his father to preserve the sacred records, plates, and interpreters, and to keep a record of the people. His father teaches him that God uses small and simple means, including records and the Liahona, to fulfill wise purposes, reveal truth, guide people, and bring souls to salvation. Helaman is instructed to keep the commandments of God, counsel with the Lord, preach repentance and faith in Jesus Christ, and teach the people to reject sin, iniquity, and the secret works of darkness. The unit also explains the preservation and restoration of the Lamanites, the destruction of wicked workers of darkness, and the symbolic relationship between the Liahona and the word of Christ.

Chapter 38 body

The unit concerns counsel to Shiblon about faithfulness, obedience, teaching, humility, self-control, and enduring to the end. Shiblon is reminded that trusting God brings deliverance from trials and afflictions and that God’s commandments bring prosperity, while disobedience cuts people off from God’s presence. The speaker teaches that salvation comes only through Christ and recounts receiving remission of sins after crying to the Lord Jesus Christ for mercy. Shiblon is instructed to continue teaching the word diligently, temperately, boldly, humbly, and with love.

Chapter 39 body

The speaker admonishes his son for boasting, forsaking the ministry, and following Isabel, and commands him to repent, seek counsel, and refrain from iniquity. The son is told to return to the Zoramites, acknowledge his faults, and no longer lead people toward wickedness. The unit teaches that Christ will come to take away the sins of the world and declare glad tidings of salvation. It explains that the plan of redemption is made known beforehand because souls are equally precious to God at all times.

Chapter 40 body

The unit concerns the resurrection and the state of men's souls between death and the appointed time of resurrection. It explains that God has appointed a time when all shall rise, though men do not know when it will be. Righteous spirits enter paradise, while wicked spirits are cast into outer darkness until resurrection. At resurrection, souls and bodies are reunited, restored to their proper and perfect frame, and brought before God for judgment according to their works.

Chapter 41 body

The speaker explains to his son that restoration is required by the justice of God and that all things will return to their proper order through the power and resurrection of Christ. Men will be restored according to their works and the desires of their hearts, receiving either good and happiness or evil and misery. The speaker teaches that wickedness cannot be restored to happiness and that restoration returns evil for evil and good for good. He urges his son to be merciful, deal justly, judge righteously, and do good continually.

Chapter 42 body

The speaker explains to his son how God’s justice and mercy operate in the punishment and redemption of sinners. He teaches that Adam and our first parents were cut off from the tree of life and from the presence of the Lord, bringing temporal and spiritual death upon mankind. The probationary state gives mankind time to repent, while the atonement allows mercy to satisfy the demands of justice without destroying justice. The speaker urges his son to repent, trust God’s justice and mercy, and preach the word to bring others to repentance.

Chapter 43 body

Alma and his sons preach the word, while the account returns to the wars between the Nephites and the Lamanites. Led by Moroni, the Nephites prepare to defend their lands, families, rights, liberty, and worship of God against Zerahemnah’s Lamanite armies, which include Amalekites and Zoramites. Alma reveals that the Lamanites are marching toward Manti, allowing Moroni to arrange an ambush near the river Sidon. The Nephites defeat and surround the more numerous Lamanite armies after praying for freedom, and Moroni commands his men to stop shedding their blood.

Chapter 44 body

Moroni tells Zerahemnah that the Lord has delivered the Lamanites into the Nephites’ hands because of their faith and religion, and demands that they surrender their weapons and covenant not to return to war. Zerahemnah initially refuses to take an oath, and after Moroni returns his weapons, a Nephite soldier breaks his sword and removes his scalp. Many Lamanites surrender after seeing the scalp, but Zerahemnah stirs the remainder to fight until Moroni’s forces begin killing them. Zerahemnah then promises a covenant of peace, the fighting stops, and the surviving Lamanites depart, while many Nephites and Lamanites have died.

Chapter 45 body

The people of Nephi rejoice and give thanks to the Lord after being delivered from their enemies, but Alma prophesies that they will later dwindle in unbelief, suffer wars, pestilences, famines, and bloodshed, and become extinct. Alma teaches Helaman, receives his belief in the records and in Jesus Christ, and charges him to keep the commandments and record the prophecy. Alma blesses Helaman, his other sons, the land, and the church before departing from Zarahemla and never being heard of again. Helaman and his brethren reestablish the church throughout the land, but some become proud because of their riches and refuse to heed their words.

Chapter 46 body

Amalickiah leads dissenters who seek power and want him to be king, flattering many people away from the church and the foundation of liberty. Moroni responds by creating and proclaiming the title of liberty, gathering the people, and marching against Amalickiah’s forces. Amalickiah flees to the land of Nephi, while many of his followers are captured, returned to the land of Zarahemla, or required to covenant to support freedom. The Nephites regain peace, and Helaman and the high priests maintain order in the church.

Chapter 47 body

Amalickiah stirs the Lamanites against the Nephites and schemes to dethrone the king of the Lamanites. He gains control of the armies at Onidah by persuading Lehonti to join forces with him, then causes Lehonti to be poisoned and becomes chief commander. Amalickiah’s servants assassinate the king, and he uses deception to blame the king’s servants before taking possession of the city of Nephi. He marries the queen and becomes king, while the king’s servants flee to Zarahemla and join the people of Ammon.

Chapter 48 body

Amalickiah becomes king over the Lamanites and stirs them to attack the Nephites, seeking to rule all the land and place the Nephites in bondage. Moroni strengthens the Nephite armies and fortifications while preparing the people to remain faithful to God and defend their liberty, lands, families, and religion. Moroni is described as a mighty, understanding man who dislikes bloodshed and labors for the welfare and safety of his people. Helaman and his brethren preach and baptize, bringing four years of peace among the Nephites before war with the Lamanites resumes.

Chapter 49 body

The Lamanites, or Amalickiahites, attacked the Nephite cities of Ammonihah and Noah but were repeatedly repelled by defenses prepared under Moroni’s direction. Moroni stationed an army at Ammonihah, fortified the cities, and appointed Lehi as chief captain over Noah. The Lamanite chief captains were slain, more than a thousand Lamanites were killed, and no Nephites were slain, though about fifty were wounded. The Lamanites fled to inform Amalickiah, who became angry and cursed God and Moroni, while the Nephites thanked the Lord for their deliverance.

Chapter 50 body

Moroni strengthens Nephite defenses, drives the Lamanites from the east wilderness, establishes armies and cities, and helps the Nephites prosper in peace. A conflict between the people of Lehi and the people of Morianton leads Morianton to flee northward, but Teancum defeats his army and restores peace. Nephihah dies after serving as chief judge, and Pahoran is appointed to the judgment-seat. The unit teaches that the Lord's promises to Lehi are fulfilled as the obedient prosper and the disobedient suffer destruction, bondage, or unbelief.

Chapter 51 body

The people of Nephi divide over whether the law should be altered and whether Pahoran should remain chief judge, but the freemen prevail by the voice of the people. Amalickiah leads the Lamanites in an invasion, captures several Nephite cities, and is stopped and killed by Teancum near the land Bountiful. Moroni suppresses the king-men, killing or imprisoning their leaders and compelling the remainder to defend their country. Teancum and his men then resist the Lamanites and place the title of liberty on their cities and towers.

Chapter 52 body

Amalickiah is found dead in his tent, and his brother Ammoron becomes king over the Lamanites. Teancum and Moroni defend the Nephite lands, recover the city of Mulek through a stratagem, and defeat the Lamanite army led by Jacob. Jacob is killed, Moroni is wounded, and many Lamanites surrender their weapons and become prisoners. The Nephites take more prisoners than the number slain in the battle.

Chapter 53 body

Moroni places Lamanite prisoners under guard, has them bury the dead, and makes them build fortifications around the land Bountiful after capturing the city of Mulek with Lehi. Moroni prepares the Nephites for war and protects their women and children, while armies of the Lamanites gain several cities on the west sea because of dissensions and intrigue among the Nephites. The people of Ammon want to take up arms to defend the Nephites but are restrained by Helaman because of their oath against shedding blood. Two thousand of their sons form a covenant to defend the Nephites and choose Helaman as their leader.

Chapter 54 body

At the commencement of the twenty and ninth year of the judges, Ammoron asked Moroni to exchange prisoners. Moroni agreed only if Ammoron would deliver a man, his wife, and his children for one prisoner, while threatening to fight if Ammoron refused. Moroni and Ammoron exchanged epistles concerning the war, the justice of God, their peoples, and control of the government. Ammoron agreed to exchange prisoners but declared that the war would continue until the Nephites were subjected or eternally extinguished.

Chapter 55 body

Moroni refuses to exchange prisoners with Ammoron and sends Laman to deceive the Lamanite guards at city Gid with wine. After the Lamanites become drunk and fall asleep, Moroni arms the Nephite prisoners, surrounds the city, captures the Lamanites, and takes possession of city Gid. He liberates the Nephite prisoners, strengthens the city’s fortifications, and sends the Lamanite prisoners to city Bountiful. The Nephites regain ground and begin winning victories, while Moroni prepares to attack city Morianton.

Chapter 56 body

Helaman writes to Moroni about the warfare in his part of the land and the defense of Judea. Two thousand young descendants of Laman, called Helaman’s sons and stripling Ammonites, join the army of Antipus, which faces the Lamanites. Helaman leads a stratagem that draws the strongest Lamanite army away from Antiparah, allowing the army of Antipus and Helaman’s force to surround and capture them. None of the two thousand young men falls, and the prisoners of war are sent toward Zarahemla while Helaman returns with the remaining forces to Judea.

Chapter 57 body

Helaman rejects Ammoron's offer to exchange prisoners of war for the city of Antiparah, and Antiparah falls into Nephite hands after its people flee. In the twenty-ninth year, Helaman's army surrounds Cumeni, captures its provisions, and eventually obtains the city after the Lamanites lose hope of support. A Lamanite attack causes prisoners to rebel, but Helaman's two thousand and sixty sons of the Ammonites and the men escorting the prisoners help preserve Cumeni. Two hundred of the two thousand and sixty are wounded but all survive, which Helaman attributes to their faith and the miraculous power of God.

Chapter 58 body

Helaman and the Nephites sought to obtain the city of Manti while facing a more numerous Lamanite army and receiving little support from Zarahemla. After praying, they received assurances from the Lord God, used a stratagem involving Gid and Teomner, and captured Manti without shedding blood. The Lamanites fled, and the Nephites regained their cities and lands, though many women and children were taken prisoner. Helaman reported that the sons of the people of Ammon remained faithful and that he trusted God despite the weakness of the Nephite armies.

Chapter 59 body

Moroni rejoices at Helaman’s success in regaining lost lands and tells his people to rejoice. He sends an epistle to Pahoran requesting men to strengthen Helaman’s armies, then plans to recover the remaining cities taken by the Lamanites. The Lamanites attack the people of Nephihah, slaughter many, and force the survivors to join Moroni’s army. After Nephihah is lost, Moroni and his chief captains sorrow, doubt because of the wickedness of the people, and Moroni becomes angry with the government for its indifference to the freedom of their country.

Chapter 60 body

Moroni writes a letter to Pahoran, the chief judge and governor in Zarahemla, condemning the government for neglecting the armies and failing to send provisions and men. He describes the suffering, hunger, slaughter, and deaths among the people while the Lamanites take lands, murder people, and carry women and children captive. Moroni warns that the judgments of God and the sword of justice will come upon the government unless it repents, supports the armies, and defends the country and its freedom. He declares that he seeks neither power nor worldly honor, but the glory of God and the freedom and welfare of his country.

Chapter 61 body

Pahoran writes to Moroni explaining that rebels have driven him from the judgment-seat and taken Zarahemla, where they appointed a king and formed an alliance with the Lamanites. Pahoran has fled to Gideon, gathered freemen, and plans to resist the rebels and reclaim Zarahemla. He asks Moroni to come with a few men while Lehi and Teancum continue conducting the war. Pahoran says God will deliver those who stand fast in the liberty and freedom God has given them.

Chapter 62 body

Moroni joins Pahoran to defeat Pachus, restore Pahoran to the judgment-seat, and enforce the law against those who fight against the freedom of their country. Moroni and Pahoran capture the city of Nephihah, while many Lamanite prisoners join the people of Ammon. Teancum dies while attacking Ammoron, and Moroni, Lehi, and their armies drive the Lamanites from the land. Peace is restored, the church of God is reestablished, and the people of Nephi prosper until Helaman dies in the thirty and fifth year of the reign of the judges.

Chapter 63 body

Shiblon took possession of the sacred things from Helaman and later conferred them upon Helaman, the son of Helaman, before his death. Moroni died, and later Shiblon died while Corianton had gone to the land northward with provisions. Hagoth built ships that carried many Nephites, with provisions, women, and children, toward the land northward, though some ships were never heard from again. Dissenters stirred the Lamanites to war against Moronihah’s army, but the Lamanites were defeated and driven back to their own lands, and the account of Alma, Helaman, and Shiblon ended.
