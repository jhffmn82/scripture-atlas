Asa ki War (Asa di Var)

Guru Nanak · 25 sections
Asa ki War (Asa di Var), translated by Max Arthur Macauliffe, The Sikh Religion, vol. 1 (1909), public domain, via sacred-texts.com. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://sacred-texts.com/skh/tsr1/

Abstract written by a model

Asa ki War (Asa di Var), translated by Max Arthur Macauliffe in The Sikh Religion (1909), is a devotional and moral composition containing pauris and sloks attributed chiefly to Guru Nanak and Guru Angad, with Guru Har Gobind named. It begins by affirming one true, self-existent, fearless, beneficent Creator and repeatedly presents the Guru as essential: without the Guru, neither vast light, learning, ritual, nor outward religious practice can dispel spiritual darkness or bring salvation. Across its sequence, the work describes God as creator, sustainer, judge, and ruler of every world, creature, action, life, death, and destiny. God records deeds, appoints Dharmraj for judgment, separates the truthful from those attached to mammon, and alone remains fearless, true, and permanent amid a passing world. Human beings die, become ashes, face the account of their acts, and may undergo transmigration; grace, the divine Name, truth, humility, love, and the Guru’s instruction provide the way through this condition. The text persistently rejects pride, greed, falsehood, hypocrisy, caste pretension, dishonest earnings, ritual purity, purchased religious marks, pilgrimages, austerity, and merely verbal or external devotion. It contrasts them with inward purity, honest work, mercy, contentment, continence, charity, service, supplication, and remembrance of God. Its spiritual janeu is mercy, contentment, continence, and truth rather than a cotton thread; true impurity lies in greed, falsehood, coveting, slander, superstition, and attachment. The later portions deepen its ethical warning: worldly beauty, authority, possessions, relationships, and religious display cannot survive death, while people must bear their own deeds. The faithful servant effaces himself and follows the master’s will; one who competes with the master loses honour. The work concludes by returning to God’s comprehensive power: God creates, orders, fills, sustains, regenerates, and governs all beings, while His greatness is beyond expression and He alone is the abiding Place.

Asa ki War (Asa di Var) body

Asa ki War (Asa di Var) is presented in a translation by Max Arthur Macauliffe in The Sikh Religion, volume 1, published in 1909. The unit states that one God is true-named, the Creator, fearless, without enmity, immortal, unborn, self-existent, great, and beneficent. Guru Har Gobind is named. The War includes sloks attributed to the first Guru, which are to be sung to the air of “Tunda As Raja.”

Slok .. Pauri I body

The unit praises the Guru and describes the darkness that remains without him, even under immense light. Guru Nanak is praised as a teacher who makes demigods out of men and warns that clever people who disregard the Guru will be abandoned like spurious sesames. Guru Angad teaches that a hundred moons and a thousand suns cannot dispel the darkness without the Guru. Pauri I describes God as the Creator who made the world, gave names to things, created Maya, and gives and takes life.

Slok II .. Pauri II body

Guru Nanak declares that God's regions, universes, worlds, creation, acts, thoughts, order, court, command, favour, signs, power, strength, praises, eulogies, and might are true. Guru Nanak teaches that those who meditate on the True One are true, while those who are born and die are the falsest of the false. Guru Angad describes the world as the True One's chamber and dwelling, where God acts according to His order and reveals Himself to those who become holy. Pauri II states that God created animals, recorded their names, appointed Dharmraj to judge their acts, and separates the truthful from those attached to mammon.

Slok III .. Pauri III body

Guru Nanak describes the wonder of God's word, knowledge, creatures, natural elements, human experiences, and the meeting and parting from God. He teaches that all things, including creation, virtues, vices, honour, dishonour, and religious compositions, exist by God's power. Pauri III explains that man eventually becomes ashes, while the soul passes away and ministers of Death take the dead for judgment. The Judge reads an account of his acts and passes sentence, leaving the condemned without shelter.

Slok IV .. Pauri IV body

Guru Nanak describes fear as governing winds, rivers, fire, earth, Indar, Dharmraj, the sun, the moon, spiritual beings, stars, heroes, and the movements of people, while God, the Formless One, alone is without fear. He teaches that worldly rulers, performers, ornaments, bodies, and religious compositions do not provide divine knowledge. Divine knowledge is obtained by God's grace rather than by words, skill, or orders. The Pauri says that the true Guru is obtained through the Kind One's kindness, gives the Word to the wandering soul, removes pride, and leads people to the True One.

Slok V .. Pauri V body

Guru Nanak describes worldly performers, objects, and beings as caught in entanglements and turned according to their own acts. He teaches that those without divine knowledge are consumed by the minister of death, while the Formless One creates the whole world and shows kindness to favored worshippers. Guru's instruction gives knowledge and saves those upon whom the Kind One looks with favor. The name, good acts, humility, fear of God, and love are presented as beneficial, while death comes when a person's measure is full.

Slok VI .. Pauri VI body

The unit contrasts the religious practices of Musalmans, Hindus, and Jogis with the service of God and the conduct of generous, sinful, and worldly people. It teaches that saints hunger to praise God and rely on the true Name, abiding in everlasting joy. Guru Nanak reflects on the fate of a Musalman's ashes and leaves the matter to the Creator. Pauri VI teaches that none finds God without the true Guru, through whom salvation is obtained.

Slok VII .. Pauri VII body

The unit concerns man, pride, God, Nanak, transmigration, and people who have meditated on God. It teaches that pride affects nearly every aspect of man's life and conduct, and that when pride is removed, God's gate is seen. Pride subjects man to repeated transmigration, but God's grace and the Guru's instruction provide a medicine for it. People who have meditated on God as the truest of the true have worshipped, lived honestly, rejected evil, and found Him.

Slok VIII .. Pauri VIII body

The unit concerns God’s creation and care of the world, including men, trees, animals, lands, waters, and the universe. It teaches that God creates animals and takes care of them, and that the Creator who made the world also takes thought for it. Guru Nanak says that good deeds, charities, penances, bravery, study, and reading sacred works are of no avail without the mark of grace, while God has decreed transmigration. The passage teaches that truth comes from God and is obtained by meeting the true Guru, but the fool wastes life through obstinacy.

Slok IX .. Pauri IX body

This unit concerns Guru Nanak’s teachings on books, religious practices, actions, and devotion to God. It says that reading, pilgrimage, religious garb, austerity, silence, wilderness dwelling, and other outward practices are useless without God's name and guidance from a guru. Man suffers through attachment to mammon and must bear the results of his own acts, while those who meet the true Guru and fix God's name in their hearts obtain comfort and freedom from hopes, fears, and pride. The saints please God and gain entrance to His gate, while distinctions of caste are contrasted with the speaker’s low-caste identity and meditation on God.

Slok X .. Pauri X body

Guru Nanak teaches that worldly rulers, possessions, relationships, beauty, sweetness, and the whole world are false and pass away, while only God is wholly true. He explains that man becomes true through truth in the heart, love for the True One, knowledge of the true way, mercy, charity, and instruction from the true Guru. Truth is described as medicine that removes sin, and man is urged to seek the dust of the saints' feet, abandon false covetousness, meditate on the Unseen One, and serve according to his allotted reward.

Slok XI .. Pauri XI body

Guru Nanak describes an age in which truth is scarce, falsehood prevails, and greed, sin, and lust govern society. He teaches that the whole seed of the Name germinates, and that fear, modesty, and devotion can remove falsehood from a person. He says that man's evil is seen by the True One, that human effort succeeds only through what the Creator does, and that caste has no power in the next world. In Pauri XI, God is said to create the different worlds, unite some beings with Himself, lead others astray, and reveal Himself through the favour of the Guru.

Slok XII .. Pauri XII body

Guru Nanak teaches that pain can serve as medicine while worldly pleasure is a disease that turns people from God. He praises God as the Doer whose light fills all creation and says that uttering divine praise brings salvation. Guru Angad teaches that repeating the name of the one God is the highest duty, above the duties of different social groups. Guru Nanak and Guru Angad both connect divine knowledge with understanding God and the soul, while Guru Nanak says that divine knowledge requires a guru. The unit also teaches that both literate and illiterate people will be judged according to their actions.

Slok XIII .. Pauri XIII body

Guru Nanak teaches that the body and its guiding qualities change across the four ages, with passion and falsehood characterizing the Kal age. He describes what the four Veds say about the Lord, God's name, truth, and the Kal age, and says that love, devotion, and humility lead to salvation. He declares himself devoted to the true Guru, who teaches divine instruction, enables the sight of God in the world, and mercifully saves those who recognize him as a boat. Those who abandon the Lord for mammon are wrecked.

Slok XIV .. Pauri XIV body

Guru Nanak teaches that outward greatness, ritual, and bowing are worthless without inner purity and the guidance of the true Guru. The simmal-tree of the desert disappoints birds because its fruit, flowers, and leaves are useless, while a lowly tree with sweet fruit has exquisite qualities. He warns that the wicked man may bow repeatedly while retaining an impure heart, and that people must leave their beauty and possessions behind and face the results of their good and bad deeds. The dead must travel the narrow road, may be sent naked to hell, and will regret their sins.

Slok XV .. Pauri XV body

Slok XV teaches that a true janeu is made of mercy, contentment, continence, and truth, and that it does not break, soil, burn, or get lost. Guru Nanak contrasts this spiritual thread with the purchased cotton janeu, which falls away at death and does not restrain theft, adultery, falsehood, abuse, or other wrongdoing. Guru Nanak says that adoring and praising the Name gives honour and a true thread fit for God's court, while criticizing the absence of moral strings for the body and senses. Pauri XV teaches that the Lord's compassion leads a person to do the Master's work, obey God's order, reach God's court, and receive the desired fruit and a robe of honour.

Slok XVI .. Pauri XVI body

The unit concerns hypocrisy in religious practice, especially among those who wear Hindu marks and rosaries while eating the bread of malechhas and associating with Muhammadans. Guru Nanak condemns false dealings, ritual defilement, and violence, and urges meditation on the True One. It teaches that God sees all, assigns duties, bestows greatness, and can reduce monarchs to poverty.

Slok XVII .. Pauri XVII body

The unit concerns the consequences of dishonest offerings, truthful purity, and the failure of worldly people who forget God. It says that ancestors receiving stolen offerings will be known as thieves, and that only earnings gained through work will benefit a person in the next world. Guru Nanak teaches that falsehood is despised and that true purity comes from having God in the heart. It describes men who enjoy adorned women, grand dwellings, and authority but forget death and ultimately fail when old age comes.

Slok XVIII .. Pauri XVIII body

The unit concerns impurity, divine knowledge, God, Guru Nanak, and the True Guru. It teaches that impurity is present in everything if judged by physical contamination, but that impurity is truly rooted in greed, falsehood, coveting, slander, superstition, and worldly attachment. Guru Nanak states that divine knowledge washes away impurity and that those who know God have no impurity. The True Guru leads people to behold God's greatness, brings God's praises into the heart, removes evil, and grants the nine treasures when God is pleased.

Slok XIX .. Pauri XIX body

The unit describes the Brahman purifying himself, preparing sacred food, and reciting Sanskrit verses while eating. It teaches that food becomes impure when it enters a sinful body, and that a mouth which eats without uttering the Name is like something spat upon. Guru Nanak explains that people depend on a vessel throughout life, while only the True God is independent of a vessel. Pauri XIX teaches that each person bears the result of their own acts, should avoid pride, call no one bad, and not dispute with a fool.

Slok XX .. Pauri XX body

Guru Nanak teaches that evil people are rejected and punished in God's court, while good people devote themselves to God. Those who are inwardly foul remain impure despite ritual bathing, whereas those who love God may weep, laugh, or remain silent in devotion. God created the world, governs its changes, and will judge the accounts of the wicked. Everyone must eventually depart, so people should arrange their affairs with God in mind.

Slok XXI .. Pauri XXI body

Guru Angad teaches that true love is absorption in God rather than attachment to worldly things. Nanak says that judging good and bad in this worldly manner is not true love. Guru Angad also teaches that saluting while criticizing God's works is vain and prevents a person from obtaining a place in God's court. The Pauri urges remembrance and worship of the Lord, avoidance of evil deeds, and careful conduct to gain profit with the Lord.

Slok XXII .. Pauri XXII body

Slok XXII, attributed to Guru Angad, teaches that a servant should efface himself, perform service faithfully, and avoid pride, quarrelsomeness, and excessive talk. It says that a person's heart reveals itself, that poison does not yield ambrosia, and that friendship with a fool is unprofitable and short-lived. It contrasts falsehood with the pleasure of praising God and says that supplication, not command, is appropriate toward God. Pauri XXII says that a servant who follows the master's wishes receives greater honour and double wages, while one who competes with the master loses his salary and is beaten.

Slok XXIII .. Pauri XXIII body

Guru Angad teaches that a gift obtained through personal asking differs from the wonderful gift received when the Lord is pleased. He also teaches that true service is marked by absorption in the love of the master rather than freedom from fear. Nanak says that God's end and boundaries are unseen, and that God Himself creates and destroys. The unit says that God causes people to act, and that He who made the world takes care of it.

Slok XXIV .. Pauri XXIV body

The unit concerns God’s creation, ordering, sustenance, and power over all beings. Guru Nanak teaches that God makes and fills vessels, determines their conditions, and regenerates those upon whom He looks with favour. Guru Angad states that God arranges all things and oversees the birth and death of animals. The Pauri declares that God’s greatness cannot be expressed, that man must do the work destined for him, and that God alone is the abiding Place.
