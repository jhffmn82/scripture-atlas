1 Chronicles

author unknown · 449 to 399 BC · 29 sections
1 Chronicles, from the World English Bible, Catholic Edition, dedicated to the public domain by eBible.org. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://ebible.org/eng-web-c/

Abstract written by a model

1 Chronicles 1-29 is a Hebrew-scripture history that opens with long genealogies and then centers on King David's reign and his preparations for the temple. Chapters 1-9 trace the human line from Adam through Noah's sons, Abraham, Isaac and Israel, then the twelve tribes. Judah and David's descendants (including the royal line to the exile) receive special attention, along with Simeon, Reuben, Gad, Manasseh, Levi (priests, singers, cities), Issachar, Benjamin, Ephraim and Asher. Reuben's birthright passes to Joseph's sons, and the eastern tribes are exiled by Assyria for trespass. Chapter 9 lists those resettled in Jerusalem after Babylon, including priests, Levites and gatekeepers. Chapter 10 turns to narrative: Saul and his sons die at Gilboa against the Philistines, and the LORD gives the kingdom to David. In chapters 11-12 all Israel anoints David at Hebron, he takes Jerusalem's Zion stronghold with Joab as chief, and his mighty men and the tribal warriors who came to make him king are listed. Chapters 13-16 describe the ark: Uzza dies when it is moved on a cart, it rests with Obed-Edom, and David then brings it to Jerusalem carried properly by Levites with music, sacrifices and a thanksgiving psalm, while Michal despises him. Chapter 14 records Hiram's help and victories over the Philistines. In chapter 17 Nathan relays God's refusal to let David build a house; instead God promises David a house and an enduring throne through an offspring who will build the temple. Chapters 18-20 recount David's wars against Philistines, Moab, Zobah, Syria, Edom and Ammon, with Joab and Abishai prominent. Chapter 21 describes David's census prompted by Satan, the pestilence that kills seventy thousand, and his purchase of Ornan's threshing floor, where he builds an altar. Chapters 22-29 show David preparing for Solomon: he stockpiles materials, explains that bloodshed barred him from building, and charges Solomon. He organizes Levites, priestly divisions, singers, gatekeepers, treasurers, army divisions and officials. He gives Solomon the temple plans, and he and the leaders donate lavishly. The book closes with David's prayer, Solomon's second anointing as king, and David's death after forty years of reign.

1 Chronicles / Chapter 1 body

1 Chronicles 1 opens a genealogy running from Adam through Seth, Enosh and the line down to Noah, whose sons are Shem, Ham, and Japheth. It lists the descendants of Japheth, Ham (including Cush, who fathered Nimrod, and Mizraim, from whom the Philistines came), and Shem down through Arpachshad, Eber, Peleg and Joktan to Abram, also called Abraham. It then lists Abraham's sons Isaac and Ishmael, the sons of Ishmael, and the sons of Keturah, Abraham's concubine. Isaac's sons Esau and Israel are named, followed by Esau's descendants and the sons of Seir. The chapter ends with the kings who reigned in Edom before any king reigned over Israel and the chiefs of Edom.

Chapter 2 body

Chapter 2 lists the twelve sons of Israel and then concentrates on the descendants of Judah. Judah's sons Er, Onan, Shelah, Perez and Zerah are named, with Er killed for wickedness and Tamar bearing Perez and Zerah. The line of Hezron is traced through Ram to Jesse and his seven sons, ending with David, along with Jesse's daughters Zeruiah and Abigail and their children. The chapter then gives the branches of Hezron's sons Jerahmeel and Caleb, including Sheshan's daughters and his Egyptian servant Jarha, and closes with Caleb's descendants and the families tied to Kiriath Jearim, Bethlehem and the scribes at Jabez.

Chapter 3 body

Chapter 3 lists the descendants of David. It first names the six sons born to him in Hebron, where he reigned seven years and six months, and then the sons born in Jerusalem, where he reigned thirty-three years, including Solomon by Bathshua. It then traces the royal line from Solomon through Rehoboam down to Josiah, and names Josiah's four sons. Finally it follows the line through Jehoiakim and Jeconiah the captive, then Pedaiah and Zerubbabel, and on to later generations ending with the seven sons of Elioenai.

Chapter 4 body

Chapter 4 continues the genealogies of the sons of Judah, listing descendants of Perez, Hur, Ashhur, Kenaz, Caleb, Ezrah and Shelah, including various families of Zorathites, craftsmen, potters and linen workers. It pauses to note Jabez, who was more honorable than his brothers and whose prayer for blessing and protection God granted. The chapter then lists the sons of Simeon and their descendants, noting that Shimei had many children but the family did not multiply like Judah. It names the cities where Simeon's families lived until David's reign. In the days of Hezekiah, princes of Simeon's families found good pasture near Gedor and destroyed the Meunim there, and five hundred Simeonites went to Mount Seir and struck the remnant of the Amalekites.

Chapter 5 body

Chapter 5 records the genealogy of Reuben, Israel's firstborn, whose birthright passed to Joseph's sons because he defiled his father's couch, while Judah prevailed and produced the prince. It lists Reuben's sons and the line of Joel down to Beerah, whom Tilgath Pilneser of Assyria carried away captive, and it names the Gadites living in Bashan. In the days of Saul and later, Reuben, Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh fought the Hagrites, Jetur, Naphish, and Nodab, crying to God and trusting him, and they took vast plunder in livestock and men. The half-tribe of Manasseh grew from Bashan to Mount Hermon and listed its mighty heads of houses, but they trespassed against God by following the gods of the land's peoples. God therefore stirred up Pul and Tilgath Pilneser, kings of Assyria, who carried the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh to Halah, Habor, Hara, and the river of Gozan.

Chapter 6 body

Chapter 6 lists the descendants of Levi through his sons Gershom (Gershon), Kohath, and Merari, tracing the high-priestly line from Aaron's son Eleazar down to Jehozadak, who went into captivity under Nebuchadnezzar. It names the Levites whom David set over the service of song after the ark came to rest, with Heman of the Kohathites, Asaph of the Gershomites, and Ethan of the Merarites. It says Aaron and his sons made offerings and atonement for Israel as Moses commanded, while the other Levites served the tabernacle. The chapter ends by listing the cities and pasture lands given by lot to the Aaronite priests and the Kohathite, Gershomite, and Merarite families from the tribes of Israel.

Chapter 7 body

Chapter 7 gives genealogies and military counts for several tribes. It lists the sons of Issachar (Tola, Puah, Jashub, Shimron), the sons of Benjamin (Bela, Becher, Jediael), and brief entries for Naphtali. It then covers Manasseh's line through Machir and Gilead, and Ephraim's line, including the killing of his sons by the men of Gath, the birth of Beriah, his daughter Sheerah's building of towns, and the descent down to Joshua. The chapter lists the territories where Joseph's children lived and closes with the sons of Asher and a count of twenty-six thousand men for war.

Chapter 8 body

Chapter 8 gives genealogies of the tribe of Benjamin. It begins with Benjamin's five sons and the sons of Bela, then lists the heads of households of Geba and the descendants of Shaharaim, including the sons of Elpaal, Beriah, Shimei, and Shashak, who lived in Jerusalem. It then names the family of the father of Gibeon, who lived in Gibeon and Jerusalem. The chapter traces Ner to Kish to Saul and Saul's sons, continues through Jonathan's son Merib-baal down to Azel's six sons and the sons of Eshek, and ends by noting that Ulam's sons were mighty archers with 150 sons and grandsons.

Chapter 9 body

Chapter 9 records the genealogies of Israel and lists those who resettled in Jerusalem after Judah's captivity in Babylon. It names residents from Judah, Benjamin, Ephraim and Manasseh, then the priests (1,760 able men) and Levites who lived there. A long section details the gatekeepers, 212 chosen ones descended from the Korahites, whom David and Samuel ordained, and their duties guarding the gates, rooms, treasuries, vessels, and provisions of God's house. The chapter also mentions the singers who lived in the rooms and worked day and night, and closes by repeating the Benjamite line of Jeiel and Ner through Saul, Jonathan, and Merib-baal down to Azel's six sons.

Chapter 10 body

The Philistines fight Israel at Mount Gilboa, and the men of Israel flee and fall slain. The Philistines kill Saul's sons Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchishua, and archers overtake Saul, who then falls on his own sword after his armor bearer refuses to kill him. The armor bearer also falls on his sword, and the Israelites abandon their cities to the Philistines. The Philistines strip Saul's body, take his head and armor, and display them in the houses of their gods and of Dagon. The valiant men of Jabesh Gilead retrieve the bodies of Saul and his sons, bury their bones, and fast seven days; the chapter explains that Saul died for his trespass against the LORD, who turned the kingdom over to David the son of Jesse.

Chapter 11 body

All Israel gathers to David at Hebron, affirms he is their bone and flesh, and with the elders anoints him king after he makes a covenant with them before the LORD. David and Israel then go to Jerusalem (Jebus), where the Jebusites taunt him, but he takes the stronghold of Zion, which becomes David's city; Joab goes up first and is made chief. David grows greater because the LORD of Armies is with him. The chapter then lists David's mighty men, including Jashobeam, Eleazar, Abishai, and Benaiah, and recounts how three of them broke through the Philistine army to draw water from the well of Bethlehem for David, who poured it out to the LORD rather than drink it. A long roster of further mighty men closes the chapter.

Chapter 12 body

Chapter 12 lists the warriors who joined David while he was a fugitive from Saul at Ziklag and in the wilderness stronghold. These included skilled archers and slingers from Saul's own tribe of Benjamin, lion-faced Gadite captains, men of Judah and Benjamin whom David tested and whom Amasai pledged to him, and Manasseh captains who helped him against raiders. The chapter then gives tribal troop counts of those who came armed to Hebron to turn Saul's kingdom to David according to the LORD's word, among them Judah, Simeon, Levi, Ephraim, Issachar, Zebulun, Naphtali, Dan, Asher and the tribes east of the Jordan. All came with a whole heart to make David king over all Israel. They feasted with him three days on provisions that neighboring tribes brought in abundance, for there was joy in Israel.

Chapter 13 body

David consults the leaders of Israel and proposes, with the assembly's approval, to bring God's ark back because it was not sought in the days of Saul. He gathers all Israel and goes to Baalah, also called Kiriath Jearim, to fetch the ark. The ark is carried on a new cart from Abinadab's house, driven by Uzza and Ahio, while David and all Israel celebrate with music. At Chidon's threshing floor, Uzza puts out his hand to steady the ark when the oxen stumble, and the LORD strikes him dead. Afraid, David names the place Perez Uzza and diverts the ark to Obed-Edom the Gittite's house, where it stays three months and the LORD blesses that household.

Chapter 14 body

Hiram king of Tyre sends messengers to David with cedar trees, masons, and carpenters to build him a house, and David perceives that the LORD has established him as king over Israel. David takes more wives in Jerusalem and fathers more children, whose names are listed. When the Philistines go up to seek David after he is anointed king over all Israel, he inquires of God and is told to go up, and he defeats them at Baal Perazim, burning their abandoned gods. When the Philistines raid the valley again, God directs David to circle around and attack opposite the mulberry trees at the sound of marching, and David strikes the Philistine army from Gibeon to Gezer. David's fame spreads into all lands, and the LORD brings fear of him on all nations.

Chapter 15 body

David builds houses in David's city and prepares a tent for God's ark, declaring that only the Levites may carry it. He assembles all Israel at Jerusalem and gathers the sons of Aaron and the Levite family heads, including Uriel, Asaiah, Joel, Shemaiah, Eliel, and Amminadab, along with the priests Zadok and Abiathar, telling them to sanctify themselves because the LORD's earlier anger came from not seeking him according to the ordinance. The Levites carry the ark on their shoulders with poles as Moses commanded, while David has Levite singers and instrumentalists appointed, led by Heman, Asaph, and Ethan, with Chenaniah over the singing and priests blowing trumpets. David, the elders, and the captains bring the ark up from the house of Obed-Edom with joy, sacrificing seven bulls and seven rams, and all Israel shouts and sounds instruments. As the ark enters David's city, Michal the daughter of Saul looks from a window, sees king David dancing and playing, and despises him in her heart.

Chapter 16 body

The ark of God is brought into the tent David pitched for it, and David offers burnt offerings and peace offerings, blesses the people in the LORD's name, and gives every Israelite bread, meat, and a raisin cake. He appoints Levites, with Asaph as chief, to minister before the ark with instruments, while priests Benaiah and Jahaziel blow trumpets continually. On that day David first ordains a song of thanksgiving through Asaph and his brothers, which calls Israel to seek, remember, and praise the LORD for his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and calls all the earth to worship him as creator above idols. The people say "Amen" and praise the LORD. David leaves Asaph and his brothers at the ark, places Obed-Edom and others as doorkeepers, and stations Zadok and the priests at the tabernacle at Gibeon for daily offerings, with Heman and Jeduthun also giving thanks. Then everyone goes home and David returns to bless his house.

Chapter 17 body

David tells Nathan the prophet that he lives in a cedar house while the ark of the LORD's covenant sits in a tent, and Nathan at first tells him to do all that is in his heart. That night God sends Nathan to tell David he will not build a house for the LORD, who has gone from tent to tent and never asked any judge of Israel for a cedar house. The LORD recounts taking David from the sheep pen to be prince over Israel, promises to make his name great, to plant Israel securely in its own place, and to build David a house. He promises to set up David's offspring after him, who will build the LORD's house and have a throne established forever, with the LORD as his father and loving kindness never taken from him. David sits before the LORD and responds in prayer, marveling at the honor, praising the LORD's uniqueness and Israel's redemption from Egypt, and asking that the promise be established forever.

Chapter 18 body

David defeats the Philistines, taking Gath and its towns, and subdues Moab, which then pays tribute. He defeats Hadadezer king of Zobah, capturing chariots, horsemen, footmen, gold shields, and much bronze, and strikes the Syrians of Damascus who come to help Hadadezer, then garrisons Syria. Tou king of Hamath sends his son Hadoram with gifts of gold, silver, and bronze, and David dedicates these and other spoils from many nations to the LORD. Abishai the son of Zeruiah strikes eighteen thousand Edomites and garrisons Edom. The chapter closes by noting that David reigned over all Israel with justice and listing his officials: Joab, Jehoshaphat, Zadok, Abimelech, Shavsha, Benaiah, and David's sons.

Chapter 19 body

After Nahash king of the Ammonites died, David sent messengers to comfort his son Hanun, but Ammonite princes persuaded Hanun that they were spies, and he shaved them and cut off their garments before sending them away. David told the humiliated men to wait at Jericho until their beards grew back. Realizing they had made themselves odious to David, the Ammonites hired chariots and horsemen with a thousand talents of silver from Mesopotamia, Aram-maacah, and Zobah. Joab led Israel's army, splitting it with his brother Abishai, and both the Syrians and the Ammonites fled. When the Syrians regrouped under Shophach, David crossed the Jordan and defeated them, killing Shophach, after which Hadadezer's servants made peace with David and served him.

Chapter 20 body

At the season when kings go out to war, Joab led the army against the children of Ammon, wasted their country, besieged Rabbah, and overthrew it, while David stayed at Jerusalem. David took the Ammonite king's golden, jewel-set crown, which was placed on his own head, carried off much plunder, and had the people of the city cut with saws, iron picks, and axes, doing the same to all the Ammonite cities before returning to Jerusalem. Wars with the Philistines followed: Sibbecai the Hushathite killed Sippai, Elhanan the son of Jair killed Lahmi the brother of Goliath, and Jonathan the son of Shimea, David's brother, killed a man of great stature at Gath with twenty-four fingers and toes. The chapter closes by stating that these giants born in Gath fell by the hand of David and his servants.

Chapter 21 body

Satan moves David to order a census of Israel, and David sends Joab to count the people despite Joab's protest that it will bring guilt on Israel. Joab reports 1,100,000 swordsmen in Israel and 470,000 in Judah, omitting Levi and Benjamin, and God, displeased, strikes Israel. David confesses his sin, and through the seer Gad the LORD offers him three punishments; David chooses to fall into the LORD's hand, and a pestilence kills seventy thousand men. As the LORD's angel is about to destroy Jerusalem, the LORD relents, and David pleads that the punishment fall on himself and his house rather than on the people. At the angel's command David buys Ornan the Jebusite's threshing floor for six hundred shekels of gold, builds an altar, and offers sacrifices, and the LORD answers by fire and has the angel sheathe his sword.

Chapter 22 body

David declares the site to be the house of the LORD God and the altar of burnt offering for Israel, then gathers foreigners and masons and stockpiles iron, bronze, cedar, gold, and silver for the temple because his son Solomon is young and tender. He summons Solomon and tells him that the LORD forbade David to build the house because he had shed much blood in great wars. The LORD promised a peaceful son named Solomon who would build the house, be God's son, and have his throne established over Israel forever. David charges Solomon to keep the law, be strong and courageous, and begin the work with the abundant materials and skilled workmen provided. He also commands Israel's princes to help Solomon and to bring the ark of the covenant and holy vessels into the new house.

Chapter 23 body

In old age David makes his son Solomon king over Israel and assembles the princes, priests, and Levites. The Levites aged thirty and up are counted at thirty-eight thousand, and David assigns them to oversee the LORD's house, serve as officers and judges, act as doorkeepers, and praise the LORD with instruments. They are divided by the three sons of Levi, Gershon, Kohath, and Merari, with the family heads listed under each, including Aaron and Moses among the Kohathites. Aaron and his sons are set apart for the most holy things, incense, ministry, and blessing, while Moses's sons are counted among the tribe of Levi. Because the LORD has given rest to his people and dwells in Jerusalem, David's last words lower the counting age to twenty and the Levites no longer carry the tabernacle, instead assisting the sons of Aaron in the house's service, offerings, and daily praise.

Chapter 24 body

Chapter 24 records how David, with Zadok of the sons of Eleazar and Ahimelech of the sons of Ithamar, divided the sons of Aaron into twenty-four orders for priestly service. Nadab and Abihu died childless before their father, so only Eleazar and Ithamar served as priests; Eleazar's line had sixteen chief houses and Ithamar's eight. The divisions were assigned impartially by lot, recorded by the scribe Shemaiah the son of Nethanel before the king, the princes, Zadok, Ahimelech, and the heads of the fathers' houses, with the lots running from Jehoiarib first to Maaziah twenty-fourth. The chapter then lists the remaining Levite houses, including descendants of Amram, Izhar, Hebron, Uzziel, and Merari. These too cast lots in the same way, the chief's house and the younger brother's house being treated alike.

Chapter 25 body

David and the captains of the army set apart certain sons of Asaph, Heman, and Jeduthun to prophesy with harps, stringed instruments, and cymbals in the service of God's house. The chapter lists the sons of Asaph (four), Jeduthun (six), and Heman (fourteen, with three daughters), all serving under their fathers and under the king's order. Together with their brothers, the skilled and instructed singers numbered two hundred eighty-eight. They cast lots for their offices, with small and great, teacher and student treated alike. The lots produced twenty-four divisions, each of twelve sons and brothers, assigned in order from Joseph of Asaph's house to Romamti-Ezer.

Chapter 26 body

Chapter 26 lists the Levitical doorkeepers and treasurers of David's organization. The doorkeeper divisions come from the Korahites (Meshelemiah, Obed-Edom) and from Merari (Hosah), and lots were cast by fathers' houses to assign each gate: east, north, south, west and the storehouse. Other Levites were put over the treasuries of God's house and the dedicated things, with Shelomoth and his brothers in charge of the dedicated gifts given by David, Samuel, Saul, Abner, Joab and others. The chapter closes with Chenaniah, Hashabiah and Jerijah of the Izharites and Hebronites, who were appointed as officers, judges and overseers for Israel on both sides of the Jordan, in matters of the LORD and the king.

Chapter 27 body

Chapter 27 lists the organization of Israel's king David's administration. Twelve army divisions of twenty-four thousand each served month by month, each under a named captain such as Jashobeam, Dodai, Benaiah, and Asahel. The chapter also names a ruler over each tribe of Israel, notes that David did not count those twenty and under and that Joab's unfinished census brought wrath on Israel and was not recorded in the chronicles. It then lists officials over King David's treasures, fields, vineyards, trees, herds, camels, donkeys, and flocks, and closes with David's counselors Jonathan, Jehiel, Ahithophel, Hushai, Jehoiada, and Abiathar, with Joab as captain of the army.

Chapter 28 body

David gathers all the princes, captains, rulers, officers, and mighty men of Israel at Jerusalem and addresses them. He explains that God forbade him to build the temple because he was a man of war who shed blood, and that the LORD chose Solomon to build it and to sit on the throne of the LORD's kingdom. David charges the assembly to keep the LORD's commandments and charges Solomon to serve God with a perfect heart, warning that forsaking him means being cast off forever. He gives Solomon the plans for the temple, its courts, rooms, treasuries, priestly divisions, and the weights of gold and silver for its vessels, which he says he received in writing from the LORD's hand. David then encourages Solomon to be strong and not fear, promising that God will be with him and that skilled volunteers, the priests, Levites, captains, and people will help him.

Chapter 29 body

King David tells the assembly that his young son Solomon has been chosen by God to build the temple, and he announces the gold, silver, and other materials he has prepared plus a personal gift of three thousand talents of gold and seven thousand talents of silver. The princes, tribal leaders, captains, and royal officers respond by giving large amounts of gold, silver, bronze, iron, and precious stones. David praises the LORD, acknowledges that everything comes from God, and prays that the people's hearts stay devoted and that Solomon receives a perfect heart. The assembly blesses the LORD, offers thousands of sacrifices, and makes Solomon king a second time, anointing him with Zadok as priest. The chapter closes with Solomon's rise, David's death after forty years of reign, and a note that David's acts are recorded in the histories of Samuel, Nathan, and Gad.
