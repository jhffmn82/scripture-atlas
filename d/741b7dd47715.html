The Gospel According to John

author unknown · 21 sections
The Gospel According to John, from the World English Bible, Catholic Edition, dedicated to the public domain by eBible.org. Shown here as plain text, with facts drawn from it. https://ebible.org/eng-web-c/

Abstract written by a model

This is the Gospel According to John, chapters 1 to 21, a narrative of the life, teaching, death and resurrection of Jesus, centered on his identity as the Word made flesh, the Son of God and the Christ. The most important figures are Jesus, John (the witness who baptizes), Simon Peter, Judas Iscariot, Thomas, Nicodemus, Mary Magdalene, Pilate, and the disciple whom Jesus loved, who is named at the close as the witness who wrote these things. Chapter 1 opens with the Word who was with God and was God, then John's testimony that Jesus is the Lamb of God, and the calling of Andrew, Peter, Philip and Nathanael. Chapters 2 to 4 present early signs and encounters: water turned to wine at Cana, the temple cleansing, Nicodemus told he must be born anew, the Samaritan woman at the well, and the healing of a nobleman's son. Chapters 5 to 10 show escalating conflict with the Jews. Jesus heals on the Sabbath, feeds five thousand, walks on water, and declares himself the bread of life, the light of the world, the door and the good shepherd. Many disciples turn back, Peter confesses him as the Christ, and he says 'before Abraham came into existence, I AM' and 'I and the Father are one.' Crowds divide, a blind man is healed, and attempts to stone or seize Jesus recur. Chapter 11 brings the raising of Lazarus, after which the chief priests and Pharisees, with Caiaphas speaking, resolve to kill Jesus. Chapter 12 has Mary anointing him, the entry into Jerusalem, and his warning to walk in the light. Chapters 13 to 17 take place with the disciples before the Passover. Jesus washes their feet, identifies Judas as the betrayer, gives a new commandment to love one another, and foretells Peter's denial. He comforts them as the way, the truth and the life, promises the Counselor, the Spirit of truth, calls himself the true vine, warns of persecution, and prays for his disciples and future believers to be one. Chapters 18 and 19 recount the arrest in the garden, Peter's three denials, the trial before Pilate, the flogging, the demand for crucifixion, the death at Golgotha with 'It is finished,' and the burial by Joseph of Arimathaea and Nicodemus. Chapter 20 tells of the empty tomb, Mary Magdalene meeting the risen Jesus, his appearance to the disciples, and Thomas declaring 'My Lord and my God!' Chapter 21 closes with Jesus appearing at the sea of Tiberias, the large catch of fish, breakfast on the beach, and Peter's three-fold commissioning to feed his sheep.

The Gospel According to John / Chapter 1 body

John chapter 1 opens by declaring that the Word was with God and was God, made all things, and became flesh as Jesus Christ, full of grace and truth. John, a man sent from God, testifies about the light, denies being the Christ, Elijah, or the prophet when priests and Levites from the Pharisees question him, and calls himself the voice crying in the wilderness. He identifies Jesus as the Lamb of God and the Son of God after seeing the Spirit descend on him like a dove. Two of John's disciples, one of them Andrew, follow Jesus, and Andrew brings his brother Simon, whom Jesus renames Cephas (Peter). Jesus then calls Philip, who finds Nathanael, and Nathanael confesses Jesus as Son of God and King of Israel after Jesus tells him he saw him under the fig tree.

Chapter 2 body

At a wedding in Cana of Galilee on the third day, Jesus' mother tells Jesus the wine has run out, and after the servants fill six stone water pots at his word, the water becomes wine that the ruler of the feast praises. John calls this the beginning of Jesus' signs, and his disciples believe in him. Jesus then goes down to Capernaum with his mother, brothers, and disciples, and later goes up to Jerusalem for the Passover. There he drives the sellers of oxen, sheep, and doves and the money changers out of the temple with a whip of cords, and the Jews ask for a sign. Jesus answers that if they destroy this temple he will raise it in three days, which the text explains refers to his body, and the disciples remember this after he is raised from the dead; many believe in his name, but Jesus does not entrust himself to them.

Chapter 3 body

Nicodemus, a Pharisee and ruler of the Jews, comes to Jesus by night and acknowledges him as a teacher from God. Jesus tells him that one must be born anew, of water and Spirit, to see and enter God's Kingdom, and Nicodemus is puzzled. Jesus explains that God so loved the world that he gave his only born Son so that whoever believes should not perish but have eternal life, and that people are judged by whether they come to the light or love the darkness. Jesus then goes into Judea and baptizes while John baptizes at Enon near Salim, and John's disciples report that everyone is going to Jesus. John replies that he is not the Christ, that he is the bridegroom's friend, and that Jesus must increase while he must decrease, adding that the Father loves the Son and that those who believe the Son have eternal life.

Chapter 4 body

Jesus leaves Judea after the Pharisees hear he is making more disciples than John, and passes through Samaria, where he rests at Jacob's well near Sychar. There he speaks with a Samaritan woman, offering her living water, telling her about her five husbands, teaching that true worshipers worship the Father in spirit and truth, and revealing that he is the Messiah. The woman tells her city, many Samaritans believe, and after Jesus stays two days they declare him the Savior of the world. Jesus then goes to Galilee, where a nobleman from Capernaum begs him to heal his dying son. Jesus says the son lives, the man believes and goes home, and his servants confirm the child recovered at the very hour Jesus spoke, in what is called the second sign Jesus did after coming out of Judea.

Chapter 5 body

Jesus goes up to Jerusalem for a feast and at the Bethesda pool heals a man who had been sick for thirty-eight years, telling him to take up his mat and walk. Because it is the Sabbath, the Jews object to the man carrying his mat, and after the man learns who healed him and tells them it was Jesus, they persecute and seek to kill Jesus. Jesus answers that his Father is still working and so is he, and he describes the Son's dependence on the Father, his power to give life and to judge, and the coming resurrection of life or of judgment. He then lists the witnesses to himself: John, his works, the Father, and the Scriptures. He concludes that the Jews will not come to him for life and that Moses, in whom they hope, will accuse them.

Chapter 6 body

Jesus crosses the sea of Galilee, where a great multitude follows him, and he feeds about five thousand men with a boy's five barley loaves and two fish, with twelve baskets left over. The multitude wants to make him king, so he withdraws to the mountain, and his disciples cross the sea at night until he comes to them walking on the water. The next day the multitude finds him at Capernaum, and he tells them to seek the food that remains to eternal life, declaring himself the bread of life who came down from heaven and whose flesh and blood give life. The Jews murmur and contend, many disciples turn back saying it is a hard saying, and Simon Peter, answering for the twelve, confesses Jesus as the Christ, the Son of the living God. Jesus then says that one of the twelve is a devil, referring to Judas, son of Simon Iscariot, who would betray him.

Chapter 7 body

During the Feast of Booths, Jesus stays in Galilee to avoid the Jews who seek to kill him, rejects his unbelieving brothers' urging to go to Judea publicly, then goes up secretly and teaches in the temple midway through the feast. He says his teaching comes from the one who sent him, defends his Sabbath healing, and tells the crowd he will be with them only a little while longer. The multitude is divided over whether he is a good man, the prophet, or the Christ, with some objecting that he comes from Galilee. The Pharisees and chief priests send officers to arrest him, but the officers return without him, saying no man ever spoke like him. Nicodemus asks that the law be followed in hearing Jesus, and the others rebuke him before everyone goes home.

Chapter 8 body

In John 8, Jesus teaches in the temple, where the scribes and the Pharisees bring a woman taken in adultery and test him about stoning her; he challenges the sinless to throw the first stone, and after her accusers leave he tells her he does not condemn her and to sin no more. He then declares himself the light of the world, and the Pharisees dispute his testimony, which he says is validated by the Father who sent him. In a long dispute with the Jews, including those who had believed him, Jesus speaks of truth and freedom, sin as bondage, and their descent from Abraham versus the devil. The Jews accuse him of having a demon, and when he says Abraham rejoiced to see his day and 'before Abraham came into existence, I AM,' they take up stones, but he hides himself and leaves the temple.

Chapter 9 body

Jesus sees a man blind from birth and, after telling his disciples that the blindness is not due to sin but so that the works of God might be revealed, he anoints the man's eyes with mud made from spit and sends him to wash in the pool of Siloam. The man returns seeing, and his neighbors argue over whether he is the same person. Because the healing happened on a Sabbath, the Pharisees interrogate him, divide among themselves, and call his parents, who confirm he was born blind but defer to him out of fear of being put out of the synagogue. The healed man defends Jesus as a prophet and as someone from God, and the Pharisees insult him and throw him out. Jesus finds him, the man believes and worships him, and Jesus tells the Pharisees that their claim to see leaves their sin in place.

Chapter 10 body

In John chapter 10, Jesus gives a parable of the sheep fold and then explains it, calling himself the door of the sheep and the good shepherd who lays down his life for the sheep and will bring other sheep into one flock. The Jews are divided over his words, some saying he has a demon and others pointing to his healing of the blind. At the Feast of the Dedication in Solomon's porch, the Jews demand he say plainly whether he is the Christ, and Jesus points to his works, says his sheep hear him, and declares 'I and the Father are one.' The Jews take up stones for what they call blasphemy, Jesus answers from Scripture and his works, and they try to seize him but he escapes beyond the Jordan, where many believe in him.

Chapter 11 body

Lazarus of Bethany, the brother of Martha and Mary, falls sick, and Jesus delays two days before going to Judea despite his disciples' fear of the Jews. Jesus tells them Lazarus is dead, and Thomas urges the others to go and die with him. Martha and Mary each tell Jesus that Lazarus would not have died had he been there; Jesus tells Martha he is the resurrection and the life, weeps, and then calls Lazarus out of the tomb, who comes out bound in wrappings. Many of the Jews believe, but some report to the Pharisees, and the chief priests and Pharisees gather a council. Caiaphas says one man should die for the people, and they resolve to kill Jesus, who withdraws to Ephraim as the Passover nears and a command goes out to report where he is.

Chapter 12 body

In Chapter 12, six days before the Passover, Jesus comes to Bethany, where Mary anoints his feet with costly nard ointment at a supper attended by Martha and Lazarus. Judas Iscariot objects, and Jesus defends Mary, saying she kept it for the day of his burial. Crowds come to see Jesus and Lazarus, the chief priests plot to kill Lazarus, and Jesus enters Jerusalem on a young donkey to cries of 'Hosanna!' from a multitude carrying palm branches. When some Greeks ask to see him, Jesus says the time has come for the Son of Man to be glorified, speaks of the grain of wheat dying, and prays 'Father, glorify your name,' after which a voice from the sky answers. The chapter ends with Jesus' warning to walk in the light, the narrator's explanation of the people's unbelief through Isaiah, and Jesus' declaration that he came as light to save the world and speaks only what the Father commanded.

Chapter 13 body

Before the Passover, Jesus, knowing his time to return to the Father had come, rises from supper and washes his disciples' feet. Simon Peter first objects, then asks to be washed more fully, and Jesus tells him that the disciples are clean but not all of them, since he knows who will betray him. Jesus explains the washing as an example for the disciples to follow, then announces that one of them will betray him. He identifies Judas Iscariot by giving him a dipped piece of bread, Satan enters Judas, and Judas goes out into the night. Jesus then speaks of glorification, gives a new commandment that the disciples love one another, and tells Peter that Peter cannot follow him now and will deny him three times before the rooster crows.

Chapter 14 body

In this chapter Jesus comforts his disciples, telling them not to be troubled and that he is going to his Father's house to prepare a place for them and will come again to receive them. Thomas asks how they can know the way, and Jesus answers that he is the way, the truth, and the life and that no one comes to the Father except through him. Philip asks to be shown the Father, and Jesus replies that whoever has seen him has seen the Father, who lives in him and does his works. Jesus promises answered prayer in his name, ties love for him to keeping his commandments, and promises the Father will send another Counselor, the Spirit of truth, who will teach them and remind them of his words. He leaves his peace with them, explains that the Father is greater than he, says the prince of the world is coming, and ends by telling them to arise and go from there.

Chapter 15 body

In Chapter 15, Jesus describes himself as the true vine, his Father as the farmer, and his followers as branches who must remain in him to bear fruit. Branches that bear no fruit are taken away and burned, while fruitful ones are pruned to bear more. Jesus commands his followers to remain in his love and to love one another as he has loved them, calling them friends because he has made known to them everything he heard from his Father. He warns that the world will hate and persecute them as it hated him, because they are not of the world and the world does not know the one who sent him. He promises that the Counselor, the Spirit of truth, will testify about him, and that the disciples will also testify because they have been with him from the beginning.

Chapter 16 body

In Chapter 16, Jesus tells his disciples that they will be put out of the synagogues and killed by people who do not know the Father or him, and he explains that he is telling them this so they will remember and not stumble. He says it is to their advantage that he goes away, because then he will send the Counselor, the Spirit of truth, who will convict the world about sin, righteousness, and judgment and guide the disciples into all truth. Jesus speaks of 'a little while' when they will not see him and then will see him again, and the confused disciples are told their sorrow will turn into joy, like a woman's anguish in childbirth giving way to joy. He promises that they may ask the Father in his name and that the Father himself loves them. When the disciples claim they now believe because he speaks plainly, Jesus warns that they will be scattered and leave him alone, yet he is not alone because the Father is with him, and he tells them to cheer up because he has overcome the world.

Chapter 17 body

In Chapter 17, Jesus lifts his eyes to heaven and prays to the Father. He asks to be glorified so that he may glorify the Father, and says he has finished the work given to him. He prays for the disciples whom the Father gave him out of the world, asking that the Father keep them from the evil one, sanctify them in truth, and make them one. He then extends the prayer to those who will believe through the disciples' word, asking that they be one in the Father and Son so that the world may believe he was sent. He closes by desiring that they be with him to see his glory, and says he has made the Father's name known and will keep doing so, so that the Father's love may be in them.

Chapter 18 body

Jesus goes with his disciples to a garden across the Kidron, where Judas arrives with a detachment of soldiers and officers from the chief priests and Pharisees. Jesus identifies himself, the group falls to the ground, and he asks that his disciples be let go; Peter strikes Malchus, the high priest's servant, and is told to put away his sword. Jesus is bound and taken to Annas, then Caiaphas, while Peter denies being a disciple three times before the rooster crows. The Jews bring Jesus to Pilate at the Praetorium, where Pilate questions him about kingship and truth, finds no basis for a charge, and offers to release him, but the crowd demands Barabbas instead.

Chapter 19 body

Pilate has Jesus flogged, and the soldiers mock him with a crown of thorns and a purple garment. Pilate presents him to the chief priests and officers, who demand crucifixion, claiming he made himself the Son of God and pressing Pilate with the charge that he opposes Caesar. Pilate yields and delivers Jesus to be crucified at Golgotha between two others, with a title in Hebrew, Latin, and Greek reading "JESUS OF NAZARETH, THE KING OF THE JEWS." The soldiers divide his garments, Jesus entrusts his mother to the disciple he loved, says "It is finished," and dies; a soldier pierces his side. Joseph of Arimathaea and Nicodemus then bind his body with spices and lay it in a new tomb in a nearby garden.

Chapter 20 body

On the first day of the week, Mary Magdalene finds the stone removed from the tomb and tells Simon Peter and the other disciple whom Jesus loved, who run to the tomb, find the linen cloths, and the other disciple believes. Mary stays weeping, sees two angels, then meets Jesus, whom she at first takes for the gardener, and recognizes him when he calls her name. Jesus sends her to tell his brothers he is ascending to his Father, and she reports to the disciples. That evening Jesus appears to the disciples behind locked doors, shows his hands and side, sends them, and breathes the Holy Spirit on them. A week later he appears again for Thomas, who had doubted, and Thomas declares 'My Lord and my God!'; the chapter closes by stating the purpose of the written signs.

Chapter 21 body

Jesus reveals himself again to seven disciples at the sea of Tiberias, including Simon Peter, Thomas, Nathanael, and the sons of Zebedee. After a night of catching nothing, they follow Jesus' instruction from the beach to cast the net on the right side and haul in one hundred fifty-three fish without the net tearing. The disciple whom Jesus loved recognizes the Lord, Peter throws himself into the sea, and Jesus serves the disciples bread and fish for breakfast. Jesus then asks Peter three times whether he loves him, commissions him to feed his lambs and sheep, and signifies the kind of death by which Peter will glorify God, telling him to follow. When Peter asks about the beloved disciple, Jesus says it is no concern of Peter's, and the text clarifies that Jesus did not say that disciple wouldn't die; the chapter closes by identifying that disciple as the witness who wrote these things.
