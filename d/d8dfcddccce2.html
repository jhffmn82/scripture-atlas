Letter to Titus / Chapter 1 body

Paul, a servant of God and apostle of Jesus Christ, writes to Titus, whom he calls his true child in a common faith, greeting him with grace, mercy, and peace and recalling God's promise of eternal life. Paul explains that he left Titus in Crete to set in order what was lacking and to appoint elders in every city. He lists the qualifications for these elders, or overseers: blameless, the husband of one wife, with believing children, hospitable, self-controlled, and holding to the faithful word so as to exhort in sound doctrine and convict those who contradict. He warns of many unruly men, vain talkers and deceivers, especially those of the circumcision, who overthrow whole houses for dishonest gain and must be silenced. Quoting one of their own prophets, who called Cretans liars, evil beasts, and idle gluttons, Paul tells Titus to reprove them sharply so they may be sound in the faith, and he describes the defiled as professing God while denying him by their deeds.

Chapter 2 body

Chapter 2 instructs the addressee to speak what fits sound doctrine and to teach each group in the community. Older men are to be temperate, sensible, and sound in faith, love, and perseverance. Older women are to be reverent and good teachers who train young wives to love their husbands and children and to be chaste, kind, and workers at home. Younger men are to be sober minded, and servants are to be subject to their masters, honest, and faithful. The addressee must be an example of good works with sound speech, and the chapter grounds this conduct in the grace of God, which brings salvation, teaches godly living, and looks for the appearing of God and Savior Jesus Christ, who gave himself to redeem and purify a people zealous for good works.

Chapter 3 body

Chapter 3 instructs the readers to submit to rulers and authorities, to be gentle and humble toward all, and to be ready for every good work. It recalls that the believers were once foolish, disobedient and hateful, but that God our Savior, through his kindness and mercy and not their works, saved them by the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit poured out through Jesus Christ our Savior, making them heirs of eternal life. The writer urges that believers be careful to maintain good works, shun foolish questionings, genealogies, strife and disputes about the law, and avoid a factious man after two warnings. He closes with instructions to come to him at Nicopolis when Artemas or Tychicus is sent, to speed Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their way, and with greetings and a blessing of grace.
