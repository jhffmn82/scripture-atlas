This section, in brief written by a model
Dai Ying Zhi tells Mencius that he cannot immediately levy only a tithe and abolish duties at passes and markets. He proposes lightening the tax and duties until next year, then ending them. Mencius compares this delay to a man who takes his neighbor’s strayed fowls and argues that known unrighteousness should be ended without waiting.

Chapter 8 Dai Ying Zhi said to Mencius, 'I am not able at present and immediately to do with the levying of a tithe only, and abolishing the duties charged at the passes and in the markets. With your leave I will lighten, however, both the tax and the duties, until next year, and will then make an end of them. What do you think of such a course?' Mencius said, 'Here is a man, who every day appropriates some of his neighbour's strayed fowls. Some one says to him, "Such is not the way of a good man;" and he replies, "With your leave I will diminish my appropriations, and will take only one fowl a month, until next year, when I will make an end of the practice." If you know that the thing is unrighteous, then use all despatch in putting an end to it - why wait till next year?'
