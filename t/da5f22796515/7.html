This section, in brief written by a model
Mencius says that Zi Lu rejoiced when told of his faults, while Yu bowed when he heard good words. Shun delighted in what was good, regarded virtue as common to himself and others, and gave up his own way to follow others. From his early work as a farmer, potter, and fisherman until he became emperor, Shun continually learned from others. Mencius teaches that taking example from others to practise virtue helps them practise it, and that helping men practise virtue is the greatest attribute of the superior man.

Chapter 8 Mencius said, 'When any one told Zi Lu that he had a fault, he rejoiced. When Yu heard good words, he bowed to the speaker. The great Shun had a still greater delight in what was good. He regarded virtue as the common property of himself and others, giving up his own way to follow that of others, and delighting to learn from others to practise what was good. From the time when he ploughed and sowed, exercised the potter's art, and was a fisherman, to the time when he became emperor, he was continually learning from others. To take example from others to practise virtue, is to help them in the same practice. Therefore, there is no attribute of the superior man greater than his helping men to practise virtue.'
