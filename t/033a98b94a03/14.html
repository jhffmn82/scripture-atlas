This section, in brief written by a model
Guru Nanak teaches that outward greatness, ritual, and bowing are worthless without inner purity and the guidance of the true Guru. The simmal-tree of the desert disappoints birds because its fruit, flowers, and leaves are useless, while a lowly tree with sweet fruit has exquisite qualities. He warns that the wicked man may bow repeatedly while retaining an impure heart, and that people must leave their beauty and possessions behind and face the results of their good and bad deeds. The dead must travel the narrow road, may be sent naked to hell, and will regret their sins.

Slok XIV Guru Nanak The simmal-tree of the desert is very tall and very thick. Why should the birds which go to it with hopes depart disappointed? Because its fruit is insipid, its flowers unwholesome, and its leaves useless. The tree which yieldeth sweet fruit is lowly, O Nanak, but its qualities and virtues are exquisite. Every one boweth to himself; no one boweth to another. If anything be put into a scale and weighed, the side which descendeth is the heavier. The wicked man like a deer-stalker boweth twice more than any one else; But what availeth bowing the head, if the heart be impure? Guru Nanak You read books, perform your twilight devotions, argue, worship stones, and sit like cranes; You utter falsehoods as excellent jewels; you meditate on the Gayatri three times a day; You wear necklaces, put sacrificial marks on your foreheads, carry two dhotis, and put towels on your heads. If you knew God's designs, you would know that yours is verily a vain religion. Saith Nanak, verily reflect that without the true Guru you shall not find the way. Pauri XIV Raiment and pleasing beauty man must leave on earth and depart. Man shall obtain the fruit of the bad or good deeds he hath done: He may have exercised sovereignty to his heart's content, yet must he proceed by the narrow road. He shall be sent naked to hell, which will then appear very formidable to him; And he shall regret the sins he committed.
