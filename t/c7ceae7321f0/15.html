This section, in brief written by a model
The unit concerns a man’s conduct in bed, at home, in public, and at entertainments. He does not lie like a corpse in bed or put on formal deportment at home. He changes countenance or salutes ceremoniously in the presence of people in mourning, people in full dress, blind people, population tables, abundant provisions, thunder, or violent wind. He bows forward to the crossbar of his carriage when greeting a person in mourning or someone bearing population tables.

CHAP. XVI. 1. In bed, he did not lie like a corpse. At home, he did not put on any formal deportment. 2. When he saw any one in a mourning dress, though it might be an acquaintance, he would change countenance; when he saw any one wearing the cap of full dress, or a blind person, though he might be in his undress, he would salute them in a ceremonious manner. 3. To any person in mourning he bowed forward to the crossbar of his carriage; he bowed in the same way to any one bearing the tables of population. 4. When he was at an entertainment where there was an abundance of provisions set before him, he would change countenance and rise up. 5. On a sudden clap of thunder, or a violent wind, he would change countenance.
