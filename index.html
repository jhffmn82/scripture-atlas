Scripture Atlas

The scriptures as a navigable knowledge graph, grounded in their own words
Walk

Scripture Atlas maps sacred texts into a graph you can walk: the people, places, events and ideas of each book, the facts the text states about them, and the timeline of each tradition's major events. A model organizes the text into the graph, but it is never the source of truth: the text is. Every fact on this site links to the verbatim words it was read from, and every quotation opens the chapter it came from. The summaries and timelines around the facts are written by a model, and every page marks them so.

Nothing is merged. When the same person, place or event appears in two books, one page links both, and each claim stays with the book that makes it. The search below runs in your browser over every fact, summary and verse: by keywords, by meaning, or both, and it can walk the graph from what it finds.

About

Each religion is one collection. The Bible is the World English Bible, Catholic Edition: the 73 books of the Catholic canon in their traditional order, a modern English translation dedicated to the public domain by eBible.org. Its footnotes are left out; the words of the text are as eBible.org prints them.

The site is built by the ThreadAtlas pipeline, a knowledge graph designed so that a person can check every claim against its source. Claude Sonnet read the Bible, found its entities and facts, and wrote their summaries; OpenAI's GPT-5.6 models (Luna and Terra) read the other texts, judged which entities across books are the same one, and placed the timelines. Code located every quotation in the text before a fact was kept.

A timeline places each collection's most salient events in the order its own story tells them. Salience is counted from the text: how many chapters, across every book of the collection, carry an account of the event. The order, the eras and any traditional date are a model's placement, marked as such, and each event opens its page, its claims and their verses.

The store this site was built from holds 102 documents. 102 are the books and papers on this site: The Bible, 73 works; Confucian Classics: Analects, Great Learning, Doctrine of the Mean, and Mencius, 29 works. Across all of it, 6,387 entities link their appearances in different documents.

How it works

  1. Extraction. Each book is read on its own and cut into its chapters. Every boundary must copy text found on the line it cites.
  2. Ingestion. Per book: the entities, the facts with verbatim quotes, an account of each entity in each chapter, and summaries. A fact is kept only if its quote is found in its chapter's text.
  3. Global merge. Which entities in different books are the same one? A judge model rules on each offered pair; one page links the instances without merging them. Each religion is its own collection, sorted by a model into groups of books.
  4. Timeline. Each collection's events, ranked by how many chapters tell of them, placed by a model in the order of the tradition's own story.
  5. Wiki. This site, rendered from the store by one deterministic pass: no page is written by hand.
